## The view

Naturalists use logic like everyone else. The disagreement is about what makes it binding, and there are several live answers.

**Realism.** Russell argued that the so-called laws of thought are misnamed. What matters "is not the fact that we think in accordance with these laws, but the fact that things behave in accordance with them." The law of contradiction "is not a thought, but a fact concerning the things in the world."<Cite source="russell-problems-philosophy" locator="chs. VII–VIII" /> On this view logic binds because reality has that structure, and we grasp it by a rational capacity.<Cite source="sep-logical-truth" locator="§1.1" />

**Convention.** Others have held that logical truths are true by convention or in virtue of meaning: they reflect the rules of our language. Quine pressed a famous objection. There are infinitely many logical truths and only finitely many conventions, so logic itself is needed to get from the conventions to the truths.<Cite source="sep-logical-truth" locator="§1.1" />

**Revisability.** Quine's own view was that logic sits at the centre of our web of belief. We would give it up last, but "no statement is immune to revision."<Cite source="sep-quine" locator="§3.3" note="Quine 1951, as quoted by SEP" /> Logic binds because it is so deeply woven into everything else we accept.

**A posteriori naturalism.** Recent naturalists such as Penelope Maddy hold that logical truths are known from experience, yet cannot be overturned by a single observation, because they are "deeply embedded in our conceptual machinery."<Cite source="sep-logical-truth" locator="§1.1" />

Some, finally, treat logical necessity as basic, needing no further ground. Russell himself called the laws of logic "self-evident logical principles" that "must be granted before any argument or proof becomes possible."<Cite source="russell-problems-philosophy" locator="ch. VII" />

## What this explains well

Each option takes seriously that logic is used by natural creatures. Realism explains why logic applies to the world; the other options explain how finite beings could know it without a mysterious faculty.

## Christian response

The Christian agrees with Russell that logic is discovered and holds of things. The pressure lies in the choice naturalism must make. Realism about necessary logical facts sits awkwardly in a physicalist picture of reality, which has trouble placing abstract truths.<Cite source="sep-physicalism" locator="§5.5" /> The more physicalist-friendly options, convention and revisability, weaken the very necessity that makes logic binding. This is a tension, not a contradiction: a naturalist may accept abstract logical facts or accept a softer necessity. The Christian holds that logic grounded in God's truthfulness explains both its necessity and its fit with the world (see <QuestionLink question="logic-binding" worldview="christianity">the Christian answer</QuestionLink>).

## Pressure questions

- If logic is a fact about things, what kind of fact is it, and where does it fit in a physical world?
- If logic is revisable, by what logic would we judge a revision correct?

## Deep dive

#### Russell's argument

Russell's argument against calling logic a "law of thought" is short. If we believe the law of contradiction, we believe something about trees and beeches, not about our minds: that if a tree is a beech, it cannot also be not a beech. "If this... were not true of the things in the world, the fact that we were compelled to think it true would not save the law of contradiction from being false."<Cite source="russell-problems-philosophy" locator="ch. VIII" />

#### Convention and Quine's regress

Conventionalism was championed by Carnap and others, and in a revised "tacit agreement" form by Paul Boghossian. Quine's 1936 objection, as SEP summarizes it, is that "logical rules are presumably needed to derive an infinite number of logical truths from a finite number of conventions."<Cite source="sep-logical-truth" locator="§1.1" /> A similar regress threatens any attempt to justify logic by inference, since the inference would already use logical rules.

#### Realism with a naturalist epistemology

Some naturalists combine these options. Gila Sher pairs a Quinean account of how we come to know logic with a realist view of what makes logical truths true, and Jody Azzouni holds that our sense that logic must be true comes from its being psychologically ingrained.<Cite source="sep-logical-truth" locator="§1.1" />

#### Not nominalism by default

It is a mistake to treat naturalists as nominalists who deny abstract objects as a class. Russell treated logical laws as facts about the world, and Sher defends a realist view of what makes logical truths true.<Cite source="sep-logical-truth" locator="§1.1" /> What naturalists share is a commitment to the methods of inquiry described under <QuestionLink question="ultimate-authority" worldview="naturalism">the ultimate authority for truth</QuestionLink>, not a single metaphysics of logic.
