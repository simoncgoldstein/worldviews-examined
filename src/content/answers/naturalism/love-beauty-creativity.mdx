## The view

The organizing principle is that love, aesthetic response and making are capacities of an evolved social animal, amplified by culture. Hume analysed love between the sexes as a compound of "the pleasing sensation arising from beauty; the bodily appetite for generation; and a generous kindness or good-will."<Cite source="hume-treatise" locator="2.2.11.1" /> Beauty, for Hume, is an order and construction fitted to give pleasure to the soul, discerned by sentiment and taste.<Cite source="hume-treatise" locator="2.1.8.2" />

Taste is not mere preference. Hume argues that no one seriously holds a minor poet equal to Milton, and that the standard of taste is "the joint verdict" of critics with strong sense, delicate sentiment, practice and freedom from prejudice.<Cite source="hume-standard-of-taste" locator="ST 8, 23" /> Darwin held that animals share a sense of beauty, that ornament and song evolved partly through sexual selection, and that musical notes and rhythm were probably first acquired to charm the opposite sex.<Cite source="darwin-descent" locator="ch. III, pp. 92–93; ch. XIX, p. 572" /> Creativity rides on cumulative culture, which lets each generation build on the last.<Cite source="tennie-ratchet" locator="Abstract" />

Origin does not settle value. Objectivist naturalists count creative work and beauty among the things that make a life meaningful whatever a person happens to want.<Cite source="sep-meaning-of-life" locator="§3.2" />

## What this explains well

The account explains why love and beauty are universal yet culturally varied, why something like a sense of beauty appears in other animals, and why art accumulates across generations.

## Christian response

The Christian accepts that these capacities have natural histories. The question is whether naturalism can say why the joint verdict of good critics tracks real excellence rather than a widely shared reaction. Hume grounds it in the near-uniform constitution of human nature;<Cite source="hume-standard-of-taste" locator="ST 23" /> the Christian thinks beauty's claim on us outruns that ground. That is explanatory incompleteness, not contradiction.

## Pressure questions

- If beauty is pleasure fitted to our constitution, what makes one judgment of taste truer than another?
- Does an evolutionary account of love explain why it should be faithful when it is costly?

## Deep dive

#### Hume's standard

Hume admits that beauty is a sentiment, not a quality in things, yet denies that all tastes are equal. Rules of art are drawn from what has pleased in all ages, and the sound judge is as rare as the sound organ.<Cite source="hume-standard-of-taste" locator="ST 7–9, 12" /> The principles of taste are nearly the same in all people, but few are qualified to judge.<Cite source="hume-standard-of-taste" locator="ST 23" />

#### Love as genuine care

Evolutionary "altruism" is defined by effects on reproductive fitness, but evolution can also produce genuine psychological concern for others; love need not be selfishness in disguise.<Cite source="sep-altruism-biological" locator="§5" /> Hume likewise treats the kindness in love as real good-will, joined to beauty and appetite rather than reducible to them.<Cite source="hume-treatise" locator="2.2.11.1" />

#### Scope

Contemporary naturalists hold no single settled theory of beauty or art, and this answer does not adjudicate among the proposals. Russell's early essay finds foreshadowings of ideal value in music, architecture and poetry.<Cite source="russell-free-mans-worship" locator="para. 14" /> The evolved organism behind these capacities is described in <QuestionLink question="what-is-man" worldview="naturalism">the human-nature anchor</QuestionLink>.
