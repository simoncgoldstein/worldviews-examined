## The view

Naturalism explains reproduction as the engine of evolution, and human family life by the same history. Darwin counted sexual love and maternal love among the instincts humans share with other animals,<Cite source="darwin-descent" locator="ch. III, p. 66" /> and placed parental and filial affection among the social instincts from which conscience grows.<Cite source="darwin-descent" locator="ch. IV, p. 98" /> He judged it unlikely that early humans lived in general promiscuity, and gave sexual selection a large role in shaping human pairing.<Cite source="darwin-descent" locator="ch. XX, pp. 590–591" />

Hume made the family the seed of society. The natural appetite between the sexes unites them "till a new tye takes place in their concern for their common offspring," and this he calls "the first and original principle of human society."<Cite source="hume-treatise" locator="3.2.2.4" /> Biologists add kin selection: care directed to relatives can spread because relatives share genes, a pattern summarized by Hamilton's rule.<Cite source="sep-altruism-biological" locator="§2" /> None of this makes parental love selfish in disguise,<Cite source="sep-altruism-biological" locator="§5" /> nor does it say how families ought to be ordered.

## What this explains well

The account explains the universality of kinship, the strength of parental love and the long dependence of human children within one framework. It also explains the variety of family forms across cultures.

## Christian response

The Christian accepts much of the biology; Scripture also roots marriage in bodily union.<Cite source="bible-esv" locator="Genesis 2:24" /> The question is whether an account that explains family by reproductive success can say why family bonds deserve the faithfulness they demand when it is costly and brings no advantage. Naturalists answer through ethics rather than biology; the Christian thinks that ethics then needs a ground the biology does not supply. That is explanatory incompleteness, not contradiction.

## Pressure questions

- Naturalists ground fidelity in ethics rather than biology. What makes a lifelong promise binding once keeping it has become costly and the evolved feelings that supported it have faded?
- If the sense that one owes special care to one's own children was shaped by kin selection, does that history give reason to trust the sense or to discount it?

## Deep dive

#### Explanation and evaluation

Philosophers distinguish three questions about evolved behaviour: how it arose, what we ought to do, and what moral claims are.<Cite source="sep-morality-biology" locator="§1.1" /> A biological account of family answers the first. It does not, by itself, show that any family form is good or bad. This is why naturalists can agree on the evolutionary story and disagree sharply about marriage, gender roles and reproduction. Humanists answer the normative questions in terms of fulfilment through relationships and care for others.<Cite source="aha-humanist-manifesto-iii" locator="affirmations on fulfillment, relationships and social benefit" />

#### Scope
