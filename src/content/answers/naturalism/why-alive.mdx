## The view

Naturalism denies that human life has a purpose assigned from outside nature. Humans exist because of natural causes, and those causes aimed at nothing. Russell put this starkly: man is the product of causes "which had no prevision of the end they were achieving."<Cite source="russell-free-mans-worship" locator="para. 4" />

That does not settle whether life can be meaningful. Most naturalist philosophers think it can, and they divide over how.<Cite source="sep-meaning-of-life" locator="§3" /> Subjectivists hold that meaning comes from getting what one cares about or from engaging in projects one finds compelling. Russell's later statement is subjectivist: "It is we who create value, and our desires which confer value."<Cite source="russell-what-i-believe" locator="ch. 1" /> Objectivists hold that some things make a life meaningful whether or not a person happens to want them, such as moral achievement, understanding and creative work.<Cite source="sep-meaning-of-life" locator="§§3.1–3.2" /> Nihilism, the view that no life is meaningful, is a minority position and has been waning in analytic philosophy.<Cite source="sep-meaning-of-life" locator="§4" />

Flourishing, then, is a good human life rather than the fulfilment of a cosmic role. Russell's formula was that "the good life is one inspired by love and guided by knowledge."<Cite source="russell-what-i-believe" locator="ch. 2" /> Humanist statements locate fulfilment in relationships, ethical commitment and service to others.<Cite source="aha-humanist-manifesto-iii" locator="affirmations on fulfillment, relationships and social benefit" />

## What this explains well

The view takes seriously what the sciences show about human origins without inferring that life is therefore worthless. It explains how people find deep meaning in love, work, art and justice without a shared religious framework. It honours autonomy, and it expects the disagreement about meaning that we observe.

## Christian response

The Christian agrees that love, knowledge and creative work are real goods, and that many naturalists live lives of great meaning. Scripture itself commends doing ordinary things well.<Cite source="bible-esv" locator="Ecclesiastes 9:7–10" /> The disagreement concerns grounding. Subjectivism answers the question by relocating it: if desire confers value, the cruel project and the generous one are equally meaningful to those who care about them, unless something beyond desire ranks them. Objectivist naturalism avoids that result, but it then needs an account of why moral achievement or understanding has objective worth in an unguided universe. The question concerns explanatory adequacy; it is not a charge of contradiction.

## Pressure questions

- If meaning is conferred by caring, is a life devoted to a worthless project as meaningful as one devoted to justice?
- If some goods are objectively worth pursuing, what makes them so in a universe with no purpose?
- Does Russell's "we create value" leave room for the early Russell's ideals that deserve reverence?

## Deep dive

#### Three naturalist positions

The debate map distinguishes three positions within naturalism. Subjectivism makes meaning depend on a person's mental states: what they want, choose or find compelling. Objectivism holds that certain conditions, often summarized as the good, the true and the beautiful, confer meaning independently of attitudes, though they must be pursued in the right way.<Cite source="sep-meaning-of-life" locator="§§3.1–3.2" /> Some naturalists go further and argue that God's existence would reduce meaning, for instance by removing the meaning of being one's own master.<Cite source="sep-meaning-of-life" locator="§3.3" /> Nihilists deny that any life has meaning, but the position is less common than popular discussion suggests.<Cite source="sep-meaning-of-life" locator="§4" />

#### Early and later Russell

Russell's own views moved. In "A Free Man's Worship" (1903) he speaks of ideals that deserve a kind of worship and of building a temple for them in a hostile world.<Cite source="russell-free-mans-worship" locator="para. 13" /> By 1925 he held that value is conferred by desire and that humans are the final arbiters of it.<Cite source="russell-what-i-believe" locator="ch. 1" /> The first sounds objectivist and the second subjectivist. Naturalism as such does not choose between them, and representative naturalists continue to defend both.

#### Origin is not value

An evolutionary history explains why humans care about offspring, status and companionship. It does not show that these are the only things worth caring about, nor that caring about them is illusory. Most naturalists keep the explanation of where values came from separate from the question of which values are worth holding. Flourishing is therefore an aim, not a guaranteed outcome, and it ends with death.
