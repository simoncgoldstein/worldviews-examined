## The view

The Qur'an presents the world as measured and reliable. "We have created all things in due measure."<Cite source="quran-haleem" locator="54:49" /> God "created all things and made them to an exact measure."<Cite source="quran-haleem" locator="25:2" /> "The sun and the moon follow their calculated courses," and God "has set the balance."<Cite source="quran-haleem" locator="55:5–7" /> Look at the heavens, it says, and you will not see any flaw.<Cite source="quran-haleem" locator="67:3" /> And "you will never find any change in God's practice."<Cite source="quran-haleem" locator="35:43" /> The world is orderly because its Creator is wise and constant, and its regularities are signs pointing to him.

How does God's governance relate to the causes we see? Here classical thinkers divided.

- **Ash'ari occasionalism**, defended by al-Ghazali: there is no necessary link between what we call a cause and its effect. When cotton touches fire, God creates the burning. We observe the burning happening *with* the fire, not *by* it. The regularity is God's custom, which he could change (our paraphrase of the Arabic).<Cite source="averroes-tahafut-bouyges" locator="Phys. Disc. I = Tahāfut 17, Alg. §§1–7, pp. 517–518" /><Cite source="sep-arabic-islamic-religion" locator="§3.2.2" />
- **Maturidi theology**: created things have real natures, dispositions with genuine effects, which God creates, sustains and coordinates.<Cite source="harvey-transcendent-god" locator="pp. 89–93" />
- **Ibn Taymiyya**: God creates by means of secondary causes. He makes plants grow by means of rain, and causes death by means of poison. From the human side such causes look natural; from God's side they are purely instrumental.<Cite source="sep-ibn-taymiyya" locator="§4.5" />
- **The philosophers** (Ibn Sina, Ibn Rushd): natural causation is real and necessary. Ibn Rushd called the denial of observed causes "sophistry."<Cite source="averroes-tahafut-bouyges" locator="Phys. Disc. I, Ibn Rushd, p. 519" />

## What this explains well

The view explains nature's reliability while keeping it dependent on God at every moment. It leaves room for miracles without making them violations of an independent order. And it treats every regularity as a sign that invites reflection on its Maker.

## Christian response

The Christian shares the conviction that order is the work of a wise, faithful Creator. On causation the Reformed position, that God works through real second causes,<Cite source="wcf" locator="5.2–3" /> stands closer to Maturidi and Ibn Taymiyya than to Ash'ari occasionalism. With the occasionalist the Christian raises a question of explanatory adequacy. If fire has no power to burn, in what sense do creatures act at all, including human beings, whose acts are judged? Ash'ari theologians have their own answers to this. It is a debate among theists about how God governs, not a dispute over whether he does.

## Pressure questions

- If God alone produces every effect, in what sense are human actions truly the agent's own?
- If God's custom never changes, how does it differ from what others call a law of nature?

## Deep dive

#### Al-Ghazali's seventeenth discussion

In *The Incoherence of the Philosophers*, al-Ghazali denies that the connection between what is habitually believed to be a cause and its effect is necessary. Neither implies the other. Their joining is due to God's prior decree, which creates them side by side. The agent of burning is God, directly or through angels; fire is inanimate and has no action. Observation shows only that burning occurs *at* contact with fire (*ʿindahu*), not that it occurs *by* it (*bihi*) (our paraphrase of the Arabic).<Cite source="averroes-tahafut-bouyges" locator="Phys. Disc. I = Tahāfut 17, Alg. §§1–7, pp. 517–518" /> The scanned Arabic edition is noisy, so the passage is paraphrased, with the scholarly survey as a check.<Cite source="sep-arabic-islamic-religion" locator="§3.2.2" />

#### Ibn Rushd's reply

Ibn Rushd answered that denying the efficient causes we observe in sensible things is sophistry.<Cite source="averroes-tahafut-bouyges" locator="Phys. Disc. I, Ibn Rushd, p. 519" />

#### Order as a sign

Whatever their view of causes, all these thinkers read the world's regularity as a sign. "There truly are signs in the creation of the heavens and earth, and in the alternation of night and day, for those with understanding," who reflect and say, "Our Lord! You have not created all this without purpose."<Cite source="quran-haleem" locator="3:190–191" /> The dispute over causation is a dispute about how God governs, never about whether the order is his.

#### Natures and wisdom

Al-Maturidi argued that the world's order, its opposing natures combined in stable harmony, points to one wise arranger. A world of rival creators would fall into disorder, as the Qur'an says.<Cite source="harvey-transcendent-god" locator="pp. 63, 120–121" /> Ibn Taymiyya added that God creates for wise purposes, which makes the order of creation purposive as well as regular.<Cite source="sep-ibn-taymiyya" locator="§4.5" />
