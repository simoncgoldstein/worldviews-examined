## The view

Before he died, the Buddha appointed no successor. He told his followers, "Let the teaching be your island and your refuge, with no other refuge," and promised that "the teaching and training that I have taught and pointed out for you shall be your Teacher after my passing."<Cite source="digha-nikaya-sujato" locator="DN 16:2.26.1; 6.1.5" /> The final reference is the Dhamma itself, not a person, office or council. When a disciple wanted to gaze at the Buddha's body, he was told, "One who sees the teaching sees me."<Cite source="samyutta-nikaya-sujato" locator="SN 22.87:3.2" />

How is the genuine teaching recognized? A claim to have heard something from the Buddha, a community of monks or learned elders is neither accepted nor rejected out of hand. It is compared with the discourses and with the monastic training. If it does not fit, "Clearly this is not the word of the Blessed One," and it is set aside.<Cite source="anguttara-nikaya-sujato" locator="AN 4.180" /> So received scripture checks new claims.

Yet the teaching is not to be held merely on authority. The Kālāma Sutta tells people confused by rival teachers not to settle matters by tradition, scripture, logic or a teacher's reputation alone, but by knowing "for yourselves" what is unskilful and harmful, as "sensible people" also judge.<Cite source="anguttara-nikaya-sujato" locator="AN 3.65:4.1–4.3" /> This is not a charter to believe nothing on authority. The same discourses commend placing faith in a teacher once he has been examined, and they call faith tested in this way "grounded faith that's based on evidence."<Cite source="majjhima-nikaya-sujato" locator="MN 95:20.2; MN 47:16.1" /> The final verification is direct realization: one comes to see the truth "with penetrating wisdom."<Cite source="majjhima-nikaya-sujato" locator="MN 95:20.2" /> Even then the teaching is "a raft: for crossing over, not for holding on."<Cite source="majjhima-nikaya-sujato" locator="MN 38:14.1" />

Dharmakīrti later made the limits of scriptural authority explicit. The Buddha is a means of knowledge "in that way," a reliable guide, not an eternal authority.<Cite source="dharmakirti-pv-gretil" locator="PV II.7–10" note="Sanskrit; our paraphrase" /> As the Stanford Encyclopedia reports a chapter not available here in a checked edition, scripture should not decide what perception or reasoning can settle. On "radically inaccessible" matters, such as the detailed workings of karma, it must be relied on "because of the absence of any other means."<Cite source="sep-dharmakirti" locator="§4" note="on PV I.213–217" />

## What this explains well

The account gives authority without blind deference. It checks new claims against the received discourses and ties authority to results that practitioners can verify in their own lives.

## Christian response

The Christian agrees that claims should be tested and that spirits should not all be believed.<Cite source="bible-esv" locator="1 John 4:1" /> The pressure comes at two points. First, the final court is realization. What distinguishes a veridical insight into impermanence and non-self from a deeply trained conviction? Second, by Dharmakīrti's own account the teaching's most far-reaching claims, about karma and rebirth, cannot be tested by the practitioner. They are accepted on the Buddha's word because there is no other way. So the system's final appeal includes testimony that cannot be individually verified. Dharmakīrti is candid about this; the scholar Tom Tillemans calls his position "surprisingly fallibilist."<Cite source="sep-dharmakirti" locator="§4" /> The Christian also holds that the deepest truths are known by trusting a trustworthy speaker. The disagreement is over which speaker is trustworthy and on what evidence, a question that is theological and historical, not a contradiction in the Buddhist view.

## Pressure questions

- If the most important claims cannot be tested by the practitioner, what grounds trust in the teacher who made them?
- If realization is the final test, what distinguishes realization from conviction?

## Deep dive

#### The Kālāma Sutta in context

The Kālāmas complain that teacher after teacher praises his own doctrine and attacks the others, and they are "doubting and uncertain."<Cite source="anguttara-nikaya-sujato" locator="AN 3.65:3.1–3.8" /> The Buddha's list of grounds not to rely on alone includes oral transmission, lineage, testament, canonical authority, logic, inference, reasoned thought, an accepted view, apparent competence and the teacher's status.<Cite source="anguttara-nikaya-sujato" locator="AN 3.65:4.1" /> The test he offers is practical and social: what leads to harm and is "criticized by sensible people." He then gives two consolations that hold whether or not there is rebirth.<Cite source="anguttara-nikaya-sujato" locator="AN 3.65:4.3, 43–44" /> The passage is about choosing among teachers and practices, not about rejecting every authority. Why no single ground settles a question is explained in the discourse discussed under <QuestionLink question="knowledge-possible" worldview="buddhism">why we can know anything</QuestionLink>.

#### Dharmakīrti on the Buddha's authority

Dharmakīrti defines a means of knowledge as a non-deceptive cognition. He says that validity is established through practice and that the teaching's role is to remove delusion.<Cite source="dharmakirti-pv-gretil" locator="PV II.1, II.5" note="Sanskrit; our paraphrase" /> He denies that any means of knowledge is eternal, and he calls the Buddha a means of knowledge in that limited sense.<Cite source="dharmakirti-pv-gretil" locator="PV II.7–10" note="Sanskrit; our paraphrase" /> His commentator Śākyabuddhi says that scripture is indispensable for those setting out on the path but is not grounded in objective facts.<Cite source="sep-dharmakirti" locator="§4" />

#### Other Buddhist positions

Nāgārjuna's Madhyamaka declines to advance a thesis of its own.<Cite source="nagarjuna-vigrahavyavartani-gretil" locator="v. 29" note="Sanskrit; our paraphrase" /> Later Mahāyāna traditions accepted further sūtras as the Buddha's word and developed rules for telling definitive teachings from provisional ones. That question of canon belongs to the Revelation & History domain and is not treated here.
