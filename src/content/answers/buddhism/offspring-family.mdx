## The view

Buddhism does not explain family by a creator's purpose, but the Buddha gave household life a detailed ethic. He found the young Sigālaka ritually revering the six directions and taught him to revere them as relationships instead, with parents as the east and partner and children as the west.<Cite source="digha-nikaya-sujato" locator="DN 31:27" /> Children support those who supported them and maintain the family line; parents keep children from wrongdoing, train them in a trade and find them a suitable partner.<Cite source="digha-nikaya-sujato" locator="DN 31:28" />

Parents receive the highest reverence. A family where children honour their parents is said to "live with divinity."<Cite source="anguttara-nikaya-sujato" locator="AN 4.63" /> Parents cannot be repaid even by a lifetime of carrying them, except by establishing them in faith, ethics, generosity and wisdom.<Cite source="anguttara-nikaya-sujato" locator="AN 2.33" />

Still, household life is not the goal. The ignoble quest seeks security in things liable to birth and death, among them "partners and children."<Cite source="majjhima-nikaya-sujato" locator="MN 26:6" /> "Life at home is cramped and dirty, life gone forth is wide open."<Cite source="digha-nikaya-sujato" locator="DN 2:41.4" />

## What this explains well

The account dignifies ordinary family life with practical, reciprocal ethics. It honours parents deeply. And it explains why attachment to family can cause suffering even when family is good.

## Christian response

The Christian shares the honour of parents and the reciprocal ethic of DN 31, which parallels Paul's household instructions.<Cite source="bible-esv" locator="Ephesians 5:25–6:4" /> The difference is that the Christian treats marriage and children as goods of creation in their own right. Buddhism values them highly, but finally as conditions to be seen through. That is a disagreement about ultimate reality, not an inconsistency.

## Pressure questions

- If partners and children belong to the ignoble quest when sought as refuge, can loving them ever be part of the noble one?
- Why should parents be revered like gods in a teaching without a creator?

## Deep dive

#### Lay and monastic

The early community had two tiers: monastics who went forth and laypeople who supported them. Spouses who share faith, ethics, generosity and wisdom are said to be able to meet again in future lives.<Cite source="anguttara-nikaya-sujato" locator="AN 4.55" /> Parents are described as having five reasons for wanting a child, including that the child will care for them and continue the family.<Cite source="anguttara-nikaya-sujato" locator="AN 5.39" /> There is no commandment to procreate. Love within the family is meant to become mettā, goodwill free of the greed that Buddhaghosa calls its near enemy.<Cite source="buddhaghosa-path-of-purification" locator="IX.98" />

#### Setting and scope

In DN 31 a husband honours his wife, is faithful and gives her authority in the home; a wife manages the household, is faithful and guards their earnings.<Cite source="digha-nikaya-sujato" locator="DN 31:30" /> That division of roles reflects its society, though its reciprocity is notable. Later traditions differ in their family and clerical practice and are not surveyed here.
