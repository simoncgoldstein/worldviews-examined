## The view

The organizing principle is the difference between clinging and the purified mind. Love in its highest Buddhist sense is *mettā*, goodwill extended without limit. The heart's release by love, cultivated and made a basis, brings eight benefits, among them sleeping at ease and being loved by humans and non-humans; even "loving just one creature with a hateless heart makes you a true person."<Cite source="anguttara-nikaya-sujato" locator="AN 8.1" /> Buddhaghosa warns that greed is love's "near enemy," because it looks like love while seeking possession.<Cite source="buddhaghosa-path-of-purification" locator="IX.98" /> Good friendship is "the whole of the spiritual life."<Cite source="samyutta-nikaya-sujato" locator="SN 45.2" />

Beauty is real but impermanent. The Buddha grants the pleasure of a young person at the height of beauty, then asks his hearers to picture the same person aged, ill and dead: the gratification, the drawback and the escape must all be seen.<Cite source="majjhima-nikaya-sujato" locator="MN 13:18–21" /> On observance days lay followers, like monastics, give up dancing, singing, music and adornment.<Cite source="anguttara-nikaya-sujato" locator="AN 8.41" /> Yet the Buddha's relics are honoured "with dance and song and music and garlands and fragrances."<Cite source="digha-nikaya-sujato" locator="DN 16:6.23.10" />

Creativity belongs to the mind. A famous painting was devised by the mind, says the Buddha, "but the mind is even more diverse than that."<Cite source="samyutta-nikaya-sujato" locator="SN 22.100" />

## What this explains well

The account explains why love can turn possessive and why beauty brings sorrow when clung to. It offers a practical path to purify both.

## Christian response

The Christian shares the warning against possessive love and idolized beauty. The difference is that Christians hold beauty to be a lasting reflection of God's glory, to be enjoyed rightly rather than seen through.<Cite source="bible-esv" locator="Psalm 27:4" /> That is a disagreement about ultimate reality.

## Pressure questions

- If beauty must be seen with its drawback, can it ever be enjoyed without loss?
- Is mettā love for this particular person, or for beings in general?

## Deep dive

#### Love and its near enemy

The tradition pairs love (*mettā*) with compassion, sympathetic joy and equanimity as the four boundless states. Their near enemies resemble them: greed mimics love because both see good qualities in another.<Cite source="buddhaghosa-path-of-purification" locator="IX.98" /> Śāntideva traces the world's happiness to wishing happiness for others.<Cite source="shantideva-bodhicaryavatara" locator="8.129" />

#### Love within the household

Love between spouses is honoured when it is joined to shared virtue: a couple equal in faith, ethics, generosity and wisdom may hope to meet again in future lives.<Cite source="anguttara-nikaya-sujato" locator="AN 4.55" /> Ordinary affection is not condemned; the training is to purify it of grasping.

#### Arts and scope

SN 22.100 uses art as an image of the mind's power to produce and to corrupt; the same passage warns that the mind has long been corrupted by greed, hate and delusion.<Cite source="samyutta-nikaya-sujato" locator="SN 22.100" /> Later Buddhist arts, including Tibetan iconography and Zen arts, are not surveyed here.
