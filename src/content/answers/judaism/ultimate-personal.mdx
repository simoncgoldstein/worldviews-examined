## The view

In the Torah God is known as someone who speaks. Israel heard "God's words" from the midst of the fire at Sinai,<Cite source="tanakh-jps-gender-sensitive" locator="Deuteronomy 4:36" /> and God chose Israel "out of love for your ancestors."<Cite source="tanakh-jps-gender-sensitive" locator="Deuteronomy 4:37" /> He commands, judges, forgives and is addressed in prayer. Jewish life is built around this relationship, and its piety assumes throughout that God hears.

The philosophers agreed that God knows and wills. They argued over how our words apply to him. Maimonides teaches that God, his knowledge and his life are one: God does not know by a knowledge separate from himself.<Cite source="maimonides-mishneh-torah-touger" locator="Yesodei ha-Torah 2:10" /> In the *Guide* he goes further. No positive description captures God's essence. True statements about God either deny limitations of him or describe his actions.<Cite source="maimonides-guide-friedlander" locator="I.51, I.58" /> When Moses asked to know God's ways, he was shown God's actions, such as mercy and graciousness.<Cite source="maimonides-guide-friedlander" locator="I.54" />

Maimonides does not deny that God is personal. He denies that personal language describes what God is in himself. Judah Halevi, writing about half a century earlier, largely agreed about the words: he too treats "merciful" as describing God's acts and "living" as a negation.<Cite source="halevi-kuzari-hirschfeld" locator="II.2" /> His protest was about how God is known. The Aristotelian philosophers, he argued, reasoned their way to a God who knows nothing of our prayers. The God of Israel is named by those who hear his address and command.<Cite source="halevi-kuzari-hirschfeld" locator="IV.3" /> In the *Kuzari*'s summary, the God of Abraham is sought with love and longing; the God of Aristotle by speculation.<Cite source="halevi-kuzari-hirschfeld" locator="IV.16" />

## What this explains well

The view holds together a living covenant relationship and a God beyond every likeness. It explains why prayer, command and repentance are meaningful. Maimonides' caution guards against making God a magnified human. Halevi's insistence guards against thinning God into an abstraction no one could love.

## Christian response

The Christian lane shares the God who speaks, chooses and loves, and the conviction that he is beyond comparison. On the internal debate, Reformed theology shares Halevi's insistence that God is known through his own address. It goes further than both Halevi and Maimonides in holding that revealed personal language is analogical and true of God, not only of his effects. The deeper difference is the Trinity. Christians hold that God is personal *within himself*: the Father loved the Son before the world existed.<Cite source="bible-esv" locator="John 17:24" /> So God's love does not depend on creation for an object. Judaism's unity excludes such inner distinction, and the disagreement turns on whether the New Testament is revelation.

## Pressure questions

- Halevi agrees with Maimonides that "merciful" describes God's acts, yet holds that God is known through his own address. What does that address disclose about God that an attribute of action does not?
- If only negations describe God's essence, what in God answers to the prayer that addresses him as hearing and merciful?

## Deep dive

#### Maimonides' negative theology

Maimonides argues that any positive attribute of essence would imply composition in God, since an attribute is something added to its subject. He therefore holds that we know *that* God exists, not *what* he is. All we understand, he says, is that God exists, that no creature resembles him, that he contains no plurality, and that he is never too feeble to produce other beings.<Cite source="maimonides-guide-friedlander" locator="I.58" /> Attributes of action are different. They describe God's works, the way we might call a person "merciful" from what he does without knowing his inner states.<Cite source="maimonides-guide-friedlander" locator="I.54" /> On this view Scripture's personal language is true, but it is true of God's governance, not a description of his essence.

#### Saadia's middle path

Saadia, earlier, affirmed that God is living, powerful and wise, and that these are not three things in God. The mind grasps them in a single insight; language forces us to use three words for what is one.<Cite source="saadia-emunot-ibn-tibbon" locator="II.4" /> His position lets positive attributes stand while denying that they divide God.

#### Halevi's divine names

Halevi distinguishes the name *Elohim*, which reason can reach as the ruler of the world, from the personal name *Adonai*, which belongs to the God who reveals himself. As the king sums up the rabbi's teaching, humans yearn for the latter "as a matter of love, taste, and conviction."<Cite source="halevi-kuzari-hirschfeld" locator="IV.16" /> Knowledge of God through his address is, for Halevi, the fuller kind.

#### God's will

Even Maimonides insists that the world was created by God's will rather than by necessity.<Cite source="maimonides-guide-friedlander" locator="II.13, II.25" /> That keeps choice at the heart of the Jewish God, however carefully his essence is guarded.
