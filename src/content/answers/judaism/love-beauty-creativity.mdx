## The view

The organizing principle is imitation of God and service through the commandments. Love is commanded twice: to love God with all one's heart, soul and might,<Cite source="tanakh-jps-gender-sensitive" locator="Deuteronomy 6:5" /> and to love one's fellow as oneself.<Cite source="tanakh-jps-gender-sensitive" locator="Leviticus 19:18" /> Love that "does not depend on anything" never ceases, like the love of David and Jonathan.<Cite source="mishnah-kulp" locator="Avot 5:16" /> Maimonides describes the love of God as a lovesickness that fills the mind, which is why the Song of Songs is its parable.<Cite source="maimonides-mishneh-torah-touger" locator="Teshuvah 10:3" />

Beauty is received with blessing. One who sees beautiful creatures or trees says, "Blessed … Who has such things in His world."<Cite source="talmud-bavli-koren" locator="Berakhot 58b" /> Beauty is also offered back. The sages read "This is my God and I will glorify Him" as "beautify yourself before Him in mitzvot": a beautiful sukkah, shofar, Torah scroll and ink. Abba Shaul reads the same words as "be similar to Him": as God is compassionate and merciful, so be you.<Cite source="talmud-bavli-koren" locator="Shabbat 133b" />

Skill and creativity come from God. Bezalel is endowed with a divine spirit of skill and knowledge in every craft.<Cite source="tanakh-jps-gender-sensitive" locator="Exodus 31:1–6" /> Soloveitchik describes "majestic Adam," commanded to fill and master the earth, as a creative being whose technology and culture fulfil a divine mandate.<Cite source="soloveitchik-lonely-man-of-faith" locator="pp. 12–14" />

## What this explains well

The account joins love, beauty and creativity to everyday religious life, so that aesthetic care becomes a form of devotion and craftsmanship a calling.

## Christian response

The Christian shares almost all of this, including Bezalel and the command to love. The difference is where love is finally displayed: Christians hold that God's love is shown supremely in Christ's self-giving.<Cite source="bible-esv" locator="1 John 4:9–10" /> That is a revelatory disagreement.

## Pressure questions

- Is beautifying a commandment an expression of love, or a separate duty of honour?
- What distinguishes majestic Adam's creativity from human self-assertion?

## Deep dive

#### Love from contemplation

Maimonides holds that love and awe of God arise when a person contemplates God's works and sees his wisdom.<Cite source="maimonides-mishneh-torah-touger" locator="Yesodei ha-Torah 2:1–2" /> The Mishnah requires love of God with both inclinations, so that even desire is drawn into devotion.<Cite source="mishnah-kulp" locator="Berakhot 9:5" />

#### The greatest principle

Rabbi Akiva called "love your fellow as yourself" the great principle of the Torah; Ben Azzai named a greater one, "This is the book of the generations of Adam," because it grounds love in the image every person bears.<Cite source="midrash-rabbah-sefaria" locator="Bereshit Rabbah 24:7" /> Love is also enacted: following God means clothing the naked, visiting the sick, comforting mourners and burying the dead, as God did.<Cite source="talmud-bavli-koren" locator="Sotah 14a" />

#### Images and art

The second commandment limits images in worship. Jewish attitudes to figurative art have varied across periods and communities, and no single position is assumed here.
