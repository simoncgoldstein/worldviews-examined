## The view

The Hebrew Bible ties the world's order to God's wisdom: "You have made them all with wisdom."<Cite source="tanakh-jps-gender-sensitive" locator="Psalms 104:24" /> God established the sun for the day and "the laws of moon and stars for light by night."<Cite source="tanakh-jps-gender-sensitive" locator="Jeremiah 31:35" /> He made the heavens endure, "establishing an order that shall never change."<Cite source="tanakh-jps-gender-sensitive" locator="Psalms 148:6" /> Jeremiah even ties God's covenant with Israel to "the laws of heaven and earth."<Cite source="tanakh-jps-gender-sensitive" locator="Jeremiah 33:25" /> Nature is regular because God is faithful.

The rabbis added a striking image. When a king builds a palace, he works from an architect's plans. "So too, the Holy One blessed be He looked in the Torah and created the world."<Cite source="midrash-rabbah-sefaria" locator="Bereshit Rabbah 1:1" /> The world's order reflects the same divine wisdom as the Torah. The Mishnah says the world was created with ten divine utterances.<Cite source="mishnah-kulp" locator="Avot 5:1" />

Jewish thinkers then disagreed about how God's governance works day to day. **Maimonides** held that God is the world's efficient, formal and final cause.<Cite source="maimonides-guide-friedlander" locator="I.69" /> He held that divine providence watches over individual human beings, while the rest of the natural world below the heavens is governed in a general way, through its natural order.<Cite source="maimonides-guide-friedlander" locator="III.17" /> **Nachmanides** insisted that the events of a life lived under the covenant are "hidden miracles." In them there is "no natural or customary way of the world," for the public or for the individual. Reward and punishment come by divine decree.<Cite source="ramban-torah-chavel" locator="on Exodus 13:16" />

## What this explains well

The view joins natural order and moral covenant: the same faithful God stands behind both. It explains why the world can be studied, since its order expresses wisdom, and why that order does not exclude God's care or miracles. Its internal debate keeps both stable nature and providence in view.

## Christian response

The Christian shares this account of order: one wise God, fixed ordinances, faithfulness behind regularity. The Christian identifies the wisdom by which God made the world with the Son, through whom all things were created and in whom they hold together.<Cite source="bible-esv" locator="Colossians 1:16–17" /> On providence, the Reformed lane is closer to Nachmanides than to Maimonides. Calvin holds that God's special providence reaches every creature, down to a sparrow.<Cite source="calvin-institutes-beveridge" locator="I.16.1" /> Yet with Maimonides it affirms real natural causes. These are theological disagreements within a shared monotheism.

## Pressure questions

- If general providence governs most of nature, does God care for each sparrow, or only for each human?
- If every event in a faithful life is a hidden miracle, what remains of nature's own order?

## Deep dive

#### The Torah as blueprint

Bereshit Rabbah reads Proverbs 8:30, in which Wisdom says, "I was with Him as an *amon*," as describing the Torah as God's instrument or plan. It reads the first word of Genesis, *bereshit*, as "by means of the Torah," since Proverbs calls wisdom the *reshit*, the beginning, of God's way.<Cite source="midrash-rabbah-sefaria" locator="Bereshit Rabbah 1:1" /> The world's order and Israel's law come from one source.

#### Maimonides on providence

Maimonides surveys five theories of providence. He rejects Aristotle's, which sees no difference between a leaf falling and a ship of good people sinking. He also rejects the Ash'ari theology, which holds that every falling leaf is individually willed. His own view is that providence follows the divine intellectual influence. Human beings, who receive it, are under individual providence. Other species are governed as species, by their natural order.<Cite source="maimonides-guide-friedlander" locator="III.17" /> He presents this as his reading of the Law and the Prophets, not as a demonstration. He also rejects Aristotle's eternal, unchangeable order, because it would rule out miracles.<Cite source="maimonides-guide-friedlander" locator="II.25" />

#### Nachmanides' hidden miracles

For Nachmanides, the open miracles of the Exodus teach people to recognize hidden ones. Belief in hidden miracles is "the foundation of the whole Torah": no one has a part in the Torah of Moses unless he believes that all its words and events are miraculous, with no natural or customary way of the world in them.<Cite source="ramban-torah-chavel" locator="on Exodus 13:16" /> Nature still runs regularly, but the outcomes of a covenantal life are God's direct doing.
