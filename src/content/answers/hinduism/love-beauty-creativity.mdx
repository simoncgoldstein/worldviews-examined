## The view

The organizing principle is that love and beauty find their source in the divine. In the Gītā, Krishna says that whatever is glorious, prosperous or mighty springs from a portion of his splendour.<Cite source="sastri-gita-shankara" locator="10.41" /> The Taittirīya Upaniṣad calls Brahman "flavour" (*rasa*): by tasting it a person becomes blissful.<Cite source="muller-upanishads-2" locator="Taittirīya II.7, pp. 58–59" />

Love's highest form is devotion (*bhakti*). The Lord accepts a leaf, flower, fruit or water offered with love.<Cite source="sastri-gita-shankara" locator="9.26" /> Rāmānuja reads this as impassioned love that the Lord himself enjoys,<Cite source="govindacharya-gita-ramanuja" locator="9.26" /> and teaches that the Lord chooses his lover and generates in him the love by which he is reached.<Cite source="govindacharya-gita-ramanuja" locator="8.14, pp. 272–273" />

Human loves are dear for a deeper reason. Yājñavalkya teaches that a husband, wife or son is dear not for their own sake but "for the love of the Self."<Cite source="muller-upanishads-2" locator="Bṛhadāraṇyaka II.4.5, pp. 109–110" /> Advaita reads this as pointing to the one Self in all.

## What this explains well

The account explains why love and beauty feel like glimpses of something greater, and it gives devotion an intimate, reciprocal character.

## Christian response

The Christian sees in Rāmānuja's teaching that the Lord initiates love a striking parallel to grace.<Cite source="bible-esv" locator="1 John 4:10, 19" /> With Advaita the question is whether love between persons survives when individuality is not ultimate. That is a theological disagreement about ultimate reality.

## Pressure questions

- If a spouse is dear "for the love of the Self," is the spouse loved for their own sake?
- Does Rāmānuja's divinely initiated love leave room for the devotee's free response?

## Deep dive

#### Desire and its limits

The Gītā calls the Lord "desire unopposed to dharma," but Śaṅkara restricts this to desire for what bare bodily sustenance requires.<Cite source="sastri-gita-shankara" locator="7.11, commentary" /> The householder's legitimate pleasure (*kāma*) stands within dharma, yet Advaita treats it as something to be transcended.

#### Two readings of love

For Advaita, love's deepest meaning is recognition: what is loved in another is the one Self, so that love points beyond the distinction of lover and beloved.<Cite source="muller-upanishads-2" locator="Bṛhadāraṇyaka IV.5.6, p. 182" /> For Rāmānuja, love is an eternal relation between the soul and the Lord, in which the Lord delights in his devotee's offering.<Cite source="govindacharya-gita-ramanuja" locator="9.26" /> The difference reaches the question whether love between persons is ultimate.

#### Scope

Indian classical aesthetics, including rasa theory in drama and poetry, is a major tradition but is not treated here. The Upaniṣadic "flavour" quoted above is a statement about Brahman, not a theory of art. Hindu arts of temple sculpture, music and dance are likewise not surveyed.
