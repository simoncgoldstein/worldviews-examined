## The Christian view

Scripture grounds family in creation, not in sin. God blesses the first humans to be fruitful and multiply,<Cite source="bible-esv" locator="Genesis 1:28" /> judges that it is not good for the man to be alone, makes a helper fit for him, and joins the two as one flesh.<Cite source="bible-esv" locator="Genesis 2:18–24" /> Jesus appeals to that beginning as the pattern of marriage.<Cite source="bible-esv" locator="Matthew 19:4–6" /> Children are a heritage from the Lord,<Cite source="bible-esv" locator="Psalm 127:3–5" /> and parents are to teach God's words at home.<Cite source="bible-esv" locator="Deuteronomy 6:4–9" /> Yet Paul calls singleness a gift that frees a person for undivided devotion to the Lord.<Cite source="bible-esv" locator="1 Corinthians 7:7, 32–35" />

Marriage serves more than one end. The Westminster Confession names three: mutual help, the increase of mankind and of the church, and the prevention of uncleanness.<Cite source="wcf" locator="24.2" /> Procreation belongs to marriage without being its only point. Children belong to the visible church with their believing parents, so the household is the first place where the faith is taught.<Cite source="wcf" locator="25.2" /><Cite source="bible-esv" locator="Ephesians 6:4" /> Because humans were not made for a solitary life, marriage is the ordinary calling, and Calvin counts lasting continence a special gift given to few.<Cite source="calvin-institutes-beveridge" locator="II.8.41–42" /> Family also serves a purpose larger than any one home. Bavinck, who treats marriage, household and child-rearing as ordinances grounded in creation, holds that the image of God is unfolded only in humanity as a whole, across its generations.<Cite source="bavinck-reformed-dogmatics-monergism-2" locator="Vol. 2, §34.4–5, PDF pp. 690–693" />

## What this explains well

The account explains why family is both natural and sacred: rooted in bodily sex and dependence, yet ordered to love and faithfulness. It values children without making them the sole point of marriage. And it honours single people without treating marriage as a lower state.

## Strongest objection

The objection is that this sanctifies one historical family form. Biology explains pair-bonding and parental care,<Cite source="darwin-descent" locator="ch. IV, p. 98" /> and the households of Scripture include polygamy and patriarchal arrangements that Christians themselves no longer follow.

## Christian reply

Scripture's narratives record polygamy without endorsing it, Westminster forbids it,<Cite source="wcf" locator="24.1" /> and Jesus reads marriage from the creation pattern of one man and one woman, not from later practice.<Cite source="bible-esv" locator="Matthew 19:4–6" /> A biological account of pair-bonding fits a creation ordinance; it does not show that the ordinance is merely biological. The Reformed tradition does differ internally over headship, as <QuestionLink question="what-is-man" worldview="christianity">the human-nature anchor</QuestionLink> notes.

## Deep dive

#### Marriage, celibacy and the Reformation

Calvin rejected vowed celibacy as presumptuous: no one may bind themselves to a gift God has not given, and marriage is honourable for all.<Cite source="calvin-institutes-beveridge" locator="IV.13.3" /> The Larger Catechism lists among the sins against the seventh commandment "entangling vows of single life," while also forbidding undue delay of marriage.<Cite source="westminster-larger-catechism" locator="Q. 139" /> This was a polemic against compulsory clerical celibacy, not a rejection of Paul's gift of singleness, which remains a calling in its own right.

#### Children and covenant

Westminster defines the visible church as all who profess the true religion "and of their children."<Cite source="wcf" locator="25.2" /> Parents are to pass on what God has done to the next generation,<Cite source="bible-esv" locator="Psalm 78:4–7" /> children are to honour parents,<Cite source="bible-esv" locator="Exodus 20:12" /> and fathers are not to provoke their children but bring them up in the Lord's instruction.<Cite source="bible-esv" locator="Ephesians 6:1–4" /> Marital love is modelled on Christ's self-giving love for the church.<Cite source="bible-esv" locator="Ephesians 5:25–33" /> The household is therefore a place of instruction and worship, which is why Westminster also commends family worship.<Cite source="wcf" locator="21.6" />
