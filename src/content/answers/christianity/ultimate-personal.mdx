## The Christian view

The God of Scripture acts like a person because he is one. He speaks, and the heavens are made.<Cite source="bible-esv" locator="Psalm 33:6, 9" /> He "works all things according to the counsel of his will."<Cite source="bible-esv" locator="Ephesians 1:11" /> His understanding is unsearchable,<Cite source="bible-esv" locator="Isaiah 40:13–14, 28" /> and "God is love."<Cite source="bible-esv" locator="1 John 4:8" /> Knowing, choosing, speaking and loving are not human traits projected onto the universe. They belong first to its Creator.

This does not make God a large human being. "God is spirit,"<Cite source="bible-esv" locator="John 4:24" /> without body, parts or passions.<Cite source="wcf" locator="2.1" /> When Scripture speaks of God's eyes or hands, Calvin says, God "lisps with us as nurses are wont to do with little children," accommodating himself to our weakness.<Cite source="calvin-institutes-beveridge" locator="I.13.1" /> The language is adapted to us, but it is not false. God really knows, wills and loves, in a way higher than ours.

Christianity adds something no other lane claims: God is personal *within himself*. He is one God in three persons. Before the world existed, the Father loved the Son: Jesus prays that his disciples may see the glory the Father gave him "because you loved me before the foundation of the world."<Cite source="bible-esv" locator="John 17:24" /> Love, knowledge and communication are therefore eternal in God. They did not begin when God made something to love.

## What this explains well

If the ultimate is personal, persons are not strangers in the universe: knowledge, love and moral address belong to its deepest level, which is why <QuestionLink question="what-is-man" worldview="christianity">human beings as God's image</QuestionLink> make sense. And the Trinity explains how God can be love without depending on creation for someone to love.

## Strongest objection

Maimonides gives the classic objection from within monotheism. Any positive description of God's essence, he argues, would introduce composition or likeness to creatures, since knowledge, will and love as we know them involve change and limitation. We can say only what God is not, or describe what he does.<Cite source="maimonides-guide-friedlander" locator="I.51, I.58" /> On this view calling God "personal" mistakes the language of his actions for a description of his being. Advaita Vedānta presses a similar point: personal qualities belong to the Lord as we encounter him, not to the highest reality.

## Christian reply

Reformed theology agrees that God is simple, not composed of parts,<Cite source="turretin-institutio-1847" locator="III.7.1–4" /> and that our concepts never comprehend him. But it denies that only negation is true. Scripture describes not only God's acts but his eternal life: the Father loved the Son before any act of creation. That is a statement about God, not his effects. Turretin distinguishes God's will toward himself, which is necessary, from his free will toward creatures,<Cite source="turretin-institutio-1847" locator="III.14.1–7" /> so the will is real in God without being a series of changing choices. Personal language is analogical: adapted to us, yet true of God in a higher way. The reply depends on revelation, which the objector may not accept.

## Deep dive

#### "Personal" and "three persons"

Two claims must be kept apart. "God is personal" means the one God knows, wills and loves. "God is three persons" means that within that one being there are three who are really distinct: Father, Son and Spirit. Calvin defines a person in the Trinity as a "subsistence" in God's essence, distinguished from the others by a property that cannot be shared.<Cite source="calvin-institutes-beveridge" locator="I.13.6" /> The persons are distinct but not divided.<Cite source="calvin-institutes-beveridge" locator="I.13.17" /> Augustine adds that the persons are distinguished only by their relations to one another, so the Trinity does not make God less simple.<Cite source="augustine-city-of-god" locator="XI.10" /> The persons are not three separate centres of being sharing a species, as three humans do.

#### Bavinck on the living God

Bavinck argues that the Trinity is what shows God to be the truly living God and the fullness of being, rather than an abstract unity.<Cite source="bavinck-reformed-dogmatics-monergism-2" locator="Vol. 2, §27.25, PDF pp. 385–387" /> He also argues against pantheism that it cannot distinguish God's will from the world's own process. Scripture, by contrast, gives creatures their own being because God wills them freely.<Cite source="bavinck-reformed-dogmatics-monergism-2" locator="Vol. 2, §26.16, PDF p. 262" /> A personal God can create something other than himself and let it be.

#### The confessional summary

The Westminster Confession describes God as "most loving, gracious, merciful, long-suffering," working all things by the counsel of his will. In the unity of the Godhead there are three persons "of one substance, power, and eternity."<Cite source="wcf" locator="2.1, 2.3" /> The Larger Catechism adds that the three are distinguished by their personal properties.<Cite source="westminster-larger-catechism" locator="Q. 9–10" /> The Belgic Confession says the persons are "neither divided nor fused or mixed."<Cite source="belgic-confession" locator="Art. 8" />

#### Where the comparison bites

Maimonides and the Advaita tradition both treat personal predicates as finally inadequate to the highest reality. Rāmānuja, Madhva, the Qur'an's names of God and Halevi's God of Abraham all affirm a personal ultimate. The Christian claim is distinctive in grounding that personhood in relations within God, which is why the Trinity matters for <QuestionLink question="one-and-many" worldview="christianity">the one and the many</QuestionLink>.
