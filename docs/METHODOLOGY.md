# Methodology

## Purpose

The site asks which worldview gives the most coherent account of reality as actually encountered: existence, knowledge, logic, personality, morality, beauty, evil, guilt, suffering, death, history, and human destiny.

The project is explicitly written from a **confessional Reformed Christian** standpoint. It does not claim a neutral standpoint above all worldviews. At the same time, every competing worldview must be presented in a form that a serious adherent or scholar could recognize as fair.

## The six comparison lanes

1. Reformed Christianity
2. Naturalistic atheism
3. Rabbinic Judaism
4. Classical Islam
5. Hindu traditions
6. Buddhist traditions

These are comparison families, not claims of total internal uniformity.

## Steelman rule

Before writing a Christian critique, the entry must answer:

1. What does this worldview actually claim?
2. Which major school or thinker is being represented?
3. What explanatory work does the view genuinely perform well?
4. Are important internal alternatives being omitted?
5. Can the steelman be supported from a primary source or serious internal authority?

A critique is not ready until those questions are answered.

## Comparison structure

### Non-Christian entries

**View**  
The strongest concise account of the position in its own terms.

**What it explains well**  
Real explanatory strengths, without conceding the truth of the whole system.

**Christian response**  
The Reformed critique, distinguishing internal contradiction, explanatory insufficiency, historical conflict, or dependence on premises not grounded by the worldview.

**Pressure point**  
One or two precise questions suitable for respectful apologetic dialogue.

### Christian entries

**Christian view**  
The positive Reformed account.

**What it explains well**  
The explanatory integration of the doctrine with the wider Christian system.

**Strongest objection**  
A serious objection, preferably one actually made by major critics rather than an invented weak objection.

**Christian reply**  
The best confessional Reformed response.

## Master tests

The site does not reduce worldview testing to one argument. It evaluates systems across six domains:

1. **Ultimate Reality**
2. **Knowledge & Truth**
3. **Man & Human Nature**
4. **Morality, Evil & the Human Problem**
5. **Salvation, Liberation & Human Destiny**
6. **Revelation & History**

## Reformed / Van Tilian commitments

The Christian analysis should preserve these distinctions:

- Creator and creature are absolutely distinct, yet creation is wholly dependent on God.
- God is not one hypothesis inside a reality more ultimate than Himself.
- Scripture is self-attesting because no authority stands above God, while evidence and historical argument remain genuine and important.
- There are no ultimately brute facts. Facts belong to God's created and providentially ordered world.
- Human knowledge is real but derivative and finite.
- Non-Christians genuinely know many truths because they remain image-bearers living in God's world.
- The apologetic claim is not "non-Christians cannot reason." It is that their successful reasoning may outrun what their professed ultimate commitments can consistently ground.
- General revelation, the remaining image of God, and common grace explain extensive knowledge, culture, civil virtue, and intellectual achievement outside explicit Christian faith.
- Sin affects intellect, will, and affections, including the suppression and reinterpretation of known truth.
- Conversion is not produced by intellectual technique. God ordinarily uses arguments and evidence as means, but regeneration is monergistic.

## Types of critique

Every Christian response should identify what kind of claim is being made.

### Internal coherence
Does the view undermine something it must use to state or defend itself?

### Explanatory adequacy
Does it describe a mechanism while leaving teleology, normativity, identity, or meaning unexplained?

### Historical conflict
Do its claims contradict well-attested events or earlier documents?

### Revelatory conflict
Does it accept some claimed revelation while rejecting elements that the accepted source appears to entail?

### Moral / anthropological adequacy
Can it explain both human dignity and human corruption, conscience and rebellion, love and cruelty, greatness and misery?

### Redemptive adequacy
Does it provide an account of guilt, justice, mercy, transformation, and final destiny proportionate to its diagnosis of the human problem?

Do not call something a contradiction when it is merely a disputed premise or unanswered question.

## Christianity is also tested

Christianity does not receive a free pass. Its entries should directly face difficult questions such as:

- divine sovereignty and evil;
- the Trinity;
- incarnation;
- election and responsibility;
- the problem of suffering;
- biblical authority;
- resurrection evidence;
- apparent divine hiddenness;
- religious disagreement.

The Christian reply should be the strongest confessional Reformed answer available.

## Tone

- precise;
- non-mocking;
- no triumphalist scoring;
- no "gotcha" quotations;
- pressure questions should expose an issue, not insult an adherent;
- distinguish "the Christian argues" from neutral historical description.
