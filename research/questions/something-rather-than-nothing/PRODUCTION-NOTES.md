## Framing decisions

- The question is compared by **explanatory structure**, not forced into a cosmological-argument template. Hindu and Buddhist theses open by rejecting the "from nothing" premise; the naturalist thesis opens by denying the "something from nothing" caricature.
- Christian: free will of a God who needs nothing → *ex nihilo* → God's existence explained differently (link to the anchor). Aseity, decree and hypothetical necessity appear only in the reply and the Deep dive.
- Naturalism: brute fact (Carroll; Russell via SEP), necessary universe (Hume D 9.7, Cleanthes), questioning the question (Hume E 4.6; Carroll's "coherence" option; SEP Nothingness §3). Hume's speakers identified.
- Judaism: Genesis and Psalm 148 → Rashi/JPS grammar → Bereshit Rabbah 1:9 → Nachmanides → Saadia and Maimonides; Ḥagigah 2:1 restraint and Halevi I.67 in the Deep dive.
- Islam: Qur'an 2:117, 52:35–36, 44:38–39 → three timelines kept distinct (kalām origination; Avicennian eternal necessitation; Ibn Taymiyya's perpetual creation). Hadith variants 3191/7418 explained.
- Hindu: ChU VI.2 (being not from non-being) → beginningless cycles → *līlā* (Śaṅkara, Rāmānuja) and Madhva's "nature of the Lord" → Advaita's "apparent world only" → Nyāya/Udayana in the Deep dive, with Rāmānuja's rejection of inference.
- Buddhist: SN 15.1, AN 4.77, MN 63 → rejection of creator-origin (DN 1, AN 3.61) and of causeless origin (DN 1:2.30–2.33) → Vasubandhu's karma conclusion; his argument set out in the Deep dive. The Christian lane answers Vasubandhu as its strongest objection (cross-lane link).

## Caveats carried into public prose

- Naturalism: Russell's 1948 wording reported via SEP; Oppy unreadable; Carroll a physicist's philosophical argument, not a consensus of physics; D 9 arguments are Cleanthes's.
- Islam: al-Ghazali (Bouyges OCR, approximate page) and Ibn Sina (Forget OCR) paraphrased only, with SEP AIR as control, and the public text says so.
- Judaism: *ex nihilo* not secured by Gen 1:1 grammar alone; Halevi's eternal-matter concession disclosed.
- Hindu: Advaita's "apparent world only"; Udayana's God orders atoms rather than creating from nothing; Rāmānuja rejects the inference.
- Reformed: Christian theologians differ on the *possibility* of an eternal world (Turretin vs Aquinas, interlocutor, named only).
