## Framing decisions

- **Question separation.** Every answer is organized around *what finally adjudicates*, not around how knowledge arises: final court → why it needs no higher certification → what reason and community do under it → how internal disputes are settled. Repeated material from `knowledge-possible` (MN 95's "preserving truth"; Nasafi's denial of *ilhām*) was removed from the Deep dives and replaced by links.
- **Self-certification.** Each lane states how its final authority is or is not certified, using the dossier's framing:
  - Reformed: deliberate self-attestation (WCF 1.4–5; Turretin II.6.11 regress; Bavinck §12 on principia and on Rome's equal circle). The reply states the comparative test in the brief's words: coherence, self-consistency, adequacy. It does not say "everyone is circular so it doesn't matter", and it concedes that self-attestation does not settle *which* book (deferred to Revelation & History).
  - Naturalism: "no first philosophy" as deliberate; Fumerton's charge given; the Christian response says "not a refutation" and turns to what the network uses but does not ground (normativity, logic, induction).
  - Mīmāṃsā: intrinsic validity and the authorless Veda as deliberate self-certification (Deep dive).
  - Dharmakīrti: fallibilist; SEP's "surprisingly fallibilist" attributed to Tillemans.
  - Islam: both books claim self-attestation, so the question becomes historical.
- **Reformed.** God speaking in Scripture (Num 23:19; John 17:17; 2 Tim 3:16; 2 Pet 1:21; WCF 1.10; Belgic 7) → no higher court (WCF 1.4–5; Spirit's witness gives no new message, WCF 1.6) → reason ministerial (Turretin I.10.3, our translation of "organice et ministerialiter, non despotice"; I.8.6 Hagar and Sarah; WCF 20.2 "and reason also") → church as instrument (Turretin II.6.6) → Scripture interprets Scripture (WCF 1.9). "Reason is rejected" is never said. Van Til is in the Deep dive as the apologetic use.
- **Naturalism.** No canon; a method → Quine's holism, revisability and no first philosophy (all via SEP) → Hume's "reflections of common life, methodized and corrected" → Russell's "intrinsic evidence" as a non-empirical element. No invented canon.
- **Judaism.** Sinai's public revelation (Deut 4:32–35; YT 8:1) → Written and Oral Torah as the tradition understands itself (Avot 1:1), explicitly not "later human tradition" → the court (Deut 17:8–11; Ramban on 17:11) → BM 59b → reason interprets and tests prophets (Saadia III.8; YT 9:1). Evaluation of Sinai deferred.
- **Islam.** Qur'an and Sunna (4:59) → reason recognizes revelation (4:82; Nasafi) → al-Shafi'i's four sources **as summarized by Macdonald 1903**, with the caveat stated in the citation note and in the Deep dive → al-Ghazali's rule vs. Ibn Taymiyya's no-conflict thesis (SEP). "Not Qur'an only" is explicit.
- **Hindu.** Śruti for the supersensible (Gītā 16.24; Manu II.10–11, paraphrased) → why: authorless (Kumārila) vs. authored (NS 2.1.69) → Vedānta: scripture alone for Brahman; reason's place (Śaṅkara II.1.11) → three readings, no neutral court → Advaita: scripture as lower knowledge relative to realization.
- **Buddhist.** The Dhamma as teacher (DN 16; SN 22.87) → AN 4.180 → AN 3.65 in context, with tested faith (MN 95, MN 47) beside it so that "believe nothing on authority" is excluded → realization and the raft → Dharmakīrti's limited scriptural authority (PV II.7–10 paraphrase; PV I.213–217 via SEP).

## Self-review

- **Elementary-answer test.** All six theses name the final court in the first clause. The Christian, Hindu and Buddhist theses use the brief's wording.
- **Naturalness.** Each view opens with the final court and why it is final, before any thinker.
- **Question separation.** No answer re-explains perception or inference. Where the mechanism matters, the answer links to `knowledge-possible`.
- **Internal diversity.** Calvin vs. Turretin emphasis; scientism vs. a priori reason; Maimonides vs. Halevi; al-Ghazali vs. Ibn Taymiyya; authorless vs. authored Veda and three Vedānta readings; canon vs. Dharmakīrti vs. Madhyamaka.
- **Argument strength.** Christian evaluations are classified: comparative coherence and adequacy (naturalism); theological and historical disagreement (Judaism, Islam, Buddhist); an unverifiable final premise in Advaita, "though it does not make the view incoherent".
- **Caveats.** Macdonald 1903; Quine and Ibn Taymiyya via SEP; PV I via SEP; Oral Torah in the tradition's own terms; Kālāma not "believe nothing".
