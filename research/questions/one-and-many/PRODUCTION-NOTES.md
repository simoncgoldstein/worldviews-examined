## Every lane carries a narrow caveat (dossier), and each shapes the prose

- **Reformed:** the doctrine is stated (one God, three persons; created unity-in-diversity), and Bavinck's argument is presented at Bavinck's own strength: in creatures there is only a weak analogy, and the Trinity is what lets theology hold God's connection with the world and his distinction from it. The strongest objection says the Trinity "names the puzzle"; the reply concedes it is revealed and not a demonstration. **Van Til's transcendental argument is not attributed.** The Deep dive says only that "some later Reformed apologists" pressed a stronger version that could not be checked. A draft sentence attributing that argument to Van Til was removed before commit.
- **Naturalism:** opens by saying the question is not native to naturalism; answers through physicalism, levels and emergence, and composition (Hume D 9.9; Russell's "organization").
- **Judaism:** opens by saying Judaism has no formal doctrine of the one and the many; Maimonides (YT 1:7; *Guide* I.72) with Saadia II.4; Ḥabad disclosed as a variant; Kabbalah marked unresearched.
- **Islam:** opens by saying Islam does not pose the problem formally; tawḥīd (21:22) and created plurality (17:44); attribute distinction; Ibn 'Arabi is contested, the *waḥdat al-wujūd* label is traced to Ibn Taymiyya with SEP's own hedge ("seems to have been"), and it is not the creed.
- **Hindu:** the three schools are kept distinct throughout; Madhva's five differences are via IEP with Madhva's BSBh I.1.17 as primary support for real difference.
- **Buddhist:** neither one nor many ultimate; emptiness explicitly not a hidden One; MMK 25.19–20 read as shared emptiness, not monism; Tiantai one example; Huayan unresearched.
