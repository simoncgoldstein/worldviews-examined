## Editorial hierarchy

Each thesis gives the elementary answer with no thinker names: God alone from himself, creation real but dependent (Reformed); no supernatural order, mostly physicalist, with open disputes (naturalism); one Creator, everything dependent (Judaism); God alone uncreated and incomparable, all else created by command (Islam); Brahman as source, with the distinctness dispute in the same sentence (Hindu); no permanent substance, conditioned arising, liberation not union with a ground (Buddhist). Opening paragraphs explain the belief before naming any interpreter; Maimonides, Ibn Sina, Śaṅkara, Rāmānuja, Madhva, Buddhaghosa and Nāgārjuna enter only after the core answer.

## Caveats carried into public prose

- Naturalism: no naturalist creed; physicalism presented as the majority, not the definition; Russell marked non-materialist; Oppy unread (stated in the Deep dive).
- Judaism: Maimonides' sphere cosmology marked as not the argument; Halevi as counterweight; Ḥabad and Kabbalah marked as variants (Kabbalah unresearched).
- Islam: Ibn Sina explicitly "not the creed"; Ibn 'Arabi's label attributed to Ibn Taymiyya's polemic; Ibn Sina paraphrased.
- Hindu: Advaita's world "not simply unreal" with Śaṅkara's own wording; Madhva's five differences via IEP; Śaiva/Śākta not covered.
- Buddhist: nirvāṇa explicitly not a ground or creator; emptiness "not another, deeper thing"; two renderings of Ud 8.3 disclosed.
- Reformed: strong simplicity noted as questioned by some recent Reformed writers; the reply labelled comparative judgment, not proof, and revelation-dependent.
