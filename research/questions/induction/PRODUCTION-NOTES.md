## Framing decisions

- **The problem stated first.** The naturalist view opens with Hume at full strength: no demonstrative argument (E 4.18), no probable argument without "going in a circle" (E 4.19), Russell's chicken. Then Hume's own "custom" (E 5.6).
- **Naturalist responses** in three visible families: benign circle (with Quine's evolution), add a principle (laws by explanation, Bayesian priors), change the question (Strawson, Reichenbach). The Deep dive lists all seven with type labels: accepts a benign circle / adds a principle / redefines justification / accepts fallibility. The Christian response classifies each family, says the theist "pays a price too", and calls the Humean residue "explanatory incompleteness, not contradiction". It does not say naturalism has no answer.
- **Reformed.** Gen 8:22, Jer 33:25, providence through second causes (WCF 5.2–3), Augustine DDC II.30.47. Then the required distinction, in the thesis and the view: a theological reason to expect regularity vs. a formally non-circular proof. The objection quotes Hume's circle against the theist; the reply grants that the Christian does not escape the circle and claims only comparative explanatory adequacy. Van Til's p. 120 claim ("the only presupposition…") is in the Deep dive as a later transcendental argument that would need to defeat every rival.
- **Judaism.** Jer 33:25; AZ 54b "the world follows its course"; Maimonides vs. the kalām "habit" (*Guide* I.73); Nachmanides by link to `order` to avoid duplication. The view states that no classical Jewish source addresses Hume. The Christian response records shared ground, not disagreement.
- **Islam.** Al-Ghazali's Tahāfut 17 opening (via SEP, with Bouyges paraphrase) → denial of *necessary* connection, not of all causal connection → God's *sunna* (Iḥyāʾ 31 via SEP), with the explicit sentence "Al-Ghazali applies the language more broadly, to the order of nature" and a Deep-dive section reading 33:62, 48:23 and 35:43 in context → created powers (Ibn Taymiyya, Maturidis) → falsafa necessity.
- **Hindu.** *Vyāpti* → the Cārvāka critique from Mādhava's survey (regress; *upādhi*; "thunderbolt-like fallacy of reasoning in a circle"; success "accidental") → Nyāya method (observation, no known counter-case, defeaters, hidden conditions, *tarka*) → fallibilism → "an Indian parallel to Hume's problem, not a translation of it".
- **Buddhist.** SN 12.20 → identity and causation (NB II.21) → discovery by presence and absence ("fivefold examination") → the Gillon/Tillemans dispute stated in both directions → "it would be wrong to say Buddhism solved induction, and equally wrong to say it has no account of it."

## Self-review

- **Elementary-answer test.** Each thesis gives the lane's reason to expect regularity and names the limit: no escape from Hume (Christianity, Judaism); several answers, none universal (naturalism); constant custom and a causal dispute (Islam); the Indian form of the problem (Hindu); disputed success (Buddhist).
- **Honesty about the problem.** Every lane says what it does *not* claim. No lane is said to have "solved" induction, and naturalism is not said to lack answers.
- **Argument strength.** The Reformed reply grants the objection's core. The Van Tilian "only" is marked as requiring an argument against every rival.
- **Caveats.** Q 33:62/48:23 context; al-Ghazali's denial of necessity, not of all causation; the Cārvāka's survival only in a hostile doxography; Gillon vs. Tillemans.
