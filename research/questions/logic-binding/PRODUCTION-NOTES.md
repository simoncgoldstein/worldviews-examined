## Framing decisions

- **Using vs. grounding.** Every view first says the tradition reasons rigorously and then states what, if anything, it says about why logic binds. Judaism and Islam say plainly that they have no developed metaphysics of logical necessity.
- **Reformed.** Augustine DDC II.32.50 → Scripture reasons (Isa 1:18; Heb 6:18) but no verse states a theory → "grounded in God" presented explicitly as a **theological inference** from God as truth, the Word (John 1:1–3) and the created fit of mind and world → Turretin and Bavinck → the sentence "None of these sources says that logic simply *is* God's nature, and none makes it a product of arbitrary divine will." The objection is the Euthyphro-style dilemma plus naturalist alternatives. The reply rejects both horns and labels itself "a claim of better explanation, not a proof". The Deep dive separates doctrine, epistemology and Van Til's apologetic claim (three bullets) and says the third "needs its own argument", noting that a realist like Russell does not hold logic to be a product of chance. Bahnsen is not used.
- **Naturalism.** Five positions: Russell's realism; convention with Quine's 1936 regress; Quinean revisability; Maddy's a posteriori view (Sher and Azzouni in the Deep dive); basic self-evidence, sourced to Russell ch. VII rather than invented. "Not nominalism by default" is a Deep-dive heading. The Christian response calls the physicalism/abstracta problem "a tension, not a contradiction".
- **Judaism.** No rabbinic doctrine → Saadia II.13 (the absurd is nothing; Hebrew, our translation of phrases) → Maimonides III.15 quoted exactly, including "not the result of some agent" → explicit statement that Maimonides does not place logic in God's mind → intellect vs. imagination (I.73) in the Deep dive.
- **Islam.** Immediate necessary knowledge (Nasafi; al-Juwayni via SEP; Ibn Taymiyya via SEP) → al-Ghazali's acceptance of logic vs. Ibn Taymiyya's critique, with "neither uniformly Aristotelian nor uniformly anti-Aristotelian".
- **Hindu.** Logic as the theory of knowledge-yielding inference; valid-but-unsound counted fallacious (SEP §5); pervasion as a real relation; fallacies; *tarka* (NS 1.1.40 quoted); Mīmāṃsā and Advaita noted. The modern form/content split is not imposed; there is a Deep-dive section titled "Not formal validity".
- **Buddhist.** Opens "Buddhism has a sophisticated tradition of logic and inference" → MN 76 read as a warning about reasoning-alone views, not logic → triple criterion → identity and causation (NB II.21 quoted) → Madhyamaka reductio. The nominalism/necessity tension is the Christian pressure point (SEP §1.5), labelled internal tension. The Gillon/Tillemans dispute is reserved for `induction`.

## Self-review

- **Elementary-answer test.** Each thesis answers "why does logic bind?" in its lane's terms: God's making of world and mind; several naturalist options; no rabbinic doctrine, with the impossible as nothing; immediate God-given knowledge; inference tracking real pervasion; real dependence, with a Madhyamaka dissent.
- **Restraint.** No lane is made to produce a modern metaphysics of logic it lacks.
- **Argument strength.** The Christian answer says three times, in different words, that grounding logic in God is an inference, and the Van Tilian claim is marked as needing argument. Other evaluations: tension (naturalism, Buddhist), theological disagreement (Judaism), difference of explicitness (Islam), explanatory disagreement (Hindu).
- **Caveats.** "Logic is God's nature" is explicitly disclaimed. Maimonides is not assimilated to Augustine. Naturalists are not called nominalists. Ibn Taymiyya is via SEP. Buddhist warnings are distinguished from rejecting logic.
