## Framing decisions

- The question is posed intuitively ("why isn't reality just chaos?"), and each answer explains before naming terms. "Laws of nature" in the modern Western sense is not imposed: *ṛta*/*dharma* is explicitly cosmic-moral-ritual order; SN 12.20's "law of nature" is explained as specific conditionality; Islamic "God's practice" and custom are kept in their own terms; naturalism keeps the Humean "laws describe" option.
- Reformed: wisdom and covenant faithfulness, then real second causes (WCF 5.2–3), explicitly not occasionalist. Calvin's special providence and Bavinck's stable natural order are shown as two held emphases.
- Naturalism: Hume's regularity → three accounts of laws → natural selection and Philo's self-sustaining order → fine-tuning responses. Christian response presses on induction and grounding as comparative adequacy, not contradiction.
- Judaism: wisdom, fixed laws, Torah as blueprint → Maimonides (general vs individual providence, not demonstrative) vs Nachmanides (hidden miracles).
- Islam: measure and unchanging practice → four positions on causation kept distinct (Ash'ari/al-Ghazali; Maturidi; Ibn Taymiyya; Ibn Rushd). Bavinck's polemic against "Mohammedan" occasionalism is not used as evidence.
- Hindu: *ṛta* → command of the Imperishable and the inner ruler → karma dispensation → Śaṅkara against a non-intelligent cause → Rāmānuja's will "establishes an order for existences"; Nyāya inference vs Vedānta scripture-only.
- Buddhist: SN 12.20 conditionality → SN 12.1 chain → karma within order → Vasubandhu's no single cause → MMK 24.18–19, 15.1–2 so that emptiness is not set against order and is not reified.
