# Steelman QA review: `great-and-terrible`

Independent steelman reviewer. Date: 2026-10-05. Scope: the six packets in
`research/questions/great-and-terrible/packets/` (christianity, naturalism, judaism, islam,
hinduism, buddhism), with the closest attention on the draft MDX at the end of each packet
(The view / The Christian view, What this explains well, Deep dive). `christianity-objection.md`
was read only to compare tone.

This review does not write Christian critique and does not rewrite the packets. Each finding gives
a concrete fix. Severity: **blocking** = must be fixed before the answer can move to `reviewed`;
**major** = must be fixed before publication; **minor** = should be fixed, or the editor should
record why not.

## 1. Reformed Christianity (`christianity`)

**Verdict: pass with required fixes.** An informed confessional Presbyterian would recognise this
account immediately. It has primary-text depth from Calvin (Beveridge), Augustine (NPNF), the WCF,
the WLC and Edwards, and it is candid about internal debates (1924; imputation; Romans 7). Every
spot-check passed. The concerns are that the draft softens the Confession's hardest sentence, and
that "What this explains well" carries comparative claims that work as critique of other lanes.

**CHR-1 · major · Draft, "The Christian view" and Deep dive "A ruined image", whole account of corruption.**
- *Problem:* The draft quotes WCF 6.2 ("wholly defiled") and WLC 25 ("spiritually good"). It never
  quotes WCF 6.4: "utterly indisposed, disabled, and made opposite to all good, and wholly inclined
  to all evil". The Deep dive then argues that corruption is "extensive rather than maximal".
  Those two choices together present the most palatable form of the doctrine. A strict confessional
  reader would say the Confession's own strongest sentence has been left out. A critic would call
  it selective. The objection packet makes WCF 6.4 load-bearing, so the positive account and the
  objection would not meet. By contrast, the Hindu draft voluntarily prints its own hard text
  (caste; see P-3).
- *Evidence:* `christianity-objection.md` "The target" quotes WCF 6.4. Packet §12.7 acknowledges
  "opposite to all good" (6.4) but the draft does not use it.
- *Fix:* In the Deep dive paragraph that begins "This is why the Reformed doctrine of corruption
  is extensive…", quote WCF 6.4 in full with `<Cite source="wcf" locator="6.4" />`. Then give
  the WLC 25 and WCF 16.7 qualification as the Confession's own gloss, rather than as a
  replacement for it.

**CHR-2 · major · Draft, "What this explains well", sentences 1–2.**
- *Problem:* "The account takes both halves of the phenomenon at full strength. Dignity is not
  reduced to an illusion, and evil is not reduced to ignorance". This is framed by contrast. In a
  six-card comparison the reader will take it as aimed at naturalism ("illusion"), Advaita and
  Buddhism ("ignorance"), and Socratic intellectualism. That is Christian critique placed inside a
  positive section. No other lane's "explains well" section makes comparative claims.
- *Evidence:* Brief §7 says "What this explains well" should state "genuine strengths". Packet §10
  item 1 makes the contrast explicit ("not … to ignorance, maladaptation or social conditioning").
- *Fix:* State the strength in positive terms only. For example: "The account affirms both halves
  at full strength: human dignity is real and permanent, and wrongdoing can be knowing and
  willing, not only mistaken." Keep the Calvin II.2.23 cite.

**CHR-3 · minor · Draft, "The Christian view", sentence 3 ("By Adam's sin the race fell…").**
- *Problem:* Packet §12.3 itself says to mark federal headship as the confessional position,
  because a historical Adam is disputed in contemporary Reformed scholarship. The draft does not
  mark it anywhere.
- *Fix:* Add one clause in Deep dive "Adam, freedom and remedy": "This is the Confession's
  position; the historicity of Adam is debated among contemporary Reformed scholars." It can stay
  uncited, as context, or be cited once a source is checked.

**CHR-4 · minor · Draft, "The Christian view", last sentence ("the remedy is not self-improvement but renewal").**
- *Problem:* "Self-improvement" is a loaded description that implicitly targets other lanes' remedies.
- *Fix:* Write "the remedy must reach the will itself: renewal 'after the image of its creator'".

**CHR-5 · minor · Deep dive "Where Reformed theologians differ".**
- *Problem:* The Van Til sentence rests only on Frame's secondary account. The Van Til page itself
  (*Defense*, p. 170) was not read. Every other Reformed claim in the draft is primary. The 1924
  sentence and the imputation sentence are uncited (drafting notes acknowledge this).
- *Fix:* Read *Defense of the Faith* 3rd ed. p. 170 and cite it, or drop the Van Til sentence.
  Keep the 1924 sentence only once a checked source exists (for example, the Acts of Synod 1924
  or a scholarly history).

**CHR-6 · minor · Deep dive, Edwards *Original Sin*.**
- *Problem:* The packet read *Original Sin* I.1.1 (a tendency inferred from its constant effect,
  against Taylor and Turnbull), but the draft does not use it. It is the strongest classical
  Reformed argument that universality is evidence for a nature-level cause. Using it would make
  the account stronger, not more flattering.
- *Fix (optional):* Add one sentence with `edwards-works-hickman-1` locator "Original Sin I.1.1".

Answers to the ten questions, where no finding was raised: (2) The strongest internal account was
selected, apart from CHR-1. (3) Schools are not collapsed; the debates table is exemplary. (5)
Every spot-check supported its clause. (8) Not applicable. (10) The section is genuine and
specific, subject to CHR-2.

## 2. Naturalistic atheism (`naturalism`)

**Verdict: pass with required fixes.** The scope choice (evolutionary-sentimentalist anthropology,
kept separate from metaethics) is exactly what a philosophically serious naturalist would want.
The Darwin and Hume material is accurate and well chosen. Problems: the definitional sentence
about morality in The view is anti-realist; the account of human *greatness* is thin; and the
most directly relevant contemporary science is missing.

**NAT-1 · blocking (for `reviewed`) · Deep dive, "One source, two faces" and "Agency and the self".**
- *Problem:* The draft cites `greene-point-and-shoot` (unverified; read only in a preprint) and
  `dennett-self-artifact` (unverified; page numbers inferred) four times.
- *Fix:* Confirm Dennett pp. 39, 48 and 49 on the Wiley PDF, and Greene's published pagination.
  Otherwise drop the Greene sentence, or replace it with `sep-weakness-will` §3.4.2.

**NAT-2 · major · Draft, "The view", paragraph 3, sentence 1.**
- *Problem:* "On this view, morality is a set of natural and cultural devices that make up for our
  limited sympathies," cited to Mackie p. 108. This is Mackie's and Warnock's functional
  (anti-realist) account, presented as *the* naturalist view of what morality is. A Railton-style
  realist, or a Cornell realist, would object that morality is not only a device: moral facts are
  facts. The packet itself says (§13) that Mackie's error theory "should not stand for naturalism".
  The next sentence ("Naturalists disagree…") does not undo the framing.
- *Fix:* Rewrite: "Naturalists agree that moral *capacities* have this natural history. They
  disagree about what morality *is*. For Mackie it is a human device for counteracting limited
  sympathies<Cite mackie p. 108/>; for realists such as Railton, moral facts concern what would be
  approved if everyone's interests counted equally<Cite railton p. 190/>."

**NAT-3 · major · Draft, "The view" and "What this explains well": greatness side; missing literature.**
- *Problem:* Rationality, creativity, science and art (half of the brief's phenomenon) are reduced
  to "clever". The strongest current naturalist accounts of *both* sides are absent:
  - Richard Wrangham, *The Goodness Paradox* (2019). Its whole subject is this question:
    self-domestication, low reactive aggression, high proactive and coalitionary aggression.
  - Christopher Boehm, *Moral Origins* (2012), which the packet names in its §10 table but does
    not use.
  - Michael Tomasello on shared intentionality and cumulative culture.
  - Frans de Waal on primate empathy and reconciliation.

  "Explains well" asserts the animal-continuity claim ("rudiments of empathy and of coalitionary
  violence appear in other social animals") with no citation.
- *Fix:* Add one View sentence on cumulative culture, language and shared intentionality as the
  naturalist explanation of science, art and civilisation (cite Tomasello or SEP once checked).
  Cite Wrangham or de Waal for the animal-continuity sentence. Consider making Wrangham the
  contemporary empirical voice in "One source, two faces".

**NAT-4 · minor · Draft, "What this explains well", sentence 4.**
- *Problem:* "The loyalty that inspires sacrifice in war also fuels hatred of the enemy," cited to
  Darwin ch. V p. 130. Darwin says only that a tribe of "courageous, sympathetic and faithful
  members … would succeed better and conquer the other". He says nothing about hatred.
- *Fix:* Cut "fuels hatred of the enemy" back to "is selected in competition between groups", or
  attach a source that makes the hostility claim (for example, Wrangham or Choi and Bowles, once
  checked).

**NAT-5 · minor · Draft, "The view", paragraph 2, last sentence (double cite).**
- *Problem:* Hume T 2.3.3.10 says "Men often act knowingly against their *interest*". That is
  prudential akrasia. Hume does not mention moral wrong or remorse. The sentence it supports is
  about "knowingly doing wrong … and remorse follows". This also creates a cite cluster.
- *Fix:* Drop the Hume cite from The view and keep Darwin pp. 113–114. In the Deep dive, keep
  Hume but say "act knowingly against their interest", which the draft already quotes correctly.

**NAT-6 · minor · Draft, "The view", sentence 2.**
- *Problem:* "…shaped by biological and cultural evolution, not fallen from a higher state," cited
  to Russell ch. 1. Russell ch. 1 says "Man is a part of Nature, not something contrasted with
  Nature". It mentions neither evolution nor a fall. The "not fallen" clause also defines the
  view against Christianity (see P-2).
- *Fix:* "Human beings are part of nature<Cite russell ch.1/>, products of biological and
  cultural evolution<Cite darwin…/>."

**NAT-7 · minor · Scope: representatives.**
- *Problem:* The two "central" voices are Hume (religious position contested) and Darwin
  (self-described agnostic). Oppy, the lane's registered contemporary primary, was not read. This
  is acceptable for an anthropological question, and the packet discloses it, but a naturalist
  reader may notice that no avowed atheist philosopher carries the visible section.
- *Fix:* Read and cite one Oppy text, or Kitcher's *Life After Faith*, in the Deep dive scope
  paragraph. Make sure the Darwin thinker profile states the agnosticism.

Answers to the ten questions, where no finding was raised: (3) Realist and anti-realist
naturalists are kept distinct in the Deep dive, apart from NAT-2. (4) The Russell 1903 and 1925
positions are kept apart. (10) The section is genuine and specific.

## 3. Rabbinic Judaism (`judaism`)

**Verdict: pass with required fixes.** This is strong and recognisable. The generative *yetzer
ha-ra* (Bereshit Rabbah 9:7; Yoma 69b) is the right centre for this question, and Maimonides,
Nachmanides and Soloveitchik are correctly distinguished. Almost every Sefaria spot-check
passed. The problems: one visible citation shows the opposite of what it is cited for, and the
Hasidic and Musar anthropology is missing. Much of contemporary traditional Judaism reads this
question through those two.

**JUD-1 · major · Draft, "The view", sentence 5 ("God formed them with two inclinations").**
- *Problem:* This is cited to b. Berakhot 61a. In that passage Rav Naḥman bar Rav Ḥisda's
  two-inclinations reading of the double *yod* is immediately *rejected*. Rav Naḥman bar Yitzḥak
  "strongly objects": animals have an inclination too. The Gemara then substitutes R. Shimon ben
  Pazi's "woe unto me from my Creator … from my inclination". The packet (§2) presents R. Shimon's
  saying as a gloss on the two-yetzer reading, but it is offered as its replacement.
- *Evidence:* Sefaria, William Davidson Edition, Berakhot 61a.
- *Fix:* Cite m. Berakhot 9:5 instead: "with all your heart — with your two inclinations, the
  good inclination and the evil inclination" (`talmud-bavli-koren` or `mishnah-kulp`, Berakhot 9:5;
  checked on Sefaria). Or cite Bereshit Rabbah 14:4. If 61a is kept in the Deep dive, describe it
  accurately as a debated derivation.

**JUD-2 · blocking (for `reviewed`) · Deep dive, "Eden, mortality…" and "The philosophers".**
- *Problem:* `maimonides-guide-friedlander` is cited three times (I.2, III.8, III.12), but its
  metadata is unverified (1903 or 1904). It is quoted verbatim ("all corruption … from matter").
- *Fix:* Confirm the edition statement against a catalogue record. Or switch to `maimonides-guide`
  (Pines) with canonical locators and no verbatim Friedländer wording.

**JUD-3 · major · Missing strands (Q9).**
- *Problem:* The packet deliberately leaves out Ḥasidism and Musar (§1 and §13.9). For *this*
  question that omission is material:
  - *Tanya* (Shneur Zalman of Liadi) chs. 1–2 and 12–13: the divine soul and the animal soul, and
    the *beinoni*, who never sins in deed yet still desires. This is perhaps the most influential
    modern traditional Jewish account of exactly "knowing the good while desiring otherwise".
  - Rabbi Eliyahu Dessler's *nekudat ha-beḥirah* ("point of choice", *Mikhtav me-Eliyahu*).
  - Ramḥal's *Mesillat Yesharim*.

  A Ḥabad or yeshiva-world reader would notice the absence at once.
- *Fix:* Read *Tanya* chs. 1, 12–13 (Sefaria hosts the Lessons in Tanya English and the Hebrew).
  Add one Deep dive paragraph, "Ḥasidic and Musar psychology", with a checked citation. At
  minimum, add one sentence naming the strand as a material alternative.

**JUD-4 · minor · Draft, "The view", "Greatness and wickedness therefore spring largely from the same created energy".**
- *Problem:* In the sources, the image, intellect and kindness (Avot 3:14; BR 8:5) are not *yetzer*
  energy. The View folds all greatness into the inclination. Maimonides' form/matter account (in
  which merits come from form and vices from matter) is a strong, opposite rabbinic-philosophical
  answer, and it does not appear in the visible section at all.
- *Fix:* Add one clause: "…in the aggadic strand; Maimonides instead traces merits to the
  intellect and vices to matter." Cite *Guide* III.8 once JUD-2 is resolved, or *Eight Chapters*.

**JUD-5 · minor · Draft, "The view", "Guilt remains personal." (uncited).**
- *Problem:* This is a correct and important claim, but it stands uncited and reads as a contrast
  with Christianity (see P-2).
- *Fix:* Attach Ezekiel 18:20, or move it into the Deep dive, where it is already cited.

**JUD-6 · minor · Draft, "What this explains well", last sentence.**
- *Problem:* "It holds together dignity that no crime erases, responsibility without fatalism, and
  a concrete, hopeful practice of moral change." This is generic and uncited, unlike the rest of
  the section.
- *Fix:* Make one item concrete. For example: *teshuvah*'s test of facing the same temptation
  and refraining, with a Hilkhot Teshuvah 2:1–2 cite.

Answers to the ten questions, where no finding was raised: (4) Soloveitchik is labelled as modern.
Reform and Conservative are noted briefly but adequately. (6) Halevi's particularism is
appropriately kept out of the visible text. (7) The packet is primary-heavy. This is the best lane
on question 7.

## 4. Classical Islam (`islam`)

**Verdict: pass with required fixes.** Making al-Ghazali's *Ihya'* the principal voice is
defensible and representative. Its four qualities, the four stages of an act, and "humanity
defined by return" are a genuinely rich steelman. Every Qur'an spot-check matched Abdel Haleem
exactly. The weak point is that the second most important voice for this question, Ibn Taymiyya,
is handled entirely through the SEP. No other lane does that for a thinker of comparable weight.

**ISL-1 · blocking (for `reviewed`) · Deep dive, "Honour, trust and the angels' question".**
- *Problem:* It cites `ibn-kathir-tafsir-abridged` (unverified: print metadata not confirmed).
- *Fix:* Confirm the Darussalam abridgement metadata. Or drop the cite and keep the sentence as
  "Commentators disagree…", which is already supported by the Qur'an citation and §13.

**ISL-2 · major · Deep dive "Fitra without inherited guilt" and "School differences" (Q7).**
- *Problem:* All five Ibn Taymiyya claims (*fitra* as an original disposition; humans
  "necessarily will, love, worship, and obey something"; creation of acts through human will;
  failure as privation) are cited to SEP (Hoover). Two of them are quoted as if they were Ibn
  Taymiyya's words, but the wording is Hoover's. Ibn Arabi, Ibn Sina and the Ash'ari
  revelation-grounded obligation are also SEP-only. Primary texts exist in translation:
  - Ibn Taymiyya's *al-'Ubudiyya* (English trans. *Ibn Taymiyyah's Essay on Servitude*), the
    source of the "necessarily loves and serves" thesis;
  - the *fitra* excerpts from *Dar' ta'arud* translated in Hoover's and El-Tobgui's scholarship.
- *Fix:* Read and cite *al-'Ubudiyya* for the love and worship thesis. Change "He adds that humans
  'necessarily will, love, worship, and obey something'" to attribute the paraphrase ("in Jon
  Hoover's summary…") unless the primary text is read.

**ISL-3 · minor · Draft, "The view" and "What this explains well"; Deep dive heading.**
- *Problem:* "Evil is created power misdirected, not inherited guilt", "without positing inherited
  guilt", and the heading "Fitra without inherited guilt" define the Islamic account three times
  by negating a Christian doctrine. The Islamic positive claim is personal responsibility plus
  *fitra*. See P-2.
- *Fix:* Keep the point once, in the Deep dive. In The view write "Evil is created power
  misdirected; each soul answers for its own deeds" (6:164 is already cited). Rename the heading
  to "Fitra and personal responsibility".

**ISL-4 · minor · Draft, "The view", sentence 5 (*fitra*).**
- *Problem:* "a God-given orientation toward its Creator" adopts one reading of *fitra*. Packet
  §13.4 lists three readings and notes al-Ghazali's "no preponderance" text.
- *Fix:* "…born on the *fitra*, the original nature, which many scholars read as an orientation
  toward its Creator, and is then shaped by upbringing."

**ISL-5 · minor · Draft, "The view", paragraph 2.**
- *Problem:* Two cites support less than their clauses claim:
  - "Man is created weak and hasty" is cited to 4:28 only. 4:28 says "weak"; "hasty" is 17:11
    and 21:37.
  - "The remedy is guidance, repentance and purification … under God's mercy and judgment" is
    cited to 91:9–10, which says only that the one who purifies succeeds.
- *Fix:* Add `<Cite … locator="21:37" />` after "hasty". Narrow the last sentence ("The remedy is
  purification of the soul<91:9–10>, through repentance and guidance under God's mercy<39:53>"),
  or add 2:38 and 39:53.

**ISL-6 · minor · Draft, "What this explains well", sentence 6.**
- *Problem:* "It holds universal dignity and real moral ranking together" is cited only to 49:13,
  which is the ranking half.
- *Fix:* Add 17:70 for the universal half.

**ISL-7 · minor · Missing strands (Q9), and scannability.**
- *Problem:* Strands missing for this question: Ibn Qayyim al-Jawziyya on diseases of the heart
  and misdirected love (the most developed Hanbali counterpart to al-Ghazali); al-Raghib
  al-Isfahani's *al-Dhari'a*, on *khilafa* as the basis of human nobility, which al-Ghazali drew
  on; and any Shi'i voice. The last is acknowledged as unverified. The View packs 12 citations
  into about 160 words, the densest of the six (see P-5).
- *Fix:* Optional: one sentence on Ibn Qayyim if read. Cut the View to roughly 7–8 cites by
  removing 20:115 and one of 2:37 or 6:164.

Answers to the ten questions, where no finding was raised: (2) Selecting al-Ghazali is the
strongest classical choice. (3) Ash'ari, Maturidi, Athari, falsafa and Ibn Arabi are tabulated
carefully, and the Maturidi interpreters are kept distinct. (6) The View names "Mainstream Sunni
thought" and al-Ghazali, which is fine. (10) The section is genuine and specific.

## 5. Hindu traditions (`hinduism`)

**Verdict: pass with required fixes.** The Gītā 3.36 anchor is excellent; it is the exact
question. Advaita and Viśiṣṭādvaita get equal weight, and Dvaita is correctly flagged as changing
the answer. Primary-text checks of Sastri, the SBE volumes and Śrī Bhāṣya passed. Problems: a
theistic claim is cited to an Advaitin commentator; the visible text generalises past Dvaita; and
the most directly relevant non-Vedānta material for this question (Yoga kleśas, Śaiva *mala*) is
missing.

**HIN-1 · major · Draft, "The view", sentence 1.**
- *Problem:* "…in theistic Vedānta, belongs to God: the Bhagavad Gītā calls the individual self
  God's 'higher nature' and 'a portion of Myself'," cited to `sastri-gita-shankara` 7.5; 15.7.
  Two problems:
  1. In Sastri the verse of 15.7 reads "A ray of Myself"; "integral portion" is in Śaṅkara's
     commentary.
  2. On the next page Śaṅkara glosses the jīva as "only an *imaginary* portion of Myself" and says
     "as a matter of fact, Jīva is not a portion of the Supreme Self."

  So the theistic reading is sourced to the commentator who explicitly rejects it.
- *Evidence:* archive.org `bhagavadgitawith00maharich` djvu text, Disc. XV pp. 361–362.
- *Fix:* Cite `govindacharya-gita-ramanuja` 7.5 and 15.7 for the theistic reading. Or cite
  Sastri for the verse wording only ("a ray of Myself") and note that Advaita reads it as an
  apparent portion.

**HIN-2 · major · Draft, "The view", last two sentences.**
- *Problem:* "Greatness and wickedness are therefore two states of one self … The cure is
  knowledge, disciplined action, and devotion and surrender to God." This generalises to "Hindu
  traditions" two claims the packet says do not hold across schools:
  - Packet §6 and the §10 table: Dvaita distributes virtue and vice partly across innately
    different souls.
  - For Advaita the cure is ultimately knowledge, not surrender to God.

  The visible card never mentions Madhva, so a Mādhva reader is left out of the summary.
- *Fix:* "For both, greatness and wickedness are two states of one self… Madhva's Dvaita
  differs, distributing them partly across innately different souls. The schools' remedies
  range from liberating knowledge (Advaita) to devotion and surrender to God (Rāmānuja, Madhva)."

**HIN-3 · major · Missing strands (Q9).**
- *Problem:* For a question about knowingly doing wrong and an innate disorder, three classical
  Hindu resources are absent:
  1. Patañjali's *Yoga Sūtra* 2.3–2.9 on the *kleśas* (avidyā, asmitā, rāga, dveṣa,
     abhiniveśa), the most compact classical Hindu psychology of affliction. Packet §1 sets
     "Sāṃkhya-Yoga" aside as "its own lane", but it is inside the Hindu lane.
  2. Śaiva Siddhānta's *āṇava mala*, an innate, beginningless impurity of the soul. This is the
     closest Hindu analogue to a nature-level disorder and would materially strengthen the
     "terrible" half.

## 6. Buddhist traditions (`buddhism`)

**Verdict: pass with required fixes.** This is the most text-dense lane: Pali, Sanskrit and
Japanese read directly. Its "one mind, two conditions", near-enemy and MN 14 material is excellent
for this question. SuttaCentral spot-checks matched Sujato exactly. Four visible or near-visible
claims overstate or misdescribe the tradition, and three unverified sources sit in the draft.

**BUD-1 · major · Draft, "The view", sentence 7.**
- *Problem:* "…Mahāyāna texts describe beings as good by nature with faults that are
  adventitious," cited to BCA 6.40. BCA 6.39–40 is a *dilemma*: *if* harming is beings' nature,
  anger is as pointless as anger at fire; *if instead* the fault is adventitious and beings are by
  nature good, anger is as pointless as anger at the sky for smoke. Śāntideva does not assert the
  second horn. Packet §9 even says "in a conditional argument", but the draft drops the
  qualification. A Madhyamaka (Gelug) reader would also object to "good by nature" as a general
  Mahāyāna claim.
- *Evidence:* Translations of BCA 6.39–40 (for example, studybuddhism.com "Showing Patience";
  Tsadra *Explore/Chapter 6*).
- *Fix:* Replace with "and buddha-nature texts teach that the mind's nature is luminous and its
  defilements adventitious", cited to `ratnagotravibhaga-johnston` I.63 (checked). Move BCA 6.40
  to the Deep dive with its conditional form stated.

**BUD-2 · blocking (for `reviewed`) · Draft, "The view" and Deep dive.**
- *Problem:* Unverified sources are cited in the draft:
  - `dharmakirti-pramanavarttika` II.219, in The view (numbering caveat);
  - `shantideva-bodhicaryavatara`, 9 cites (first-printing metadata);
  - `dogen-shobogenzo`, 3 cites;
  - `vasubandhu-abhidharmakosa`, 1 cite.
- *Fix:* Follow the packet's own swap (BCA 8.134 for PV II.219) once BCA metadata is confirmed.
  Confirm the Crosby & Skilton 1995 imprint. For Dōgen, register the Sōtō Zen Text Project
  translation or a checkable Japanese edition as the cited source.

**BUD-3 · major · Draft, "What this explains well", sentence 6.**
- *Problem:* "Latent tendencies present even in infants explain why the disorder is universal
  before anyone chooses it." On the tradition's own account, the infant's *anusaya* are the
  results of volitional action in beginningless past lives. They are not prior to *all* choosing,
  only prior to this life's choosing. A Buddhist would object that the sentence imports a
  non-karmic, nature-level account.
- *Fix:* "…explain why the disorder appears in everyone before they choose anything in this life;
  the tradition traces it to habit carried over from beginningless past action."

**BUD-4 · major · Deep dive, "The remedy", sentence 1 ("Every strand holds the condition curable").**
- *Problem:* Packet §10.6 records the *icchantika* question. East Asian Faxiang (Yogācāra)
  explicitly taught a class of beings lacking the seed for nirvāṇa (*agotra*, within the
  five-*gotra* doctrine). The absolute claim collapses a real school difference.
- *Fix:* "Nearly every strand holds the condition curable for all beings; some Yogācāra and
  Faxiang teachers held that a class of beings lacks the potential for nirvāṇa, a view
  buddha-nature texts reinterpret or reject." Cite RGV vyākhyā on I.41, or a checked academic
  source.

**BUD-5 · major · Missing strands (Q9).**
- *Problem:* Two East Asian positions would materially strengthen the "terrible" half:
  1. Tiantai's doctrine that buddha-nature *inherently includes evil* (*xing'e*; Zhiyi, Zhili), a
     direct rejection of the "adventitious" model presented as the Mahāyāna view.
  2. Shinran's Pure Land claim that the *evil person* is the true object of Amida's vow (*Tannishō*
     3), the most radical Buddhist account of human incapacity.

  The packet flags Pure Land but not Tiantai.
- *Fix:* Add a sentence to the Deep dive's "Buddha-nature" paragraph naming Tiantai inherent evil
  as a dissenting Mahāyāna position (checked source: Ziporyn, *Evil and/or/as the Good*, Harvard
  2000, or SEP "Tiantai", if read). Give the existing Pure Land sentence a *Tannishō* 3 citation
  once read.

**BUD-6 · minor · Draft, "The view", sentence 1.**
- *Problem:* "do not explain the human paradox by two natures, one noble and one fallen". This
  defines the view against an apparent Christian position, and the Reformed packet explicitly
  rejects that position ("Wickedness is not a second nature beside the good one"). The contrast
  is therefore also inaccurate about Christianity. See P-2.
- *Fix:* Begin positively: "Buddhist traditions explain the human paradox by one mind under two
  conditions."

**BUD-7 · minor · Draft, "The view", sentence on human birth.**
- *Problem:* "Human birth is rare and uniquely suited to awakening," cited to SN 56.48. That
  sutta establishes rarity and the present opportunity (2.4). "Uniquely suited" comes from AN 9.21
  and BCA 1.4.
- *Fix:* Add `AN 9.21` after "uniquely suited to awakening".

Answers to the ten questions, where no finding was raised: (3) Theravāda and tathāgatagarbha are
carefully distinguished on whether the good is primary (§6 nuance, §10.1). (8) The "dignity" and
"conscience" vocabulary is handled with explicit caution (§13.8). This is good practice.
