### R-1 — Important — Christianity: the strongest objection omits divine determination of the problem

**File:** [Christianity answer](/C:/Personal/worldviews-examined/src/content/answers/christianity/great-and-terrible.mdx:27)

> “What remains is a disputed ultimate premise that neither side demonstrates: whether responsibility requires self-origination.”

**Problem:** This leaves the objection substantially narrower than the confessional account requires. The difficulty is also why a good God ordains the Fall and subsequent sins, creates agents whose corrupted wills require divine renewal, and withholds that renewal from some. The distinction between natural and moral inability does not itself answer that objection.

**Evidence:** Westminster teaches comprehensive divine decree in 3.1, passing over some people in 3.7, providential governance extending to the Fall and other sins in 5.4, and judicial withholding of grace in 5.6. It simultaneously denies that God authors sin and preserves secondary agency. Those qualifications deserve their strongest defense, but the answer presently omits the difficulty they address. [Westminster Confession, chapters 3–5](https://www.opc.org/wcf.html)

**Strongest opposing reply:** “I need not demand that creatures create themselves. I can question whether voluntary wrongdoing sufficiently explains culpability when God ordains its occurrence and controls the indispensable remedy.”

**Remedy:** Include this objection alongside inherited guilt. Present the Reformed distinction between divine ordination and creaturely sinful intention, then identify what that distinction resolves and what remains disputed. Do not reduce the remaining disagreement to self-origination.

### R-2 — Important — Naturalism: obligation is conflated with desire-independent reasons for action

**File:** [Naturalism answer](/C:/Personal/worldviews-examined/src/content/answers/naturalism/great-and-terrible.mdx:53)

> “The Christian argues that obligation binds the knave too, because it is owed to God…”

**Problem:** The contrast suggests that binding the unwilling wrongdoer is something Christianity supplies beyond Railton. Railton explicitly preserves that obligation. His concession concerns instrumental rational motivation, not exemption from moral duties.

**Evidence:** On printed p. 201, Railton distinguishes moral obligation from rational motivation. On p. 203, he expressly rejects the inference that knavish desires license exemption from obligation. The article’s concluding discussion of the logic analogy partly recognizes this distinction, but the main response and first pressure question still trade on its collapse. [Railton, “Moral Realism,” pp. 201–203](https://r.jordan.im/download/philosophy/railton1986.pdf)

**Strongest opposing reply:** “The knave remains obligated on my account. Your objection requires an argument that genuine obligation must also furnish a reason relative to every agent’s ends.”

**Remedy:** Distinguish applicability of obligation, practical reasons, and motivation. Challenge Railton’s separation explicitly rather than presenting universal obligation as the missing feature. Explain why a divine lawgiver solves the particular problem identified.

### R-3 — Important — Judaism: the sacrifice question presupposes an unnecessarily exclusive account of forgiveness

**File:** [Judaism answer](/C:/Personal/worldviews-examined/src/content/answers/judaism/great-and-terrible.mdx:31)

> “Leviticus ties atonement to a life given on the altar… since the Temple’s destruction, how should that link be understood? Has it been replaced, fulfilled in another form, or does it await restoration?”

**Problem:** The options invite the inference that forgiveness without an offered life needs a post-Temple replacement or fulfillment. They omit the strongest Jewish answer: sacrificial atonement and non-sacrificial forgiveness already coexist in the shared Scriptures.

**Evidence:** Leviticus 5:11–13 permits a flour sin offering and explicitly promises forgiveness. First Kings 8:46–50 anticipates forgiveness for repentant exiles praying toward their homeland and Temple, without describing a sacrifice performed by them. Leviticus 17:11 establishes a function of sacrificial blood; it does not establish an exclusive condition of every divine pardon. [Leviticus 5](https://www.esv.org/Leviticus+5/), [1 Kings 8](https://www.esv.org/1+Kings+8/)

**Remedy:** Address these passages before posing the question. Distinguish the continuing significance of Temple sacrifice from the claim that forgiveness requires a sacrificed life in every case. Include the existing non-sacrificial account among the possible Jewish answers.

### R-4 — Important — Buddhism: the pressure question falsely separates unskillfulness from interpersonal wrongdoing

**File:** [Buddhism answer](/C:/Personal/worldviews-examined/src/content/answers/buddhism/great-and-terrible.mdx:56)

> “When someone feels they have *wronged* another and need forgiveness, not merely that they acted unskilfully, is that feeling a further confusion to be let go, or does it track something real?”

**Problem:** Buddhist unskillfulness can include actual wrongdoing against another person, confession, amends, and reconciliation. The question makes an ordinary Buddhist answer sound like a concession Christianity must extract. Related language about a missing addressee and forgiveness versus release reinforces this misleading contrast.

**Evidence:** AN 3.4 commends recognizing transgression, making amends, and properly accepting another’s confession. SN 11.24 concerns one monk transgressing against another and faults the refusal to accept his confession. MN 61 requires examination of harm and confession of harmful conduct. [AN 3.4](https://dhammatalks.net/suttacentral/sc2016/sc/en/an3.4.html), [SN 11.24](https://thebuddhaswords.net/sn/sn11.24.html), [MN 61](https://accesstoinsight.org/tipitaka/mn/mn.061.than.html)

**Strongest opposing reply:** “The wrong and the person wronged are conventionally real. Rejecting a creator does not make interpersonal accountability a confusion.”

**Remedy:** Include these practices in the comparison. Frame the specifically Christian addition as an offense against God, rather than implying Buddhism lacks real interpersonal offense or reconciliation.

### R-5 — Important — Islam and Hinduism: mercy is tested against an unargued satisfaction requirement

**Files:** [Islam answer](/C:/Personal/worldviews-examined/src/content/answers/islam/great-and-terrible.mdx:41), [Hinduism answer](/C:/Personal/worldviews-examined/src/content/answers/hinduism/great-and-terrible.mdx:29)

> “When God forgives the repentant, is anything done about the wrong itself, or is it remitted by sovereign mercy alone?”

> “The Christian asks how this mercy upholds justice.”

**Problem:** These comparisons do not adequately explain why justice requires the kind of satisfaction Christianity supplies. The Islamic question additionally excludes a major existing answer about victims’ rights. Calling the issue “redemptive adequacy” can imply a demonstrated deficiency where the argument currently establishes a theological disagreement.

**Evidence:** Bukhari 2449 requires seeking pardon from the person wronged and describes settlement through transfer of deeds at judgment. Muslim 2581 depicts otherwise observant people losing their merits to those they abused or injured. This is plainly an account of addressing the wrong, not simply ignoring it through mercy. [Bukhari 2449](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:2449), [Muslim 2581](https://sunnah.com/muslim:2581)

**Strongest opposing reply:** A Muslim can distinguish God’s own claims from victims’ claims. A theistic Vedāntin can ask why the Lord cannot justly remit his own displeasure. Both can question whether another’s suffering is a necessary or sufficient condition of just pardon.

**Remedy:** Include the Islamic treatment of victims’ rights. Explain the proposed justice criterion and engage the strongest objection to satisfaction. Otherwise retain this as an explicitly Reformed theological difference without implying that the competing remedies have already failed a shared test.

### R-6 — Important — Hinduism: metaphysical ignorance is answered as though it meant ignorance of moral duties

**File:** [Hinduism answer](/C:/Personal/worldviews-examined/src/content/answers/hinduism/great-and-terrible.mdx:27)

> “The Christian reverses the order. Paul describes people who knew God yet ‘suppress the truth’…”

**Problem:** Knowing that an act is wrong does not establish freedom from Advaita’s fundamental ignorance about selfhood. The comparison moves between different kinds of knowledge without explaining the transition. Consequently, knowingly doing wrong is presented as evidence against a diagnosis that accommodates it.

**Evidence:** Śaṅkara’s relevant ignorance concerns identification with a limited body and mind, producing self-concern and attachment. Liberating knowledge concerns that identification. Ethical discipline, dispassion, and control of mind prepare for such knowledge; merely learning a correct proposition is insufficient. [SEP, “Śaṅkara,” §§3.4 and 4.1](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/shankara/)

**Strongest opposing reply:** “A person can know a moral prohibition while still misunderstanding who they are and pursuing the interests of that misconceived self.”

**Remedy:** Distinguish moral knowledge from liberating self-knowledge. Challenge the claimed explanatory priority of misidentification directly. Do not treat the phenomenon of knowingly sinning as a counterexample without showing why it excludes that account.

### R-7 — Important — Judaism: present transformation is reduced to governance, using Calvin outside the relevant comparison

**File:** [Judaism answer](/C:/Personal/worldviews-examined/src/content/answers/judaism/great-and-terrible.mdx:23)

> “The Christian argues that this concedes the diagnosis. Torah, *teshuvah* and God’s present help govern the heart… its cure is God’s remaking of the heart.”

**Problem:** The passage creates a governance-versus-cure contrast without establishing that Judaism denies present inward transformation. Future removal of the inclination does not imply that present repentance merely manages unchanged corruption. Christianity likewise distinguishes present renewal from final freedom from sin.

**Evidence:** Calvin II.11.7 deliberately considers law apart from its promises of mercy. II.11.8 explicitly rejects the inference that no Jews were converted. His abstraction therefore cannot establish the inadequacy of Torah, repentance, **and God’s present help together**. The answer itself also describes complete repentance and restored relationship with God. [Calvin, *Institutes* II.11.7–8](https://ccel.org/ccel/calvin/institutes/institutes.iv.xii.html)

**Strongest opposing reply:** “God can transform penitents now while reserving complete elimination of the inclination for the messianic future.”

**Remedy:** Specify what alleged incapacity the Jewish texts actually concede. Engage present transformation through repentance and divine assistance. Compare present and final remedies at equivalent stages, and preserve Calvin’s qualification.

### R-8 — Important — Islam: universality does not establish the superiority of inherited corruption over the stated developmental explanation

**File:** [Islam answer](/C:/Personal/worldviews-examined/src/content/answers/islam/great-and-terrible.mdx:33)

> “The Christian argues that a fall without exception is better read as a tendency of the nature itself…”

**Problem:** The answer has just supplied al-Ghazali’s explanation: desire develops before intellect, followed by habituation. A universal developmental process can itself produce a universal tendency. Observed universality therefore does not discriminate between that explanation and inherited corruption.

The first pressure question repeats the problem by treating initial lack of preponderance as though it implied equal influence throughout development.

**Evidence:** This follows from the argument’s own stated premises. Nothing presented shows that the developmental mechanism cannot account for the outcome, or that inherited corruption predicts something it does not.

**Strongest opposing reply:** “Original receptivity is not permanent equilibrium. The developmental sequence you quoted explains why passion gains the advantage.”

**Remedy:** Identify evidence or an explanatory burden the developmental account actually fails to meet. Otherwise describe the competing interpretations as underdetermined by universality, and revise the pressure question to test the mechanism rather than overlook it.

### R-9 — Important — Naturalism: a theory’s substantive commitments are treated as absence of grounding

**File:** [Naturalism answer](/C:/Personal/worldviews-examined/src/content/answers/naturalism/great-and-terrible.mdx:55)

> “Railton says his criterion ‘presupposes’ a substantive moral theory, and capacity-based views make dignity a matter of degree.”

**Problem:** The first clause selectively uses Railton’s methodological admission to suggest he supplies no defense. The second asserts a consequence that does not follow without further argument: variable capacities need not entail proportionally variable dignity or rights.

**Evidence:** Railton’s p. 190 says his criterion presupposes **and purports to defend** a substantive moral theory. He distinguishes that theory from his metaethical project. Identifying its commitments does not by itself refute its justification. Likewise, capacity-sensitive accounts can employ thresholds or distinguish capacities from equal consideration; the answer identifies no particular account whose implications it establishes. [Railton, “Moral Realism,” p. 190](https://r.jordan.im/download/philosophy/railton1986.pdf)

**Strongest opposing reply:** “Your image doctrine also has substantive commitments. Show why my defense fails, and specify which account actually makes vulnerable people less worthy.”

**Remedy:** Engage Railton’s proposed defense. Name and assess the capacity-based position being criticized. Narrow the conclusion about inadequate grounding to what those arguments establish.

## Cross-worldview parity

- **Disclosure is uneven.** The competing entries disclose difficult material about divine determination, caste exclusion, graded souls, covenantal particularity, and restrictions on liberation. The Christian entry discusses inherited guilt but omits its own decree, passing over, and hardening doctrines. R-1 is the largest parity problem.
- **Standards of grounding differ.** Christianity is allowed to identify disputed revelatory premises. Competitors are sometimes faulted for retaining substantive premises without an equivalent examination of their defenses, especially naturalism.
- **Remedies receive unequal scrutiny.** The other traditions face questions about whether their remedies reach the root or satisfy justice. Christianity’s indispensable regeneration and satisfaction are presented with less examination of divine selection, the origin of the first evil will, and why substitution answers the justice objection.
- **The five responses are structurally repetitive.** Each grants descriptive insight, then moves to a deeper root or redemptive requirement supplied by Christian revelation. Repetition itself is a polish issue. It becomes substantive when this pattern substitutes for engaging the opponent’s actual answer: developmental psychology in Islam, metaphysical ignorance in Advaita, repentance in Judaism, reconciliation in Buddhism, and obligation in Railton.
- **Coverage is generally substantial within the declared scopes.** I found no sufficiently supported case that merely adding a missing famous thinker would correct a material distortion. The required additions are arguments and practices relevant to the existing comparisons, not a larger roster.
