## Question separation: `revelation` vs. `ultimate-authority`

Every answer asks *why believe that this alleged revelation is genuine in the first place*, and each now says so and
links to `ultimate-authority` for the separate question of what governs once the framework is accepted:
- **Reformed:** self-attestation and the Spirit's witness appear only as strand 1 of four, labelled "the ground of
  faith, not an argument offered to outsiders", with a link; strands 2–4 (coherence, public signs, resurrection) carry
  the answer.
- **Naturalism:** "How naturalism settles disputed claims in general is set out under ultimate authority; the question
  here is whether any claim to revelation deserves belief."
- **Judaism:** "How the Torah then governs interpretation is treated under ultimate authority; the question here is why
  Sinai should be believed at all." `ultimate-authority` had quoted YT 8:1 ("Our eyes saw"); this answer quotes YT 8:2
  instead.
- **Islam:** "Once the revelation is accepted, the Qur'an and Sunna govern... But how is it recognized in the first
  place?"
- **Hindu:** the authorless/authored dispute is summarized and linked; Kumārila's argument (already in
  `ultimate-authority`'s Deep dive) was removed from this Deep dive in the consistency pass.
- **Buddhist:** AN 4.180 and the Kālāma material are linked, not repeated; the new material is SN 12.65 (discovery),
  Mahāyāna Buddha-word and Śāntideva's parity argument, which `ultimate-authority` had deferred to this domain.

## Framing decisions

- **Reformed.** Ps 19 and Rom 1 (general revelation) → Heb 1:1–2 (special revelation in history, centred on Christ;
  Bavinck §10) → four strands kept apart: Scripture's voice, coherence (WCF 1.5), public signs (Acts 2:22; Luke 1:1–4),
  the resurrection (Acts 17:31; 1 Cor 15:17). Strongest objection combines rival claims (Hume 10.24), religious experience
  (SEP RE §3), textual mediation and circularity. The reply is cumulative and states the prior openly. Deep dive: Bavinck
  on revelation as history (p. 379: known "the ordinary way in all history, that is, the testimony", paraphrased);
  Calvin I.8 secondary helps and I.8.13 "foolish to attempt to prove to infidels"; Augustine's three incredibles
  (XXII.5); Bavinck §14 vs Calvin vs Van Til as a difference of emphasis; prophecy treated as an internal argument because
  the dating of Daniel and Isaiah 40–55 was not researched; one standard for Calvin I.8.5 and Sinai.
- **Naturalism.** Not "miracles are impossible": Hume 10.36 (eight days of darkness) and SEP §3.1.1 (impossibility is
  Spinoza's and Voltaire's, "sometimes mistakenly attributed to Hume"). Maxim (10.13), love of wonder (10.17), rival
  religions (10.24), the Bayesian restatement (SEP §2.2.4), religious-experience defeaters (SEP RE §3). The Christian
  response asks the three questions the brief set (appropriate prior; whether evidence can move it; Powell's fact/cause
  distinction, SEP §4.1) and calls Hume 10.38 "a substantive premise, not a neutral finding". Deep dive: Hume's two
  parts, Queen Elizabeth (10.37), prophecy (10.41); four positions (impossibility; Hume; Campbell/Earman/Babbage vs
  Ahmed; methodological, SEP §3.3); Alston/Plantinga.
- **Judaism.** Exod 19–20, Deut 5 → Maimonides YT 8:1–2 (Sinai, not wonders) → Halevi I.8 → I.87 → I.25 → Deut 13 and
  18, YT 9:1, 10:1–2, Saadia III.7. **Correction to the dossier:** *Kuzari* I.8's "great multitudes" criteria are spoken
  by the Khazar king, not the rabbi (Sefaria segment 14); the answer attributes them to the king, with the rabbi's Sinai
  answer (I.87) and the king's concession (I.88). Historical evaluation: Farber (no external evidence for the exodus as
  narrated; numbers; composite text; "is not the exodus story anymore") and Tal vs Gottlieb on the Kuzari principle. The
  Pentateuch's date is explicitly not settled; the Christian point is limited to documentary distance. No "archaeology
  disproves the Exodus".
- **Islam.** 33:40, 53:3–4, 26:192–195, 15:9 → the challenge (2:23–24; 17:88) as inimitability of union of language,
  structure and meaning (not "sounds beautiful"), with 29:48 and 29:50–51 → 4:82, 10:37, 26:196–197 → al-Ghazali's
  converging lines (*Munqidh* pp. 54–56). Caveats kept: language-dependence (Halevi I.6, the king), al-Ghazali's own
  warning about eloquence (p. 55), preservation ≠ inspiration (Birmingham; "dates the parchment, not the writing"; no
  claim about variants either way). Deep dive adds al-Jurjani via SEP and Macdonald's 1903 report of early dissent
  (pp. 149–151).
- **Hindu.** Śruti as eternal word found by seers, re-found each kalpa (BS I.3.29–30) → smṛti (I.3.28) → the schools'
  reasons (authorless; reliable authors; "breathed forth", I.1.3; realization) → avatāra (Gītā 4.6–8 with Śaṅkara and
  Rāmānuja). Historical issue stated exactly: Jamison & Witzel's "bards or bardic families, over a period of several
  hundred years" bears on the Mīmāṃsā argument from "absence of the remembrance of an agent"; preservation shows
  transmission, not authorlessness; dating alone does not refute the eternal-word claim.
- **Buddhist.** No "divine revelation": discovery (SN 12.65; SN 12.20) and testing. Mahāyāna appears in the view:
  later appearance (SEP Abhidharma §1), skilful means (Lotus II pp. 40–41), eternal Buddha (Lotus XV pp. 299, 301–302),
  Śāntideva's parity (BCA 9.42–44, our paraphrase), "no single theory covers every Mahāyāna canon". The response separates
  historical-memory framing (late appearance counts) from the defenders' justification (not refuted by dating). AN 8.8
  derivation vs identity flagged as not examined. The Christian response's parity point (the Spirit's witness faces the
  same test) is stated as a parity, not a gotcha.
