# Buddhist traditions — Salvation, Liberation & Human Destiny

Research packet, 2026-10-05. Codes: G = `guilt`; A = `after-death`; F = `final-end`; S = `self-salvation`. REUSE = locator established in prior dossiers; NEW = passage read in Phase A; NEW SOURCE = work registered here. Canonical-locator alternate access follows `docs/SOURCES.md`; paraphrase only where editions differ.

## 1. Core account

The diagnosis is dukkha sustained by craving and ignorance; wrong action conditions suffering. The Noble Eightfold Path integrates ethical conduct, meditation and wisdom. Training requires effort, a teacher and spiritual companions, rather than an isolated ego saving itself. No-self does not abolish conventional responsibility. Confession, disclosure, restraint and amends address wrongdoing, while karmic effects cannot be identified with a creator's legal sentence. Cultivation can change the result of a trivial bad deed; the salt simile is not a promise that any atrocity is instantly erased.

Rebirth is conditioned continuity, not an enduring soul transferring bodies. A liberated one's fate after death is not responsibly captured by the alternatives exists, does not exist, both or neither. Nirvana is the cessation of greed, hatred and delusion, not a permanent heavenly ego. Mahāyāna's bodhisattva ideal brings others' awakening into the goal. Pure Land texts add reliance on Amitābha's vows; Shinran's Other Power is a material exception to blanket self-salvation language, with settled birth distinguished from emotional enthusiasm and moral license.

## 3. Question map

G: remorse/confession and restraint, cultivation of conditions, Pure Land alternative; distinguish karmic effects from forensic absolution. A: conditioned rebirth versus enduring soul; liberated one's after-death predicates; Pure Land birth for awakening. F: nirvana and Mahāyāna awakening/compassion; avoid everlasting self and annihilation alternatives. S: craving/ignorance → path → effort with teaching/community → cessation; clearly bounded Other Power contrast.

## 6. Remaining gaps

Ready with narrow caveat: neither a universal Buddhist afterlife geography nor one formula for postmortem nirvana is supplied. Tibetan intermediate-state details and complete Mahāyāna Buddhahood metaphysics are outside scope. Pure Land textual exclusions and Shinran's interpretation must be acknowledged, not reduced to a single slogan. No permanent consciousness or soul migrates in the Nikāya account.
