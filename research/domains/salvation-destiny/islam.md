# Classical Islam — Salvation, Liberation & Human Destiny

Research packet, 2026-10-05. Codes: G = `guilt`; A = `after-death`; F = `final-end`; S = `self-salvation`. REUSE = locator established in prior dossiers; NEW = passage read in Phase A; NEW SOURCE = work registered here. Canonical-locator alternate access follows `docs/SOURCES.md`; paraphrase only where editions differ.

## 1. Core account

Humans begin on fitra but are weak, forgetful and vulnerable to desire and self-deception; responsibility is personal. Guidance, faith, tawba, purification and righteous action answer this condition under God's mercy. God turns toward sinners and guides; repentance involves knowledge, remorse and a resolve issuing in changed conduct. Deeds matter but do not independently purchase paradise. Claims of mercy do not make victims' rights irrelevant. Forgiveness is a real divine act, not only the easing of an emotion.

Death leads to barzakh, then bodily resurrection and judgment. Divine knowledge and restoration preserve accountability of the person; individual wrongs are not escaped by dissolution of the body. Paradise and God's good pleasure are final goods, with the vision of God especially significant in Sunni texts. Intercession is by God's permission; punishment is not identical for all, and hadith describe deliverance of some believers from fire. Hope in mercy must not be converted into a guarantee for any named person. This packet represents a Sunni center of gravity, not every philosophical or mystical eschatology.

## 3. Question map

G: tawba/mercy and victims' rights; not a pure balance sheet. A: barzakh → resurrection → judgment, no speculative grave geography. F: paradise and good pleasure/vision, preserve Creator–creature distinction. S: fitra and acquired disorder → guidance and return → faith, repentance, deeds → divinely given mercy → purification and hopeful accountability → paradise. Grace language is comparative, not the Reformed doctrine silently inserted.

## 6. Remaining gaps

Ready with narrow caveat on assurance and judgment: do not claim universalist salvation, a fixed number saved or an infallible personal guarantee. Qur'an 4:48 must be read with repentance texts, not as excluding forgiveness of every former idolater. Classical debates over the duration of hell are not reopened; distinguish reports concerning some believing sinners from the fate of every unbeliever. Ghazali's death volume remains inaccessible but canonical sources suffice.
