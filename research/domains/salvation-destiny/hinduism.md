## 1. Core account

The Vedānta center of gravity diagnoses bondage through ignorance, desire and karma. The enduring self outlives bodily death and ordinarily enters further births; liberation is not another pleasant birth. Karma-yoga, disciplined senses, instruction and contemplation prepare the seeker, but the decisive remedy differs. For Advaita, liberating knowledge removes the superimposition of body/mind and agency upon the nondual Self. For Rāmānuja, a real soul's karma-obscured knowledge is restored in dependence on the personal Lord; devotion and surrender receive divine aid. For Madhva, real souls remain distinct from Viṣṇu and each other; bliss is dependent and graded.

Sin and guilt cannot be made one forensic category across these schools. Śaṅkara's knowledge renders unfructified actions powerless while the karma sustaining the present body runs its course. Rāmānuja describes removal of divine displeasure, while expressly excluding a license for deliberate misconduct. Liberation preserves individuality in the theistic schools; Advaita rejects ultimate plurality. Assurance should be described as the relevant promise/realization, not imported from WCF 18.

Govindacharya's later comparative footnotes are not Rāmānuja's own prose. Existing 18.66 locator supports surrender without attributing every later Śrīvaiṣṇava prapatti scheme to the original author. Do not make Advaita's nondual liberation a form of eternal personal communion.

## 3. Question map

G: distinguish karmic consequence, sin/divine displeasure and liberating knowledge; no antinomian inference. A: rebirth/continuity before liberation; contrast ultimate plurality and nonduality. F: moksha beyond temporary heavens, with three different accounts of individuality. S: discipline and yoga serve knowledge/devotion; contrast self-effort and divine aid within schools rather than a single Hindu mechanism.

## 6. Remaining gaps

Ready with narrow caveat: Vedānta scope is disclosed; no claim to cover every Hindu system, popular afterlife practice or expiation ritual. School debates about prapatti and jīvanmukti are represented only at the level established here. Madhva's hierarchy is sensitive but materially relevant to universal destiny; retain the established III.1.21 distinction without treating it as all Hindu teaching.
