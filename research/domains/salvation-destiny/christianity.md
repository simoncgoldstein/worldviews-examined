## 1. Core account

Scripture diagnoses both culpability and bondage: Romans 3:21–26 and 5:12–19 concern guilt; Ephesians 2:1–10 and Romans 8:7–8 concern corruption and inability. Christ supplies reconciliation through his obedience, death and resurrection; the Spirit applies his benefits. Faith receives Christ, repentance turns to God, justification concerns acceptance, and sanctification concerns actual renewal. Neither renewed conduct nor the act of believing supplies the meritorious ground of acceptance. Calling and regeneration are God's work; the renewed person really believes and acts. Perseverance depends on God's preservation, and assurance can be real yet interrupted. At death believers are with Christ, awaiting resurrection. Final blessedness is embodied communion with God in renewed creation; judgment includes everlasting punishment. This is the confessional Reformed lane, not a consensus of all Christians.

The deliberate check covers sin/guilt, inability, calling, regeneration, faith, repentance, justification, imputation, atonement, sanctification, perseverance, assurance, death, intermediate state, resurrection, judgment, glorification and eternal life. Scripture leads every public account. WCF and the other standards clarify it; Bavinck is interpretive support, not the doctrinal foundation.

## 3. Question map

G: distinguish guilt from guilty feeling, ground from response, forgiveness from restitution. A: separate immediate presence from resurrection; judgment is not optional. F: define communion, holiness and embodied service, rather than mere survival. S: synthesize guilt/corruption → Christ → Word/Spirit/faith → grace with real effort → renewal and preservation → glory. Link focused pages rather than reproducing them. Assurance is not identical to the essence of saving faith (WCF 18.3).
