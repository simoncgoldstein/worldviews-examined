## 1. Core account

Wrongdoing is a breach of God's commands and human relationships; the evil inclination must be governed, not treated as a uniformly evil substance. Torah, repentance (teshuvah), confession, changed conduct and God's mercy belong together. Yom Kippur does not bypass reconciliation with those one injured. Sacrifice and repentance must not be collapsed, and penitence is not a purchase price for God's favor. The covenantal and communal dimensions remain important; this is not merely private self-improvement.

Rabbinic resurrection and accountability have primary support, but the afterlife is not one universally agreed timetable. Maimonides describes the World to Come as incorporeal life and knowledge of God; that differs from identifying final reward with a permanently embodied resurrected state. His messianic era facilitates Torah and wisdom and is distinguishable from ultimate reward. Do not read his rationalist account as exhausting Judaism. Divine help includes Torah as antidote and the promised circumcision of the heart; genuine repentance may restore closeness, without importing WCF assurance categories.

## 3. Question map

G: lead Tanakh then Yoma; repair interpersonal wrongs, divine mercy and confession. A: Daniel/resurrection before Maimonides; no uniform intermediate-state narrative. F: final good beyond geopolitical security, distinguish messianic life from Maimonides' ultimate reward. S: covenantal return rather than Reformed original guilt/monergism; Torah, effort, divine aid and mercy; restored proximity and World to Come.

## 6. Remaining gaps

A and F ready with narrow caveat: the relation between resurrection and ultimate incorporeal/embodied reward is unresolved across interpreters. Preserve the distinction; do not invent a timeline. Gehinnom durations, transmigration and a full Nachmanidean afterlife account are outside the minimum corpus. No assurance formula for every Jewish school is supported. The absence of those details does not prevent the central account with explicit scope.
