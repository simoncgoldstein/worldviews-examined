## How to use

1. Open the lane packet: `christianity.md`, `naturalism.md`, `judaism.md`, `islam.md`, `hinduism.md`,
   `buddhism.md`. Each has seven sections plus a cross-domain note:
   1. core account;
   2. expected-major-source check;
   3. core texts checked ("already have", "core works needing passages", "important missing core source");
   4. question map: main answer, texts, interpreters, internal difference, exact caveat, Christian comparative issue;
   5. reusable passage map;
   6. important internal distinctions;
   7. remaining gaps.

   The Christian packet adds the Reformed Scripture audit (§3a).
2. Status marks:
   - **REUSE**: established in an earlier pass (P3, ME, SD, MH, UR).
   - **NEW**: read in this pass in the registered edition, or in an accessible text with the same canonical locator.
   - **NEW SRC**: a work registered or upgraded to `checked` in this pass.
3. Translation discipline:
   - **Our translation / paraphrase only:**
     - Turretin (Latin 1847);
     - Saadia (Hebrew);
     - Bouyges *Tahāfut* (Arabic, noisy OCR);
     - PV, *Vigrahavyāvartanī*, MMK, Triṃśikā (Sanskrit).
   - **Published translations:** ESV, JPS, Kulp, Koren/Davidson, Friedländer, Touger, Chavel, Hirschfeld, Abdel Haleem,
     Field, Macdonald, Vidyabhusana, Jha, Thibaut, Subba Rau, Müller, Sastri, Bühler, Cowell & Gough, Stcherbatsky,
     Sujato, New Advent (Dods, Pilkington, Shaw, Haddan), Beveridge, davidhume.org (Hume), Gutenberg (Russell).
   - **Automated translation:** Bavinck Monergism. The consequential wording used here was checked against Dutch vol. 1.
   - Recheck any wording before public quotation. Ledger ESV quotations.
   - **SEP and IEP** are maps. Their quotations of primaries (Quine; Ibn Taymiyya's *Manṭiqiyyīn*; PV I.213–217;
     al-Ghazali's *Iḥyāʾ* 31) must be attributed "as quoted in SEP…".

## Method

1. Read:
   - the reuse map, `METHODOLOGY.md`, `SOURCES.md` and the question catalog;
   - the flagship dossier, the four completed domain dossiers and both registries.
2. For each lane, listed what was already checked, which checked works needed new passages, and which major core
   texts were missing. Then searched only for named needs, e.g. "public-domain Van Til *Defense of the Faith* 1955
   rights" → Princeton Theological Seminary scan with HathiTrust public-domain review.
3. Applied **core texts → major interpreters → formal synthesis → specialists**. For the Reformed lane:
   **Scripture → Augustine, Calvin, Turretin, Bavinck (→ Van Til) → Westminster/Belgic**.
4. Read only the needed passages and recorded exact locators. Read every canonical baseline in context:
   - the Kālāma Sutta;
   - MN 95;
   - Deut 17 with Ramban;
   - Q 33:62 and 48:23 in context before al-Ghazali's application.
5. Stopped each lane when:
   - foundational texts and major interpreters were checked;
   - competing schools that change the answer were represented;
   - further reading would add witnesses rather than change the account.

## Authority structure by worldview (for UA and Revelation & History)

| Lane | Final court | What it is *not* | How internal disputes are adjudicated |
|---|---|---|---|
| Reformed | God speaking in Scripture; Spirit as efficient cause of persuasion (WCF 1.4–5, 1.10; Turretin II.6.6) | Not reason as autonomous judge; not the church as ground | Scripture interprets Scripture (WCF 1.9); illumined reason judges consequence (Turretin I.10). No infallible human court |
| Naturalism | Methodological network: experience, logic, IBE, coherence, public revision | Not a canon; not a "first philosophy" | Ongoing inquiry; no final court in principle |
| Judaism | God's revelation at Sinai: Written and Oral Torah | Not a heavenly voice against the majority (BM 59b); not a later prophet's sign (YT 9:1) | Authorized court and majority (Deut 17; Ramban) |
| Islam | Qur'an and authentic Sunna (4:59), with reason establishing prophethood | Not *ilhām* (Nasafi); not "Qur'an only" | *Ijmāʿ*, *qiyās*, school methods; demonstration-*taʾwīl* (al-Ghazali) vs. no-conflict (Ibn Taymiyya) |
| Hindu | Śruti for the supersensible (Gītā 16.24; Manu II.10–11; BS I.1.3 in all three schools) | Not reasoning against the Veda (BS II.1.11) | No school-neutral court: the three Vedānta schools read the same śruti differently (UR). Advaita: realization finally supersedes even śruti as "lower knowledge" |
| Buddhist | The Dhamma, verified in direct knowledge (DN 16; AN 3.65; MN 95) | Not the teacher's status (MN 38); scripture fallible outside the inaccessible (PV I.216 via SEP) | Great references: check against discourses and training (AN 4.180) |

None of the six lanes has a mechanism that certifies its final authority from outside itself:
- the Reformed lane holds this on principle (autopistia);
- naturalism embraces it (no first philosophy);
- Mīmāṃsā asserts it (intrinsic validity);
- Dharmakīrti tempers it with fallibilism.

The comparative question for production is not *whether* a lane's final court is self-certifying, but *what makes its
self-certification credible*. This is recorded here, not argued.

## Naturalist response to induction and logic (summary for production)

- **Induction.** Hume at full strength comes first: E 4.18–19, 4.21; T 1.3.6.11; Russell VI. Then the responses (SEP
  Induction):
  1. rule-circular or externalist justification;
  2. IBE with non-Humean laws;
  3. Bayesian reasoning;
  4. Strawson's dissolution;
  5. Reichenbach's pragmatic vindication;
  6. naturalized selection (Quine);
  7. Hume's own mitigated scepticism.

  Production should say which kind each response is: a benign circle, an added non-empirical premise, or a redefinition
  of justification. It should grant the theist the same scrutiny.
- **Logic.** The options are:
  - realism (Russell 1912);
  - conventionalism, with Quine's 1936 regress against it;
  - Quinean revisability;
  - a posteriori naturalism (Maddy, Azzouni, Sher);
  - primitivism.

  The naturalist cost is placing necessary abstracta in a physicalist ontology (UR). That is a tension, not a
  contradiction.

## Major epistemological distinctions retained

- Practical vs. interpretive vs. epistemic authority vs. metaphysical ground of truth (UA, all lanes).
- Using logic vs. having a theory of why logic binds (LB, all lanes).
- Theological reason to expect regularity vs. a non-circular justification of induction (IN, theistic lanes).
- Archetypal/ectypal (Reformed); four roots of knowledge (Saadia); three sources (Nasafi); pramāṇa counts by school
  (Nyāya); intrinsic validity (Mīmāṃsā, Dharmakīrti); two truths (Madhyamaka).
- Certainty vs. exhaustive comprehension (Reformed, Maimonides).
- Knowledge restored by grace: al-Ghazali's light and the Reformed testimonium. Both are distinguished from a source of
  new propositions (Nasafi denies *ilhām*; Bavinck: testimonium gives no new revelation).

## Internal quality pass (single Opus self-review)

- **Explaining how knowledge is possible, not listing trusted sources?** Yes. Each KP cell states a *mechanism*:
  - Reformed: one Logos, image, analogy;
  - naturalism: selection, reliability, science;
  - Judaism: Saadia's roots with error-correction;
  - Islam: senses, report, reason, *fiṭra*;
  - Hindu: pramāṇa and *svataḥ prāmāṇya*;
  - Buddhist: causal perception and inference, non-deception.

  Each also states its limits.
- **Ultimate authority distinguished from ordinary evidence?** Yes. The table above separates the final court from the
  means of learning in every lane. Turretin's ground/cause/instrument distinction is the Reformed model.
- **Logic treated carefully?** Yes. Every LB cell separates *using* logic from *grounding* it. The Reformed cell
  forbids "logic is God's nature". Naturalism has five options and no strawman nominalism. Buddhism is explicitly not
  "anti-logic". Maimonides is not assimilated to Augustine.
- **Hume at full strength?** Yes: E 4.18–19, 4.21, 4.23; T 1.3.6; Russell VI; plus his "pre-established harmony" and
  his naturalist mood, so Hume is not reduced to scepticism.
- **Naturalist responses fair?** Mostly. Seven induction responses and the full EAAN reply set are represented from
  naturalist-friendly sources. Weakness: Quine is second-hand.
- **Reformed epistemology from Scripture and broad Reformed theology, not Van Til alone?** Yes. Scripture audit
  first; Augustine, Calvin, Turretin and Bavinck carry every cell; Van Til adds the apologetic form in KP/LB/IN, and
