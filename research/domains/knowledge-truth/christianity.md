## 1. Core account

Knowledge is possible because the same God made both the knower and the known. God knows himself and all things
perfectly, by one act and without dependence on creatures (Ps 147:5; Isa 40:13–14; Rom 11:33–36; WCF 2.2). The world
is made "by wisdom" (Prov 3:19; Ps 104:24) and through the Word who is "the true light, which gives light to everyone"
(John 1:1–9). Human beings are made in God's image (Gen 1:26–27), with faculties fitted to the world: "The hearing
ear and the seeing eye, the LORD has made them both" (Prov 20:12; Ps 94:9–10, "He who teaches man knowledge"). So
human knowledge is **real but creaturely**. It is:
- derived from God's knowledge, not original;
- true as far as it goes, but never exhaustive ("now I know in part", 1 Cor 13:12; Deut 29:29; Eccl 8:17).

Theologians name this archetypal and ectypal knowledge (Turretin I.2.6; Bavinck I §6.2–4), and Van Til calls it
*analogical* (Defense, pp. 56–61).

Sin does not abolish the faculties. Image-bearers still know God and much else (Rom 1:19–21; 2:14–15; Calvin
II.2.12–16, REUSE). But they "suppress the truth" (Rom 1:18) and become "futile in their thinking" (Rom 1:21; Eph
4:17–18). The damage is ethical and religious more than formal: unbelievers reason with the same logic but put it to
work against the knowledge of God (Van Til, Defense pp. 296–297; 2 Cor 4:4; 1 Cor 2:14).

God remedies this by revelation, which is twofold:
- General revelation, "the light of nature and the works of creation and providence", leaves people without excuse
  but is not enough for salvation (Ps 19:1–4; Rom 1:20; WCF 1.1; Belgic 2).
- Special revelation is now inscripturated (Heb 1:1–2; 2 Tim 3:16; 2 Pet 1:19–21). It acts like spectacles that
  gather confused impressions of God into a clear view (Calvin I.6.1).

Scripture is received as God's word because God speaks in it. Its marks evidence its divinity, but full persuasion
comes from the Spirit's inward testimony (Calvin I.7.4–5; Turretin II.6.6; WCF 1.4–5). It is therefore the
**ultimate authority**: "the supreme judge… can be no other but the Holy Spirit speaking in the Scripture" (WCF 1.10).
This does not dethrone reason as a faculty; it dethrones reason as an autonomous final court:
- Reason judges whether propositions and inferences cohere (Turretin I.9–10).
- God's word judges what is true about God (Turretin I.9.3; Van Til, Defense p. 298).

Logic is not invented but discovered. Valid consequence "exists eternally in the reason of things, and has its origin
with God" (Augustine, DDC II.32.50). The "rules of good consequence" are implanted by God in the rational creature
(Turretin I.10.1). Bavinck adds that one and the same Logos created both reality outside us and the laws of thought
within us, so that the two correspond (Bavinck I §7, PDF p. 217).

Regularity and induction rest on God's faithful providence, not on a self-standing necessity in nature (Gen 8:22;
Jer 33:25; Ps 119:89–91; WCF 5). This gives a *theological reason to expect* uniformity, which Van Til turns into a
transcendental claim (Defense p. 120). It is not a non-circular proof of induction, and the packet keeps the two
apart (§6).

**Audit verdict.** Scripture establishes the substance of every cell:
- God's perfect knowledge and the creature's dependent, partial knowledge;
- sin's suppression of truth;
- revelation as the final word;
- God's truthfulness;
- a covenantally fixed order.

The theologians carry the argument (Augustine 4/4, Calvin 3/4, Turretin 4/4, Bavinck 4/4, Van Til 4/4). Two claims
are **theological inference, not biblical statement**, and must be presented that way:
1. That logical necessity is *grounded in God* (Augustine, Turretin, Bavinck and Van Til agree in substance but word it
   differently; no verse says it). Do not write "logic is God's nature": no source in this packet says that.
2. That God's providence is the *only* account of uniformity (Van Til's transcendental claim). Scripture establishes
   a promised regularity; the "only" is the apologetic argument.

## 4. Question map

- **KP (anchor).**
  - *Main answer:* we can know because God, who knows all things, made a world ordered by his wisdom and made us in
    his image with fitted faculties. Our knowledge is analogical and finite, true as far as it goes, never exhaustive.
    Sin distorts its use, especially toward God, without destroying it.
  - *Foundational texts:* Prov 3:19; 20:12; Ps 94:9–10; John 1:1–9; Rom 1:18–21; 1 Cor 13:12.
  - *Interpreters:* Bavinck I §7, pp. 204, 217, 220; Turretin I.2.6; Calvin I.1.1, II.2.12–16; Van Til, pp. 56–61;
    Augustine CoG XI.26, *De Trin.* XII.15.24.
  - *Internal difference:* Augustinian illumination (an "incorporeal light") vs. Bavinck's Thomist-realist account
    (sense awakens the intellect, *tabula rasa*, p. 208) vs. Van Til's stress that every fact is pre-interpreted by
    God. All three keep the faculties real. They differ on mechanism, not on dependence.
  - *Exact caveat:* do not imply that unbelievers know nothing. Van Til explicitly rejects that reading ("we mean
    nothing so absurd as that", p. 120), and the confessions attribute real knowledge to the light of nature.
  - *Christian comparative issue:* whether rival accounts can explain why mind and world should *correspond*
    (naturalist reliability; Advaita's pramāṇas operating within *avidyā*; Buddhist momentariness and no-self).
- **UA (anchor).**
  - *Main answer:* God speaking in Scripture is the final court. Scripture is self-authenticating because there is no
    higher authority by which God's word could be certified. Its divinity is evidenced by its marks and sealed by the
    Spirit. Church and reason are ministerial: the church as instrument (Turretin II.6.6), reason as judge of coherence
    and consequence (Turretin I.10).
  - *Texts:* 2 Tim 3:16; 2 Pet 1:21; John 17:17; Isa 8:20; Acts 17:11.
  - *Interpreters:* Calvin I.7.4–5; Turretin II.6.6, II.6.11, I.9–10; Bavinck §12 p. 462, §18 pp. 609–612.
  - *Internal difference:*
    - Calvin and Bavinck put the weight on the testimonium. Bavinck notes that it "began to lose its place of honour"
      with Turretin, who stresses Scripture's marks (*notae*) as the argument (Bavinck §18, p. 612; Turretin II.6.6).
      This is a difference of emphasis inside a shared doctrine.
    - Van Til adds the apologetic claim that every method presupposes God or denies him (pp. 116–118).
  - *Exact caveats:*
    1. Distinguish **ground** (God's authority), **cause** (the Spirit), **instrument** (church, preaching) and
       **criterion of coherence** (reason). Do not write "Reformed Christians reject reason."
    2. Bavinck's line that Scripture is "instrumental… in a certain sense contingent" (§6.3, p. 187; Dutch
       *instrumenteele… toevallige*) concerns its role relative to knowledge of God in the believer's consciousness. It
       is not a denial of its normativity: he defends autopistia at §12. Do not quote the p. 187 line without that
       context.
    3. The circularity charge is real and should be faced. Bavinck answers that every science rests on principia that
       cannot be demonstrated and that Rome's church-proves-Scripture alternative is equally circular (p. 462). Van Til
       says all reasoning about ultimates is circular (p. 118).
  - *Comparative issue:* each lane's final court, and whether it can be judged by a higher one (§§ in the other
    packets). The structural question for UA: does the claimed final authority certify itself, and on what grounds?
- **LB (medium).**
  - *Main answer:* logical laws are discovered, not made. They are true of things, not just of thought. Their necessity
    depends on God, not on human convention or chance, and they bind all minds because the one God made all minds and
    all things.
  - *Texts:* John 1:1–3; Heb 6:18; Titus 1:2.
  - *Interpreters:* Augustine DDC II.32.50; Turretin I.9.5, I.10.1–3; Bavinck §7 pp. 208, 217; Van Til pp. 265,
    296–297.
  - *Internal difference:* Turretin's moderate scholastic view (God-implanted rules of consequence; reason as
    ministerial judge, even in mysteries) vs. Van Til's insistence that the unbeliever's *use* of the law of
    contradiction against revelation is controlled by an assumed autonomy (pp. 100, 297–298). Both deny two logics
    (Van Til p. 296).
  - *Exact caveat:* sources ground logic in God (Augustine: "origin with God"; Turretin: implanted by God; Bavinck: the
    one Logos; Van Til: "foundation in God"). None says "logic *is* God's nature", and none treats logical laws as
    created by an arbitrary divine will. Turretin I.9.9 also distinguishes the *incomprehensible* (Trinity) from the
    *incompossible* (impossible). Use that distinction against the charge that mysteries violate logic.
  - *Comparative issue:* naturalist conventionalism/revisability vs. realism; the Mīmāṃsā/Nyāya/Buddhist debate over
    intrinsic validity; Maimonides' "impossible has a permanent nature" (Guide III.15), a close Jewish parallel.
- **IN (medium).**
  - *Main answer:* we may expect the future to resemble the past because the world is governed by a faithful God who
    has promised a stable order (Gen 8:22; Jer 33:25), working through second causes ordinarily, yet free.
  - *Interpreters:* Augustine DDC II.30.47; Calvin I.16; Turretin VI.1; Bavinck III §35.5; Van Til p. 120.
  - *Internal difference:* classical Reformed theologians never addressed Hume's problem as such. Van Til is the one
    who turns providence into a transcendental answer to "uniformity of nature" (p. 120).
  - *Exact caveat:* Scripture gives a **reason to expect** regularity, and Van Til an **argument** that only theism
    accounts for it. Neither is a non-circular *demonstration* of induction. Scripture itself also allows God to work
    "without, above, and against" means (WCF 5.3). So the Christian answer grounds *general* reliability, not
    exceptionless prediction. Present it as a better ground for the practice, not a proof that escapes Hume.
  - *Comparative issue:* Hume's custom and "pre-established harmony" (E 5.21) is an unexplained correspondence that
    the Christian account explains by design. Al-Ghazali's divine *ʿāda* gives a parallel theistic answer with a
    different (occasionalist) causal theory.

## 6. Important internal distinctions

- **Archetypal vs. ectypal** knowledge: genuinely supported (Turretin I.2.6; Bavinck §6.2–4). Van Til's "analogical"
  is his apologetic restatement (pp. 56–61). Bavinck warns against a merely *quantitative* reading in which our
  knowledge is a smaller slice of God's (p. 188).
- **Natural vs. saving revelation:** WCF 1.1; WLC 2; Belgic 2; Turretin I.2.7.
- **Reason as faculty vs. reason as autonomous authority:**
  - reason as faculty: Turretin I.9–10; WCF 20.2 ("reason also");
  - reason as autonomous authority: Van Til pp. 116–120, 298.
- **Certainty vs. exhaustiveness; knowledge vs. comprehension:** Van Til pp. 57–61; Turretin I.9.9; 1 Cor 13:12.
- **Objective revelation vs. subjective illumination:** *principium cognoscendi externum/internum* (Bavinck §6.3–4);
  WCF 1.5–6.
- **Ground vs. cause vs. instrument of faith in Scripture:** Turretin II.6.6. Emphasis differs: Calvin and Bavinck put
  it on the testimonium; Turretin on the *notae* (Bavinck §18, p. 612).
- **Sin's effects on cognition without total ignorance:**
  - Calvin II.2.12–16 (REUSE);
  - Van Til p. 120 ("nothing so absurd") and p. 57 (sin ethical, not metaphysical);
  - Turretin I.9.5 (principles of natural light survive).
- **Theological reason for regularity vs. proof of induction:** §4 IN.

## 7. Remaining gaps

1. **Bahnsen**: no lawful text. Not material, because Van Til 1955 carries the argument.
2. **Van Til's later writings** (*Survey of Christian Epistemology*, *Christian Theory of Knowledge*): readable copies
   lack rights provenance. *Defense* 1955 is the only checked Van Til text. His p. 297 statement about the law of
   contradiction quotes his *Introduction to Theology* syllabus, so cite it as "quoted in Defense".
3. **Augustine, *De libero arbitrio* II** (number and wisdom as immutable truths) is not on New Advent. It would add
   depth to LB but is not needed.
4. **Bavinck vol. 2** on God's knowability (§§ on incomprehensibility) was not re-mined. Vol. 1 §6 and UR's vol. 2 work
   suffice.

## Cross-domain consistency (Ultimate Reality → Knowledge & Truth)

- **Fits UR.** The Creator–creature distinction (UR §1) is exactly what makes knowledge *analogical*: God's
  knowledge is original, ours derived (Van Til pp. 56–59; Bavinck §6.2). God's simplicity and aseity (UR) ground the
  claim that his knowledge is "independent upon the creature" (WCF 2.2).
- **Fits MH/ME.** Sin as suppression (Rom 1:18) was established in ME and P3. Here it is applied to cognition, with
  the same guardrail: image-bearers still know.
- **Real tension to disclose.** Van Til's claim that reasoning about ultimates is inevitably circular (p. 118) sits
  awkwardly beside Turretin's confidence that reason can independently judge contradiction from natural principles
  (I.9.5, I.10). The tension is real but limited:
  - Turretin limits that judgment to coherence and consequence, and to *illumined* reason (I.10.1);
  - Van Til affirms one formal logic for all (p. 296).

  Present them as two emphases (scholastic and apologetic) inside one doctrine, not as one voice.
