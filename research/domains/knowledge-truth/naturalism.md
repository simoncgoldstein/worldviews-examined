# Naturalistic atheism — Knowledge & Truth

Research packet, 2026-10-07. Codes: KP = `knowledge-possible`; UA = `ultimate-authority`; LB = `logic-binding`;
IN = `induction`. Marks: **REUSE** (P3/ME/MH/UR), **NEW**, **NEW SRC**.

**Lane method.** Naturalism has no canon. The order used is:
- historical primaries: Hume, Russell, Darwin;
- the most influential contemporary programme: Quine's naturalized epistemology, represented through SEP because no
  lawful primary text is readable;
- specialist maps (SEP) for the live options on induction, logic, realism and reliability;
- the evolutionary-reliability debate, through a peer review that sets out both sides.

SEP is used as a map and labelled as such. Quine's own wording is quoted only as SEP quotes it.

## 1. Core account

There is no single naturalist epistemology. There is, however, a recognizable family that shares four commitments:
1. knowledge is a natural relation between organisms and their environment;
2. the faculties that produce it (perception, memory, inference) are products of evolution and learning;
3. there is no "first philosophy" outside science from which science could be certified (Quine, via SEP Quine §4;
   SEP Naturalism in Epistemology §2);
4. the best available method for finding out about the world is the self-correcting, public practice of empirical
   inquiry.

Reliability is explained, not presupposed. Creatures whose expectations were "inveterately wrong… have a pathetic but
praiseworthy tendency to die out" (Quine 1969c, quoted SEP NE §3.2). Scientific success is best explained by our
theories being approximately true (the "no miracles" argument, SEP Scientific Realism §2.1). Error is expected and
corrigible: "no statement is immune to revision" (Quine 1951, quoted SEP Quine §3.3).

Hume supplies the founding problem and the founding modesty.
- *The problem:* all reasoning about matters of fact rests on cause and effect, known only by experience; and
  experience cannot justify the assumption that "the future will be conformable to the past" without "going in a
  circle" (E 4.19).
- *The modesty:* custom, not reason, carries us forward (E 5.5–6). Nature "by an absolute and uncontroulable
  necessity" makes us judge (T 1.4.1.7). Hume even speaks of "a kind of pre-established harmony between the course of
  nature and the succession of our ideas", whose source he leaves unexplained (E 5.21).

Contemporary naturalists answer in several ways (§4 IN): inductive or externalist justification, inference to the best
explanation, Bayesian reasoning, pragmatic vindication, or dissolving the demand.

Logic is the hardest case. Options range across:
- realism about logical facts (Russell 1912: the law of contradiction "is a fact concerning the things in the world");
- conventionalism (Carnap; answered by Quine's 1936 regress);
- Quinean holism, in which logic is central but revisable;
- Maddy's naturalism, on which logic is a posteriori yet deeply embedded in cognition and the world's structure
  (SEP Logical Truth §1.1).

There is no canonical naturalist ground for logical necessity. Several naturalists deny that it needs one.

## 4. Question map

- **KP (anchor).**
  - *Main answer:* knowledge is possible because evolved and learned cognitive systems are, in normal environments,
    reliable enough for survival. Science extends and corrects them through public method, instruments and criticism.
    Its predictive success is best explained by approximate truth.
  - *Texts:* Quine via SEP NE §§2–3; SEP Scientific Realism §2.1; Hume E 5.21–22.
  - *Interpreters:* Quine (replacement naturalism); Kornblith (normativity retained, practical grounding, SEP NE
    §4.2); reliabilism (via SEP Induction §4.1).
  - *Internal difference:*
    - realists (no-miracles) vs. anti-realists and empiricists, who cite the pessimistic induction and
      underdetermination (SEP SR §3);
    - replacement naturalism (Quine) vs. moderate naturalism that keeps normative epistemology (SEP NE §§4–5).
  - *Major objection to represent:* the evolutionary-reliability worry. Darwin's "horrid doubt" whether "the convictions
    of man's mind… are of any value". Plantinga's EAAN: P(R|N&E) is low or inscrutable, so the naturalist has a
    defeater for R.
  - *Naturalist replies (Post, NDPR):*
    - truth-tracking pays: Ramsey, Fodor, Fales;
    - commonsense or Reidian basic beliefs: Bergmann, Sosa;
    - defeater principles: Van Cleve, Talbott, Merricks, Alston;
    - conditionalizing on further information: Otte, O'Connor;
    - Millikan's teleosemantics as the "missing strand" that makes content causally relevant;
    - a meta-inductive "gaps close" argument.

    Plantinga's reply, also reported by Post: content epiphenomenalism remains unexplained.
  - *Exact caveats:*
    1. Darwin's doubt is aimed at his **theistic** conviction that the universe "is not the result of chance", not at
       cognition in general. Do not present it as Darwin conceding general unreliability.
    2. Do not say "naturalism cannot explain reliability." Say that naturalists offer a selectionist and teleosemantic
       account, and that the contested point is whether truth (rather than mere adaptive behaviour) is what selection
       tracks.
  - *Christian comparative issue:* whether reliability for survival suffices for reliability about abstract,
    theoretical and moral truth; and whether "pre-established harmony" (Hume E 5.21) is better explained by design.
- **UA (anchor).**
  - *Main answer:* there is no final court in the revelatory sense. The final appeal is a methodological network:
    - experience (observation and experiment);
    - logic and mathematics;
    - inference to the best explanation;
    - coherence of the whole theory;
    - public repeatability and revision.

    No proposition is beyond revision (Quine via SEP Quine §3.3). There is no extra-scientific standpoint from which
    science is judged (SEP Quine §4; SEP Naturalism §2, REUSE).
  - *Texts:* Hume E 12.25 (confine inquiry to "common life… practice and experience"); Russell *Problems* VI.
  - *Internal difference:*
    - some naturalists privilege science as the measure of what exists (scientism; SEP Naturalism §1);
    - others keep a place for a priori reason (Russell 1912; BonJour-style rationalism in SEP LT §1.1).
  - *Exact caveat:* the absence of a canon is a structural feature, not a refutation (METHODOLOGY). The fair pressure
    concerns self-application. Can the claim "only empirically revisable methods yield knowledge" be established by
    those methods? Quine embraces the circle ("no first philosophy"); Fumerton calls it "pathetic circularity" (SEP NE
    §3.2).
  - *Comparative issue:* every lane's final court involves a self-certifying element. Naturalism's is the method
    itself, the Reformed lane's is God speaking.
- **LB (medium).**
  - *Main answer:* there is no single answer. The live options are:
    1. **realism** (Russell: logical laws are facts about things, known a priori);
    2. **conventionalism/analyticity** (Carnap; Boghossian's tacit-agreement version, where logical truth is true in
       virtue of meaning), which faces Quine's regress: infinitely many logical truths cannot follow from finitely many
       conventions without using logic;
    3. **Quinean holism** (logic central but in principle revisable);
    4. **naturalized a posteriori accounts** (Maddy: logic reflects the basic structure of the world and of our
       cognition; Azzouni; Sher's realism with Quinean epistemology).

    Some deny that logic *needs* metaphysical grounding: its necessity is primitive, or it consists in rules
    constitutive of reasoning.
  - *Texts:* Russell VII–VIII; SEP LT §1.1.
  - *Exact caveat:* do not treat all naturalists as nominalists or conventionalists. Russell (1912) is a realist about
    universals and logical facts, and Sher is a modal realist. The real naturalist cost is where abstract necessary
    truths fit in a physicalist ontology (SEP Physicalism §5.5, REUSE UR). That is a tension, not a contradiction.
  - *Comparative issue:* why should one evolved species' logic be binding everywhere and always? The Augustinian claim
    that validity is "not devised by men" (DDC II.32.50) agrees with Russell's realism, and the dispute is over what
    grounds it.
- **IN (medium).**
  - *Hume at full strength:*
    - no demonstrative argument, since the course of nature may change without contradiction (E 4.18);
    - no probable argument without circularity (E 4.19);
    - repetition adds no insight into "secret powers" (E 4.21);
    - so inductive inference is the work of custom (E 5.5).

    Russell restates it. The inductive principle is unprovable *and* undisprovable by experience; "we must either
    accept the inductive principle on the ground of its intrinsic evidence, or forgo all justification" (*Problems*
    VI).
  - *Naturalist responses (SEP Induction):*
    1. **externalist / rule-circular:** inductive justification of induction is rule-circular, not premise-circular,
       and acceptable if induction is in fact reliable (Van Cleve, Papineau; §4.1);
    2. **nomological-explanatory / IBE:** regularities are best explained by laws or natural necessity (Armstrong,
       BonJour; §3.2). This sits uneasily with Humean laws (SEP Laws §2, REUSE);
    3. **Bayesian:** priors plus conditionalization (§3.3), with the problem shifted to priors;
    4. **ordinary-language dissolution:** being reasonable *is* proportioning belief to inductive evidence (Strawson;
       §5.2);
    5. **pragmatic vindication:** if any method succeeds, induction does (Reichenbach; §5.3);
    6. **naturalized:** evolution selects good inducers (Quine, via SEP NE §3.2);
    7. **living with scepticism:** Hume's own stance (SEP §6; E 12.22–25).
  - *Exact caveat:* do not write "naturalism cannot justify induction." Write that every naturalist response either
    (a) accepts a circle it argues is benign, (b) adds a non-empirical premise (a priori IBE, priors, natural
    necessity), or (c) changes what "justification" means. Say which, and grant that the theist also needs (a) or (b).
  - *Christian comparative issue:* the Christian answer (providence) also does not escape the circle in Hume's sense.
    Its claim is to *explain* why the circle is trustworthy rather than to break it.

## 6. Important internal distinctions

- **Replacement vs. moderate naturalized epistemology:** Quine vs. Kornblith (SEP NE §§2, 4).
- **Scientific realism vs. anti-realism:** SEP SR §§2–3.
- **Logical realism vs. conventionalism vs. revisability vs. a posteriori naturalism:** SEP LT §1.1; Russell VIII.
- **Humean vs. non-Humean laws**, which affects whether IBE can answer Hume (SEP Laws, REUSE; SEP Induction §3.2).
- **Hume's two moods:** the sceptic (E 12.22) and the naturalist who lets custom and nature rule (E 5.6, 5.21; T
  1.4.1.7). Both are Hume; do not present only one.
- **Darwin's doubt (theistic conviction) vs. Plantinga's EAAN (all beliefs).**

## Cross-domain consistency (Ultimate Reality → Knowledge & Truth)

- **Fits UR.** Physicalism with emergence (UR) implies that cognition is a natural process. That is exactly what
  naturalized epistemology studies.
- **Real tension 1.** The UR packet recorded the "abstracta" problem (SEP Physicalism §5.5). Here it resurfaces:
  - Russell's 1912 realism about universals and logical facts does not sit comfortably with strict physicalism;
  - the physicalist-friendly options (conventionalism, revisability) give up the strong necessity Russell defends.

  Record this as a choice naturalists must make, not a refutation.
- **Real tension 2.** Humean laws (UR OR cell) make the nomological-explanatory answer to induction unavailable.
  Non-Humean laws make it available but bring back natural necessity, which some naturalists resist.
