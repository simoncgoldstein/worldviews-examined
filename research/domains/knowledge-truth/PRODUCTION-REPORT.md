## Editorial method

The Ultimate Reality hierarchy was kept: **core truth → natural explanation → important nuance → technical Deep Dive**.
- Theses give the lane's answer in the first clause, without thinker names (school names such as Advaita or Nyāya appear where the answer depends on them).
- Openings explain the mechanism or the final court before any interpreter.
- Named thinkers appear in visible prose for a distinctive formulation (Saadia's roots of knowledge, al-Ghazali's crisis, Kumārila's intrinsic validity, Dharmakīrti's definitions, Quine's naturalization, Hume's circle).
- Technical epistemology is in the Deep dives: archetypal/ectypal knowledge, illumination vs. realism, ground/cause/instrument, the four *uṣūl*, *svataḥ* vs. *parataḥ*, Uddyotakara's three conditions, the seven responses to Hume, the Gillon–Tillemans dispute.

## Question separation: `knowledge-possible` vs. `ultimate-authority`

- **`knowledge-possible`** answers *how a mind comes to know*. Every answer is organized around faculties, perception, inference, testimony, error and correction, and limits:
  - same Maker of knower and known;
  - evolved and corrected faculties;
  - Saadia's roots;
  - senses, report and reason with *fiṭra*;
  - pramāṇas and their validity;
  - tested cognition and the two pramāṇas.

  Scripture appears only as a means of knowledge or as a corrective, and each answer links forward.
- **`ultimate-authority`** answers *what finally adjudicates*. Every answer follows the order: final court → why it needs no higher certification → what reason and community do beneath it → how internal disputes are settled.
  - The self-certification point is made in every lane in the dossier's framing. The Reformed answer is deliberate self-attestation, judged by coherence, self-consistency and adequacy, not "everyone is circular". Naturalism's "no first philosophy", Mīmāṃsā's intrinsic validity and Dharmakīrti's fallibilism are each stated in their own terms.
  - Duplicated Deep-dive material (MN 95's "preserving truth", Nasafi on *ilhām*) was replaced by links to `knowledge-possible`.

## Treatment of logic

- Every lane separates *using* logic from *theorizing* why it binds.
- **Reformed:**
  - Augustine's "origin with God", then Turretin and Bavinck;
  - "None of these sources says that logic simply *is* God's nature";
  - grounding in God is stated three times as a theological inference.
  - The Deep dive separates the doctrine, the epistemology and Van Til's apologetic claim, which "needs its own argument". It notes that a realist like Russell does not hold logic to be a product of chance.
- **Naturalism:** five options. It is "a tension, not a contradiction", and naturalists are "not nominalists by default".
- **Judaism:** no rabbinic doctrine. Saadia's "the absurd is nothing"; Maimonides' "not the result of some agent", explicitly not placed in God's mind.
- **Islam:** immediate necessary knowledge, and the al-Ghazali/Ibn Taymiyya contrast.
- **Hindu:** logic as knowledge-yielding inference, with *vyāpti*, fallacies and *tarka*, and no imposed form/content split.
- **Buddhist:** it opens by affirming a sophisticated logic. Triple criterion; identity and causation; nominalism as the internal pressure point; Madhyamaka's critique "carried out with logic".

## Treatment of induction

- **Naturalism:** Hume at full strength (E 4.18–19, the circle quoted). Three visible families of response; a seven-item Deep-dive map, each labelled as accepting a benign circle, adding a principle, redefining justification or accepting fallibility. The Christian response says the theist "pays a price too" and calls the Humean residue explanatory incompleteness. It never says naturalism has no answer.
- **Reformed:** the thesis itself separates a theological reason to expect regularity from a non-circular proof. The objection quotes Hume's circle against theism, and the reply grants it. Van Til's "only presupposition" is a Deep-dive argument that "would need to defeat every rival".
- **Judaism:** a theological ground only, and "No classical Jewish source addresses it".
- **Islam:**
  - al-Ghazali denies necessary connection, not all causal connection;
  - *sunna*, with "Al-Ghazali applies the language more broadly, to the order of nature" and a Deep-dive reading of 33:62, 48:23 and 35:43 in context;
  - created powers; falsafa.
- **Hindu:** the Cārvāka challenge and the Nyāya method, called "an Indian parallel to Hume's problem, not a translation of it".
- **Buddhist:** Dharmakīrti and the Gillon–Tillemans dispute. "It would be wrong to say Buddhism solved induction, and equally wrong to say it has no account of it."
