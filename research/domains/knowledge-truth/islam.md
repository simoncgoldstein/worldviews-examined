# Classical Islam — Knowledge & Truth

Research packet, 2026-10-07. Codes: KP, UA, LB, IN. Marks: **REUSE**, **NEW**, **NEW SRC**.

Editions and caveats:

- **Al-Ghazali, *Munqidh*:** Field 1909. Field abridges, so paraphrase where wording matters.
- **Al-Ghazali, *Tahāfut*:** Bouyges 1930 Arabic, with noisy OCR. Paraphrase only, and use SEP al-Ghazali as the
  readable control.
- **Al-Nasafi's creed:** in Macdonald 1903, translated from Cureton's 1843 edition.
- **Ibn Taymiyya:** via SEP (Hoover 2024), which quotes *al-Radd ʿalā al-manṭiqiyyīn* with page references.

**Lane method.** Qur'an and sound hadith → the theological schools (Ash'ari, Maturidi), al-Ghazali, Ibn Taymiyya →
falsafa (Ibn Sina, Ibn Rushd) only as the counter-position. Falsafa must not stand for Sunni Islam.

## 1. Core account

Humans come from the womb "knowing nothing". God gives "hearing and sight and minds" (16:78) and "taught Adam all the
names" (2:31). Knowledge is a trust: "Do not follow blindly what you do not know to be true: ears, eyes, and
heart, you will be questioned about all these" (17:36). People are born with a God-oriented *fiṭra* (30:30; REUSE
Bukhari 1385) and have already confessed "Am I not your Lord?" (7:172). Creation is full of "signs for those who use
their minds" (2:164; 3:190–191; 41:53). The Qur'an argues, too:
- Abraham reasons from setting stars to the one Creator (6:75–79);
- "If there had been… any gods but Him, both heavens and earth would be in ruins" (21:22);
- opponents are told to "Produce your evidence" (2:111);
- "assumptions can be of no value at all against the Truth" (10:36).

The classical Sunni creed systematizes this. Al-Nasafi, the standard Maturidi school text, begins: "the real natures
of things are validly established and… the science of them is certain", against the Sophists. "The sources of
knowledge for mankind are three: the sound Senses, true Narration (*khabar*), and Reason (*ʿaql*)" (Macdonald 1903,
pp. 308–309). Narration includes:
- *mutawātir* reports, so widely transmitted that collusion in a lie is unimaginable, which give **necessary**
  knowledge;
- "Narration by the Apostle aided by miracle", which gives **deduced** knowledge as firm as necessary knowledge.

Reason gives intuitive knowledge ("everything is greater than its parts") and inferential knowledge (fire from
smoke). Private inspiration (*ilhām*) is **not** a source of knowledge (p. 309). Ibn Taymiyya's list is the same set
(sense, report, reason) with *fiṭra* framing all three (SEP Ibn Taymiyya §3.1).

Al-Ghazali's *Munqidh* stages the classic crisis.
- Certitude must be such that a miracle-worker claiming three is more than ten would not shake it (Field pp. 14–15).
- Testing his knowledge, he finds the senses corrected by reason. Reason itself might be corrected by a higher judge,
  as waking corrects dreams (pp. 15–18). For two months he was "morally and essentially a thoroughgoing sceptic".
- He was healed "not to a concatenation of proofs and arguments, but to the light which God caused to penetrate into
  my heart". The "primary assumptions of reason recovered… all their stringency and force" (pp. 18–19).
- Beyond reason there is a further faculty, prophecy, which perceives what reason cannot (pp. 51–52).
- Logic itself "contains nothing for or against religion" and is used by theologians and philosophers alike. Its
  danger lies only in lending false prestige to philosophers' metaphysics (pp. 30–32).

Final authority belongs to God's word (Qur'an) and the Prophet's Sunna. "Refer it to God and the Messenger" when in
dispute (4:59). Reason establishes that the Qur'an is from God (4:82: no inconsistency) and that the messenger is
true. Al-Shafi'i's jurisprudence then defines four sources of law: Qur'an, prophetic usage, analogy (*qiyās*) and
agreement (*ijmāʿ*), with agreement coming to "put its stamp on every rule" (Macdonald pp. 106–107).

The schools differ on how much reason does unaided (§6). Al-Ghazali sets a "universal rule": a valid demonstration is
never wrong, so a demonstrated truth that contradicts the outward sense of revelation requires figurative reading
(*taʾwīl*). Without demonstration, the outward sense stands (SEP al-Ghazali §4). Ibn Taymiyya denies there is any real
conflict between sound reason and authentic revelation (*Darʾ taʿāruḍ*; SEP §4.2).

## 4. Question map

- **KP (anchor).**
  - *Main answer:* God created humans with senses, reason and a God-oriented *fiṭra*, in a world full of signs. "The
    real natures of things are validly established" and can be known with certainty (Nasafi).
  - *Texts:* 16:78; 17:36; 30:30; 2:164; 41:53.
  - *Interpreters:* Nasafi; Ibn Taymiyya (SEP §3.1); al-Ghazali, *Munqidh* pp. 14–19.
  - *Internal difference:* al-Ghazali's certitude is restored by divine light, not by argument. Ibn Taymiyya appeals to
    the collective *fiṭra* and *tawātur* to secure axioms against corrupt kalām reasoning. Maturidi confidence in
    reason (Harvey) is stronger than Ash'ari caution.
  - *Exact caveat:* al-Ghazali's light is not *ilhām* as a source of propositions. It restores trust in the primary
    assumptions, which "have not got to be sought for, since they are always present" (p. 19). Nasafi denies that
    *ilhām* is a cause of knowledge (p. 309). Keep these apart.
  - *Christian comparative issue:* very close to the Reformed account (created faculties, innate knowledge of God,
    signs, suppression: cf. 27:14 in ME). The comparative question moves to UA: which revelation, and on what grounds.
- **UA (anchor).**
  - *Main answer:* God's speech in the Qur'an, with the authentic Sunna, is final ("refer it to God and the
    Messenger", 4:59). Reason establishes the prophethood (Nasafi: the Apostle "aided by miracle"; 4:82). Agreement and
    analogy extend the law.
  - *Interpreters:*
    - al-Ghazali's demonstration-*taʾwīl* rule (SEP §4);
    - Ibn Taymiyya's no-conflict thesis (SEP §4.2);
    - Razi's rule giving reason priority in apparent conflict, which Ibn Taymiyya opposes (SEP §4.2).
  - *Internal differences:* do not reduce Sunni epistemology to "Qur'an only". Distinguish:
    1. **Qur'an and Sunna** (revelatory authority);
    2. ***ijmāʿ*** (communal-interpretive authority, which comes to stamp every rule: Macdonald p. 107);
    3. ***ʿaql*** (establishes revelation and judges contradiction);
    4. **school methods** (Ash'ari, Maturidi, Hanbali).
  - *Exact caveat:* the four *uṣūl* come from a 1903 secondary source. Cite them as al-Shafi'i's scheme "as summarized
    by Macdonald" and keep the claim general. Do not attribute wording to the *Risāla*.
  - *Christian comparative issue:* both lanes hold a self-attesting scripture tested by coherence (4:82; WCF 1.5). The
    historical question of which claimed revelation is genuine belongs to Revelation & History.
- **LB (medium).**
  - *Main answer:* the laws of thought are among the primary, necessary items of knowledge:
    - Nasafi: "everything is greater than its parts" known by intuition;
    - al-Juwayni: every sound mind grasps the necessary, possible and impossible by an immediate impulse of reason
      (SEP al-Ghazali §7.3);
    - Ibn Taymiyya: non-contradiction and excluded middle are immediate, necessary rational knowledge, created in the
      *fiṭra* (SEP §3.1).

    Al-Ghazali accepts Aristotelian logic as religiously neutral and useful (*Munqidh* pp. 30–31). Ibn Taymiyya
    accepts the syllogism's formal validity but rejects its claimed epistemic privilege and the Avicennan theory of
    definition (SEP §3.2).
  - *Exact caveat:* Islamic thinkers do not speak with one voice about Aristotelian logic. Present al-Ghazali
    (acceptance) and Ibn Taymiyya (critique) as the two poles. Neither makes logic contingent on divine will: the
    Ash'ari modal theory places possibility and impossibility in rational judgment (SEP §7.3).
  - *Christian comparative issue:* is the necessity of logic simply given (*badīha*), or does it have a ground? Ibn
    Taymiyya's placing of axioms in the God-created *fiṭra* is the nearest analogue to the Reformed view.
- **IN (medium).**
  - *Main answer (Ash'ari, al-Ghazali):* there is no necessary connection between what is "habitually believed to be a
    cause" and its effect. God creates them "side by side" by his prior decision (*Tahāfut* 17, via SEP §7.2; REUSE
    Bouyges). Regularity is God's *ʿāda*/*sunna*. Our confidence that it will continue rests on God's informing us that
    he will not change it ("You will not find any change in God's practice", 33:62, 48:23, cited in *Iḥyāʾ* 31; SEP
    §7.4).
  - *Internal difference:*
    1. Maturidi and Ibn Taymiyya affirm real created natures and causal powers (UR; SEP Ibn Taymiyya §4.5).
    2. Ibn Taymiyya's empiricism: universal claims about the world rest on induction from particulars. Complete
       induction is hard, so such knowledge is usually only probable (SEP §3.2).
    3. Falsafa holds necessary causation (Ibn Sina; Ibn Rushd calls denying causes "sophistry", Bouyges p. 519).
  - *Exact caveats:*
    1. In 33:62, 48:23 and 35:43, "God's practice" refers in context to his dealings with earlier peoples. Al-Ghazali
       extends it to natural order. Say "as al-Ghazali applies these verses".
    2. Al-Ghazali does not deny causal connection outright; he denies its *necessity*, and allows secondary causes
       (SEP §7.2, 7.4).
  - *Christian comparative issue:* al-Ghazali's answer is structurally closest to the Reformed one (divine faithfulness
    secures regularity). The difference is occasionalism vs. WCF 5's real second causes.

## 6. Important internal distinctions

- **Ash'ari vs. Maturidi vs. Hanbali (Ibn Taymiyya)** on what unaided reason knows (KP, UA).
- **Al-Ghazali's acceptance of logic vs. Ibn Taymiyya's critique** (LB).
- **Occasionalism (Ash'ari, al-Ghazali's First Approach) vs. created natures (Maturidi, Ibn Taymiyya) vs. necessary
  causation (falsafa)** (IN; matches UR).
- **Qur'an/Sunna vs. *ijmāʿ* vs. *ʿaql* vs. school method** (UA).
- **Demonstration-governed *taʾwīl* (al-Ghazali, Ibn Rushd) vs. no-conflict thesis (Ibn Taymiyya)** (UA).

## Cross-domain consistency

- **Fits UR.** The UR packet established occasionalism vs. natures vs. falsafa necessitation. The induction answers
  follow from those causal theories exactly.
- **Real tension 1.** The Nasafi creed, a Maturidi text, affirms that "the real natures of things are validly
  established" (p. 308) while also teaching kalām atomism (substances and accidents, p. 309). Whether the creed's
  "natures" are compatible with the occasionalism many Ash'aris draw from atomism is a live intra-Sunni question,
  not a settled harmony.
- **Real tension 2.** Al-Ghazali's certainty in reason's primary assumptions is restored by divine light (KP). His
  confidence in regularity is restored by revelation (IN). Both are grounded theologically, not by reason alone.
  That is consistent, but it makes al-Ghazali less of a "rationalist" on these questions than his *taʾwīl* rule
  suggests.
