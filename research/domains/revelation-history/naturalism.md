# Naturalistic atheism: Revelation & History

Research packet, 2026-10-07. Codes RV, HI, JE. Marks: **REUSE**, **NEW**, **NEW SRC**.

Hume is quoted from davidhume.org (paragraph anchors). Historical-Jesus material is in `jesus-evidence.md`.

**Lane method.** Naturalism has no canon or revelation. Organize by problem:
- the philosophy of miracle testimony (Hume; the SEP map);
- religious experience as evidence (SEP);
- historical method (Ehrman, Martin, Lowder);
- progress and cosmic history (REUSE: Railton, Darwin, SEP; Carroll from UR).

## 1. Core account

**Revelation.**
- Naturalism accepts no supernatural revelation. Claims of revelation are treated as **human testimony and historical
  claims**, assessed like any other.
- The strongest representative stance is **not** "miracles are impossible":
  - that is Spinoza's or Voltaire's argument, and "sometimes mistakenly attributed to Hume" (SEP Miracles §3.1.1);
  - Ehrman: "I won't say they're impossible" (debate 2006).
- The stance is that miracle and revelation claims carry an extremely low prior probability against uniform experience,
  so testimony must be of a kind "that its falsehood would be more miraculous" than the event (Hume, E 10.13).
- Hume's four "particular" arguments then lower the credibility of actual religious testimony (E 10.15–24):
  - no miracle attested by sufficiently many educated, honest, reputable witnesses in a public place;
  - the human love of wonder, and "if the spirit of religion join itself to the love of wonder, there is an end of
    common sense" (10.16–17);
  - such reports abound "among ignorant and barbarous nations" (10.20);
  - rival religions' miracles cancel each other: "whatever is different is contrary" (10.24).
- Hume himself concedes that testimony *could* establish a violation of nature (eight days of universal darkness,
  10.36). His claim is that it can never establish one "so as to be the foundation of a system of religion" (10.36,
  10.38). The Queen Elizabeth example (10.37) states the case against resurrection testimony.
- **Religious experience** as a source of revelation faces two main defeaters (SEP Religious Experience §3):
  - religious diversity, which works "the same way that wildly conflicting eyewitness reports undermine each other";
  - naturalistic explanations (Freud; Marx; cognitive science, not read this pass).

**What could rationally move a naturalist?**
- Hume (10.36): a public, uniform, widely and independently attested anomaly.
- SEP Miracles §§2.2.4, 4.2 (Bayesian): evidence whose probability on naturalism is lower than the prior of the miracle.
  Since "background beliefs loom large", a naturalist who grants that theism is not certainly false must assess the
  specific evidence rather than dismiss it by definition.
- Ehrman marks the limit as historical method itself: historians "can only establish what probably happened". A
  naturalist could therefore grant every historical fact and still decline the inference *as a historian*. Whether to
  accept it *as a person* is a metaphysical judgement.

Production should state this fairly: the serious naturalist is open in principle and sceptical in practice. The verdict
rests on priors and on how the specific evidence is weighed, not on a definitional ban.

**History.**
- Nature supplies no guaranteed moral or redemptive goal.
- There are physical arrows of time (thermodynamics; UR, Carroll REUSE), but no purpose.
- Biological evolution is directional only in a weak, contingent sense (Darwin, REUSE).
- Real progress exists but is contingent and relative to standards:
  - Darwin: the widening of sympathy (*Descent* ch. IV, REUSE);
  - Railton: moral progress as an "uneven secular trend" explicable by social rationality (REUSE).
- History has no final consummation. The cosmological future (heat death, UR REUSE) is an end, not a fulfilment.
- **Do not**:
  - reduce naturalism to nihilism: meaning and progress are made, not given (`why-alive` answers, REUSE);
  - make Marxism the naturalist default: it is one historicist naturalism, not representative.

**Jesus.**
- Serious critical naturalism (and critical non-confessional scholarship) holds:
  - Jesus existed (Martin L13; Ehrman);
  - a Jewish apocalyptic prophet (Martin L13);
  - crucified under Pilate;
  - his followers had experiences they took to be appearances, and proclaimed his resurrection early;
  - the resurrection itself is not a historical conclusion;
  - the best natural explanation combines grief or visionary experiences, scriptural interpretation (vindication), and
    legendary growth (Ehrman 2006 closing; JE Kohler 1906, a Jewish parallel);
  - empty-tomb scepticism varies: Lowder grants slightly better than even odds of an empty tomb and offers reburial;
    Ehrman disputes it.
- Mythicism is **not** representative (Martin L13: "reputable historical scholars all admit that Jesus of Nazareth
  existed").

## 4. Question map

- **RV (anchor).**
  - *Main answer:* there is no good evidence that any revelation has occurred. Revelation claims are testimony to be
    weighed against uniform experience and natural explanations; rival revelations cancel one another (E 10.24).
  - *Texts / interpreters:* Hume E 10.4, 10.12–13, 10.15–24, 10.36–38; SEP Miracles §3; SEP RE §3.
  - *Internal difference:*
    1. impossibility (Spinoza, Voltaire: rare among naturalists);
    2. Humean in-principle credibility (a strong prior against);
    3. Bayesian case-by-case (Earman, via SEP: Hume's maxim trivial or misapplied; the evidence decides);
    4. Ehrman's methodological limit for historians.
  - *Exact caveat:*
    - never write "naturalism assumes miracles are impossible";
    - Hume concedes possible proof of an anomaly (10.36);
    - SEP reports serious criticism of Hume (Earman; Campbell).
  - *Comparative issue:* the Christian lane can accept Hume's demand for public, multiple, early witnesses and argue that
    1 Cor 15 approximates it. The naturalist replies with Hume's religious discount (10.38) and the bereavement analogy.
    The real dispute is the prior.
- **HI (medium).**
  - *Main answer:* history has no built-in direction or goal. There is contingent progress, judged by human standards,
    and physical time's arrow without purpose.
  - *Texts:* Darwin *Descent* IV (REUSE); Railton (REUSE); Carroll (UR, REUSE).
  - *Internal difference:* optimistic progressivists (humanist manifestos, REUSE AHA 2003) vs. tragic or contingent
    views (Russell's "Free Man's Worship", REUSE).
  - *Exact caveat:* progress claims require a standard. On naturalism that standard is human valuing (Railton) or
    evolved sympathy (Darwin), not a cosmic telos. Present it as a real, chosen aim, not as nihilism.
  - *Comparative issue:* can "progress" be objective without a goal? This is a pressure question, not a refutation. It
    links to `know-the-good` (REUSE).
- **JE (anchor).**
  - *Main answer:* Jesus was a real first-century Jewish apocalyptic prophet, executed by Rome. His followers' resurrection
    belief arose from visionary experiences and scriptural interpretation and grew in legend. No miracle is required, and
    none can be established historically.
  - *Interpreters:* Ehrman (2006); Martin (L13); Lowder; Hume (philosophy, not history: "Hume is not a historian of
    Jesus").
  - *Internal difference:*
    - Ehrman denies that historians can affirm miracles;
    - Lowder allows an empty tomb and argues explanation by reburial;
    - mythicists (not representative) deny existence.
  - *Exact caveat:*
    - the strongest critic grants claims 1–8 of `jesus-evidence.md` §B (Lowder grants the burial and nearly the tomb;
      Ehrman grants the visions);
    - do not present the critical position as denying the crucifixion or the early proclamation.
  - *Comparative issue:* whether naturalistic combinations explain group appearances, Paul's and James' turn, and the
    rapid rise of worship of Jesus by monotheistic Jews (Hurtado's point) better than the resurrection does, given the
    priors.

## 6. Historical-accountability notes

- Hume's argument is philosophical. It sets the prior and the discount. The *historical* case is in
  `jesus-evidence.md`.
- Ehrman's "historian's problem" is a claim about method, which SEP Miracles §3.3 places on one side of "a deep
  conceptual fault line" in biblical studies. Present it as a contested methodological stance, not a neutral rule.
- Naturalist explanations should be judged by the same standards: scope, power, ad hocness (Lowder's own criteria, §2).

## 7. Important internal distinctions

- Metaphysical naturalism (no supernatural) vs. methodological naturalism (historians bracket the supernatural) vs. a
  low prior probability vs. outright impossibility.
- Fact vs. explanation: Powell's distinction (SEP §4.1). Testimony bears on facts; the cause is a matter of judgement.
- Religious experience as evidence (the Alston/Plantinga parity) vs. as an explanandum (naturalistic explanation).
- Progress relative to human aims (real) vs. cosmic teleology (denied).

## Cross-domain consistency

- **With K&T.** Hume's induction (E IV) and his miracles argument (E X) share one empiricism: uniform experience grounds
  both expectation and the "proof" against miracles. The tension Christian critics press: if induction has no rational
  ground (E 4.18–21, REUSE), can uniform experience deliver a "full proof" against miracles? Record this as a *pressure
  question*, not a contradiction: Hume's mitigated scepticism (E 12, REUSE) accepts custom as our guide.
- **With UR.** Physicalism and causal closure (UR) set the low prior. Consistent.
