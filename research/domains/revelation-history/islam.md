## 1. Core account

**Revelation.**
- God speaks through messengers sent to every community (10:47; 16:36; 4:163–165, "so that mankind would have no excuse").
  The chain culminates in Muhammad, "the seal of the prophets" (33:40).
- The Qur'an is sent down, not composed:
  - "he does not speak from his own desire. The Quran is nothing less than a revelation" (53:3–4);
  - brought down by "the Trustworthy Spirit... to your heart... in a clear Arabic tongue" (26:193–195);
  - beginning "Read! In the name of your Lord" (96:1–5).
- Muhammad is unlettered, which removes the suspicion of borrowing: "You never recited any Scripture before... never wrote
  one down with your hand" (29:48). Compare 16:103 against the charge that a foreigner taught him.
- Preservation is promised: "We Ourself will guard it" (15:9); "falsehood cannot touch [it] from any angle" (41:42).

**How would we know?**
1. **Inimitability (*iʿjāz*)**, the Qur'an's own challenge: "produce a single sura like it... If you cannot do this- and
   you never will" (2:23–24; cf. 10:38; 11:13; 17:88; 52:33–34).
2. **Internal consistency:** "If it had been from anyone other than God, they would have found much inconsistency in it"
   (4:82).
3. **Prophecy and prior scriptures:** "foretold in the scriptures of earlier religions... the learned men of the Children
   of Israel have recognized it" (26:196–197); "the unlettered prophet they find described in the Torah... and in the
   Gospel" (7:157); Jesus' announcement of "Ahmad" (61:6).
4. **Signs and miracles** are in God's hands: "Do they not think it is enough that We have sent down to you the Scripture
   that is recited to them?" (29:50–51).

**Major interpreters on authentication:**
- **Al-Ghazali (*Munqidh*, Field pp. 53–56).** Certainty about a particular prophet comes from:
  - knowing the facts "by ocular evidence or by reliable tradition";
  - studying the Qur'an and traditions;
  - verifying his teaching "in experience thousands of times".

  "It is not a question of finding out whether a rod has been changed into a serpent, or whether the moon has been split
  in two." Miracles "in isolation" may be confused with magic. "If... eloquence of style is a proof of inspiration, it is
  possible that an eloquent style composed with this object may inspire us with a false belief." Conviction forms from
  converging lines, like *tawātur*. Even al-Ghazali qualifies *iʿjāz*-as-eloquence as a standalone proof.
- **Nasafi creed** (Macdonald 1903, REUSE K&T): knowledge from the senses, true report and reason; prophethood confirmed
  by miracle; *ilhām* (inspiration) is not a source of knowledge.
- ***Iʿjāz* theory.** Al-Jurjani grounded rhetorical excellence in *naẓm*, the arrangement of meanings, "spurred by debate
  about how to judge the inimitability of the Koran" (SEP Arabic and Islamic Philosophy of Language and Logic §2.2,
  NEW SRC `sep-arabic-islamic-language`, used for this one point).
  - Macdonald (1903) records early challenges to the doctrine: Ibn al-Muqaffaʿ's reputed imitation; Bashshar's mockery;
    the Muʿtazili reluctance about a miraculous text.
  - Al-Baqillani's *Iʿjāz al-Qurʾān* and the Muʿtazili *ṣarfa* doctrine (God *prevented* rivals) were **not read**: gap.
- **Halevi's Muslim interlocutor** (*Kuzari* I.5) states the classical claim exactly: the Book is "a miracle which we are
  bound to accept for its own sake, since no one is able to bring anything similar to it".

**History.**
- Linear sacred history: creation and vicegerency (2:30, REUSE) → a succession of messengers to every community (10:47;
  16:36; 4:163) → Muhammad, the seal (33:40) → the Hour, resurrection and judgement:
  - "We shall roll up the skies as a writer rolls up [his] scrolls" (21:104);
  - the Trumpet and judgement (39:68–69);
  - "an atom's-weight of good" (99:6–8);
  - cosmic dissolution (81:1–6);
  - "Everything will perish except His Face" (28:88; 55:26–27).
- Within that line, recurring patterns of rise and fall teach moral lessons:
  - "God's ways have operated before your time: travel through the land, and see what was the end of those who
    disbelieved" (3:137; 30:9; 6:6);
  - "God does not change the condition of a people... unless they change what is in themselves" (13:11);
  - "a time set for every people" (7:34).
- **Ibn Khaldun:** dynasties ordinarily last about three generations (120 years); "empires, like individuals, have an
  existence, a life of their own", and their hour "no one can advance or delay" (de Slane pp. 349–350). The last phrase
  echoes 7:34.
- **Interpretive rule:** Ibn Khaldun's dynastic cycles occur *inside* a linear sacred history ending in judgement. Do not
  conclude that "Islam has cyclical history".

**Jesus.**
- ʿĪsā ibn Maryam is:
  - a Word from God and "a spirit from Him" (4:171);
  - "the Messiah" (3:45);
  - born of a virgin (3:47; 19:16–29);
  - like Adam, created by "Be" (3:59);
  - he spoke in the cradle (19:29–33);
  - he worked miracles "with God's permission": the clay bird, healing, raising the dead (3:49; 5:110);
  - he confirmed the Torah and announced Ahmad (61:6);
  - he is a messenger and servant, not divine: "Those who say, 'God is the Messiah, son of Mary,' have defied God"
    (5:72); not "the third of three" (5:73); "nothing more than a messenger" (4:171; 5:75); denial of sonship (19:35).
- On the Day he disowns those who worshipped him and his mother (5:116–117).
- **Crucifixion** (4:157–158): "They did not kill him, nor did they crucify him, though it was made to appear like that
  to them [*wa-lākin shubbiha lahum*]... they certainly did not kill him [*wa-mā qatalūhu yaqīnan*]. God raised him up to
  Himself."
  - The Qur'an names no substitute.
  - 3:55 "*innī mutawaffīka wa-rāfiʿuka ilayya*" (Haleem: "I will take you back and raise you up to Me").
  - 5:117 "*fa-lammā tawaffaytanī*" (Haleem: "Ever since You took my soul").
  - 19:33 peace "the day I die and the day I am raised to life again".
  - These are the lexical basis for minority readings (§4).
- **Return:**
  - Bukhari 3448–3449: the son of Mary "will soon descend amongst you", judge justly, "break the Cross and kill the
    pigs", abolish the jizya;
  - 4:159 (every one of the People of the Book will believe in him "before his death") is linked to this by Abu Hurayra
    in the hadith;
  - 43:61 "*wa-innahu la-ʿilmun li-l-sāʿa*" (Haleem: "This [Quran] gives knowledge of the Hour"; other readings: Jesus
    is a sign of the Hour). Flag it.

## 4. Question map

- **RV (anchor).**
  - *Main answer:* God has revealed his guidance through messengers to every people, finally and completely in the
    Qur'an sent down to Muhammad.
  - *How do we know?*
    - the Qur'an's inimitability (the challenge never met);
    - its internal consistency;
    - its confirmation of, and by, earlier scriptures;
    - its transforming truth verified in experience (al-Ghazali);
    - the miracles God gave the Prophet;
    - *tawātur* (mass transmission) of these facts.
  - *Texts:* 2:23–24; 4:82; 10:37–38; 17:88; 26:192–197; 29:48–51; 53:2–11.
  - *Interpreters:* al-Ghazali *Munqidh* pp. 53–56; Nasafi (REUSE); Halevi's Muslim doctor (*Kuzari* I.5).
  - *Internal difference:*
    - *iʿjāz* as literary excellence (Jurjani, *naẓm*);
    - *iʿjāz* as *ṣarfa* (God prevents imitation; Muʿtazili), not read;
    - al-Ghazali's converging-evidence model, which subordinates any single miracle;
    - Ashʿari vs Maturidi on what reason establishes (K&T REUSE).
  - *Exact caveat:*
    - textual **preservation is not inspiration**. The Birmingham leaves (parchment 568–645) support an early, stable
      text; they say nothing about divine origin;
    - literary **excellence is not historical proof** of divine origin. Its force depends on judging human
      impossibility, and that judgement is inaccessible to non-Arabic readers (Halevi I.6) and contested (Macdonald).
  - *Christian comparative issue:*
    - The Christian authenticating event is public and historical (the resurrection). The Islamic one is a text whose
      excellence is perceived.
    - Both lanes appeal to self-authentication plus signs; compare Calvin I.7–8 with 29:51 ("is it not enough...").
    - The Qur'an confirms the Torah and Gospel (10:37; 61:6) while contradicting the Gospel narratives at the cross.
      That is the "revelatory conflict" category (METHODOLOGY).
- **HI (medium).**
  - *Main answer:* linear sacred history from creation through the prophets to Muhammad and the coming Hour of
    resurrection and judgement. Communities rise and fall by moral law. Everything perishes but God.
  - *Texts:* 3:137; 13:11; 7:34; 21:104; 39:68–69; 99:6–8.
  - *Interpreters:* Ibn Khaldun (dynastic cycle).
  - *Internal difference:* eschatological detail (the Mahdi, Jesus' return: hadith-based) vs. the Qur'anic minimum.
  - *Exact caveat:*
    - recurring rise and fall ≠ cyclical cosmos;
    - Ibn Khaldun's cycles are political, within linear sacred history.
  - *Christian comparative issue:* shared linearity and judgement. The difference is the centre: Christ's death and
    resurrection vs. the final prophetic revelation.
- **JE (anchor).**
  - *Main answer:* Jesus is the Messiah, virgin-born, a Word and spirit from God, a great prophet and miracle-worker by
    God's permission, a servant and not divine or God's son. God saved him from crucifixion and raised him to Himself;
    he will return.
  - *Texts:* §1. *Interpreters:* al-Tabari, al-Razi, Ibn Kathir; Bukhari 3448.
  - *4:157 research result:*
    1. **The text** denies that they killed or crucified him and says "*shubbiha lahum*" (it was made to appear so to
       them). It names no substitute.
    2. **Al-Tabari** (d. 923) collects substitution reports from Successor-era narrators and storytellers:
       - Wahb b. Munabbih: all in the house given Jesus' likeness; or one volunteer;
       - Qatada and al-Suddi: a volunteer disciple for paradise;
       - Ibn Ishaq: "Sergius", via a Christian convert;
       - Mujahid: "they crucified a man other than Jesus".

       Al-Tabari prefers Wahb's account. He says the disciples who reported a crucifixion were not liars: they reported
       what appeared true.
    3. **Al-Razi** (d. 1210):
       - raises the strongest internal objection: casting a likeness "opens the door of sophistry", undermines sense
         knowledge and *tawātur*, and so prophecy itself;
       - records the kalām answer that Jewish leaders crucified another man and misled the public, and that Christian
         *tawātur* reaches back only to a few who could agree on a lie;
       - lists four substitution variants, which he calls "mutually conflicting... God knows best".
    4. **Ibn Kathir** (d. 1373): a youth took the likeness; the ground for certainty is that the Qur'an comes from "the
       most truthful of speakers", who knows the hidden.
    5. **Range** (Fatoohi 2023):
       - near-universal premodern denial of death by crucifixion;
       - exception: some Ismaʿili scholars from the 4th century AH;
       - Ibn Hazm records no consensus on Jesus' return;
       - modern minorities: Sayyid Ahmad Khan (crucified but survived); Ahmadiyya; ʿAbduh, Rida, al-Maraghi, Shaltut,
         Muhammad al-Ghazali (died naturally);
       - Fatoohi himself: raised alive, died later, no return.
  - *Exact caveat:* never write "the Qur'an says someone else was crucified" without qualification. The Qur'an says "it was
    made to appear so". Substitution is the dominant *interpretation*, with conflicting versions.
  - *Historical comparison (mandatory):* see `jesus-evidence.md` §K.
    - The Qur'an is c. 600 years later.
    - It is a revelation claim, not a historical report.
    - It preserves no identifiable earlier source; the tafsir narratives are later and admittedly conflicting.
    - It contradicts every first-century source and Tacitus.
    - Its reason for preference is revelatory: God knows the hidden (Ibn Kathir).

    Production may say this plainly as an evidentiary comparison, while representing why Muslims hold it.
  - *Christian comparative issue:*
    - The crucifixion is the point of sharpest historical conflict on the site.
    - It also bears on revelatory conflict: the Qur'an confirms the Gospel (3:50; 5:110; 61:6) yet denies its central
      event.
    - Al-Razi's own *tawātur* objection is the internal pressure point.

## 6. Historical-accountability notes

- ***Iʿjāz*.**
  - *Internal steelman:* the challenge stood, and Arab eloquence never met it.
  - *Evidential evaluation:*
    - (a) "No one has matched it" is a literary judgement made by the community that accepts it. Early figures
      reportedly tried or mocked (Macdonald).
    - (b) It cannot be assessed by non-Arabic readers (Halevi I.6).
    - (c) Al-Ghazali himself warns that eloquence alone can mislead (*Munqidh* p. 55).
    - (d) Even granting unmatched excellence, the step to divine authorship is an inference about human limits.
  - Record this as weaker public evidence than al-Ghazali's converging-experience account, not as refuted.
- **Preservation.**
  - The Birmingham radiocarbon date (parchment 568–645) supports early written Qur'anic text close to the traditional
    period. Thomas: "little or no alteration".
  - Preservation does not establish revelation.
  - Pre-Uthmanic variants (Sanaa) were not researched: do not claim either "identical" or "corrupted".
- **4:157 vs first-century evidence.**
  - The comparison is between a 7th-century revelation claim and 1st-century and early 2nd-century sources, Christian
    and hostile Roman.
  - Al-Razi concedes that the Christian report is *tawātur*-like and answers by limiting its chain. The historical
    packet shows the earliest chain runs through named contemporaries (Cephas, James) and a hostile historian
    (Tacitus).
  - This is the evidentiary asymmetry. State it, then state the Muslim reason for accepting the Qur'an regardless.

## 7. Important internal distinctions

- Revelation (*waḥy*) vs. inspiration (*ilhām*, not a source of knowledge: Nasafi, REUSE).
- *Iʿjāz* as eloquence (*naẓm*) vs. as content vs. as *ṣarfa* (not read).
- The Qur'anic text (no named substitute) vs. tafsir reports (conflicting) vs. modern minority readings.
- The Qur'anic Jesus (prophet, Messiah, Word) vs. Christian Christology (incarnate Son). Shared vocabulary ("Messiah",
  "Word", "spirit"), different meaning.
- Sacred linear history vs. political cycles (Ibn Khaldun).

2. **Sanaa palimpsest** and early variants: not read. Narrow caveat for RV preservation claims.

## Cross-domain consistency

- **With UA (K&T).** The Qur'an and Sunna as final authority, with reason establishing prophethood (Nasafi, REUSE).
  RV adds *how prophethood is established*: al-Ghazali's converging evidence. Consistent.
- **With divine unity (UR) and salvation (SD).** The denial of Jesus' divinity follows *tawḥīd* (UR REUSE: Q 112:1–4). The
  denial of crucifixion removes any need for atonement, consistent with the published Islam self-salvation answer
  (mercy, repentance, no vicarious satisfaction).
- **Real tension (internal).** Al-Razi's own objection: if God can cast likenesses, sense and *tawātur* become unreliable,
  and *tawātur* is what transmits the Qur'an and the Prophet's miracles. The classical answer limits the problem to
  prophetic times or to the chain's narrow origin. Al-Razi himself rejects limiting it to prophetic times. Record this as
  a genuine internal difficulty that the tradition recognized.
