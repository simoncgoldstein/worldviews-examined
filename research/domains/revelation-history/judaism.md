## 1. Core account

**Revelation.**
- God revealed himself publicly at Sinai to the whole people:
  - "I will come to you in a thick cloud, in order that the people may hear when I speak with you and so trust you ever
    after" (Exod 19:9);
  - "on the third day GOD will come down, in the sight of all the people" (19:11);
  - "You yourselves saw that I spoke to you from the very heavens" (20:19);
  - "Face to face GOD spoke to you on the mountain" (Deut 5:4);
  - "we have seen this day that humankind may live though addressed by God" (5:21).
- Deut 4:32–35 (REUSE) frames it as unprecedented and evidential.
- Prophets after Moses are tested:
  - a prophet who urges other gods is false "even if the sign or portent... comes true" (Deut 13:2–4);
  - a prophet whose oracle fails is false (18:21–22).

**How would we know?**
- **Maimonides:**
  - "The Jews did not believe in Moses, our teacher, because of the wonders that he performed. Whenever anyone's belief
    is based on wonders, [the commitment of] his heart has shortcomings, because it is possible to perform a wonder
    through magic or sorcery."
  - The source of belief is Sinai: "Our eyes saw, and not a stranger's. Our ears heard, and not another's." All Israel
    were witnesses together, "like two witnesses who observed the same event" (YT 8:1–2).
  - Later prophets are believed because Moses commanded it, and are tested by fulfilled predictions, "many times"
    (YT 10:1–2). Signs serve a purpose; they are not proof (8:1).
- **Halevi:**
  - Only a miracle "in the presence of great multitudes, who saw it distinctly, and did not learn it from reports...
    examine[d] carefully and repeatedly" can convince that God speaks to man (*Kuzari* I.8).
  - Sinai meets this: the people "distinctly heard the Ten Commandments", saw the fire forty days and the writing
    (I.87).
  - Knowledge is transmitted thereafter by "uninterrupted tradition, which is equal to" experience (I.25, REUSE).
  - The Christian and Muslim scholars themselves appeal to the history of Israel (I.4, I.9), so the Khazar turns to the
    Jews as "the evidence for the divine law on earth" (I.10).
  - The Muslim claim that the Qur'an is a miracle is inaccessible to a non-Arab: "I could not distinguish between it and
    any other book written in the Arabic language" (I.6).
- **Saadia:**
  - reason tests prophecy (III.8, REUSE);
  - the Torah cannot be abrogated: its precepts are "for your generations"; the nation exists only through its Torah and
    endures like the heavens (Jer 31:35); Malachi's closing command to remember the Torah of Moses until the Day of the
    Lord (III.7).
  - This is the Jewish adjudication against later revelations (Christianity, Islam) that claim to supersede Sinai. See
    also YT 9:1 (REUSE).
- **Modern restatement (Gottlieb, the "Kuzari principle"):** a public national revelation cannot be fabricated when the
  nation accepts it as witnessed by its own ancestors at a time when records exist.

**History.**
- Linear and covenantal: creation → covenant with the patriarchs → exodus and Sinai → land, exile and return →
  messianic restoration → resurrection and the World to Come.
- **Maimonides** (Kings 11–12):
  - the Messiah will renew the Davidic dynasty, build the Temple, gather the dispersed and restore observance (11:1);
  - the Torah itself testifies to this (Deut 30:3–5; Num 24:17–18);
  - he need not work miracles (11:3, Rabbi Akiva and Bar Kozibah);
  - he is recognized by success: a Davidic king who compels observance, fights God's wars, builds the Temple in its place
    and gathers the exiles "is definitely the Mashiach"; if he fails or is killed, he is not (11:4);
  - "the world will continue according to its pattern"; prophetic images are metaphors (12:1);
  - "there will be no difference between the current age and the Messianic era except the emancipation from our
    subjugation to the gentile kingdoms" (12:2, citing the Sages; Berakhot 34b REUSE);
  - do not calculate the end (12:2; Sanh. 97b).
- **Talmud:**
  - six thousand years of the world (Sanh. 97a: two thousand chaos, two thousand Torah, two thousand "the period of the
    coming of the Messiah");
  - "May those who calculate the end of days be cursed" (97b);
  - Rabbi Hillel's minority view that the Messiah came in Hezekiah's day, rejected by Rav Yosef (99a).
- **Saadia:**
  - redemption is certain from Moses' and the prophets' promises and God's justice: the long exile is partly punishment
    and partly trial, "and it is impossible that it should be without end" (VIII.1);
  - fifteen proofs that the promises were not fulfilled in the Second Temple (VIII.8).
- Keep three things distinct (SD REUSE: Teshuvah 8–9): the messianic age (this world), the resurrection, and the World to
  Come. Maimonides' naturalistic messianic age is one major account, not the only one: Nachmanides' transformed heart
  (Deut 30:6, REUSE); the Talmud's apocalyptic chronologies.

**Jesus.**
- **Internal theological answer.** Jesus is not the Messiah: he did not fulfil the messianic tasks (restore the kingdom,
  build the Temple, gather Israel, bring universal peace and knowledge of God).
- **Maimonides, Kings 11:4** (uncensored, Touger):
  - "Jesus of Nazareth who aspired to be the Mashiach and was executed by the court was also alluded to in Daniel's
    prophecies... 'they shall stumble'";
  - "Can there be a greater stumbling block than Christianity?";
  - yet "all the deeds of Jesus of Nazareth and that Ishmaelite who arose after him will only serve to prepare the way
    for Mashiach's coming", spreading knowledge of Messiah, Torah and mitzvot to the nations.
- **Divinity and incarnation** are rejected as contrary to God's unity and incorporeality (UR REUSE: YT 1; Guide
  I.51–60).
- **Nachmanides at Barcelona (1263):**
  - the agenda: has the Messiah come? Is he "God himself or a complete man, born of a man and a woman"? Which Torah is
    true?
  - the Talmudic sages lived after Jesus and did not believe in him;
  - aggadah is not binding;
  - the sceptre (Gen 49:10) lapsed long before Jesus;
  - Isa 53 by "the true sense" speaks of Israel;
  - prophecies of peace and universal knowledge (Isa 2:4; Jer 31) are unfulfilled: "from the days of Jesus until now
    the world is full of violence and plunder";
  - the Messiah is "a king of flesh and blood like you";
  - the core dispute is the incarnation: what Christians believe "reason will not accept, and nature does not allow,
    and the prophets never said";
  - Daniel's end-time is in Dan 12, not Dan 9.
- **Saadia** (VIII.9): Daniel's seventy weeks (Dan 9) refer to the Second Temple period. "Messiah" there means any anointed
  priest, and the passage does not speak of one known individual.
- **Rashi** on Isa 53: the servant is Israel (P3 REUSE). Do not make Isa 53 the whole case.

## 4. Question map

- **RV (anchor).**
  - *Main answer:* God revealed himself publicly at Sinai to the entire people, who heard the Ten Words. That national
    experience, transmitted in an unbroken chain, grounds belief in Moses and the Torah. Later prophets are tested by
    Moses' Torah and by fulfilled prediction.
  - *Texts:* Exod 19:9, 11; 20:19; Deut 4:32–35; 5:4, 21; 13:2–4; 18:21–22.
  - *Interpreters:* Maimonides YT 8:1–2, 10:1–2; Halevi I.8, I.25, I.87; Saadia III.7–8.
  - *Internal difference:*
    - Halevi: historical-experiential knowledge prior to philosophy;
    - Maimonides: philosophical prophetology (YT 7:1: prophecy requires a perfected intellect), with Sinai as the unique
      public proof;
    - Saadia: reason tests prophecy and confirms what revelation gives (K&T REUSE).
  - *Exact caveat:* the internal argument is about the *form* of the evidence (national, public, ancestral). Its
    comparative force depends on historical questions it cannot settle internally (§6).
  - *Christian comparative issue:*
    - The Christian lane shares Sinai and the Tanakh.
    - The disagreement is whether a *later* public revelation (Jesus, the resurrection) can be validated against
      Deut 13 and 18 and YT 9:1's bar on changing the Torah.
    - Calvin I.8.5 uses the same eyewitness-public argument for Moses that Maimonides uses: apply one standard to both.
- **HI (medium).**
  - *Main answer:* linear covenant history from creation through exile to messianic redemption: Davidic restoration, the
    Temple, ingathering, universal knowledge of God. Then resurrection and the World to Come.
  - *Texts:* Deut 30:3–5 (via Kings 11:1); Isa 2:4, 11:6 (via Kings 12:1 and *Vikuach*); Dan 12.
  - *Interpreters:* Maimonides Kings 11–12; Saadia VIII; Sanh. 97a–b.
  - *Internal difference:*
    - Maimonides' naturalistic messianic age ("the world will continue according to its pattern") vs. apocalyptic
      expectation (Gog and Magog; resurrection);
    - the Talmud's 6,000-year schema vs. the ban on calculating;
    - R. Hillel's minority view (Sanh. 99a).
  - *Exact caveat:*
    - no single messianic expectation ("Judaism has always had one messianic expectation" is false: Sanh. 99a;
      Kings 12:2 "there is a controversy among them");
    - keep the messianic age, resurrection and World to Come distinct (SD REUSE).
  - *Christian comparative issue:* both are linear with a consummation. The dispute is whether the decisive turn has
    already happened in Christ (Gal 4:4; Heb 9:26) or is still future.
- **JE (anchor).**
  - *Main answer:* Jesus was a Jew who claimed or was claimed to be the Messiah. He failed the messianic criteria and so is
    not the Messiah. The doctrine of his divinity contradicts God's unity and incorporeality. Christianity is a
    providential instrument that spreads awareness of Torah and Messiah (Maimonides).
  - *Texts:* Deut 13; Isa 2:4; Isa 53 (Rashi); Dan 9, 12.
  - *Interpreters:* Maimonides Kings 11:4; Nachmanides *Vikuach*; Saadia VIII.9; Rashi.
  - *Internal difference:*
    - Maimonides' providential reading of Christianity (preparing the way) vs. sharper polemic;
    - Nachmanides' tactical or traditional stance on aggadah (scholars disagree, EJ).
  - *Exact caveat:*
    - Jewish denials concern Messiahship and divinity, not the crucifixion as such;
    - Maimonides' "executed by the court" and Sanh. 43a's stoning are rabbinic tradition, not first-century evidence
      (`jesus-evidence.md` §K);
    - Jewish scholarship itself (Jewish Encyclopedia 1906) treats the Talmudic Jesus passages as legend.
  - *Historical evidence involved:* the shared packet.
    - The Jewish position needs no denial of claims 1–10. It rejects 11 or reinterprets it (Kohler: apparitions), and
      rejects 12.
    - The decisive Jewish argument is not historical but criteriological: the Messiah must do certain things, and the
      Torah cannot be changed.
  - *Christian comparative issue:*
    - Turretin XIII.1 argues from Gen 49:10 and Dan 9 that the Messiah must have come before the Temple's fall. Saadia
      VIII.9 and Nachmanides read the same texts otherwise.
    - The Christian reply to "unfulfilled peace" is a two-advent history (Heb 9:28).
    - Present these as rival readings of shared texts, not as a historical fact settled by either.

## 6. Historical-accountability notes

**The Sinai argument (RV).** Represent it at full strength, then evaluate it.

- **Internal steelman:**
  - Halevi I.8 sets exacting criteria: multitudes, direct perception, repeated examination;
  - Maimonides YT 8:1–2 grounds belief in shared witness, not wonders;
  - Gottlieb's modern principle: a national-ancestral public event cannot be introduced as an invented tradition when
    records exist, and there are no other examples.
- **Evidential form.** What we possess is a *national tradition* recorded in a text, transmitted by a people. We do not
  have contemporary documentation of the event.
  - The tradition's claim is that *its own transmission* is the evidence (Halevi I.25: tradition "equal to" experience).
  - The historical question is when and how the tradition and its text arose.
- **Critical evaluation** (Farber 2015, a rabbi and biblical scholar):
  - no Egyptian or archaeological evidence for the exodus as told;
  - implausible numbers (600,000 men; Ibn Khaldun had already judged the reported Israelite army numbers implausible,
    de Slane pp. 16–17);
  - a composite text with contradictory details;
  - a genre that mixes myth, folklore and historiography;
  - at most a historical kernel.
- **Critique of the Kuzari principle** (Tal, in Gottlieb):
  - counter-cases (Joshua 10 as a worldwide public miracle unattested elsewhere; myths of origins);
  - a single source without external corroboration;
  - a tradition could arise "many generations after" and be accepted as ancestral.
- **Gottlieb's replies:**
  - the conditions (descendants of the witnesses; available records) are not met by the counter-cases;
  - absence of record is expected for others' religious history.
- **Comparative standard.** The same rule must apply to Christianity: Calvin I.8.5 makes Halevi's argument for Moses.
  - The difference production may note is *documentary distance*: Paul writes about 20 years after the events, naming
    living witnesses; the Pentateuch's date of composition relative to Sinai is far greater on critical views.
  - Jewish tradition disputes the critical dating; that dating was not sourced in detail this pass (gap).
  - State the contrast cautiously.

**JE.**
- Talmudic and medieval Jewish material is theological or polemical evidence about later Jewish-Christian relations, not
  first-century counter-testimony (Jewish Encyclopedia).
- Maimonides and Nachmanides argue from criteria (messianic tasks; divine unity), which do not depend on contesting the
  first-century facts.

## 7. Important internal distinctions

- Sinai (public, national) vs. later prophecy (individual, tested) vs. the Oral Torah's transmission (Avot 1:1, REUSE).
- Wonders as purposeful acts (YT 8:1) vs. wonders as credentials (rejected as a foundation).
- The messianic age (natural, political-religious) vs. resurrection vs. the World to Come.
- Maimonides' providential view of Christianity and Islam vs. polemic.
- Rejection of Messiahship (criteria unmet) vs. rejection of divinity (unity and incorporeality) vs. rejection of Torah
  abrogation (Saadia III.7; YT 9:1). These are three distinct Jewish objections.

## Cross-domain consistency

- **With UA (K&T).** Sinai and Torah authority match YT 8–9 and Avot 1:1. RV adds the *evidential* argument (Halevi I.8,
  I.87), not a new authority.
- **With final-end (SD).** The messianic age vs. the World to Come is kept as in Teshuvah 8–9 and Kings 12. Consistent.
- **Real tension.**
  - Maimonides YT 7:7 says a sign is given to a sent prophet "so that the people will know that God has truly sent
    him", yet YT 8:1 says belief based on wonders is defective. He resolves it: we obey later prophets' signs because
    Moses commanded it (8:2), not because signs prove.
  - Present this structure accurately rather than as a contradiction.
