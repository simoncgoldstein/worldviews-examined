# Buddhist traditions: Revelation & History

Research packet, 2026-10-07. Codes RV, HI, JE. Marks: **REUSE**, **NEW**, **NEW SRC**.

Translations:
- Pāli: Sujato (SuttaCentral bilara API), cited by sutta and segment.
- Lotus Sūtra: Kern (1884). *Mahāvaṃsa*: Geiger (1912).
- BCA: the GRETIL Sanskrit (our translation).
- Soyen Shaku: Suzuki's translation (1906).

**Terminology.** Buddhism generally does not claim revelation from a creator. The comparative question is: **by what
authority are the Buddha's teachings regarded as true, and how is a teaching recognized as Buddha-word?** Use
"disclosure", "discovery" or "Buddha-word" rather than imposing "revelation".

**Lane method:**
1. Early and Theravāda baseline (Nikāyas);
2. the Mahāyāna claim to Buddha-word (Lotus; Śāntideva);
3. scholarship on canonical chronology (SEP Buddha, SEP Abhidharma, *Mahāvaṃsa*);
4. modern receptions for JE.

## 1. Core account

**Disclosure (RV).**
- The Buddha is a *discoverer* and teacher, not a channel for a deity's word.
- "I saw an ancient path, an ancient route traveled by fully awakened Buddhas in the past", like a man who finds an
  ancient city in the forest and has it rebuilt (SN 12.65).
- Dependent origination is a law that "persists whether Realized Ones arise or not" (SN 12.20, REUSE).
- The teaching is verified by the hearer:
  - the ten insufficient grounds (AN 3.65, REUSE): "know for yourselves";
  - investigate even the Buddha (MN 47, REUSE);
  - the raft (MN 38, REUSE);
  - "one who sees the teaching sees me" (SN 22.87, REUSE).
- After the Buddha, the teaching and training are the Teacher (DN 16, REUSE).
- Claims to be Buddha-word are checked against the discourses and the training: the four great references (AN 4.180;
  DN 16, REUSE).
- Some discourses need interpretation (*neyyattha*) and some are explicit (*nītattha*). Confusing them misrepresents the
  Buddha (AN 2.24–25).
- Dharmakīrti: the Buddha is a *pramāṇa* by having taught the reliable path. Scripture is a limited, fallible guide
  outside the empirically inaccessible (PV II.1–10; SEP Dharmakīrti §4, REUSE K&T).

**Buddha-word in the early texts and in Mahāyāna.**
- **AN 8.8 (Uttara):** "whatever is well spoken is spoken by the Blessed One... Both myself and others rely completely on
  that when we speak". The heap-of-grain simile: well-spoken teaching is drawn from the Buddha's store. In context this
  is a claim of **derivation** from the Buddha.
- Later Mahāyāna sources turned the formula into a criterion of **identity** (well-spoken = Buddha-word). That later usage
  was **not read this pass** (Śikṣāsamuccaya). Flag it.
- **Mahāyāna sūtras** appear in the record later than the early discourses. By traditional accounts Mahāyāna doctrines
  arose "roughly in the first century BCE" (SEP Abhidharma §1). How, then, are they Buddha-word?
  1. **Skilful means and one vehicle (Lotus II):** all Buddhas teach "by means of only one vehicle, the Buddha-vehicle".
     The three vehicles were an adaptation to "the dispositions of creatures" (Kern pp. 39–40). Earlier teachings are
     provisional (compare *neyārtha* hermeneutics, SEP Mind in Indian Buddhist Philosophy, not registered).
  2. **The eternal Buddha (Lotus XV):** Śākyamuni's awakening at Gayā is a show. He was enlightened "many hundred
     thousand myriads of koṭis of Æons ago". "In order to produce the roots of goodness... he reveals various
     Dharma-paryāyas", all true (Kern pp. 298–302). The Buddha's teaching is not confined to his historical lifetime.
  3. **Śāntideva's parity argument (BCA 9.42–44):**
     - "If the Mahāyāna is not established, how is your scripture established?"
     - "Because it is established for both [of us]" — "It was not established for you at the start."
     - "Whatever grounds your trust there, apply it to the Mahāyāna too."
     - "If agreement of two parties made a thing true, the Vedas too would be true."
     - "If you say the Mahāyāna is disputed, then abandon your own scripture, since it too is disputed by outsiders."
- **No single theory covers all Mahāyāna canons.** Theravāda does not accept these sūtras as Buddha-word.

**History.**
- **Beginningless:** "this transmigration has no known beginning. No first point is found of sentient beings roaming and
  transmigrating, shrouded by ignorance and fettered by craving" (SN 15.1).
- **Eons are vast:** a mountain wiped once a century with fine cloth wears away before an eon ends; "we've transmigrated
  through many such eons" (SN 15.5).
- **The cosmos contracts and expands** (DN 27:10). World-systems end in fire: seven suns, "no soot or ash" (AN 7.66).
  "So impermanent are conditions" (AN 7.66).
- **Within world-ages:**
  - human morality and lifespans decline to ten-year lives and an "interregnum of swords", then recover as people take
    up skilful conduct (DN 26:19–24);
  - in that renewed age the Buddha **Metteyya** will arise (DN 26:25).
- **The Dharma itself declines:**
  - "the true teaching will remain only five hundred years" (AN 8.51);
  - it disappears "not... like a ship that sinks all at once" but when "the counterfeit of the true teaching appears",
    through "futile people" (SN 16.13).
  - East Asian *mappō* (latter Dharma) eschatology developed this later. Not researched; distinguish it.
- **Final horizon:** no cosmic consummation. Saṃsāra has no known beginning and no cosmic end-state. Buddhas recur.
- **The goal** is liberation *from* the cycles (AN 7.66 ends: "Craving for continued existence has been cut off... now
  there'll be no more future lives").
- **Do not write "Buddhism has no meaningful narratives about the future".** DN 26 and Metteyya are such narratives. The
  correct statement is *no final cosmic historical consummation*.

**Jesus.**
- Early Buddhist canonical texts say nothing about Jesus. That absence is structural.
- Modern receptions:
  1. **Soyen Shaku** (Rinzai abbot, 1906):
     - Buddhism has a "God" in the sense of the Dharmakāya, not a personal creator;
     - the Dharmakāya was "incarnated not only in Gautama-Buddha, but also in all great men... foremost among them in
       Jesus Christ";
     - Jesus' miracles are "irrelevant" ("I do not deny the miracles nor do I believe them");
     - Jesus is "the greatest master and teacher that appeared in the West after Buddha", yet he lacked the Buddha's
       calm (anger in the temple) (pp. 123–125).
  2. **The Dalai Lama** (1994, as quoted in Cobb's review of *The Good Heart*):
     - Jesus was "a fully enlightened being or a bodhisattva";
     - yet interdependence leaves "almost impossible" room for an eternal absolute truth or "a divine Creation"
       (pp. 82–83).
  3. **Thich Nhat Hanh** (*Living Buddha, Living Christ*, 1995): known for treating Jesus and Buddha as spiritual
     ancestors. **Not read** (publisher page 403; book copyrighted). Gap.
- **Metaphysical conflict.** A Buddhist may admire Jesus while rejecting:
  - a creator God (Dalai Lama; Soyen Shaku);
  - incarnation of a creator;
  - an enduring self raised bodily (no-self, SN 22.59, REUSE);
  - salvation by another's sacrifice (karma and self-cultivation; Pure Land "Other Power" as the partial exception,
    SD REUSE).

## 4. Question map

- **RV (anchor).**
  - *Main answer:* there is no creator to reveal himself. The Buddha *discovered* the timeless Dhamma (an ancient path)
    and taught it, and its truth is verified in practice and insight. Buddha-word is recognized by agreement with the
    discourses and the discipline. In Mahāyāna it is also recognized by skilful-means adaptation and the eternal
    Buddha's ongoing teaching.
  - *Texts:* SN 12.65; SN 12.20; AN 3.65; AN 4.180; DN 16; AN 8.8; AN 2.24–25; Lotus II, XV.
  - *Interpreters:* Dharmakīrti (REUSE); Śāntideva BCA 9.42–44.
  - *Internal difference:*
    - Theravāda: a closed Pāli canon, Buddha-word tested by the four great references;
    - Mahāyāna: an expanded canon justified by skilful means, an eternal or transcendent Buddha, revelation in
      meditation, and parity arguments;
    - Dharmakīrti: rational testing, with scripture fallible outside the supersensible.
  - *Exact caveat:*
    - do not call the Buddha's teaching "revelation" in the Abrahamic sense;
    - do not say "later means false": Mahāyāna's claims rest on doctrines (eternal Buddha; skilful means) that make
      historical lateness non-decisive within its own terms.
  - *Historical evidence involved:* §6.
  - *Christian comparative issue:*
    - verification in experience (the Dhamma) vs. testimony to a historical act (the gospel);
    - the parity argument (BCA 9.43) has the same structure as the Reformed claim that every ultimate authority is
      self-attesting (K&T). The Mahāyāna and Christian cases both face the question of what makes self-authentication
      credible.
- **HI (medium).**
  - *Main answer:* beginningless saṃsāra and vast cosmic eons of contraction and expansion, with world-destructions by
    fire. Within world-ages there are moral decline and recovery, with Buddhas arising (Metteyya to come). The Dhamma
    itself declines. There is no final cosmic consummation; the goal is individual liberation from the cycles.
  - *Texts:* SN 15.1, 15.5; DN 26; DN 27; AN 7.66; AN 8.51; SN 16.13.
  - *Interpreters:* Buddhaghosa (*Visuddhimagga* XIII on eons: not read, the download was truncated); Mahāyāna eternal
    Buddha (Lotus XV) and later *mappō* (not read).
  - *Internal difference:* early decline texts (500 years; counterfeit Dharma) vs. East Asian three-period *mappō*
    eschatology (a later development) vs. the Mahāyāna eternal Buddha (the Buddha is never truly absent).
  - *Exact caveat:*
    - "no final cosmic historical consummation" ≠ "no meaningful narratives of the future" (DN 26; Metteyya);
    - Metteyya is the next Buddha in the cycle, not a final redeemer.
  - *Christian comparative issue:* Augustine's argument against cycles (*CoG* XII.13) and the Buddhist claim of no first
    beginning (SN 15.1) collide directly on whether time had a beginning. See UR `something-rather-than-nothing`
    (REUSE).
- **JE (anchor).**
  - *Main answer:* the canonical texts are silent. Modern Buddhist teachers have received Jesus as an enlightened being,
    a bodhisattva, or a manifestation of the Dharmakāya, an exemplary teacher of compassion. They reject the creator,
    the incarnation of a creator, an eternal soul and substitutionary salvation.
  - *Interpreters:* Soyen Shaku (1906); the Dalai Lama (1994, via Cobb).
  - *Internal difference:* Soyen Shaku's Dharmakāya-incarnation language (Zen/Mahāyāna) vs. the Dalai Lama's "enlightened
    being or bodhisattva" (Tibetan) vs. sharper critics (Soyen Shaku himself faults Jesus' anger).
  - *Exact caveat:*
    - "Buddhism regards Jesus as a bodhisattva" is false as a general claim;
    - attribute it to the Dalai Lama;
    - analogy is not identity.
  - *Historical comparison:* receptions are interpretations of the Gospels, 19 to 20 centuries later. They do not dispute
    the crucifixion. They reinterpret or disregard the resurrection and divinity claims (Soyen Shaku: miracles
    "irrelevant").
    - Cobb's observation is apt: history "is much more important to the Bible... than to the Buddhist scriptures".
  - *Christian comparative issue:*
    - Can a figure whose earliest Jewish followers worshipped him alongside the one God (`jesus-evidence.md` §I) be fitted
      into the bodhisattva category without revising what the earliest sources say about him?
    - Conversely, Buddhist metaphysics gives a principled reason to deny the Christian interpretation, not just
      distaste.

## 6. Historical-accountability notes

- **Earliest recoverable teaching vs. later canon vs. authority.**
  - The Buddha (fl. c. 450 BCE) left no writings. The discourses were "probably rehearsed shortly after his death and
    preserved through oral transmission" and written "several centuries after his death" (SEP Buddha §1). The
    *Mahāvaṃsa* places the Pāli writing-down under Vaṭṭagāmaṇi (XXXIII.100–101; 1st century BCE by standard
    chronology).
  - The texts disagree and show sectarian insertion and deletion (SEP Buddha §1).
  - Mahāyāna sūtras emerge from about the first century BCE (SEP Abhidharma §1).
- **Rule.** When a later text claims to preserve the historical Buddha's teaching, chronology is relevant. But the
  Mahāyāna's own claim is often that its sūtras come from the transcendent or eternal Buddha (Lotus XV) or are justified
  by skilful means, not that they were memorized at Rājagṛha. Evaluate each claim on its own terms:
  - historical-lineage claims face chronology;
  - transcendent-Buddha claims face the same self-authentication question as every lane (BCA 9.43).
- **The Dhamma's self-verification** (AN 3.65; MN 95) is a *practical-experiential* criterion, not a historical one. Its
  evidential form differs in kind from Sinai or the resurrection.
- **JE.** Buddhist receptions are modern interpretations. No independent historical information.

## 7. Important internal distinctions

- Discovery (Buddha) vs. revelation (creator). Buddha-word by derivation (AN 8.8) vs. by identity of well-spokenness
  (later Mahāyāna, not read).
- Implicit vs. explicit teaching (AN 2.24–25; *neyārtha*/*nītārtha*).
- Theravāda canon (closed) vs. Mahāyāna canons (open, multiple justifications).
- Cosmic cycles vs. Dharma's decline vs. individual liberation.
- Admiring Jesus vs. accepting the Christian interpretation of him.

## Cross-domain consistency

- **With K&T.** The Dhamma verified in direct knowledge; Buddha-word tested by the great references; Dharmakīrti's
  fallibilism (UA). RV is consistent and adds the Mahāyāna authority question.
- **With UR.** Beginningless saṃsāra fits dependent origination with no first cause (UR REUSE). The Lotus's eternal
  Buddha sits uneasily with early impermanence and no-self, but Mahāyāna reads it through two truths (MMK 24, REUSE).
  Record this as an inter-school difference, not a contradiction.
- **With final-end (SD).** Liberation from the cycle, not cosmic consummation, matches the published Buddhist final-end
  (SN 56.11; MN 72).
