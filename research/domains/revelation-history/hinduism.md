## 1. Core account

**Revelation: no Abrahamic speaking creator model.**
- *Śruti* ("what is heard") is the Veda: eternal, "found" or "seen" by the ṛṣis, not composed.
- *Smṛti* ("what is remembered": Gītā, epics, Manu, Purāṇas) is authoritative insofar as it depends on śruti (Manu
  II.10–12, REUSE K&T; Śaṅkara on BS I.3.28: smṛti "depends on something else (viz. Scripture)").
- The Veda is the means of knowing what perception and inference cannot reach: dharma (Mīmāṃsā) and Brahman (Vedānta)
  (BS I.1.3 in all three schools, REUSE K&T).

**Why regard śruti as a uniquely reliable disclosure? Four distinct answers:**
1. **Authorless Veda (*apauruṣeya*), Mīmāṃsā (Kumārila, ŚV II.47–68, REUSE):**
   - every cognition is valid unless defeated (*svataḥ prāmāṇya*);
   - falsity comes only from defects in a speaker;
   - the Veda has no speaker, so there is no source of error.
   - Śaṅkara reports the ground: "the eternity of the Veda is founded on the absence of the remembrance of an agent"
     (BS I.3.29, p. 211).
2. **Reliable author, Nyāya (NS 2.1.69, REUSE; Udayana *Kusumāñjali* V, REUSE):** the Veda is reliable "like spell and
   medicine" because of the reliability of its authors. Udayana infers an omniscient author, Īśvara.
3. **Vedāntic scriptural authority (Śaṅkara on BS I.1.3, pp. 19–20):**
   - Brahman is the source of Scripture: the Ṛg-veda etc. "have been breathed forth from that great Being" (BAU II.4.10);
     an omniscient text requires an omniscient source.
   - Alternatively, scripture is the means by which Brahman is known.
   - Brahman cannot be reached by perception or inference (Rāmānuja I.1.3, REUSE).
4. **Realization (Advaita):** scripture yields knowledge of Brahman, which becomes direct realization. Śruti itself
   operates "within Nescience" (Śaṅkara intro, REUSE K&T) and is finally "lower knowledge" (Muṇḍaka I.1.4–5, REUSE).

**Cycles and revelation (Śaṅkara on BS I.3.28–30, pp. 203–213):**
- The world originates from the eternal Vedic words: Prajāpati created "at the word". Manu I.21: names and forms shaped
  "from the words of the Veda" (quoted by Śaṅkara).
- The ṛṣis "found" the Veda: "the speech found (by the ṛṣis) was permanent", citing RV X.71.3.
- The Vedas "had been hidden at the end of the yuga" and were obtained again by penance.
- In each new kalpa the world is renewed with "the sameness of name and form", in a "beginningless saṃsāra".
- **Revelation is thus not a once-for-all historical event. It is the perpetually re-found eternal word.**
- *Viṣṇu Purāṇa* III.3: in every Dvāpara age Viṣṇu as Vyāsa divides "the Veda, which is properly but one" into four
  (Wilson, p. 271 ff.).

**Avatāra (divine descent), a second form of disclosure.**
- Gītā 4.6–8: "Whenever there is a decay of religion... then I manifest Myself. For the protection of the good, for the
  destruction of evil-doers, for the firm establishment of religion, I am born in every age."
- **Śaṅkara:**
  - born "by My own Māyā";
  - "I appear to be born and embodied, through my own Maya, but not in reality, unlike others" (Sastri pp. 106–107).
- **Rāmānuja:**
  - the incarnations are real but "divine, immaterial and unique", unlike karmic births;
  - "whenever I chose, not... confined to any particular yuga";
  - knowing them truly liberates (Govindacharya pp. 141–143).

**History.**
- Cosmic time is cyclical and beginningless.
  - **Gītā:** "the day of Brahmā is a thousand yugas long and the night is a thousand yugas long". At day's coming "all
    manifestations proceed" from the Unmanifest; at night they dissolve (8.17–18, Sastri pp. 208–209). "All beings... go
    into My Prakṛti at the end of a kalpa. I send them forth again at the beginning" (9.7, p. 220).
  - **Śaṅkara** on 8.19: this shows "that, as the effect of karma caused by avidyā... all creatures involuntarily come
    into being again and again and dissolve", a teaching meant to produce detachment from saṃsāra.
  - **Manu I.68–86:** four ages (Kṛta, Tretā, Dvāpara, Kali), each shorter; in the Kṛta "Dharma is four-footed and
    entire"; in each later age it loses a foot.
  - ***Viṣṇu Purāṇa*:**
    - Viṣṇu works in each age: as Kapila in the Kṛta, as universal monarch in the Tretā, as Vyāsa in the Dvāpara, "and
      at the end of the Kali... as Kalki" (III.2, pp. 270–271);
    - at the Kali's end Kalki "will destroy all the Mlechchhas and thieves... reestablish righteousness", and "the Kṛta
      age shall return" (IV.24, p. 484).
- **Meaningful divine action within cycles:** avatāras recur "in every age" to restore dharma. History is not meaningless
  or illusory. It is repeating, morally patterned and providentially governed (Viṣṇu "preserves, creates, and at last
  destroys", VP III.2).
- **Final horizon:** there is no once-for-all consummation of world history.
  - Kalki restores a new Kṛta age; the cycle continues.
  - The ultimate end is individual liberation (*mokṣa*) *from* the cycle: Gītā 4.9, "he comes to Me". It differs by
    school (SD final-end, REUSE).

## 4. Question map

- **RV (anchor).**
  - *Main answer:* ultimate reality is disclosed in the Veda, eternal śruti heard or seen by the ṛṣis, and in divine
    descents (avatāra), and is finally known by realization. The answer to "how would we know?" depends on the school:
    1. Mīmāṃsā: the Veda has no author and so no defect; cognition is intrinsically valid;
    2. Nyāya: an omniscient author guarantees it;
    3. Vedānta: Brahman, unknowable otherwise, is known from śruti, and realization confirms it.
  - *Texts:* BS I.1.3; I.3.28–30; Manu II.10–12; Muṇḍaka I.1.4–5; Gītā 4.6–9.
  - *Interpreters:* Kumārila; Udayana; Śaṅkara; Rāmānuja; Madhva.
  - *Internal difference:* authorless vs. authored Veda (Mīmāṃsā vs. Nyāya); Advaita's subordination of śruti to
    realization vs. theistic Vedānta's permanent scripture-and-devotion.
  - *Exact caveat:*
    - do not force a "speaking creator" model;
    - Mīmāṃsā (classically) needs no God for the Veda;
    - Advaita's avatāra is apparent (Māyā), Rāmānuja's real but immaterial.
  - *Historical evidence involved:* §6 (oral preservation vs. authorlessness).
  - *Christian comparative issue:* authorless eternal word vs. God speaking in history. Hindu revelation's authority is
    deliberately *non-historical* (re-found each kalpa). The Christian claims revelation in unrepeatable events (Heb 9:26;
    Bavinck §10: "not susceptible of any repetition").
- **HI (medium).**
  - *Main answer:* cosmic time is cyclical and beginningless (yugas within kalpas within Brahmā's days and nights). Within
    each cycle dharma declines from the Kṛta to the Kali. God (Viṣṇu) acts in every age, and at the Kali's end Kalki
    restores righteousness and a new Kṛta begins. There is no final consummation of world history; the goal is
    liberation from the cycle.
  - *Texts:* Gītā 4.7–8, 8.17–19, 9.7; Manu I.68–86; VP III.2, IV.24.
  - *Interpreters:* Śaṅkara (cycles teach detachment; beginningless saṃsāra); Rāmānuja (avatāra as gracious refuge).
  - *Internal difference:* Advaita (the cycles are the field of Nescience) vs. Viśiṣṭādvaita and Dvaita (the cycles are
    the Lord's real sport and governance).
  - *Exact caveat:*
    - never "history is an illusion";
    - even Advaita grants empirical reality (*vyavahāra*) to the cycles;
    - avatāra action is meaningful *within* cycles;
    - Kalki is restoration, not consummation.
  - *Christian comparative issue:* Augustine's anti-cyclical argument (*CoG* XII.13: "once Christ died... dies no more";
    liberation that can be lost is not true blessedness) engages precisely this. The Hindu reply: liberation is *from*
    the cycle and permanent (Rāmānuja: the Lord does not return the released, SD REUSE).
- **JE (anchor).**
  - *Main answer:* there is no Vedic, Upaniṣadic or classical Vedānta teaching about Jesus. State that absence
    structurally. Modern Hindu thinkers have received Jesus within Hindu frameworks:
    1. **Ram Mohan Roy** (1820/1823): a divinely empowered moral teacher whose "precepts" suffice for "peace and
       happiness"; Jesus' Deity and the Trinity are rejected for God's unity; miracle reports would carry no weight in
       India.
    2. **Vivekananda** (1900):
       - Jesus is one of the "man-Gods, the Incarnations, the embodiments of God";
       - "If I, as an Oriental, have to worship Jesus of Nazareth, there is only one way left to me, that is, to worship
         him as God";
       - historicity is explicitly bracketed: "It does not matter at all whether the New Testament was written within
         five hundred years of his birth, nor... how much of that life is true";
       - "My Master" (1896): Ramakrishna followed "the true religion of Jesus the Christ" and reached the same goal.
    3. **Gandhi** (1927): the Sermon on the Mount "went straight to my heart". He could not believe that Jesus was "the
       only incarnate Son of God" or literal redemption "by his blood". He accepted him as "a martyr, an embodiment of
       sacrifice, and a divine teacher, but not as the most perfect man ever born".
  - *Exact caveat:*
    - "Hinduism teaches Jesus was an avatar" is false as a general statement;
    - Vivekananda's "Incarnation" language is his Advaita-Ramakrishna pluralism;
    - Gandhi explicitly denies uniqueness;
    - Roy is a Unitarian-Brahmo reformer.
  - *Historical comparison:* no independent ancient Hindu Jesus tradition exists. These receptions interpret the Gospels
    (`jesus-evidence.md` §K). They do not dispute the first-century facts so much as their significance: uniqueness,
    atonement, bodily resurrection. Vivekananda's bracketing of history is itself the key comparative point.
  - *Christian comparative issue:*
    - Christian uniqueness (one incarnation, "once for all") vs. recurring avatāras;
    - real vs. Māyā embodiment (Śaṅkara on Gītā 4.6);
    - historical facts as the ground of faith (1 Cor 15:17) vs. their irrelevance (Vivekananda).

## 6. Historical-accountability notes

- **Vedic preservation vs. divine or authorless origin.**
  - Jamison & Witzel: Vedic composition and transmission were "completely oral for the entire Vedic period and some
    considerable time afterwards" (to at least c. 1000 CE for the AV), preserved by priestly schools. Techniques such as
    the Padapāṭha attest extraordinary fidelity.
  - Their tentative dating (beginnings of the Ṛgveda c. 1500 BCE) places the texts' *composition* in history, with
    layers, schools and poets' names (the ṛṣi families).
- **The inference rule:** "accurately preserved, therefore revealed" is invalid. Preservation shows a reliable
  transmission of *what was composed*, not that it was uncomposed.
- **The authorless claim and evidence.**
  - Śaṅkara states the Mīmāṃsā ground as "the absence of the remembrance of an agent" (BS I.3.29).
  - That is an argument from *silence about authorship*. Historical scholarship's attribution of hymns to poet families
    and periods bears directly on it.
  - Do not dismiss śruti for lacking a single prophet, and do not treat the absence of a remembered author as evidence of
    authorlessness.
- **JE.** Hindu receptions are 19th–20th-century interpretations without independent historical information about Jesus.

## 7. Important internal distinctions

- Śruti vs. smṛti (Manu II.10–12; Śaṅkara on BS I.3.28).
- Authorless (Mīmāṃsā) vs. God-authored (Nyāya) vs. Brahman-breathed (Vedānta) Veda.
- Avatāra as appearance (Śaṅkara, Māyā) vs. real immaterial descent (Rāmānuja).
- Cosmic cycle (kalpa, yuga) vs. individual liberation from it vs. avatāra restoration within it.
- Classical tradition (silent on Jesus) vs. modern reformers' receptions (Roy, Vivekananda, Gandhi).

## Cross-domain consistency

- **With K&T.** Śruti as the pramāṇa for the supersensible (BS I.1.3 in all three schools) and Advaita's "lower
  knowledge" (Muṇḍaka) are consistent with RV. RV adds the eternal-word cosmology (BS I.3.28–30).
- **With UR.** Cyclical history fits beginningless saṃsāra (Śaṅkara II.1.34–36, REUSE). The eternity of the Veda
  through kalpas fits Brahman's changelessness.
- **Real tension (internal).** If the Veda is "breathed forth" from Brahman (BAU II.4.10; Śaṅkara on BS I.1.3), is it
  authorless (Mīmāṃsā)? Śaṅkara's harmonization: the world and the Veda recur with the same names and forms in each kalpa, so the Veda has
  no *originating* author (I.3.29–30, read; the exact mechanism of re-emission is not stated in the pages read). Present
  this as an inter-school difference that Advaita harmonizes, not as a contradiction.
