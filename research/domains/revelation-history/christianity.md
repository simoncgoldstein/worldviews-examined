## 1. Core account

**Revelation.**
- God reveals himself:
  - in creation and providence: "The heavens declare the glory of God" (Ps 19:1–4); "the light of nature" (WCF 1.1);
    the universe is "like a beautiful book" (Belgic 2);
  - in special revelation, given "at sundry times, and in divers manners" (WCF 1.1; Heb 1:1), climaxing in the Son
    (Heb 1:2–3; John 1:14, 18: "he has made him known").
- Bavinck's synthesis:
  - special revelation comes in three forms, theophany, prophecy and miracle, which "form one whole";
  - it "enters into history, and forms a history";
  - "the center of revelation is the person of Christ", and the incarnation is "the center of all history" (RD I §10,
    PDF pp. 338–340, 376).
- Revelation is not a set of disconnected oracles. It is a public history of God's acts with an interpreting word.

**How would we know?** The Reformed answer has three levels, which production must keep distinct:

1. **Self-attestation and the Spirit's testimony** (the ground of *faith's* certainty).
   - Scripture "carrying its own evidence" (Calvin I.7.5, REUSE).
   - Belgic 5: received "because the Holy Spirit testifies in our hearts that they are from God, and also because they
     prove themselves to be from God".
   - Turretin II.6 and Bavinck I §12 and §18 (REUSE).
   - This is not "Scripture says it is God's word, so it is". The claim is that God's speech carries marks that the
     Spirit-opened mind recognizes, as light is seen by light.
2. **Public historical signs** (the "secondary helps").
   - Calvin I.8:
     - Moses' miracles were done publicly before the eyewitnesses: "How, then, could he possibly impose on the very
       eye-witnesses?" (I.8.5);
     - fulfilled prophecy (I.8.7–8);
     - the providential preservation of the text (I.8.9–10);
     - the Gospels' simplicity and majesty (I.8.11);
     - the church's consent and the martyrs (I.8.12–13).
   - Belgic 5: "even the blind themselves are able to see that the things predicted in them do happen."
   - The OT itself sets public tests: Isaiah's challenge to the idols to predict (Isa 41:21–23; 44:6–8; 46:9–10); the
     prophet whose word fails is false (Deut 18:21–22).
   - The NT claims public signs and testimony: Luke 1:1–4 ("eyewitnesses... that you may have certainty"); John
     20:30–31; Acts 2:22 ("as you yourselves know"); 2 Pet 1:16–18 ("we did not follow cleverly devised myths... we
     were eyewitnesses"); 1 John 1:1–3.
3. **The climactic public case: Jesus and the resurrection.**
   - God "has given assurance to all by raising him from the dead" (Acts 17:31).
   - "If Christ has not been raised, your faith is futile" (1 Cor 15:17).
   - The faith stakes itself on a historical claim and lists witnesses (1 Cor 15:3–8).

**Key internal qualification (Bavinck).**
- Precisely "because revelation is history, there is no other way to know anything of it than the ordinary way in all
  history, that is, the testimony" (I §10, PDF p. 379).
- Yet the historical-apologetic proof "cannot precede faith". As a method for producing faith it "does not lead to the
  desired result" (I §14, pp. 536–537).
- Calvin: proofs are "secondary helps" that "cannot of themselves produce a firm faith... until our heavenly Father
  manifest his presence in it" (I.8.13).

So the Reformed lane affirms real public evidence while denying that evidence *alone* generates saving certainty. That is
a distinction, not an evasion: the same God who acted publicly also illumines (WCF 1.5, REUSE).

**History.**
- One redemptive history runs from creation and Fall through promise (Gen 3:15; 12:1–3), covenant and kingdom (Dan 2:44;
  7:13–14), to "the fullness of time" (Gal 4:4).
- Christ is its centre: "a plan for the fullness of time, to unite all things in him" (Eph 1:9–10). He appeared "once
  for all at the end of the ages" (Heb 9:26).
- The church's mission follows "to the end of the age" (Matt 28:18–20; Acts 1:6–11).
- It ends in the creation's liberation (Rom 8:19–23), Christ's reign until "the last enemy... death" is destroyed and
  "God may be all in all" (1 Cor 15:22–28), judgement (Acts 17:31; WCF 33), and new creation (Isa 65:17; Rev 21–22).
- **Augustine** supplies the classic theology of history:
  - the two cities run through "this whole time or world-age" from Cain and Abel "until all human generation shall
    cease" (*CoG* XV.1);
  - they are intermingled, sharing earthly peace while using it for different ends (XIX.17);
  - they are finally separated at judgement (XV.1; XX);
  - history ends in the eternal Sabbath, "an eighth and eternal day, consecrated by the resurrection of Christ" (XXII.30).
- Augustine explicitly rejects cyclical recurrence: "far be it from us to believe this. For once Christ died for our sins;
  and, rising from the dead, He dies no more... The wicked walk in a circle" (XII.13, New Advent numbering; Latin
  XII.14).
- **Bavinck:**
  - the exalted Christ "guides world history" (RD III §42, p. 495);
  - there must "once come an end to the history of the world" (IV §55, p. 520);
  - redemption is "not a second, new creation but a re-creation" (IV §56, p. 594);
  - the world is renewed by fire that "cleanses... but does not destroy" its substance (pp. 626–627).
- **WCF:** one covenant of grace "under various dispensations" (7.5–6). The benefits of redemption were communicated "in
  all ages successively from the beginning of the world" (8.6). The day of judgement is fixed but unknown, "that they may
  shake off all carnal security" (33.3).
- History is therefore *linear, Christ-centred, providentially directed and consummated*. It is not progressive in a
  naturalistic sense: Augustine's earthly city persists to the end.

**Jesus.**
- Biblical claims:
  - the eternal Word made flesh (John 1:1–3, 14);
  - "the image of the invisible God... in him the whole fullness of deity dwells bodily" (Col 1:15–20; 2:9);
  - Messiah and Son of Man (Mark 8:29–31; 14:61–62);
  - who gave "his life as a ransom for many" (Mark 10:45);
  - the "one mediator" (1 Tim 2:5);
  - bodily raised (Luke 24:36–43; John 20:24–29);
  - ascended and returning (Acts 1:9–11);
  - "declared to be the Son of God in power... by his resurrection" (Rom 1:3–4).
- Calvin: the Mediator had to be "very God and very man", since "the case was certainly desperate, if the Godhead itself
  did not descend to us" (II.12.1, 12.3). Without the resurrection "all that has hitherto been said would be defective"
  (II.16.13).
- WCF 8.2: "two whole, perfect, and distinct natures... inseparably joined together in one person, without conversion,
  composition, or confusion". WCF 8.4: he "arose from the dead, with the same body in which he suffered". WLC 52: raised
  "by his own power".
- Turretin defends against Judaism that the promised Messiah has come, from fixed prophetic times (XIII.1, vol. 2
  pp. 241–250):
  - the sceptre from Judah (Gen 49:10);
  - Daniel's seventy weeks (Dan 9:24–27);
  - the Messiah coming to the Second Temple (Hag 2; Mal 3).

**Distinction for JE.** The *doctrine* comes from Scripture: incarnation, two natures, atonement, bodily resurrection.

## 4. Question map

- **RV (anchor).**
  - *Main answer:* God has revealed himself in creation, which leaves all without excuse, and specially in a public
    history of word and act culminating in Jesus Christ, recorded in Scripture.
  - *How would we know?*
    1. God's word bears its own marks and the Spirit persuades (ground of certainty);
    2. public signs: prophecy and its fulfilment, miracles before witnesses, eyewitness testimony;
    3. above all, the resurrection, "assurance to all" (Acts 17:31).
  - *Texts:* §3a. *Interpreters:* Calvin I.7–8; Bavinck I §§10, 14; Turretin II.6.
  - *Internal difference:*
    - an evidentialist emphasis (Calvin I.8's catalogue; Turretin's *notae*; modern Craig-type);
    - presuppositional and testimonial emphases (Calvin I.7; Bavinck §14: apologetics presupposes faith; Van Til, REUSE);
    - all agree evidence is real and that the Spirit's testimony is decisive for faith.
  - *Exact caveat:*
    - do not make the argument circular ("Scripture says so") nor purely evidential;
    - say clearly where public evidence is offered (the resurrection; prophecy) and where self-attestation operates
      (canon, inspiration);
    - Belgic 5's "even the blind" see fulfilled prophecy is a confessional claim. Its force depends on the dating of
      prophecies, which critical scholarship contests (Isa 40–55, Dan 9: not researched here). Avoid prophecy overclaims.
  - *Historical evidence involved:* the shared packet (resurrection). Prophecy dating is a gap.
  - *Comparative issue:* the Christian claim is distinctive in resting its authentication on a *public, datable,
    contestable* event within living memory (1 Cor 15:6 "most... still alive"). Compare:
    - Sinai: public, but transmitted, with no external attestation (Judaism packet);
    - *iʿjāz*: literary and inaccessible to non-Arabic readers (Kuzari I.6);
    - authorless śruti: unfalsifiable by event;
    - Dharma: verified in experience, not in history.
- **HI (medium).**
  - *Main answer:* linear, with a goal. Creation → Fall → promise and covenant → Christ at "the fullness of time" →
    church and mission → resurrection, judgement → new creation. Providence directs; the two cities intermingle until
    the end.
  - *Interpreters:* Augustine XII.13, XV.1, XIX.17, XXII.30; Bavinck III §42, IV §§55–56.
  - *Internal difference:* millennial views (amillennial, postmillennial; WLC 191's hope for the gospel's spread). Keep
    secondary. Do not make it dispensational.
  - *Exact caveat:*
    - not historical progressivism: the earthly city persists, and the goal is given, not achieved by culture;
    - Bavinck §55's appeal to 19th-century physics (p. 520) is dated: do not reuse it.
  - *Comparative issue:*
    - against cycles (Hindu, Buddhist): Augustine's "once Christ died... He dies no more";
    - against naturalism: no guaranteed goal vs. providential consummation;
    - shared linearity with Judaism and Islam, but Christ is the centre.
- **JE (anchor).**
  - *Main answer:* Jesus is the eternal Son incarnate, Messiah, crucified for sins, bodily raised, ascended, returning as
    judge, true God and true man in one person.
  - *What follows if the central historical claims are true:* God has acted and spoken decisively; Jesus is vindicated as
    Lord and Christ (Acts 2:36; Rom 1:4); judgement is fixed (Acts 17:31); death is defeated (1 Cor 15:20–26); the claims
    of other revelations that deny the crucifixion or the deity of Christ are falsified at that point.
  - *Interpreters:* Calvin II.12, 16; Turretin XIII.1; Bavinck III §42; WCF 8.
  - *Historical track:* `jesus-evidence.md`. The Christian case should rest on claims 3–10 plus inference to the best
    explanation, openly stating that the inference requires that God's action not be excluded beforehand.
  - *Exact caveat:*
    - Christology from Scripture is doctrine. The historical case establishes facts and the plausibility of the
      resurrection, not Chalcedon.
    - Do not use "the disciples would never die for a lie" or "women's testimony proves the tomb" (`jesus-evidence.md`
      §F).
  - *Comparative issue:* the strongest naturalist objection is Ehrman's "historians cannot establish miracles" plus the
    vision hypothesis. Answer by distinguishing historical method from metaphysical exclusion (SEP Miracles §4.2), not by
    denying the visions literature.

## 6. Historical-accountability notes

- **RV.**
  - The Christian claim invites historical testing:
    - "most of whom are still alive" (1 Cor 15:6);
    - "as you yourselves know" (Acts 2:22);
    - Luke's investigation (Luke 1:3).
  - That is a strength to state, not to overstate: a claim's *openness* to testing is not its *passing* the test.
  - Calvin's I.8.5 argument has the same evidential form as Halevi's and Maimonides' Sinai argument: public events
    before eyewitnesses could not be imposed. It inherits the same objection: our access is through the text.
  - Production should apply one standard to both (`judaism.md` §6).
- **Prophecy.** Belgic 5 and Turretin XIII.1 rely on fulfilled prophecy (Gen 49:10; Dan 9). Critical dating (a Maccabean
  Daniel; a Babylonian or post-exilic Deutero-Isaiah) was **not researched**. Present prophecy as an internal Christian
  argument and a Jewish-Christian exegetical dispute (Saadia VIII.9; Nachmanides), not as public proof.
- **JE.** Everything historical comes from `jesus-evidence.md`. Doctrinal claims beyond §B 11–12 are theology.

## 7. Important internal distinctions

- Self-attestation (ground) vs. evidences (secondary helps) vs. the Spirit's testimony (efficient cause): Calvin I.7–8;
  Turretin II.6 (REUSE); Bavinck I §§14, 18.
- General vs. special revelation (WCF 1.1; Belgic 2). Special revelation's forms (theophany, prophecy, miracle) "fall
  together" in Christ (Bavinck §10).
- Redemptive history (one covenant, many administrations, WCF 7.5–6) vs. world history (providence; the two cities).
- Doctrine of Christ (Scripture, WCF 8) vs. historical argument (sources) vs. apologetic use (Bavinck §14's caution).
- Revelation *completed* in Christ and Scripture (Bavinck I §10, p. 380; WCF 1.1 "former ways... now ceased") vs. the
  ongoing "spiritual miracles" of regeneration (p. 381).

## Cross-domain consistency

- **With UA and KP (K&T).** RV's three levels match K&T's authority structure (WCF 1.4–5, 1.10; Turretin II.6). Do not
  re-argue autopistia here: link to `ultimate-authority`. The new element is the *public historical* level, with which
  K&T dealt only through Calvin I.8.1, 13.
- **With final-end (SD).** HI's new creation and resurrection fit the published Christian final-end (Rom 8:29–30; Rev
  21:1–4).
- **Real tension (internal).** Calvin I.8 and Turretin's *notae* give evidences real probative use. Bavinck §14 says the
  historical-apologetic method "does not lead to the desired result", and Van Til (REUSE) denies neutral evidence.
  Production should present this as a difference of emphasis inside a shared commitment to both evidence and the Spirit,
  not as one view.
