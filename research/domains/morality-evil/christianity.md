# Reformed Christianity — Morality, Evil & the Human Problem

Domain packet. Not public prose. Question codes: **KG** `know-the-good`, **FG** `fail-the-good`,
**EV** `evil`, **SU** `suffering`, **DE** `death`, **SD** `self-deception`. Status: **P3** = passage
established in Phase 3 (reused, not reread unless noted); **NEW** = mined in this domain pass;
**NEW SRC** = source newly registered in this pass.

## 1. Core account

Scripture is the primary and final authority; the confessions and interpreters below are subordinate witnesses. One history of one nature explains the whole domain: created good with the law written on the heart
(Gen 1:26–28; Rom 2:14–15; WCF 4.2; WLC 17), fallen in Adam (Rom 5:12, 18–19; WCF 6), preserved and restrained by providence and common grace,
renewed in Christ. Moral knowledge is real because the image and conscience remain. It is
suppressed and distorted because the will's loves are disordered. Evil is not a substance or a
second principle. It is good created powers turned from God (privation; perversion of the will).
Suffering and death are the penal consequence of sin for the race (WCF 6.6; WLC 27–28, 84), not
proportional to each person's sins. For believers they are transformed into fatherly discipline and
an entrance into life (WCF 5.5; HC 26–28, 42; WLC 85). Self-deception is sin's characteristic mode:
the mind is deceived and the heart suppresses known truth (Rom 1:18–21; Owen). The remedy is
monergistic regeneration and lifelong, imperfect sanctification (Eph 2:1–5; WCF 9.3–5, 10, 13; WLC 149).

## 3. Question map

### KG — Why do we know the good?
- **Main answer.** God's law is written on every heart at creation (WCF 4.2; WLC 17) and survives
  the Fall as conscience and a "light of nature" (Rom 2:14–15; Dort III/IV.4; Calvin II.8.1).
  Moral knowledge is therefore real and universal, but it is partial and sharpest in "second-table"
  civil matters (Calvin II.2.24, P3). It is suppressed and "completely distort[ed]" in use (Dort
  III/IV.4) and leaves people "inexcusable" (WCF 1.1; WLC 96; Belgic 14). Written revelation states
  it clearly (WCF 19.2; WLC 93, 95). God alone is Lord of the conscience (WCF 20.2).
- **Best passages.** Rom 2:14–15; WCF 4.2, 19.1–2, 19.5; WLC 17, 93, 95; Dort III/IV.4; Calvin II.8.1,
  II.2.22.
- **Supporting.** Calvin I.3.1 (sensus); Edwards *True Virtue* ch. 5 (natural conscience as a sense
  of self-inconsistency, P3).
- **Internal distinction.** How far natural law yields usable public ethics (Reformed natural-law
  retrieval vs. Van Tilian caution) is a real modern debate. Both sides affirm that the law is known
  and suppressed. Mention only in a deep dive.
- **Uncertainty.** None material.

### FG — Why do we fail to do the good we know?
- **Main answer.** Original corruption: we are "utterly indisposed, disabled, and made opposite to all
  good" (WCF 6.4; WLC 25). The fallen will is free from compulsion but morally unable to choose
  spiritual good (WCF 9.1, 9.3; Calvin II.3.5; Edwards FW I.4, moral vs. natural inability). Knowing
  wrongdoing comes in two modes: misjudging the particular case, and "rush[ing] into sin knowingly
  and willingly" (Calvin II.2.23). Even the regenerate fall and "daily break" the law (WCF 13.2,
  17.3; WLC 149; HC 114).
- **Best passages.** WCF 6.4, 9.3, 13.2, 17.3; WLC 25, 149; WSC 82; HC 5, 8; Dort III/IV.3; Rom
  7:15–25 (believer only; P3 caution); Calvin II.2.22–23; Conf. VIII.5.10, VIII.9.21; Edwards FW I.4.
- **Internal distinction.** Rom 7 is read of the regenerate (Calvin, later Augustine). Do not use it
  for unbelievers (P3 §12). Edwards' strong compatibilism vs. WCF 9.1's "natural liberty" is debated.
  Use only the natural/moral inability distinction.
- **Uncertainty.** None; derivative of the flagship (concise answer).

### EV — What is evil, and where does it come from?
- **Main answer.** Evil is not a substance. It is the privation or corruption of good (Ench. 11, 13,
  P3; Conf. VII.12.18), and iniquity is "a perversion of the will, bent aside from You" (Conf.
  VII.16.22). Moral evil originates in creaturely defection, which has no efficient cause but a
  "deficient" one (CoG XII.7). Sin is "any want of conformity unto, or transgression of, any law of
  God" (WLC 24; WSC 14). God ordains whatever comes to pass without being "the author of sin"
  (WCF 3.1). His providence reaches even to sin "not by a bare permission", while its sinfulness
  "proceedeth only from the creature" (WCF 5.4). The confessions refuse a full theodicy. Predestination
  is a "high mystery" (WCF 3.8). Belgic 13 declines to "inquire with undue curiosity" into God's
  hidden judgments and rejects the "Epicurean" chance view. Natural evil follows from the curse on
  creation for human sin (WLC 28; Rom 8:19–22; Bavinck § 39.5).
- **Best passages.** WCF 3.1, 3.8, 5.4, 6.1–6; WLC 24, 28; Belgic 13; Conf. VII.12.18, VII.16.22;
  CoG XII.7, XIV.13; Ench. 11, 13.
- **Supporting.** Calvin I.17.8 (God "arms" the wicked yet they act maliciously; Joseph, Gen 50:20);
  Bavinck § 39.5.
- **Internal distinction.** Infra- vs. supralapsarian ordering is not needed. Historical Adam and
  natural evil before the Fall (animal death, deep time) is a live dispute. Bavinck § 39.5 argues
  that nature "degenerated" after the curse, using paleontological claims now dated. Cite Bavinck's
  principle (form, not matter, changed), never his science. Strongest objection: Hume's *Dialogues* X–XI
  (see naturalism packet) plus the decree problem already handled in Phase 3.
- **Uncertainty.** The anchor answer needs a chosen objection. Recommended: the Humean evidential
  form (natural evil's "four circumstances"), since the Phase 3 reply already covers the decree.

### SU — Why do we suffer?
- **Main answer.** All suffering has its cause "in sin in general", not always in personal sin
  (Bavinck § 39.5, citing Luke 13:4, John 9:1; WLC 28; WSC 19). Particular calamities rarely
  disclose their purpose (Bavinck § 39.5; Belgic 13). Under providence nothing comes "by chance but
  by his fatherly hand" (HC 27; Calvin I.17.10–11). For believers, adversity is chastisement, trial
  and humbling (WCF 5.5; Calvin III.8.1–2) and is turned to good (HC 26; Rom 8:28). It frees from
  "the evil of afflictions" (WCF 20.1). Augustine: good and wicked suffer alike, but "the same
  violence of affliction proves, purges, clarifies the good, but damns, ruins, exterminates the
  wicked" (CoG I.8). Response: patience, gratitude, confidence (HC 28; Calvin I.17.7–8).
- **Best passages.** HC 1, 26–28; WCF 5.5, 5.7, 6.6, 20.1; WLC 27–28; Belgic 13; Calvin I.17.7–11,
  III.8.1–2; CoG I.8–9; Job 1:21 (via HC 28 proof-texts); Rom 8:18–28.
- **Supporting.** Bavinck § 39.5: most suffering traceable directly or indirectly to sin. Natural
  disasters are tied to humanity's sin, not individuals'. Weighing the world's pleasure against its
  pain (Schopenhauer) is presumptuous.
- **Internal distinction.** None material at the confessional level.
- **Edition.** Monergism English Volume 3 §39.5 now read and quotable; PDF pp. 208–218.
  It preserves the Dutch first-edition section numbering; do not substitute Bolt/Vriend pagination.

### DE — Why do we die, and why does death seem wrong?
- **Main answer.** Death is "the wages of sin" (WLC 84; Rom 5:12, 6:23; WCF 6.6; WSC 19). It came by
  Adam's sin as a just sentence; humanity was not made liable to death as angels are not (CoG
  XIII.1). Death is evil in itself: "good unto none" and still a punishment even when borne well
  (CoG XIII.6). The good "die well, though death is an evil" (XIII.5). Life is "a race towards death"
  (XIII.10). Bavinck answers "why it seems wrong": being dust does not make death natural. Fear of
  death is innate. The Monergism rendering reads, "We do not believe in the ground that we must die."
  Cite Volume 3 §39.6, PDF p. 220; its awkward wording should remain exact if quoted.
  Science that calls death simply natural claims more than it can justify (§39.6, PDF pp. 220–221);
  Bavinck's biological examples are historical, not current scientific evidence. Believers still die, not as payment but as the
  end of sinning and the entrance to life (HC 42; WLC 85). Souls go to God immediately; the body is
  raised (WCF 32.1–2; WLC 86; WSC 37). Calvin: nature dreads it, faith desires it (III.9.5). Death
  faced in faith is comfort "in life and in death" (HC 1).
- **Best passages.** Gen 2:17, 3:19; Rom 5:12, 6:23; 1 Cor 15:26, 55–56; WCF 6.6, 32.1–2; WLC 84–86;
  WSC 19, 37; HC 1, 42; CoG XIII.1, 4–6, 10, 15; Calvin III.9.4–5; Bavinck § 39.6.
- **Internal distinction.** Whether pre-Fall humans were naturally mortal but kept by grace, or
  constitutionally immortal, is debated (CoG XIII.19 vs. later views). Bavinck holds that death is
  penal *and* connected with materiality. Do not overstate the confession.
- **Uncertainty.** Ecclesiastes-style lament (Eccl 3:19–21) not mined; optional.

### SD — Why can humans know truth and still deceive themselves?
- **Main answer.** People "suppress the truth" they know (Rom 1:18–21; Calvin I.5.4, P3). They
  "love truth when she shines on them, and hate her when she rebukes them"; "willing to be deceived"
  about what they love (Conf. X.23.34). Augustine had placed himself "behind [his] back… unwilling to
  exercise self-scrutiny": "I had known it, but acted as though I knew it not" (Conf. VIII.7.16–18).
  Sin itself is a lie: living "according to himself… he lives according to a lie" (CoG XIV.4).
  Owen: deceit "properly affects the mind". It presents things "otherwise than they are", hides
  consequences, proceeds "by degrees", and is the "head and spring of every sin" (*Indwelling Sin*
  VIII, pp. 211–215; Jer 17:9; Heb 3:13; Jas 1:14–15). Calvin: innate self-love "blinds" and makes
  self-examination impossible (II.1.2, P3; II.8.1). Judicial hardening (WCF 5.6; WLC 28: "strong
  delusions"). God may leave believers to discover "the deceitfulness of their hearts" (WCF 5.5).
- **Best passages.** Rom 1:18–21; Jer 17:9; Conf. VIII.7.16–18, X.23.34; CoG XIV.4; Calvin I.5.4,
  II.1.2, II.8.1; Owen VIII; WCF 5.5–5.6; WLC 28; Dort III/IV.4 ("suppress it in unrighteousness").
- **Internal distinction.** None material.
- **Uncertainty.** None; concise answer.
