# Classical Islam — Morality, Evil & the Human Problem

**NEW** = mined here; **NEW SRC** = newly registered (none in this lane).

## 1. Core account

Every human is born on the *fitra*, a God-given disposition (30:30; Bukhari 1385). Every human also
witnessed God's lordship in the primordial covenant (7:172–173). The soul is inspired with knowledge
of its "rebellion and piety" (91:8). Moral knowledge is therefore natural but needs revelation. The
schools divide sharply over whether reason grounds obligation. Failure comes from created weakness,
forgetfulness, desire taken as a god and Satan's whispering. It is magnified by habit that "encrusts"
the heart (83:14), with no inherited guilt (6:164). Al-Ghazali supplies the psychology (P3). Evil and
good both fall under God's decree and creation ("both come from God", 4:78). The bad that befalls a
person is "[ultimately] from yourself" (4:79). God creates nothing purely evil: evil is relative or
serves a wise purpose (Ibn Taymiyya, P3). Suffering is principally *test* (2:155; 21:35; 29:2; 67:2).
It also comes from one's own deeds (42:30), is written beforehand (57:22), never exceeds capacity
(2:286), and is met with patience and return to God (2:156). Death is created *with* life as the
setting of the test (67:2). It is certain (3:185; 4:78; 62:8), revealing (50:19) and irreversible
(23:99–100), and it leads to resurrection and judgment. Self-deception is a named Qur'anic phenomenon.
People "deceive only themselves… though they do not realize it" (2:9). They corrupt while thinking they
reform (2:11–12). They think they are doing good (18:104). Evil deeds are "made alluring" (35:8), yet
"man is a clear witness against himself, despite all the excuses" (75:14–15). The remedy is
repentance, guidance, purification and mercy (P3).

## 3. Question map

### KG
- **Main answer.** *Fitra* (30:30; Bukhari 1385) and the primordial covenant (7:172–173) give
  humanity an innate orientation and an excuse-removing witness. The soul is inspired with its
  rebellion and piety (91:7–10). Conscience is "the self-reproaching soul" (75:2), and vice is "what
  rankles in your heart" (Muslim 2553a, P3).
- **School distinction (material).** Mu'tazila: good and bad are objective and known by reason, and
  God is bound by them. Ash'ari (incl. al-Ghazali, al-Razi): obligation comes from revelation alone,
  and reason tracks benefit. Maturidi: reason often discerns good and bad, but revelation is final
  (Harvey's wisdom-grounded reading). Ibn Taymiyya: *fitra* carries moral intuitions, and values
  track benefit and harm (SEP §7.1–7.2; Harvey p. 217–218; P3 §7).
- **Best passages.** 30:30; 7:172–173; 91:7–10; 75:2; Bukhari 1385; Muslim 2553a; SEP Phil. Rel.
  §§7.1–7.2; Harvey pp. 217–218.

### FG
- As in Phase 3 (concise): 2:44; 61:2; 12:53; 45:23; 83:14; *Marvels* pp. 118–119 (four stages);
  *Disciplining* pp. 24–26; *Repentance* p. 43 (desire matures before intellect).

### EV
- **Main answer.** All things, good and ill, are from God's decree (4:78). Yet the bad that befalls
  one is "[ultimately] from yourself" (4:79). Humans seek refuge from "the harm in what He has
  created" (113:2). If God punished all evil at once, "He would not leave one living creature"
  (16:61). Humans may dislike what is good for them (2:216). Theology: Ash'ari holds that God wills
  and creates all acts including disobedience, and humans "acquire" them (*Ibana* pp. 51–52, 104).
  Maturidi holds that God creates what humans freely choose, wills evil without approving it, and
  that sin violates God's command, not his will (P3). Ibn Taymiyya holds that God creates nothing
  purely evil. Evil is partial, relative or privative and serves a wise purpose (Hoover pp. 41–44, 201–203, P3). Iblis's pride is the paradigm of moral evil
  (7:12, P3).
- **Internal distinction.** Mu'tazili obligation on God to do the best (*aslah*) vs. its rejection by
  all Sunni schools (P3 §10). Optimism ("best of all possible worlds") in al-Ghazali/Ibn Taymiyya
  (Hoover) vs. Ash'ari voluntarism.

### SU
- **Main answer.**
  - *Test:* "We shall certainly test you with fear and hunger, and loss of property, lives, and
    crops". The steadfast say "We belong to God and to Him we shall return" (2:155–157). "We test you
    all through the bad and the good" (21:35). Believers will be tested to show who is truthful
    (29:2–3).
  - *Consequence:* "Whatever misfortune befalls you… it is because of what your own hands have done
    — God forgives much" (42:30).
  - *Decree and permission:* misfortunes are written beforehand, "so you need not grieve… or gloat"
    (57:22–23). None comes without God's permission, and God guides the believer's heart (64:11).
  - *Measure and consolation:* "God does not burden any soul with more than it can bear" (2:286).
    "Where there is hardship there is also ease" (94:5–6).
  - *Wise purpose:* evils serve purposes and are not evil from every perspective (Hoover, P3).
- **Best passages.** 2:155–157; 21:35; 29:2–3; 42:30; 57:22–23; 64:11; 2:286; 94:5–6; 2:216;
  Hoover pp. 201–203.
- **Gap.** Expiation hadith (Bukhari 5641–5642; Muslim 2999) unread; see above.

### DE
- **Main answer.** God "created death and life to test you… and reveal which of you does best"
  (67:2). "Every soul will taste death and you will be paid in full only on the Day of Resurrection…
  The present world is only an illusory pleasure" (3:185; also 21:35). Death cannot be escaped (4:78;
  62:8). Its "trance… will bring the Truth with it: 'This is what you tried to escape'" (50:19). At
  death the wrongdoer begs to return and make amends, and "a barrier stands behind" them until
  resurrection (23:99–100). Life and death frame a return to God (2:28). Death is not punishment for
  inherited sin. It is the appointed term (16:61) and the passage to judgment.
- **Best passages.** 67:2; 3:185; 21:35; 4:78; 62:8; 50:19; 23:99–100; 2:28; 16:61.
- **Internal distinction.** None material at this level. Barzakh details belong to `after-death`.

### SD
- **Main answer.**
  - *Qur'anic diagnosis:* those who "seek to deceive God… only deceive themselves, though they do
    not realize it", with "a disease in their hearts" (2:9–10). They claim "We are only putting
    things right" while causing corruption (2:11–12). The greatest losers are those who "think they
    are doing good work" (18:103–104). Evil deeds "are made alluring to them so that they think they
    are good" (35:8). Pharaoh's people denied the signs "though their souls acknowledged them" (27:14,
    P3). Desire is taken as a god (45:23, P3). Yet "man is a clear witness against himself, despite
    all the excuses he may put forward" (75:14–15).
  - *Al-Ghazali:* the "demonic" faculty presents "evil as though it were good" (*Marvels* pp. 29–30,
    P3). Rust on the heart's mirror makes one see vice as good in the terminal stage (*Marvels*
    pp. 32–33; *Disciplining* pp. 24–25, P3).
- **Best passages.** 2:9–12; 18:103–104; 35:8; 75:14–15; 27:14; 45:23; 83:14; *Marvels* pp. 29–33.
