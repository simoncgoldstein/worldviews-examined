# Domain dossier: Morality, Evil & the Human Problem

Phase 4, first domain research pass (2026-10-05). This is a **source map**, not public prose. It
equips later question-writing sessions to draft six questions without rediscovering sources:

| Code | Question id | Catalog question | Depth (reuse map) |
|---|---|---|---|
| KG | `know-the-good` | Why do we know or seek the good? | Medium |
| FG | `fail-the-good` | Why do we fail to do the good we know? | Concise |
| EV | `evil` | What is evil, and where does it come from? | Anchor |
| SU | `suffering` | Why do we suffer? | Medium |
| DE | `death` | Why do we die, and why does death seem wrong? | Medium |
| SD | `self-deception` | Why can humans know truth and still deceive themselves? | Concise |

No public answers were drafted. No subagents or external reviewers were used. One Opus session did
all the research.

## How to use

1. Open the worldview packet: `christianity.md`, `naturalism.md`, `judaism.md`, `islam.md`,
   `hinduism.md`, `buddhism.md`. Each has: (1) core account; (2) core texts checked, marked
   P3/NEW/NEW SRC; (3) question map; (4) reusable passage map with exact locators; (5) gaps.
2. Every locator marked **NEW** or **NEW SRC** was read in this pass in the edition named by the
   source record. Every **P3** locator was established and citation-checked in Phase 3 (see
   `research/questions/great-and-terrible/`) and was not reread unless noted.
3. The packets quote short phrases for orientation. Before publishing a quotation, confirm it
   against the registry note's edition rules: some sources are **paraphrase only** (Śāntideva via
   Sanskrit, Bavinck in Dutch, Saadia in Hebrew, Bukhari glosses).
4. Concise questions (FG, SD) should link to `great-and-terrible`, which carries much of their
   argument.

## Method

1. Read the reuse map, methodology, source policy, catalog, the Phase 3 packets, both registries and
   the six flagship answers.
2. For each lane, listed the texts a scholar or serious adherent would expect, compared them with the
   registry and Phase 3 locators, and searched only for named gaps. Every search targeted a source
   ("which section of Dort states the light of nature"), never a topic ("best books on evil").
3. Read only the needed passages, in the registered edition or an accessible text with the same
   canonical locator, per the verification protocol.
4. Stopped each lane once its core texts were checked, each question had support with locators, and
   further reading would add redundancy.

## Important internal distinctions retained

- **Reformed:** Rom 7 applies to believers only. Edwards' compatibilism is not the confessional
  standard. Bavinck's dated natural history is excluded, only his principle is used. Natural-law
  emphasis vs. Van Tilian caution on KG.
- **Naturalism:** realism vs. anti-realism (KG, EV); evil-skepticism vs. revivalism; logical vs.
  evidential problem; Epicurean vs. deprivationist vs. Williams on death; intentionalist vs.
  deflationary, and adaptation vs. spandrel, on self-deception.
- **Judaism:** the plural, unharmonized rabbinic voices on suffering; death penal vs. "very good";
  Saadia's rational commandments vs. Maimonides' revealed grounding (Kings 8:11).
- **Islam:** Mu'tazili, Ash'ari, Maturidi and Ibn Taymiyya on moral knowledge and on God's creation
  of evil; *aslah* rejected by Sunni schools.
- **Hindu:** Śaṅkara (one Self; plural = bodies) vs. Rāmānuja (eternal plural selves) on 2.12, which
  decides what death finally means; Mīmāṃsā's injunction-only dharma; Advaita vs. theistic remedy;
  Madhva's contested soul-types (EV).
- **Buddhist:** early "end suffering" vs. Mahāyāna use of suffering for compassion; Kālāma not a
  free-inquiry charter; Yogācāra's sub-conscious self-misconstrual; Tiantai inherent evil.

## Internal quality pass (single Opus review, 2026-10-05)

- **Core texts before convenience.** Each lane checked its canonical or confessional base first.
  Westminster was read deliberately: nine further WCF chapters, seventeen WLC questions and nine WSC questions. The Qur'an
  now carries Islam's SU, DE and SD. Rabbinic texts lead Judaism. The Gītā, Upaniṣads and Manu lead
  the Hindu lane. The Nikāyas lead Buddhism. For naturalism, primary Hume and Epicurus and a
  scientific article lead, and SEP is used only where primaries are unreadable or the topic is a
  debate map.
- **No single thinker as the whole religion.** Maimonides appears for KG, EV and SU alongside the
  Talmud and Tanakh, never alone. Al-Ghazali leads only FG and part of SD (as in Phase 3), and the
  SU/DE gap is not filled with him. Śaṅkara is always paired with Rāmānuja.
- **Breadth without proliferation.** Hindu: three Vedānta schools plus Mīmāṃsā (KG only) and
  Nyāya/Yoga (SU only). Buddhist: Pali baseline plus Śāntideva. Yogācāra, Madhyamaka and Tiantai
  reuse Phase 3 only where they change an answer.
- **Additions justified.** Fifteen sources, each tied to a named gap. Four are short confessional
  documents the source policy requires to be registered on first use.
- **Corrections made during the pass.** A recalled hadith wording and an unverified Ibn Taymiyya
  phrase were removed from the Islam packet. The Kings 8:11 variant was downgraded to "reported,
  unchecked". Bavinck renderings were marked as the researcher's own. Speaker attribution is flagged
  for Hume's *Dialogues*. Epicurus is flagged as a non-atheist antecedent.
- **Every question has a drafting path.** See the readiness table.
