# Classical Islam — Ultimate Reality

Research packet, 2026-10-06. Codes and status marks as in the README.

**Lane method.** Qur'an → sound hadith → Sunni kalām (al-Ash'ari, al-Maturidi, al-Ghazali) and the Hanbali
traditionalist Ibn Taymiyya → falsafa (Ibn Sina; Ibn Rushd's reply) where it shaped the debate → Sufi metaphysics
(Ibn 'Arabi) only for OM. Avicennian metaphysics is **not** "the Islamic view". It is the major philosophical
alternative that kalām and Ibn Taymiyya answered.

Translations:
- Qur'an: Abdel Haleem (quran.com resource 85).
- Hadith: Khan, via the fawazahmed0 mirror, because sunnah.com returns 403. Arabic checked for wording.
- Ghazali and Ibn Rushd: Bouyges 1930 Arabic. **Our translation** or paraphrase; the OCR is noisy.
- Ibn Sina: Forget 1892 Arabic. Our paraphrase; OCR noisy.

## 1. Core account

What ultimately exists is God, the One, eternal (*al-ṣamad*), unbegotten and incomparable (Q 112). He is "the First
and the Last; the Outer and the Inner" (57:3), self-sufficient while all else stands in need of him (35:15), and "there
is nothing like Him" (42:11). Everything other than God is created: he is "the Creator of all things" (13:16; 6:102),
creating by command, "Be—and it is" (36:82; 2:117). Everything but his Face perishes (28:88; 55:26–27). Two hadiths
report the Prophet's answer about the beginning of creation:
- Bukhari 3191: "God was, and there was nothing other than Him" (*ghayruhu*).
- Bukhari 7418: "nothing before Him" (*qablahu*).
The difference matters later (§4 SR).

Creation is purposeful, not play (44:38–39; 16:3; 3:191). It is ordered "in due measure" (54:49; 25:2), without flaw
(67:3–4), its sun and moon on "calculated courses" with the balance set (55:5–7). God's "practice" does not change
(35:43). All of it is a field of "signs" (2:164; 3:190).

Classical theology divides on how God relates to this world:
- **Kalām (Ash'ari, Maturidi):** the world began after not existing (*ḥudūth*) and points to its originator (SEP AIR
  §2.1; Harvey p. 109).
  - **Ash'aris and al-Ghazali:** occasionalism. The link between "cause" and "effect" is not necessary. God creates
    each in concomitance by his custom (*'āda*) and could do otherwise (*Tahāfut* 17; SEP AIR §3.2.2).
  - **Al-Maturidi:** grants things real "natures" (*ṭabā'i'*) as dispositions, combined with God's power and design
    (Harvey pp. 89–93).
  - **Maturidis:** God's creative action (*takwīn*) is an *eternal attribute* (Harvey p. 175).
- **Ibn Sina:** God is the Necessary Existent through itself. Everything else is possible in itself and necessitated
  by another. The world eternally emanates from him, "from the One only one proceeds" (SEP AIR §§2.2, 3.1.1, 3.2.1;
  *Ishārāt* Namaṭ 4).
- **Ibn Taymiyya:** God is perpetually dynamic. He has always been creating one thing after another, with wise
  purposes, through real instrumental secondary causes (SEP Ibn Taymiyya §§4.4–4.5).

## 4. Question map

- **UR (anchor).**
  - *Main answer:* God alone is uncreated and self-sufficient. Everything else is his creation and wholly dependent.
  - *Texts:* Q 112; 57:3; 35:15; 42:11; 13:16; 28:88; Bukhari 3191.
  - *Interpreters:* Ash'ari *Ibāna* pp. 50–51; Maturidi via Harvey pp. 120–121; Ibn Sina (Necessary Existent, SEP AIR
    §2.2).
  - *Internal difference:* attributes are distinct from the essence (Ash'ari/Maturidi, "neither identical nor other")
    vs identical with it (Mu'tazila; philosophers) (SEP AIR §§6.2.1–6.2.3). Ibn Taymiyya affirms all revealed
    attributes in their plain sense, uniquely befitting God (SEP IT §4.1).
  - *Caveat:* do not present Ibn Sina's necessity–possibility ontology as the creed.
  - *Christian issue:* tawḥīd and the Trinity, where Q 112 and 4:171 are polemical. Both lanes affirm the
    Creator–creature distinction.
- **SR.**
  - *Main answer (kalām, representative Sunni):* the world exists because God willed it into being after its
    non-existence, by an eternal will that decreed its existence at the moment it came to be.
  - *Texts:* 2:117; 36:82; 52:35–36 ("Were they created without any agent?"); Bukhari 3191/7418.
  - *Interpreters:*
    - al-Ghazali, *Tahāfut* Disc. 1 (Bouyges Metaph. I, Alg. §8). Our translation: "With what will you refute one who
      says the world came to be by an eternal will that required its existence at the time in which it came to exist?"
    - SEP AIR §3.1.2.
    - Maturidi's KCA (Harvey p. 109).
    - The philosophers' eternal world is one of three theses al-Ghazali calls irreligious (*Munqidh*, Field pp. 33–34).
  - *Material differences:*
    - **Ibn Sina:** the world is possible in itself, necessitated by and eternally emanating from the Necessary
      Existent. The proof reflects on existence itself, not on the world's beginning (*Ishārāt* Namaṭ 4, final
      *tanbīh*, "the verdict of the truthful", citing Q 41:53; SEP AIR §2.2).
    - **Ibn Taymiyya:** there has always been creating. The world's genus of time is beginningless, each created
      thing originated (SEP IT §4.4; Harvey p. 175 n. 1).
    - Hadith wording: Ibn Taymiyya's position is sensitive to the variant "nothing *before* Him" (7418) vs "nothing
      *other than* Him" (3191).
  - *Exact caveat:* do not say "Islam teaches the kalām cosmological argument". It is a major Sunni argument (Ash'ari
    and Maturidi traditions), not a creed.
  - *Christian issue:* shared doctrine of creation by will. Ibn Taymiyya's beginningless creating vs Turretin V.3.
  - Record: **revelation-dependent** (Qur'an as revelation).
- **UP.**
  - *Main answer:* yes. God is the Ever-Living, All-Hearing, All-Seeing, merciful, who knows, wills and speaks
    (2:255; 42:11; 59:22–24). The creed affirms knowledge, hearing, sight, power and uncreated speech, and Qur'anic
    "face", "hands" and "eyes" *bilā kayfa* (*Ibāna*, Klein pp. 50–51).
  - *Internal difference:* positive attributes real and distinct (Sunni kalām) vs strongly negative/identical
    (philosophers and Mu'tazila).
  - *Caveat:* "personal" in the sense of knowing, willing, speaking and loving, yet utterly incomparable (42:11).
    Avoid anthropomorphic gloss and avoid apophatic overstatement.
  - *Christian issue:* personality without inner plurality; how love is eternal in a unipersonal God (only if argued
    carefully; not a gotcha).
- **OR.**
  - *Main answer:* the world is ordered because God created all things "in due measure" and his practice does not
    change (54:49; 25:2; 35:43; 55:5–7).
  - *Material differences on causality:*
    1. **Ash'ari/al-Ghazali occasionalism:** observed conjunctions are God's habitual creation. "The agent of burning
       is God", directly or through angels. Observation shows co-occurrence "at" fire (*'indahu*), not "by" it
       (*bihi*) (*Tahāfut* 17, Bouyges pp. 517–518; SEP AIR §3.2.2).
    2. **Maturidi:** natures as real dispositions with causal effects, sustained and coordinated by God (Harvey
       pp. 89–93).
    3. **Ibn Taymiyya:** God creates by means of real secondary causes; instrumental from God's side (SEP IT §4.5).
    4. **Ibn Sina and Ibn Rushd:** essential/natural causation. Ibn Rushd: denying observed efficient causes is
       "sophistry" (Bouyges p. 519).
  - *Caveat:* do not present al-Ghazali's occasionalism as all of Islam. Maturidi and Ibn Taymiyya differ materially.
  - *Christian issue:* WCF 5.2's real second causes vs occasionalism. Bavinck II §26.16 (p. 267) criticizes
    "Mohammedan" occasionalism, but that is a Christian polemical witness, not evidence for the Islamic position.
- **OM (concise).**
  - *Main answer:* God is absolutely one. "If there had been… any gods but Him, both heavens and earth would be in
    ruins" (21:22). The many are his creatures, signs of his one wisdom, each glorifying him (17:44). Plurality is
    created, real and dependent.
  - Attributes: the Sunni formula holds that attributes are distinct from the essence without being "other" than God
    (SEP AIR §6.2.2), a plurality-in-unity problem internal to tawḥīd.
  - *Material alternative:* Ibn 'Arabi's school: "there is no wujūd but God"; creatures in themselves are unreal.
    The label *waḥdat al-wujūd* was first pinned on him by Ibn Taymiyya, who condemned it (SEP Ibn 'Arabi §3.1).
    Mulla Sadra's modulation of existence (SEP AIR §3.1.3) is a later Shi'i philosophical version.
  - *Caveat:* present Sufi monism as a contested strand, not the creed. The label is disputed.
  - *Christian issue:* unity of attributes vs Trinity; Avicenna's "from the One only one proceeds".

## 6. Important internal distinctions

- Kalām origination (Ash'ari/Maturidi) vs Avicennian necessitation vs Ibn Taymiyya's perpetual creation.
- Occasionalism (Ash'ari) vs dispositional natures (Maturidi) vs instrumental causes (Ibn Taymiyya) vs natural
  causation (falsafa).
- Attributes: distinct but not other (Sunni kalām); identical (Mu'tazila, philosophers); plain-sense affirmation
  (Ibn Taymiyya).
- Sufi monism (contested) vs creedal Creator–creature distinction.
- Authority: no single living mechanism adjudicates kalām disputes. Ash'ari and Maturidi are both "orthodox" Sunni
  schools; Ibn Taymiyya contests both. Record for `ultimate-authority`.
