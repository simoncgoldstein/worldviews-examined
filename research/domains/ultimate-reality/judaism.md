# Rabbinic Judaism — Ultimate Reality

Research packet, 2026-10-06. Codes and status marks as in the README.

**Lane method.** Tanakh → rabbinic material (Mishnah, Midrash, Rashi) → major philosophical interpreters (Saadia,
Halevi, Maimonides, Nachmanides) → a later mystical strand only where it materially changes the answer (Ḥabad).
Biblical and rabbinic claims are kept distinct from philosophical systematization.

Translations:
- Saadia: Hebrew (Ibn Tibbon, Leipzig 1864). Our paraphrase or our translation only.
- Tanya: Hebrew. Our paraphrase only.
- Guide: Friedländer (published translation).
- Mishneh Torah: Touger.

## 1. Core account

What ultimately exists is the one God and the world he created. The Tanakh's opening is creation by God's word:
"God said… and there was light" (Gen 1:3). God alone is God: "there is none else" (Deut 4:35, 39), and he forms light
and darkness alike (Isa 45:7). The Shema (Deut 6:4) is the touchstone of unity.

- **The rabbis:**
  - God did not find pre-existing materials. Rabban Gamliel answers a philosopher that "creation is written" of each
    supposed ingredient (Bereshit Rabbah 1:9).
  - The world was made by God looking into the Torah as an architect's plan (BR 1:1).
  - The world was made "with ten utterances" (Avot 5:1).
  - Speculation on "what came before, what after" is restricted (m. Ḥagigah 2:1).
- **The philosophers systematize this:**
  - Saadia argues the world was created from nothing (four proofs) and that God's life, power and wisdom imply no
    plurality in him (*Emunot* I.1–2; II.4).
  - Maimonides makes "a Primary Being who brought into being all existence" the "foundation of all foundations". All
    else "requires Him" and he requires none (Yesodei ha-Torah 1:1–3). God's unity surpasses every unity in the world
    (1:7). God, his knowledge and his life are one (2:10). Positive description of God's essence fails; only negations
    and attributes of action are true (*Guide* I.51, 54, 58).
  - Creation from nothing (with time created) is the view of "the Law of Moses". The eternity of the world has not
    been demonstrated, so the text is taken literally (*Guide* II.13, II.25).
  - Nachmanides: creation of all things "from absolute non-existence" is "the root of faith". God first brought forth
    a formless primary matter (*hyle*, *tohu*) from which all else was formed (on Gen 1:1).

Personal language is central to the Bible and liturgy. Its philosophical status is contested:
- **Halevi:** the God of Abraham, known by address, command and covenant, is not the God of Aristotle (*Kuzari* IV.3,
  IV.16).
- **Maimonides:** we know *that* God is, not *what* he is (*Guide* I.58).

## 4. Question map

- **UR (anchor).**
  - *Main answer:* one incomparable God, who alone exists necessarily, and a created world that exists only "from the
    truth of His being".
  - *Texts:* Deut 4:35, 39; 6:4; Isa 45:18; YT 1:1–4; BR 1:9.
  - *Interpreters:* Maimonides YT 1:1–7; Saadia I–II; *Guide* I.58.
  - *Internal difference:* Maimonides' philosophical framing (necessary existent; negative theology) vs Halevi's
    covenantal, revelation-first God of Abraham.
  - *Caveat:* do not make the *Mishneh Torah*'s philosophical first chapter "the Jewish view" without noting that it
    systematizes biblical and rabbinic monotheism. Do not use the sphere cosmology (YT 1:5) as the argument.
  - *Christian issue:* Creator–creature distinction shared. The difference is the Trinity: Maimonides' unity "not one
    in the manner of a general category" (YT 1:7) excludes plurality in God.
- **SR.**
  - *Main answer:* because God willed it. The world was created from nothing, time included. Rabbinic tradition
    rejects pre-existing materials, and philosophers argue the point.
  - *Texts:* Gen 1:1 (with Rashi's syntax); Ps 148:5; BR 1:9; Avot 5:1.
  - *Interpreters:*
    - **Saadia I.1:** four proofs (from finitude, composition, accidents, time). The fourth is "our translation"
      territory, so paraphrase it: if past time were infinite, coming-to-be could never have traversed it to reach us.
    - **Saadia I.2:** things cannot make themselves; creation from something implies a co-eternal matter and an
      infinite regress.
    - ***Guide* II.13, II.25:** Mosaic creation from nothing with time created; Aristotle's eternity not demonstrated
      and would abolish miracles; Plato's version would be compatible but is not proven.
    - **Ramban on Gen 1:1:** from absolute nothing, a first *hyle*.
    - ***Kuzari* I.67:** the arguments are "evenly balanced"; prophetic tradition tips the scale; even an eternal
      matter would not destroy the faith that *this* world was created.
  - *Internal differences:*
    - Rashi's grammatical reading of Gen 1:1, "at the beginning of God's creating". JPS: "When God began to create".
    - Maimonides' openness in principle to a Platonic eternity.
    - Halevi's concession about eternal matter.
  - *Exact caveat:* creation *ex nihilo* is the dominant rabbinic-philosophical position. It is not secured by Gen 1:1
    grammar alone, so cite BR 1:9, Saadia and Ramban, not just the verse.
  - *Christian issue:* largely shared. Contrast on whether creation is known by demonstration (Saadia) or tradition
    (Halevi).
  - Record: **revelation-dependent** (prophetic tradition, Kuzari I.67).
- **UP.**
  - *Main answer:* yes in biblical, rabbinic and liturgical terms. God speaks, commands, loves and judges; God is one
    who "knows" (YT 2:10).
  - Philosophically, Maimonides holds that essential attributes cannot be positive. They are known as negations or as
    attributes of action (*Guide* I.51, I.54 on Moses: "His attributes… were nothing but His actions", I.58). God,
    knower and knowledge are one (YT 2:10). Saadia: life, power and wisdom are grasped in one insight and imply no
    plurality; language forces three words (II.4).
  - Halevi: the philosophers' God "neither benefits nor injures, and knows nothing of our prayers". The God who
    addresses, commands and rewards is the God of Israel (*Kuzari* IV.3, IV.16).
  - *Internal difference:* Maimonidean apophaticism vs Halevi's personal-covenantal emphasis. This is material.
  - *Caveat:* do not say Maimonides denies God is personal. He denies that positive predicates describe God's
    *essence*, while affirming God knows and wills (*Guide* II.13 "by His will and desire"; YT 2:10).
  - *Christian issue:* the Trinity as the ground of divine personality; Maimonides' negative theology vs Reformed
    analogical knowledge.
- **OR.**
  - *Main answer:* the world is ordered because God made it by wisdom, with fixed "laws of heaven and earth" as his
    covenant (Jer 33:25; 31:35–36; Ps 148:6; 104:24). The Torah was the blueprint (BR 1:1).
  - *Interpreters:*
    - **Maimonides:** God the efficient, formal and final cause (*Guide* I.69). Against Aristotle's eternal "fixed
      laws" that would exclude miracle (II.25). Individual providence over humans; Aristotle's general providence for
      the rest of sublunary nature; the Ash'ari view (every leaf by decree) rejected (III.17).
    - **Ramban:** "hidden miracles". Events affecting Israel and individuals under the covenant are not simply
      natural (on Exod 13:16).
  - *Internal difference:* **Maimonides vs Nachmanides** on how far natural order is autonomous. This is material.
  - *Caveat:* Maimonides' III.17 is his own view, explicitly "not… demonstrative proof".
  - *Christian issue:* Reformed universal special providence (Calvin I.16) vs Maimonides' limited individual
    providence.
- **OM (concise).**
  - *Main answer:* God is one with a unity unlike any worldly unity (YT 1:7; *Guide* I.51). Multiplicity belongs to
    creation, which Maimonides treats as one ordered individual like a human body of many parts (*Guide* I.72).
  - *Internal difference:* in Ḥabad Ḥasidism (Tanya, *Sha'ar ha-Yiḥud* 3), every creature, continually brought from
    nothing by God's word, is "truly reckoned as nothing" relative to that creative power. The appearance of separate
    existence is due to our not seeing the divine vitality: acosmic in emphasis, while still affirming creation.
  - *Caveat:* there is no single rabbinic "one-and-many" doctrine. Present Maimonides' unity and created plurality as
    representative, with Ḥabad as a material variant. Kabbalistic *sefirot* are not researched.
  - *Christian issue:* unity without inner distinction vs triune unity. Whether created plurality has its exemplar in
    God.

## 6. Important internal distinctions

- Maimonides vs Halevi (philosophical vs covenantal-historical knowledge of God; personality).
- Maimonides vs Nachmanides (natural order vs hidden miracle).
- Rabbinic restraint (Ḥagigah 2:1) vs philosophical demonstration (Saadia).
- Ḥabad acosmism vs Maimonidean created plurality (OM only).
- Authority: no single body adjudicates these metaphysical disputes. Maimonides' principles are widely
  received but contested (Halevi, Crescas). Record for `ultimate-authority`.
