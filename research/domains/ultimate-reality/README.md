## How to use

1. Open the lane packet: `christianity.md`, `naturalism.md`, `judaism.md`, `islam.md`, `hinduism.md`,
   `buddhism.md`. Each has seven sections:
   1. core account;
   2. expected-major-source check;
   3. core texts checked (REUSE / NEW / NEW SRC, with "already have", "core works needing passages", "important
      missing core source");
   4. question map (answer, texts, interpreters, internal difference, exact caveat, Christian comparative issue);
   5. reusable passage map;
   6. important internal distinctions;
   7. remaining gaps.

   The Christian packet adds the Reformed Scripture audit (§3a).
2. Status marks:
   - **P3**: citation-checked in the flagship.
   - **ME/SD/MH**: established in the earlier domain dossiers.
   - **NEW**: read in this pass in the registered edition, or in an accessible text with the same canonical locator.
   - **NEW SRC**: a work registered in this pass.
3. Translation discipline:
   - **Our translation / paraphrase only:**
     - Turretin (Latin 1847);
     - Saadia and Tanya (Hebrew);
     - al-Ghazali and Ibn Rushd (Bouyges Arabic, noisy OCR);
     - Ibn Sina (Forget Arabic, noisy OCR);
     - MMK and AKBh (Sanskrit);
     - the literal Pāli of Ud 8.3.
   - **Published translations:** ESV, JPS, Kulp, Sefaria Midrash Rabbah, Friedländer, Touger, Chavel, Hirschfeld,
     Abdel Haleem, Khan, Klein, Field, Müller, Thibaut, Subbarau, Sastri, Govindacharya, Griffith, Cowell,
     Vidyabhusana, Ñāṇamoli, Sujato, New Advent/Dods, Beveridge.
   - **Automated translation:** Bavinck Monergism. Compare consequential wording with the Dutch.
   - Recheck any wording before public quotation. Ledger ESV quotations.

## Method

1. Read:
   - the reuse map, `METHODOLOGY.md`, `SOURCES.md` and the question catalog;
   - the flagship packets' "metaphysical context" sections;
   - the three completed domain dossiers;
   - both registries.
2. For each lane, listed what was already checked, which checked works needed new passages, and which major core
   texts were missing. Then searched only for named needs, e.g. "public-domain Arabic edition of al-Ghazali's *Tahāfut*
   causation section" → Bouyges's 1930 *Tahāfut al-tahāfut*, which prints al-Ghazali's text.
3. Applied **core texts → major interpreters → formal synthesis → specialists** in every lane. For the Reformed lane:
   **Scripture → Augustine, Calvin, Turretin, Bavinck → Westminster/Three Forms**.
4. Read only the needed passages and recorded exact locators. Compared commentators on identical loci (BS I.1.2,
   II.1.14, II.1.33; Gītā 9.4–5; Gen 1:1) so that school differences are textual, not artifacts.
5. Stopped each lane when:
   - foundational texts and major interpreters were checked;
   - competing schools that change the answer were represented;
   - further reading would add witnesses rather than change the account.

## Important internal distinctions retained

- **Reformed:**
  - possibility of an eternal world (Turretin vs Aquinas, interlocutor);
  - creation known by revelation (Bavinck §29.2) vs demonstrable (Turretin III.1);
  - immediacy of providence (Calvin I.16) vs stable second causes (Bavinck §35.5), both held by WCF 5.2–3;
  - Trinity doctrine vs Bavinck's argument vs Van Til's apologetic use.
- **Naturalism:**
  - physicalism vs broader naturalism (neutral monism; panpsychism; strong emergence);
  - brute fact vs necessary universe vs eternal regress;
  - Humean vs non-Humean vs antirealist laws;
  - realism vs fictionalism about mathematics;
  - Hume's speakers (Cleanthes vs Philo).
- **Judaism:**
  - Maimonides vs Halevi (personality; demonstration vs tradition);
  - Maimonides vs Nachmanides (natural order vs hidden miracle);
  - rabbinic restraint (Ḥagigah 2:1);
  - Rashi's grammatical reading of Gen 1:1;
  - Ḥabad acosmism.
- **Islam:**
  - kalām origination vs Avicennian necessitation vs Ibn Taymiyya's perpetual creation;
  - occasionalism vs Maturidi natures vs instrumental causes vs falsafa;
  - attributes (distinct-not-other vs identical vs plain-sense);
  - Sufi monism contested;
  - hadith variants 3191/7418.
- **Hindu:**
  - Advaita (conventional plurality; *saguṇa*/*nirguṇa*) vs Viśiṣṭādvaita (world as body) vs Dvaita (real difference);
  - Vedānta (Śruti only) vs Nyāya (inference);
  - beginningless saṃsāra;
  - *ṛta*/*dharma* not "laws of physics".
- **Buddhist:**
  - Theravāda *dhamma* realism vs Madhyamaka emptiness vs Yogācāra;
  - two vs three truths;
  - devotion vs creator;
  - rejection of both creator-origin and causeless origin.

## Historical and revelatory dependencies (for Revelation & History)

These were recorded, not tested:
- **Reformed:** creation and the Trinity are known decisively from Scripture (Heb 11:3; Bavinck §29.2).
- **Judaism:** creation is weighted by prophetic tradition (*Kuzari* I.67).
- **Islam:** Qur'anic revelation and the hadith of creation's beginning.
- **Hindu Vedānta:** Brahman is known from Śruti only (Rāmānuja I.1.3). Nyāya and Udayana claim inference as well.
- **Buddhist:** the Buddha's awakening "discloses" conditionality (SN 12.20).

None of the lanes has a single binding mechanism that settles its internal metaphysical disputes:
- Ash'ari, Maturidi and Ibn Taymiyya are all "Sunni";
- the three Vedānta schools all claim the same Śruti;
- Maimonides is contested by Halevi and Crescas;
- Buddhist schools dispute what is *nītārtha*;
- naturalism has no canon.
