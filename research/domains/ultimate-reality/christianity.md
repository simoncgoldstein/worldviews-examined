## 1. Core account

What ultimately exists is the triune God and, distinct from him, everything he has made. God alone is self-existent
("I am who I am", Exod 3:14; "from everlasting to everlasting you are God", Ps 90:2). He has life in himself (John
5:26), needs nothing (Acts 17:24–25) and owns everything (Ps 50:10–12). Everything else exists because he called it
into being by his word and will: from nothing, not from himself and not from a pre-existing material (Gen 1:1;
Ps 33:6–9; Rom 4:17; Heb 11:3; Rev 4:11). Creation is therefore not a part, emanation or necessary expression of God.
It is a free, dependent, good reality with its own creaturely being, held in existence and governed at every moment
(Col 1:16–17; Heb 1:3; Acts 17:28).

The Creator–creature distinction is the axis of the lane:
- God is not one item inside a reality larger than himself.
- The world is not a fragment of God.

The ultimate is fully personal. God knows, wills, loves, speaks and acts. And he is personal *as Trinity*: Father,
Son and Spirit, one God in three persons who are really distinct and not divided (Matt 28:19; John 1:1–3; 17:5, 24;
2 Cor 13:14). This is not a philosophical system with prooftexts added. Augustine, Calvin, Turretin and Bavinck each
build these claims from the texts. Bavinck in particular argues that the Trinity is what lets theology hold together
three things:
- God's independence from the world (against pantheism);
- God's living connection with the world (against deism);
- the unity and diversity in creation, which images, weakly, the unity-in-diversity of God.

**Audit verdict.** Scripture establishes every core claim in all five cells: self-existence, creation by word and will,
personal knowledge and love, the Trinity, ordered providence, and created manifoldness. The theologians carry argument
and defense in every cell: Augustine 5/5, Calvin 4/5, Turretin 4/5, Bavinck 5/5. The confessions are summaries. No
claim depends on a confession or theologian for something Scripture does not first say. One argued *inference* is not
itself a biblical statement and must be presented as theological reasoning: Bavinck's claim that only the Trinity
grounds unity-in-diversity.

## 4. Question map

- **UR (anchor).**
  - *Main answer:* the triune God, self-existent, simple, infinite, eternal and spiritual; plus a really existing,
    wholly dependent creation distinct from him.
  - *Best texts:* Exod 3:14; Ps 90:2; John 5:26; Acts 17:24–28; Rev 4:11.
  - *Interpreters:* Bavinck II §26.1; Turretin III.7.4; Calvin I.13.1; Augustine *Conf.* XI.4.6.
  - *Internal difference:* the doctrine of simplicity is confessionally Reformed (WCF 2.1 "without body, parts, or
    passions"; Belgic 1 "simple"). Some recent Reformed writers question strong simplicity. That is not material for a
    public answer that follows the confessional standard; mention only if pressed.
  - *Caveat:* do not present "God" as one more substance in a larger category of being. Bavinck and Turretin both make
    God "the alone fountain of all being" (WCF 2.2).
  - *Comparative issue:* the Creator–creature distinction against naturalism's single order, Advaita's nondual
    Brahman, Viśiṣṭādvaita's world-as-body, and Buddhist conditionality.
- **SR.**
  - *Main answer:* because God freely willed that there be a world. God himself exists of himself and has no reason
    outside himself. Creation is from nothing, with time, by his word, not from necessity or need.
  - *Texts:* Gen 1:1; Rom 4:17; Heb 11:3; Rev 4:11.
  - *Interpreters:* Augustine *Conf.* XI–XII, CoG XI.6, XI.24; Turretin V.1, V.3, III.14.5–6; Bavinck §29.3, 29.5, 29.9.
  - *Internal difference:* whether reason alone can show the world is not eternal (Turretin V.3 says yes, against
    Aquinas). Bavinck II §29.2 holds that creation "is known only from revelation", by faith (Heb 11:3). These are
    compatible (Bavinck still offers rational considerations) but the emphasis differs.
  - *Caveat:* **revelation-dependent** (record for Revelation & History). The Reformed lane does not rest SR on the
    kalām argument alone.
  - *Comparative issue:* the brute-fact and necessary-universe alternatives (naturalism); beginningless saṃsāra
    (Hindu/Buddhist); Avicennian necessary emanation (Islam).
- **UP.**
  - *Main answer:* yes, literally and supremely personal. God knows, wills, loves and speaks, and is tri-personal in
    himself. Bodily and passional language is accommodated (Calvin I.13.1). Personhood itself is not a projection.
  - *Texts:* Ps 33; Isa 40; Eph 1:11; 1 John 4:8; John 17:24.
  - *Interpreters:* Calvin I.13.1–2, 6, 17; Augustine CoG XI.10; Bavinck §27.25.
  - *Internal difference:* none material.
  - *Caveat:* "person" in the Trinity (*subsistentia*, Calvin I.13.6) is not three centers of separate essence.
    Keep "God is personal" and "God is three persons" distinct in prose.
  - *Comparative issue:* Maimonides' negative attributes; Advaita's *saguṇa*/*nirguṇa* distinction; Buddhist rejection
    of a creator; naturalism's impersonal ultimate.
- **OR.**
  - *Main answer:* reality is orderly because one wise God created it by wisdom and upholds and governs every creature
    through real second causes. "Laws of nature" are created ordinances (Jer 33:25), regular because God is faithful,
    not because nature is self-sufficient.
  - *Texts:* Gen 8:22; Jer 31:35–36; 33:25; Ps 104; Col 1:17; Heb 1:3.
  - *Interpreters:* Augustine CoG V.11; Calvin I.16.1–3; Turretin VI.1; Bavinck III §35.5, II §29.12.
  - *Internal difference:* Calvin I.16.2–3 stresses God's immediate special direction (every year "regulated by a new
    and special providence"). Bavinck §35.5 stresses fixed natural order and second causes. WCF 5.2–3 holds both.
  - *Caveat:* do not make the Reformed position occasionalist. WCF 5.2 and Turretin VI.1.6 retain second causes.
  - *Comparative issue:* Hume's regularity; Ash'ari custom (*'āda*); karma/ṛta; dependent origination's "law of
    nature" (SN 12.20).
- **OM (concise).**
  - *Main answer:* unity and plurality are equally ultimate in God: one essence, three persons, neither prior to the
    other. Created unity-in-diversity is a finite analogy of that, grounded in one wise plan and preserved in God's
    providence.
  - *Texts:* Deut 6:4 with Matt 28:19; 1 Cor 8:6; Rom 11:36; Ps 104:24.
  - *Interpreters:* Bavinck §27.25, §29.12; Calvin I.13.17; Augustine CoG XI.10.
  - *Internal difference:* Van Til's apologetic *use* of this as a transcendental argument against non-Christian
    philosophy is a twentieth-century development. It cannot be lawfully sourced in this pass (§7).
  - *Exact caveat:* public prose may present the doctrine (Trinity; creation; created unity-in-diversity) and
    Bavinck's argument that only the Trinity holds connection and distinction together. It must **not** attribute a
    Van Tilian transcendental argument without a checked Van Til text. "The Trinity solves the one-and-many" should be
    stated as Bavinck's claim, at Bavinck's strength ("weak analogy"), not as a demonstration.
  - *Comparative issue:* Advaita's sublation of plurality; Viśiṣṭādvaita's body–soul unity; Madhyamaka's emptiness of
    both one and many; Maimonidean absolute unity; naturalist mereology and emergence.

## 6. Important internal distinctions

- **Possible eternity of the world:** Turretin V.3 (impossible) vs Aquinas (an interlocutor, not this lane). Present
  the Reformed answer as Turretin's: actually and possibly non-eternal. Disclose that Christian theologians have
  differed on the *possibility*.
- **Creation known by revelation vs demonstrable:** Bavinck §29.2 vs Turretin III.1. Keep both: Scripture first,
  arguments second.
- **Immediacy of providence:** Calvin's "special providence" vs Bavinck's emphasis on stable natural order. Both
  confessionally held (WCF 5.2–3).
- **Simplicity:** confessional, but its strength is debated among some modern Reformed writers. Not material here.
