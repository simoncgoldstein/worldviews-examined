# Naturalistic atheism — Ultimate Reality

Research packet, 2026-10-06. Codes as in the README (UR, SR, UP, OR, OM). Status marks as in the Christian packet.
Naturalism has no canon. The account is built from founding modern texts (Hume, Russell), one contemporary naturalist
primary on the existence question (Sean Carroll), and SEP debate maps chosen *by problem area*:
- physicalism;
- laws of nature;
- emergence;
- fine-tuning;
- the cosmological argument;
- nothingness.

SEP entries are used as maps of positions held by naturalists. They are not a naturalist confession.

## 1. Core account

Ontological naturalism holds that reality is exhausted by nature: the spatiotemporal, causal order that the sciences
study. There is no God, no supernatural agent, and no cosmic purpose behind it (SEP Naturalism §1.1). Within that
commitment there is real variety:
- Most contemporary naturalists are **physicalists**: everything spatiotemporal is identical to, or constituted,
  realized or grounded by, the physical (SEP Physicalism §§1–2; Naturalism §1.1). Some are reductive; most are
  non-reductive about minds, organisms and social facts.
- Some hold **weak emergence**: higher-level kinds are real yet wholly physically determined.
- A minority entertain **strong emergence** of genuinely novel powers (SEP Emergent Properties §§3–4).
- **Russell's** "neutral" naturalism is an older alternative. Mind and matter are both "logical fictions" built out of
  events, and "everything interesting is a matter of organization, not of primal substance". His point is
  explicitly "not materialism" (*What I Believe* ch. 1).

On **why there is anything**, naturalists do not say "something came from nothing." The main options are:
1. **Brute fact.** The universe, or its most basic laws and state, simply exists without further explanation. Hume's
   character Cleanthes (D 9.5–9.9) rejects a priori necessary existence. Russell's 1948 reply was that the universe
   is "just there, and that's all" (SEP Cosmological Argument §5.2). Carroll concludes that every answer "bottoms out
   in a set of brute facts."
2. **The physical universe as itself necessary, or self-sufficient.** "Why may not the material universe be the
   necessarily-existent Being?" (D 9.7).
3. **Rejecting the question's presuppositions:**
   - the causal or sufficient-reason principle is not known a priori (Hume, *Enquiry* IV; SEP Cosmological Argument
     §5.4);
   - explaining each member of a chain explains the whole (D 9.9);
   - an eternal succession needs no first author (D 9.8).

**Order** is understood as regularity:
- **Humeans** (Mill–Ramsey–Lewis "best system") treat laws as the simplest strong summaries of what actually
  happens; there is no necessary connection we can observe (*Enquiry* VII).
- **Non-Humeans** (Armstrong, Dretske, Tooley; dispositional essentialists) posit necessitation relations among
  universals, or powers.
- A minority are **antirealists** about laws (SEP Laws of Nature §§2–5, 8).
- **Fine-tuning** is answered by:
  - accepting it as a primitive coincidence;
  - expecting new physics;
  - anthropic selection, or a multiverse.
  (SEP Fine-Tuning §§2.2–2.3, 3.2, 4.)
- **Biological order** is explained by natural selection rather than design. Hume's Philo already sketches a
  self-sustaining "œconomy" of matter (D 8.6).

## 4. Question map

- **UR (anchor).**
  - *Main answer:* only nature exists. Most naturalists are physicalists, holding that everything spatiotemporal is
    physical or grounded in the physical. Minds, life and societies are real but derivative organizations of it.
  - *Texts:* SEP Naturalism §1.1; SEP Physicalism §§1–2; Russell WIB ch. 1.
  - *Internal differences:*
    - reductive vs non-reductive physicalism;
    - weak vs strong emergence;
    - Russellian neutral monism ("not materialism").
  - *Abstracta pressure:* numbers and propositions do not obviously reduce to physical arrangements. Naturalists
    choose among:
    - realism justified by indispensability (Putnam);
    - fictionalism (Field);
    - supervenience physicalism, for which necessary abstracta "trivially supervene";
    (SEP Naturalism §1.8; Physicalism §5.5.)
  - *Caveat:* do not equate naturalism with nineteenth-century atomist materialism. Bavinck II §29.3 attacks that
    target, and it is no longer representative.
  - *Christian issue:* whether "nature" is a self-sufficient totality, and the status of logic, mathematics and
    modality (link Knowledge & Truth).
- **SR.**
  - *Main answer:* there may be no deeper reason. Reality, or its fundamental laws and state, is a brute fact, or
    possibly necessary in itself. Naturalists do not claim the universe arose from absolute nothing by itself.
  - *Texts:*
    - Carroll §5: "Brute fact… a feature of reality that has no further explanation." On a creator, "we are left to
      explain the existence of a creator… there is no reason not to include all of reality in that category."
    - Hume D 9.5–9.9.
    - SEP Cosmological Argument §§5.2, 5.4.
  - *Internal differences:*
    - **brute contingent vs necessary universe.** Carroll denies necessary beings; Cleanthes's D 9.7 contemplates a
      necessary material universe.
    - **beginning vs eternal universe.** Carroll: physics allows either, and the Borde–Guth–Vilenkin result does not
      show a beginning.
    - **rejecting the PSR vs keeping a restricted PSR.**
  - *Exact caveats:*
    - Speakers in Hume. D 9.5–9.9 is **Cleanthes**, the design theist, attacking *a priori* proofs. It is not Philo's
      voice and not simply "Hume says."
    - Carroll is a physicist offering a philosophical argument, not a consensus of physics.
    - SEP Nothingness §3 notes the "explanatory trap": any explanation of why there is something presupposes
      something.
  - *Christian issue:* whether brute contingency is a stable stopping point, given the sufficient-reason reasoning
    science otherwise uses; whether a necessary *material* universe is coherent; Bavinck's claim that "science
    presupposes being".
- **UP.**
  - *Main answer:* no. The ultimate (fundamental physics, its laws and initial state) is impersonal. Mind is a late,
    local product of natural processes, or a pattern of organization (Russell WIB ch. 1; "Free Man's Worship" ¶5).
  - *Texts:* Russell WIB ch. 1 (God "outside the region of even probable knowledge"); Carroll §5 (creator as an
    unnecessary additional brute fact).
  - *Internal difference:* panpsychist and strong-emergentist naturalists make mentality more basic. SEP Physicalism
    §4.4 flags the panpsychism problem for the physicalist conception of "the physical."
  - *Caveat:* there is no single canonical argument *against* a personal ultimate. Naturalists generally rely on
    parsimony and the absence of evidence (Russell; Carroll's "judged on ordinary empirical grounds"). This is a
    narrow caveat: the answer is ready, but present it as a burden-of-evidence stance, not a proof.
  - *Christian issue:* explaining persons, reasons and value from an impersonal base (links to `what-is-man`,
    `know-the-good`).
- **OR.**
  - *Main answer:* nature is regular; "laws" are either the best systematic summary of that regularity (Humean) or
    real necessitating relations or powers (non-Humean). Either way the regularity is not designed.
  - *Texts:*
    - Hume E 4.12 and 7.26 ("conjoined, but never connected");
    - SEP Laws of Nature §§2–3, 5, 8;
    - Hume D 8.6 (Philo: self-maintaining order);
    - SEP Fine-Tuning §§2.2, 3.2, 4.1;
    - Carroll §5 (warns against inferring a "law-enforcement agency" from regularity).
  - *Internal difference:* Humean vs non-Humean vs antirealist about laws. This matters, because Humeans deny any
    *explanation* of regularity beyond the regularity itself.
  - *Caveat:* do not force "laws govern nature" onto Humeans. For them laws describe.
  - *Christian issue:* the uniformity presupposed by induction (link `induction`); fine-tuning responses.
- **OM (concise).**
  - *Main answer:* there is no single naturalist doctrine. Unity is the unity of one causally closed spatiotemporal
    order and, for many, of one fundamental physics. Plurality is real at many levels of organization. Higher-level
    unities are weakly emergent or conventional (mereological) wholes.
  - *Texts:* SEP Physicalism §§2, 3.1; Emergent Properties §§3–4; Hume D 9.9 (the "whole" as "an arbitrary act of the
    mind"); Russell WIB ch. 1 (organization).
  - *Caveat:* this question is not native to naturalism's own agenda. Answer briefly, as an application of
    physicalism and emergence. Avoid inventing a "naturalist position on the one and the many."
  - *Christian issue:* whether unity-of-nature and lawlike universality are themselves explained or simply assumed.

## 6. Important internal distinctions

- **Physicalism vs broader naturalism** (neutral monism; panpsychist naturalism; strong emergence).
- **Brute fact vs necessary universe vs eternal regress.**
- **Humean vs non-Humean vs antirealist laws.**
- **Realist vs fictionalist about mathematics.** This is where naturalism faces internal pressure.
- **Methodological vs ontological naturalism** (SEP §2; MH/P3). Do not argue from the success of method alone.
