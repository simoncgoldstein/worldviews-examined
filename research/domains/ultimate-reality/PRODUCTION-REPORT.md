## Editorial method for this domain

This domain was written to a new public-writing hierarchy: **core truth → natural explanation → important nuance → technical Deep Dive**.
- Every thesis states the elementary answer without thinker names or bibliography.
- Every opening paragraph explains the belief before any interpreter appears.
- School differences, objections and Christian evaluation come after the core answer.
- Technical metaphysics (aseity, simplicity, necessary existence, *svabhāva*, occasionalism, Humean supervenience) and thinker-specific arguments are pushed into the Deep Dive.

Thinker names appear in visible prose only where attribution does explanatory work: a disputed school, a distinctive argument, a classic formulation.

## Domain consistency review (internal, Opus)

All 30 answers were reread together with `great-and-terrible`, `what-is-man`, `worship` and `final-end` in view. No new research occurred in this pass.

1. **Creator–creature distinction (Reformed).** Consistent across all five answers:
   - God from himself, with creation real but dependent (UR);
   - free creation from nothing, and God explained differently (SR);
   - personal *in himself*, with creatures given their own being (UP);
   - order through real second causes, not occasionalism (OR);
   - created unity-in-diversity as only a weak analogy (OM).
2. **Naturalism is a family, not a creed.**
   - Physicalism is the majority view, not the definition. Russell is non-materialist.
   - On existence: brute fact, necessary universe, or rejecting the question.
   - On laws: Humean, non-Humean and antirealist.
   - The personal question is framed as an evidence stance.
   - OM is marked as not native to naturalism.
   - No answer says naturalists believe "something came from nothing".
3. **Jewish strands.**
   - Each answer opens with biblical and rabbinic material.
   - Maimonides is never the opening definition.
   - Halevi is the counterweight in UR and UP.
   - Nachmanides is the counterweight in SR and OR.
   - Ḥabad is a disclosed variant.
4. **Islamic strands.** Kalām, falsafa and Ibn Taymiyya are kept distinct throughout:
   - attributes (UR, UP, OM);
   - timelines (SR);
   - causation (OR).
   - Ibn Sina is "not the creed".
   - Ibn 'Arabi is contested.
5. **Hindu schools.** Advaita, Viśiṣṭādvaita and Dvaita are named and held consistently in all five answers.
   - Advaita is never "illusion": Śaṅkara's own "as true as long as…" is quoted.
   - *Ṛta* is not "laws of physics".
   - Udayana is kept distinct from Vedānta's scripture-only knowledge.
6. **Buddhist guardrails.**
   - Emptiness is called "not another, deeper thing" (UR) and "not a hidden One" (OM).
   - Nirvāṇa is "not a creator or a ground", "neither person nor impersonal god" (UP).
   - Brahmā is mistaken, not the source.
   - Buddha-nature and Pure Land are not made a creator.
7. **One-and-many overclaim.**
   - The Reformed reply explicitly disclaims demonstration.
   - Naturalism, Judaism and Islam open by saying they have no formal doctrine.
   - No lane says its view "solves" the problem.
8. **SR framing.**
   - The Hindu and Buddhist theses open by rejecting the "from nothing" premise.
   - The naturalist thesis opens by rejecting the caricature.
   - The Islamic thesis names three timelines.
9. **Repetition.** A mechanical check found two repeated 12-plus-word sentences (Jewish OM/UR), which were reworded. A manual reread found topic-level duplication between Deep Dives, which was resolved by keeping each item in one place and linking from the other:
   - the Brahmā story (SR/UP);
