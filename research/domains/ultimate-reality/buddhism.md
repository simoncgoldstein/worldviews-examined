# Buddhist traditions — Ultimate Reality

Research packet, 2026-10-06. Codes and status marks as in the README.

**Lane method.** Canonical Pāli baseline (Sujato translations, SuttaCentral; Pāli root checked where wording is
sensitive) → Buddhaghosa (Theravāda systematization) → Nāgārjuna (Madhyamaka) and Vasubandhu (Abhidharma critique of a
creator; Yogācāra already P3) → East Asian Tiantai only for OM.

Guardrails:
- The question "what ultimately exists?" is not answered by naming a substance.
- Emptiness is not a substance.
- Nirvāṇa is not a creator.
- "Ultimate truth" (*paramārtha*) is not a Western absolute.

Translations:
- MMK and AKBh: Sanskrit GRETIL e-texts. Renderings below are **our translation** or paraphrase.
- Udāna: Pāli checked against Sujato.

## 1. Core account

The Buddha's teaching replaces a first cause with conditionality:
- "Whether Realized Ones arise or not, this law of nature persists, this regularity of natural principles…
  specific conditionality" (SN 12.20). That is dependent origination: each link is "impermanent, conditioned,
  dependently originated".
- Right view avoids "the duality of existence and non-existence". "'All exists' is one extreme; 'all does not exist'
  is the second extreme", and the Realized One teaches by the middle through conditionality (SN 12.15).
- Saṃsāra "has no known beginning. No first point is found" (SN 15.1).
- Speculation about the world is one of four "unthinkables" (AN 4.77).
- The questions whether the cosmos is eternal or not are left "undeclared" as not conducive to the goal (MN 63).

Creator belief is analysed, not adopted:
- A being first reborn in an empty Brahmā-mansion wrongly thinks "these beings were created by me", and others
  agree (DN 1:2.5).
- The tenet that everything one experiences is due to "God Almighty's creation" is refuted because it removes
  responsibility (AN 3.61).
- "Anomalous origination", arising without cause, is also rejected (DN 1:2.30–2.31).

The systematizers develop this differently:
- **Buddhaghosa:** "a being" is "a mere term of common usage"; in the ultimate sense there is no chariot, only
  mentality-materiality (Vism XVIII.25–28). Conditioned *dhammas* are what ultimately occur. He also refers to the
  unconditioned nibbāna, which is not a creator.
- **Vasubandhu:** argues systematically that the world has no single cause such as Īśvara. Beings' own karma
  produces them (AKBh II.64).
- **Nāgārjuna:** nothing arises from itself, from another, from both, or without cause (MMK 1.1). Dependent arising
  *is* emptiness, a dependent designation, the middle way (24.18). There is no non-empty thing (24.19). The Buddhas
  teach by two truths, conventional and ultimate, and the ultimate is not taught without relying on the conventional
  (24.8–10). Emptiness is the relinquishing of all views, and those for whom emptiness becomes a view are incurable
  (13.8).

## 4. Question map

- **UR (anchor).**
  - *Main answer:* there is no single ultimate substance or being. What there is: conditioned, impermanent phenomena
    arising in dependence on conditions according to a constant "law of nature" (SN 12.20), and the unconditioned,
    nirvāṇa, as their cessation (Ud 8.3).
  - *Material school differences:*
    - **Theravāda** (Buddhaghosa): conventional persons and things over ultimately real momentary *dhammas* (Vism
      XVIII.25–28).
    - **Madhyamaka** (Nāgārjuna): even *dhammas* lack own-being (MMK 15.1–2; 24.19). "Ultimate truth" is that
      emptiness, not another thing.
    - **Yogācāra**: appearance-only (Tvk 17, P3).
  - *Exact caveats:*
    - The singular "ultimate reality" fits poorly. Answer with the two truths.
    - Do not render *paramārtha* as "the Absolute".
    - Do not treat emptiness as a stuff. MMK 13.8 forbids holding emptiness as a view.
  - *Christian issue:* the Creator–creature distinction vs conditionality with no ground; whether an unconditioned
    cessation can be the "ultimate" without being a source.
- **SR.**
  - *Main answer:* the question is set aside or reframed.
    - Saṃsāra has no discernible beginning (SN 15.1; Vism XVII.36–37).
    - Speculation about the world is unthinkable (AN 4.77), its eternity undeclared (MN 63).
    - Both a creator-origin (DN 1:2.5; AN 3.61) and a causeless origin (DN 1:2.30–2.31) are rejected.
    - Beings and worlds arise from conditions, especially karma: "Therefore there is no single cause of the world;
      their own karma produces beings in each birth" (AKBh II.64 bhāṣya, our translation).
  - *Vasubandhu's argument* (Pradhan pp. 101–102, paraphrase):
    1. A single cause would produce everything at once, but things arise in succession.
    2. If succession is due to God's wishes, those wishes would need further causes. Then God is not the sole cause,
       and the beginningless series of causes concedes the Buddhist view.
    3. God would gain nothing from creating, unless delight, and then he is not lord over it.
    4. Creating beings tormented in hells for his pleasure invites the ironic "homage to such a lord!"
    5. A first creation by God would itself have to be beginningless.
  - *Caveat:* Buddhism does not say something came from nothing. It denies both a first beginning and causelessness.
  - *Christian issue:* the regress Vasubandhu presses vs the Reformed claim of a free, eternal, simple will
    (Turretin III.14).
- **UP.**
  - *Main answer:* no personal creator or personal ultimate.
    - Brahmā, the "Great Divinity", is a long-lived but mistaken being in saṃsāra (DN 1:2.5–2.6).
    - The unconditioned (Ud 8.3) is "unborn, unbecome, unmade, unconditioned" (our literal rendering of *ajātaṃ
      abhūtaṃ akataṃ asaṅkhataṃ*). Sujato renders it "freedom from rebirth, from what has been produced, made, and
      conditioned", deliberately non-substantive.
    - Buddhas are persons who awaken. They are not the ground of the world.
  - *Internal difference:* Mahāyāna Buddha-nature (RGV) and Pure Land devotion to Amida give a personal and
    salvific *face* to awakening (P3/SD). They do not make a creator.
  - *Exact caveat:* do not turn nirvāṇa or *dharmakāya* into God. Do not deny that some traditions relate devotionally
    to Buddhas in strongly personal terms.
  - *Christian issue:* personhood as ultimate vs personhood as conventional designation (Vism XVIII.28).
- **OR.**
  - *Main answer:* reality is orderly because conditionality is constant. "Whether Realized Ones arise or not, this law
    of nature persists" (SN 12.20). Karma is part of that order. Nobody legislates it; the Buddha discovers and
    teaches it.
  - *Texts:* SN 12.20; SN 12.1 (P3); AKBh II.64 (karma, not God); MMK 24.18 (dependent arising is what makes things
    workable).
  - *Caveat:* SN 12.20's "law of nature… regularity… surety" renders *dhammaṭṭhitatā*, *dhammaniyāmatā*,
    *idappaccayatā*. Do not import "laws of physics" connotations.
  - *Christian issue:* order without an orderer; whether conditionality's constancy itself needs explanation.
- **OM (concise).**
  - *Main answer:* neither unity nor plurality is ultimate. The extremes "all exists"/"all does not exist" are both
    rejected (SN 12.15). For Madhyamaka:
    - no own-being for either one or many (MMK 15.1–2; 24.19);
    - no difference "even subtle" between the limit of nirvāṇa and the limit of saṃsāra (MMK 25.19–20, our
      translation). This is about both being empty, not a monism.
  - Theravāda: the "one" person is a conventional designation over many *dhammas* (Vism XVIII.28).
  - Tiantai: every determinate thing is constituted by its relation to everything else. The "three truths" (empty,
    provisional, the middle) make each thing "neither same nor different" from the whole (SEP Tiantai §2.1).
  - *Caveat:* Huayan is not researched. Present Tiantai only as an East Asian example, not as "the Buddhist view".
  - *Christian issue:* created, real plurality and real unity in God vs both being empty designations.

## 6. Important internal distinctions

- Theravāda *dhamma* realism vs Madhyamaka universal emptiness vs Yogācāra appearance-only.
- Two truths (Madhyamaka) vs three truths (Tiantai).
- Personal devotional Buddhas (Pure Land; Buddha-nature) vs no personal ultimate. Devotion is not creation.
- Authority: no single adjudicator across schools. Each claims the Buddha's intent (*nītārtha*/*neyārtha*). Record
  for `ultimate-authority`.
