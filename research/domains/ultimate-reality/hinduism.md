# Hindu traditions — Ultimate Reality

Research packet, 2026-10-06. Codes and status marks as in the README.

**Lane method.** Foundational texts (Ṛgveda creation hymns; Upaniṣads; Gītā; Brahma Sūtra) → the three classical
Vedānta commentators on the *same* sūtras and verses (Śaṅkara, Rāmānuja, Madhva) → Nyāya (Udayana) where theistic
argument and causal realism matter. Commentators are compared on identical loci, so differences are not artifacts of
different texts.

Translations:
- Müller, Thibaut, Subbarau, Sastri, Govindacharya and Cowell: published (public-domain) translations. Translators'
  bracketed glosses and notes are theirs.
- Griffith (Ṛgveda): published translation.

## 1. Core account

The Upaniṣads answer "what ultimately exists?" with Brahman:
- In the beginning there was only Being, "one only, without a second". Being cannot come from non-being; Being willed
  "may I be many" (Chāndogya VI.2.1–3).
- Brahman is "that from whence these beings are born, that by which, when born, they live, that into which they
  enter at their death" (Taittirīya III.1).
- Brahman is the "inner ruler" of all (Bṛhadāraṇyaka III.7) and is described finally by "No, no" (*neti neti*,
  BAU II.3.6; III.9.26).

The Brahma Sūtra's second aphorism makes this the definition: Brahman is that "from which the origin, &c. of this
(world proceed)" (BS I.1.2). All three schools agree on this much:
- ultimate reality is Brahman, not exhausted by the empirical world;
- the world's origin, subsistence and dissolution depend on it;
- saṃsāra is beginningless and governed by karma.

They disagree sharply on two things:
- whether Brahman's personal, qualified character is ultimate;
- whether plurality is ultimately real.

**Advaita (Śaṅkara).** Brahman alone is ultimately real. Effects are "non-different" from their cause, and the
manifold is "a name merely which has its origin in speech", the clay alone being true (BSBh II.1.14, glossing ChU
VI.1.4). The scriptural doctrine of creation "does not refer to the highest reality; it refers to the apparent world
only" (II.1.33). Yet the world is *not* dismissed as nothing. "The entire complex of phenomenal existence is considered
as true as long as the knowledge of Brahman being the Self of all has not arisen", and ordinary knowledge and action
proceed "undisturbed" until then (II.1.14). The omniscient, omnipotent Lord (Īśvara) is the world's cause at the
empirical level (I.1.2).

**Viśiṣṭādvaita (Rāmānuja).** Brahman is the one personal Lord whose *body* is all sentient and non-sentient beings.
Brahman is "in its causal or in its 'effected' condition according as it has for its body intelligent and
non-intelligent beings either in their subtle or their gross state". So the effect is non-different from the cause
while souls and matter remain real (Śrī Bhāṣya I.1.1, SBE 48 p. 140). His inner rule is what "establishes an order for
existences" (Gītā 9.4–6 comm.).

**Dvaita (Madhva).** Viṣṇu alone is independent (*svatantra*). Souls and matter are really and eternally different
from him and dependent. Scripture "declares the difference" between the Lord and the soul (Madhva BSBh I.1.17), and
"the Lord who is not all is still called all (meaning He rules all)".

**Nyāya (Udayana).** An everlasting omniscient Being is *inferred* from the world's being an effect, from the
combination of atoms, from the world's support, and other grounds (*Kusumāñjali* V.1). Rāmānuja denies that such an
inference succeeds: Brahman is known from Scripture only (Śrī Bhāṣya I.1.3).

## 4. Question map

- **UR (anchor).**
  - *Main answer:* Brahman, the one source of the world's origin, subsistence and dissolution (Taitt III.1; BS I.1.2;
    ChU VI.2.1).
  - *Material school split:*
    - **Advaita:** Brahman alone is ultimately real; the manifold is conventionally real (BSBh II.1.14).
    - **Viśiṣṭādvaita:** Brahman qualified by a real body of souls and matter (Śrī Bhāṣya I.1.1 p. 140).
    - **Dvaita:** an independent Lord and really distinct dependent reals (Madhva BSBh I.1.17; IEP Madhva).
  - *Exact caveat:* do not say "everything is illusion" for Advaita. Śaṅkara affirms empirical validity until
    liberating knowledge, and the dream analogy is *about* that provisional validity (II.1.14).
  - *Christian issue:* is the world God's body (Rāmānuja) or appearance (Śaṅkara) vs a creature distinct from God
    (Reformed)?
- **SR.**
  - *Main answer:* the Vedāntic answer is not "because God chose" in the Abrahamic sense.
    - Being is primordial and cannot come from non-being (ChU VI.2.2).
    - The world issues from Brahman beginninglessly in cycles.
    - The Lord's activity is "mere sport" (*līlā*), not need: Śaṅkara BSBh II.1.33 pp. 356–357; Rāmānuja II.1.33
      p. 477. Madhva: from the Lord's bliss; "creative activity according to design is the essential nature of the
      Lord" (II.1.34, p. 103).
    - For Advaita, creation-talk belongs to the apparent world (II.1.33).
  - Ṛgveda X.129 already treats the question as near-unanswerable: "Then was not non-existent nor existent… he
    verily knows it, or perhaps he knows not" (Griffith pp. 367–368).
  - Nyāya: "Entity does not arise from non-entity" (NS IV.1.14). The world, being an effect, requires an intelligent
    maker (*Kusumāñjali* V.1).
  - *Exact caveat:* most Hindu schools do not frame a once-for-all beginning from nothing. Saṃsāra is beginningless
    (P3; BSBh II.1.34–36). The Western question is answered by dependence, not by a first moment.
  - *Christian issue:* *ex nihilo* and finite past vs beginningless cyclic dependence; *līlā* vs creation for God's
    glory.
  - Record: **revelation-dependent**. Vedānta knows Brahman from Śruti only (Rāmānuja I.1.3).
- **UP.**
  - *Main answer:* the Upaniṣads speak of Brahman both personally ("he wished, may I be many", Taitt II.6; the inner
    ruler, BAU III.7; the great Lord, Śvet IV.10) and apophatically (*neti neti*, BAU II.3.6).
    - **Advaita:** the personal Lord (*saguṇa*) is real at the empirical level. The highest Brahman is beyond
      qualities (SEP Śaṅkara §2.3, P3; BSBh II.1.33).
    - **Rāmānuja** and **Madhva:** the supreme reality *is* the personal Lord, Nārāyaṇa or Viṣṇu, with infinite
      auspicious qualities (Śrī Bhāṣya I.1.2; I.2.12, P3; Madhva BSBh I.1.17).
  - *Caveat:* the formula supported by the research is that the major Vedānta schools agree the ultimate is not
    exhausted by the empirical world, but disagree sharply about Brahman's personality and the ultimate reality of
    plurality. The research supports this precisely.
  - *Christian issue:* personal supreme Lord vs triune personal God; whether personal descriptions are finally
    transcended (Advaita).
- **OR.**
  - *Main answer:* reality is ordered by Brahman's rule and by cosmic law.
    - **Vedic *ṛta*:** "eternal Law and Truth were born" from kindled fervour (RV X.190).
    - **Upaniṣadic command:** "By the command of that Akṣara… sun and moon stand apart" (BAU III.8.9); the inner
      ruler (III.7).
    - **Karma** as the moral-causal order: the Lord dispenses according to beings' deeds (BSBh II.1.34; Madhva
      II.1.35).
  - **Śaṅkara BS II.2.1:** a non-intelligent first cause (Sāṃkhya *pradhāna*) cannot account for the world's orderly
    arrangement (pp. 363–364).
  - **Rāmānuja Gītā 9.5–6:** the Lord's will "establishes an order for existences", the air held "in the propless
    vault of space" by his power (pp. 291–292).
  - **Nyāya** (*Kusumāñjali* V.1): combination of atoms and the world's support require a volitional agent.
  - *Internal difference:* Vedānta (order known from Śruti) vs Nyāya (order proves God by inference). Rāmānuja I.1.3
    explicitly rejects the inference.
  - *Caveat:* do not translate *ṛta*/*dharma* simply as "laws of nature". They are cosmic-moral-ritual order.
  - *Christian issue:* ordered by a personal will (Rāmānuja, Madhva) vs by karma operating under a dispenser who
    "has regard" to merit.
- **OM (concise).**
  - *Main answer:* this is the central Vedāntic question, and the schools differ:
    - **Advaita:** one without a second; plurality is name and form, real only conventionally (ChU VI.1.4; BSBh
      II.1.14).
    - **Viśiṣṭādvaita:** one Brahman with a real plural body, the relation of soul and body (Śrī Bhāṣya I.1.1 p. 140;
      Gītā 9.4 comm.).
    - **Dvaita:** irreducible difference between the Lord, souls and matter, and among souls. The Lord is "called
      all" because he rules all (Madhva I.1.17). The five differences are per IEP Madhva.
  - Śvet VI.12: the one who "makes the one seed manifold".
  - *Caveat:* the answer cannot be given as one view. State the shared Upaniṣadic texts, then the three resolutions.
  - *Christian issue:* Trinity and creation vs acosmism (Advaita), panentheism (Rāmānuja) and pluralist dependence
    (Madhva).

## 6. Important internal distinctions

- Three Vedānta answers to plurality and personality (above). This is the decisive distinction.
- Vedānta (Śruti-only knowledge of Brahman) vs Nyāya (inferential theism).
- *Līlā* (Śaṅkara, Rāmānuja) vs bliss/design as nature (Madhva). These are minor wording differences around one
  shared denial of divine need.
- Authority: no single adjudicating body. Each school claims the same Śruti. Record for `ultimate-authority`.
