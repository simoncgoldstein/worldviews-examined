# Naturalism — Man & Human Nature

Research packet, 2026-10-06. Codes: WM = `what-is-man`; WA = `why-alive`; OF = `offspring-family`;
LB = `love-beauty-creativity`; WO = `worship`. Status marks as in `christianity.md` (P3, ME/SD, NEW, NEW SRC).

**Method.** Naturalism has no canon and none is invented here. The lane is organized as **founding primary texts**
(Hume, Darwin) → **representative later proponents** (Russell, Railton, Dennett, AHA) → **current debate maps** (SEP)
where primary texts are unreadable or the topic is itself a debate. Three things are kept apart throughout:
(1) descriptive evolutionary explanation; (2) philosophical naturalism (no God, soul substance or cosmic purpose);
(3) moral or existential humanism. Evolutionary origin is never treated as entailing "therefore meaningless."

## 1. Core account

A human being is a living organism, an evolved social primate wholly part of nature, whose distinctive capacities—
language, reasoning, conscience, cumulative culture—arose by natural processes (P3: Russell ch. 1; Darwin; Tennie;
SEP Naturalism). The self is a real, functionally important construct rather than an immaterial soul (Dennett, P3);
theories of personal persistence differ (SD: SEP Personal Identity). There is no given cosmic end: man is "the
product of causes which had no prevision of the end they were achieving" (Russell, *Free Man's Worship* ¶4). Purpose
is therefore either constructed by valuing agents (subjectivism: Russell 1925 "we create value") or found in
objective natural goods such as morality, enquiry and creativity (objectivist naturalism), with nihilism a minority
view (SEP Meaning of Life §§3–4).

Sex, offspring and family are explained descriptively by sexual selection, kin selection and long-dependent
offspring: Darwin lists sexual love and maternal love among shared instincts and counts parental and filial
affections among the social instincts that ground conscience; Hume makes the appetite between the sexes and the
shared concern for offspring "the first and original principle of human society." Love is a compound of beauty,
bodily appetite and benevolence (Hume T 2.2.11), and genuine psychological altruism can evolve (SEP Biological
Altruism §5). Beauty is a pleasure-producing order discerned by taste (Hume T 2.1.8), with a real though
empirically grounded standard of taste (Hume ST); Darwin roots the sense of beauty and music partly in sexual
selection. Religion is a natural phenomenon: belief in invisible agents arises from imagination, wonder, curiosity,
dreams, and hopes and fears about life's events (Darwin ch. III; Hume NHR), and contemporary cognitive science of
religion treats it as a byproduct or adaptation of ordinary cognition (SEP Religion and Science §1.4). Humanists may
redirect reverence to human ideals—Russell's "worship at the shrine that his own hands have built" (¶20)—rather than
to power.

## 3. Question map

- **WM.** *Main answer:* an evolved, social, partly altruistic animal; a mind realized in a body; a narrative self.
  *Texts:* Russell ch. 1; Darwin ch. IV; Hume T 2.3.3.4; Dennett pp. 48–49 (all P3). *Distinction:* reductive versus
  nonreductive naturalism (SEP Naturalism §1, P3); psychological versus animalist persistence (SD). *Caveat:* do not
  assume physicalism is entailed by atheism (SOURCES.md).
- **WA.** *Main answer:* no given cosmic purpose; meaning is either conferred by agents' cares (subjectivism) or
  realized in objectively valuable natural goods—morality, enquiry, creativity (objectivism); some atheists argue God
  would diminish meaning. *Texts:* Russell, *Free Man's Worship* ¶4 (no prevision), ¶¶13, 19 (temple of ideals; the
  light of love in daily tasks); *What I Believe* chs. 1–2 (we create value; good life inspired by love, guided by
  knowledge); AHA III (SD); SEP Meaning of Life §§3.1–3.3, 4. *Distinction:* Russell 1903 (Platonist ideals worth
  "worship") differs from Russell 1925 (value conferred by desire). *Caveat:* nihilism is a minority view; do not
  infer it from evolution.
- **OF.** *Main answer:* reproduction and family are explained by sexual selection, kin selection and the long
  dependence of human young; parental love is a real instinct, not a disguise for selfishness; normative family
  ethics is left to humanist argument. *Texts:* Darwin ch. III p. 66, ch. IV p. 98, ch. XX pp. 590–591; Hume T
  3.2.2.4; SEP Biological Altruism §§2, 5. *Distinction:* evolutionary function ≠ moral obligation (SEP Morality and
  Biology §1.1, P3). *Caveat:* Darwin's racial ranking language (P3 disclosure) recurs in ch. III; contemporary
  naturalists reject it.
- **LB.** *Main answer:* love compounds appetite, beauty and benevolence; beauty is order fitted to please, judged by
  a standard grounded in the joint verdict of practiced, unprejudiced critics; music and ornament have roots in
  sexual selection and the general pleasure of regular pattern; creativity rides on cumulative culture. *Texts:* Hume
  T 2.2.11.1–4, 2.1.8.2; ST 7–9, 12, 23; Darwin ch. III pp. 92–93, ch. XIX p. 572; Tennie (P3); SEP Meaning of Life
  §3.2 (creativity among objective goods). *Distinction:* adaptive explanation of the *capacity* versus the value or
  truth of its objects; Hume's taste is not mere preference (ST 8 rejects equality of all tastes). *Caveat:*
  contemporary evolutionary aesthetics not represented by a checked primary.
- **WO.** *Main answer:* religion arises naturally from cognition and passion (imagination, agency-detection, hope
  and fear); it is not a primary instinct like love of progeny (Hume) and has no truth-entailing implication from
  its naturalness either way; humanists may redirect reverence to ideals. *Texts:* Hume NHR 0.1, 2.4–2.5; Darwin ch.
  III pp. 93–96; SEP Religion and Science §1.4; Russell ¶¶6–9, 13, 20. *Distinction:* naturalistic genealogy of
  religion ≠ refutation of theism (CSR is neutral on truth; SEP §1.4 notes cultures combine natural and supernatural
  explanations). *Caveat:* **Hume's NHR professes theism** ("the whole frame of nature bespeaks an intelligent author,"
  N 0.1; again N 15.1); its sincerity is disputed. Present NHR as the founding natural history of popular religion,
  not as an atheist manifesto. Darwin calls himself agnostic (P3 profile).

## 5. Important distinctions

- **Explanation vs evaluation** in every cell: an evolutionary or psychological origin of love, beauty, family or
  religion does not settle their value or truth (SEP Morality and Biology §1.1, P3; SEP Religion and Science §1.4).
- **Subjectivist vs objectivist naturalist meaning; anti-theism** (SEP Meaning of Life §3).
- **Early vs later Russell** on the status of ideals (1903 vs 1925).
- **Hume's stated theism in NHR** versus its use as naturalistic genealogy.
- **Byproduct vs adaptation** accounts of religion (SEP §1.4).
