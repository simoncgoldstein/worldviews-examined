# Hindu traditions — Man & Human Nature

Research packet, 2026-10-06. Codes: WM, WA, OF, LB, WO as in `christianity.md`. Status: P3, ME/SD, NEW, NEW SRC.

**Method.** Upaniṣads and Gītā → the three Vedānta commentators (Śaṅkara, Rāmānuja, Madhva) → dharmaśāstra
(*Manusmṛti*) for household and life-stage norms. Other schools only where they change an answer. The broad
account comes first; school differences are added only where they alter the answer.

## 1. Core account

The human being is an embodied self (*ātman/jīva*) in a body-mind of the three *guṇas*, passing through births under
karma; the human birth is rare and is the occasion for pursuing life's aims and liberation (P3: Gītā 13.21, 15.7;
Śaṅkara on 9.33; Aitareya). What the self finally is divides the schools: for Advaita, pure consciousness identical
with Brahman; for Rāmānuja, a real eternal knower, a mode of the Lord; for Madhva, a real, dependent, innately
distinct soul (P3). Life's legitimate aims are *dharma*, *artha* and *kāma* taken together—"the aggregate of the
three" (Manu II.224)—ordered beyond themselves to *mokṣa* (SD). Action done for the world's maintenance
(*lokasaṃgraha*) without attachment is itself a path (Gītā 3.19–21).

Family is embedded in dharma and the life stages (*āśrama*): the householder is "the most excellent order" because
all other orders live from his support (Manu III.77–78); parents are images of Prajāpati and the earth (II.226–227);
"do not cut off the line of children" and "let thy mother be to thee like unto a god" (Taittirīya I.11); the three
debts—to the sages by study, to the ancestors by sons, to the gods by sacrifice—precede the pursuit of liberation
(Manu VI.35–37), after which the householder may go to the forest when he sees "the sons of his sons" (VI.1–2). Yet
renunciant texts say the sages "did not wish for offspring" (Bṛhadāraṇyaka IV.4.22), and Yājñavalkya teaches that a
husband, wife or son is dear "for the love of the Self" (BAU II.4.5; IV.5.6). Love is above all *bhakti*: the Lord
receives a leaf, flower, fruit or water offered with love (Gītā 9.26), and in Rāmānuja the Lord himself elects his
lover and generates love in him (Rāmānuja on 8.14, 9.26). Beauty and glory are sparks of the Lord's splendour (Gītā
10.41); Brahman is "flavour" (*rasa*) and bliss (Taittirīya II.7). Worship (*upāsanā*, *pūjā*) ranges from
offering everything to the Lord (9.27) to contemplation of the Imperishable; Śaṅkara ranks devotees of the personal
Lord as excellent yogins while the knowers of the Unmanifest are "one with the Lord," on a harder path (12.2–5).

## 3. Question map

- **WM.** *Main:* an eternal self embodied in a guṇa-made body-mind, transmigrating under karma; school difference
  over whether individuality is ultimate. *Texts:* Gītā 2.12–22; 13.21; 15.7; Aitareya (P3/ME). *Thinkers:*
  Śaṅkara, Rāmānuja, Madhva (P3). *Caveat:* caste ranking in the classical record (P3 disclosure).
- **WA.** *Main:* the human birth is the opportunity to pursue the aims of life—dharma, artha, kāma—and ultimately
  liberation; duty done without attachment for the world's welfare. *Texts:* Manu II.224; Gītā 3.19–21; 9.27–28;
  Śaṅkara on 9.33 (P3). *Distinction:* Advaita (liberation as knowledge; action preparatory) vs theistic Vedānta
  (eternal service and love of the Lord as the soul's nature); Mīmāṃsā's dharma-centred ethic (P3/ME).
- **OF.** *Main:* household life is a sacred stage and duty—debts to ancestors through offspring, support of all
  other orders, honor of parents—but sits within a hierarchy that culminates in renunciation. *Texts:* Taittirīya
  I.11; Manu II.225–228, III.77–78, VI.1–2, 35–37, IX.96; BAU IV.4.22. *Distinctions:* householder (gṛhastha) vs
  renouncer (saṃnyāsa); dharmaśāstra's authority varies among Hindus. *Caveats (must be disclosed):* Manu V.147–148
  (a woman "must never be independent"); IX.94 (marriage ages incl. child brides); III.55–59 (women to be honoured)
  pulls the other way. Modern Hindu reform rejects much of this; do not present Manu as all Hindu teaching.
- **LB.** *Main:* love supremely as bhakti, the soul's love for the personal Lord, reciprocated and even initiated by
  him; human loves are dear for the Self's sake (Yājñavalkya); beauty and glory manifest the Lord's splendour;
  Brahman as rasa and bliss. *Texts:* Gītā 9.26; 10.41; BAU II.4.5; Taittirīya II.7. (Gītā 12.13–20, the "dear devotee" verses, was not
  read in this pass; read before citing.)
  *Thinkers:* Rāmānuja on 8.14, 9.26; Śaṅkara on 7.11 (desire restricted to bare sustenance). *Distinction:*
  Advaita's love as recognition of the one Self vs Rāmānuja's love between distinct soul and Lord. *Caveat:*
  aesthetics/rasa theory not represented (see §2).
- **WO.** *Main:* worship is natural and diverse—offerings with love, all acts as offering, meditation on the Lord or
  the Imperishable; Śaṅkara's commentary before 9.26 contrasts the limited fruit of worshipping
  other deities with the endless fruit of worshipping the Lord (read in context; 9.23–25 verse text itself not
  separately mined—read before citing). *Texts:* Gītā 9.26–27; 12.1–5. *Thinkers:* Śaṅkara on 12.3–5 (upāsanā
  defined; Akṣara-knowers one with the Lord); Rāmānuja proem and 9.26. *Distinction:* Advaita's two-level view
  (worship of Īśvara valid conventionally) vs theistic schools (worship as the soul's eternal relation). *Caveat:*
  temple image-worship (arcā) and Pāñcarātra ritual unresearched; describe generically.

## 5. Important distinctions

- Advaita vs Viśiṣṭādvaita vs Dvaita on the self and on love/worship (one Self vs eternal loving relation).
- Householder vs renouncer; dharmaśāstra norms vs Upaniṣadic renunciation.
- Śaṅkara's ascetic reading of desire (7.11) vs the householder's legitimate kāma (Manu II.224).
