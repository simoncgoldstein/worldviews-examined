# Rabbinic Judaism — Man & Human Nature

Research packet, 2026-10-06. Codes: WM, WA, OF, LB, WO as in `christianity.md`. Status: P3, ME/SD, NEW, NEW SRC.

**Method.** Tanakh → Mishnah, Talmud and Midrash → major thinkers (Saadia, Maimonides, Nachmanides, Soloveitchik)
→ modern stream statements only where they materially differ. Maimonides is never allowed to define Jewish
anthropology alone; his intellectualist strand is marked as one strand.

## 1. Core account

The human being is created in the divine image, male and female, blessed to be fertile and to rule and tend the earth
(Gen 1:26–28; 2:15), formed from the soil and animated by divine breath (Gen 2:7). Rabbinic texts present a composite
of upper and lower traits (BR 8:11, P3), beloved for bearing the image and for being told so (Avot 3:14, P3), each
person unique (m. Sanhedrin 4:5, P3), endowed with two inclinations (Berakhot 61a; BR 14:4, P3), free and
responsible (Avot 3:15, P3). Purpose is service of God through Torah, worship and deeds of loving-kindness (Avot 1:2);
"let all your actions be for the sake of heaven" (Avot 2:12); "I was created to serve my Master" (m. Kiddushin 4:14);
creation is "for My glory" (Isa 43:7). Maimonides makes knowledge of God the aim of all activity (De'ot 3:2–3) and the
"true perfection of man" intellectual, completed by imitating God's loving-kindness, judgment and righteousness
(Guide III.54).

Procreation is a commandment (m. Yevamot 6:6; Ishut 15:1–2), grounded in Gen 1:28 and in the world's being made "to
be inhabited" (m. Gittin 4:5, Isa 45:18); not procreating "diminishes the Divine Image" (Yevamot 63b). Marriage is
joy, blessing and goodness (Yevamot 62b), parents carry duties of Torah, trade and marriage to children (Kiddushin
29a), and covenant teaching passes through the home (Deut 6:7). Love of God with all heart, soul and might (Deut
6:5) is interpreted as love with both inclinations (m. Berakhot 9:5), as lovesickness for God (Teshuvah 10:3), and as
arising from contemplating creation (Yesodei ha-Torah 2:1–2); neighbor love is commanded (Lev 19:18), and love
"not dependent on a thing" endures (Avot 5:16). Beauty is blessed ("who has such in His world," Berakhot 58b),
commandments are to be beautified (Shabbat 133b), and Bezalel's skill is a divine endowment (Exod 31:1–6); human
creativity is a mandate (Soloveitchik's majestic Adam, P3). Worship (*avodah*) is due to God alone (Exod 20:2–6);
prayer is "service of the heart" (Ta'anit 2a); idolatry began as misplaced honor to God's intermediaries
(Avodah Zarah 1:1–3).

## 3. Question map

- **WM.** *Main:* image-bearing composite of earthly and heavenly traits, two inclinations, free choice, unique.
  *Texts:* Gen 1:26–27; 2:7; Avot 3:14; BR 8:11; m. Sanhedrin 4:5. *Thinkers:* Saadia IV.1 (man the world's axis);
  Maimonides YT 4:8, Guide I.1 (image = intellect). *Distinction:* rabbinic composite/inclination anthropology vs
  Maimonidean intellectualism (P3). *Caveat:* Halevi's Israel particularism contested (P3).
- **WA.** *Main:* to serve God—through Torah, worship and acts of kindness; ordinary acts for heaven's sake.
  *Texts:* Isa 43:7; Avot 1:2, 2:12; m. Kiddushin 4:14; Saadia IV.1 (P3). *Thinkers:* Maimonides De'ot 3:2–3 and
  Guide III.54 (knowledge of God, then imitation of his attributes); Sotah 14a (walk after God's attributes).
  *Distinction:* praxis-centred rabbinic service vs Maimonides' intellectual perfection; covenantal particularity
  (Israel's Torah) vs universal human purpose (Noahide). *Caveat:* Guide III.54's ranking of perfections is
  Maimonides', not a rabbinic consensus.
- **OF.** *Main:* procreation is a mitzvah rooted in creation; marriage is good and joyful; family transmits
  covenant. *Texts:* Gen 1:28; 2:18–24; Isa 45:18; m. Yevamot 6:6; m. Gittin 4:5; Yevamot 62b, 63b; Kiddushin 29a;
  Avot 5:21; Deut 6:7; Ishut 15:1–2. *Distinctions:* (1) the obligation formally falls on the man; R. Yohanan b.
  Beroka includes the woman (m. Yevamot 6:6); (2) Hillel (male and female) vs Shammai (two males) on the minimum;
  (3) Ben Azzai's celibacy for Torah—"the world can be maintained by others" (Yevamot 63b)—a recognized but
  exceptional dissent. *Caveat:* modern streams differ on gender and family law (CCAR 1999, P3); do not import
  Orthodox halakhic detail as universal; Mal 2:15 reads "godly folk" in JPS, not "offspring."
- **LB.** *Main:* love of God is commanded and total (both inclinations; lovesick devotion); neighbor love commanded;
  enduring love is love not dependent on a thing; beauty blessed and offered back to God in beautified mitzvot;
  creativity a divine mandate. *Texts:* Deut 6:5; Lev 19:18; m. Berakhot 9:5; Avot 5:16; Berakhot 58b; Shabbat
  133b; Exod 31:1–6; Ps 27:4. *Thinkers:* Maimonides YT 2:1–2 and Teshuvah 10:3; Soloveitchik pp. 12–14 (majestic
  Adam's creative mastery, P3); BR 24:7 (Akiva and Ben Azzai on the greatest principle, P3). *Distinction:* Abba
  Shaul's reading of Exod 15:2 as imitation ("be like Him") vs beautification (Shabbat 133b). *Caveat:* the second
  commandment limits images in worship; Jewish attitudes to figurative art vary historically—no general claim.
- **WO.** *Main:* worship (avodah) of the one God alone; prayer as service of the heart; idolatry a deviation that
  begins with honoring intermediaries and ends in forgetting the Creator; Abraham's rediscovery by reflection.
  *Texts:* Exod 20:2–6; Deut 6:4–5; Avot 1:2; Ta'anit 2a; Mishneh Torah Avodah Zarah 1:1–3. *Distinction:* temple
  worship (avodah) vs prayer after the Temple; Maimonides' account of idolatry's origin vs midrashic Abraham
  narratives (Touger's notes cite Midrash HaGadol—keep separate from Maimonides' text).

## 5. Important distinctions

- Rabbinic praxis vs Maimonidean intellectualism (WA, WM).
- Male obligation vs R. Yohanan b. Beroka; Hillel vs Shammai; Ben Azzai's Torah-celibacy (OF).
- Universal image/Noahide humanity vs covenantal Israel (WA, WO).
- Orthodox halakha vs Reform/Conservative streams on family and gender—note, do not adjudicate.
