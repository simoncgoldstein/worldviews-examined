**Method for this lane (user correction, 2026-10-06).** Biblical foundation → historical theological development

## 1. Core account

Man is a creature, made by God from the dust and given the breath of life, created male and female in God's image
and likeness, blessed with fruitfulness and set to rule, till and keep the earth (Gen 1:26–28; 2:7, 15). He is one
person of body and soul: the dust returns to the earth and the spirit to God, and the soul is not killed with the
body (Eccl 12:7; Matt 10:28), yet his completion is embodied (WM; see Salvation/Destiny for resurrection). The image
survives the Fall: murder and cursing are still assaults on one made in God's likeness (Gen 9:6; Jas 3:9), while its
moral content—knowledge, righteousness and holiness—is renewed in Christ (Eph 4:24; Col 3:10).

Man exists for God: created for God's glory (Isa 43:7), from, through and to whom are all things (Rom 11:36; Col
1:16), so that eating, drinking and all ordinary work are done to God's glory (1 Cor 10:31) and God himself is the
heart's portion (Ps 73:25–26). The chief end is to glorify God and enjoy him forever (WSC/WLC Q. 1). Marriage is
God's institution from creation: the man is not to be alone; the two become one flesh; children are the Lord's
heritage; covenant instruction passes through the household (Gen 2:18–24; Matt 19:4–6; Ps 127:3; Deut 6:4–9).
Singleness is also a gift (1 Cor 7:7, 32–35). Love of God and neighbor sums the law (Matt 22:37–40), and human love
answers God's prior love (1 John 4:7–19). Beauty, craft and art are God's gifts—Bezalel is filled with the Spirit for
artistic design (Exod 31:1–6)—and the believer's highest beauty is the beauty of the Lord (Ps 27:4). Worship is
owed to God alone (Exod 20:3–6; Matt 4:10); fallen humans, knowing God, exchange his glory for images and serve the
creature (Rom 1:21–25). Idolatry is therefore the misdirection of an ineradicable religious nature, not the absence
of one.

### 3b. By question

- **WM (anchor).** *Main answer:* a dependent creature, body and soul, made in God's image male and female, set to
  rule and serve; fallen but still image-bearing; renewed in Christ. *Passages:* Gen 1:26–28; 2:7; 9:6; Jas 3:9; Eccl
  12:7; Matt 10:28; Col 3:10. *Theologians:* Turretin V.10 (image not participation in divine essence nor bodily
  shape; in nature, original righteousness and dominion; partly retained after the Fall); Bavinck II §33.8 (man *is*
  God's image, not merely bears it) and §33.10 (the whole human being, soul and body, prophet-priest-king); Calvin
  I.15.3–4. *Distinction:* broader image (nature, faculties, retained) versus narrower image (knowledge,
  righteousness, holiness; lost and renewed)—Turretin V.10.7, Calvin I.15.4. *Caveat:* Bavinck's §34.5 headship
  language (man as first bearer of dominion) is complementarian; disclose rather than generalize.
- **WA.** *Main answer:* to glorify God and enjoy him forever; life's ordinary goods are received and returned to God.
  *Passages:* Isa 43:7; Rom 11:36; 1 Cor 10:31; Ps 73:25–26. *Theologians:* Augustine *Conf.* I.1.1 (made for God;
  restless heart); Edwards *End of Creation* I.2 (God's communicative disposition as the ground of creation), II.7
  (God's glory includes the creature's knowledge, love and joy in God—so God's glory and human happiness are not
  rivals). *Distinction:* glorifying and enjoying are one end (WSC Q. 1), not a duty plus a reward.
- **OF.** *Main answer:* marriage and procreation are creation ordinances, blessed before the Fall and renewed after
  the Flood; the household is a school of covenant faith; singleness can be a gift and marriage is not the
  condition of human completeness in Christ. *Passages:* Gen 1:28; 2:18–24; Matt 19:4–6; Ps 127:3; Deut 6:7; 1 Cor
  7:7, 32–35. *Theologians:* Calvin II.8.41 (marriage provided because man was not to lead a solitary life), II.8.42
  (continence a special gift to few), IV.13.3 (against vowed celibacy); Bavinck II §34.4–5 (marriage, household,
  upbringing grounded in creation, not sin; humanity in its entirety, as one organism/family, is the fully unfolded
  image). *Confession:* WCF 24.2 (mutual help, increase of mankind and of the church with a holy seed, preventing
  uncleanness); WLC Q. 20. *Distinction:* Reformed rejection of a celibate higher state versus Paul's commendation of
  undivided devotion. *Caveat:* WCF 24.3 (OPC text) restricts marriage "in the Lord" with period polemic ("papists");
  quote with context or paraphrase.
- **LB.** *Main answer:* love is commanded, ordered toward God first and neighbor as self, and grounded in God's
  prior love; beauty is real in creation and supremely in God; craft and art are Spirit-given gifts, misusable but
  good. *Passages:* Matt 22:37–40; 1 John 4:7–19; Exod 31:1–6; Gen 4:21–22; Ps 27:4; Ps 104:24. *Theologians:*
  Augustine CoG XV.22 (virtue is "the order of love"; beauty a good to be loved in its place), *Conf.* X.27.38
  (God the beauty "ancient and new"); Calvin III.10.2 (God made things for delight and comeliness, not only use),
  I.11.12 (sculpture and painting gifts of God), II.2.15–16 (arts from the Spirit, including in unbelievers);
  Edwards *True Virtue* ch. 3 (secondary beauty of order and harmony images the primary beauty of benevolent
  consent; harmony of sounds can dispose hearts to divine love); Turretin XI.10.2–3 (images of creatures for history,
  ornament and delight lawful); Bavinck I §8.14 (religion and art distinct: art lifts in imagination but cannot
  reconcile guilt). *Distinction:* the second commandment forbids images of God and image-mediated worship (WLC Q.
  109; HC 96–97), not art; WLC Q. 139's list of forbidden "lascivious songs, books, pictures, dancings, stage plays"
  is a moral limit, not a ban on arts. *Caveat:* Reformed theologians differ on images in worship spaces (Turretin
  XI.10 against Lutherans).
- **WO.** *Main answer:* man is constitutionally religious; worship is due to the Creator alone and in the way he
  appoints; idolatry is the corruption of that religious nature, giving to creatures what belongs to God, including
  inward trust and love (HC 95). *Passages:* Exod 20:3–6; Matt 4:10; John 4:23–24; Rom 1:21–25; Ps 115:4–8; Isa
  44:9–20; Col 3:5. *Theologians:* Calvin I.3.1–3 (sense of deity ineradicable), I.11.8 (the mind a perpetual forge
  of idols; the mind conceives, the hand embodies); Bavinck I §8.5 (*semen religionis* in every person, corrupted
  into idolatry), §8.15 (religion embraces the whole person: head, heart and hand), §8.16 (no satisfactory naturalistic
  origin; engages Darwin's dog analogy; religion distinguishes man from beasts). *Confession:* WCF 21.1 (light of
  nature shows God is to be worshipped; acceptable worship instituted by God); WSC Q. 46–51; HC 94–98.
  *Distinction:* the regulative principle (WCF 21.1) is a specifically Reformed position; Lutheran and Roman
  positions differ.

## 5. Important distinctions

- **Broader and narrower image** (Turretin V.10.7; Calvin I.15.4; Bavinck §33): decisive for WM and for dignity
  claims that survive the Fall.
- **Creation ordinance versus redemptive calling** (Bavinck §34.4; 1 Cor 7): marriage is a creation good without
  being the precondition of personal completeness or salvation.
- **Art versus image-worship** (HC 97; Calvin I.11.12; Turretin XI.10): the Reformed objection is to images of God
  and image-mediated worship, not to art. Do not attribute iconoclasm of art as such.
- **Religion versus art** (Bavinck I §8.14): aesthetic experience is not a substitute religion.
- **Regulative principle** (WCF 21.1) is Reformed-specific; not "Christian" in general.
