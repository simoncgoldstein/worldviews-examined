# Buddhist traditions — Man & Human Nature

Research packet, 2026-10-06. Codes: WM, WA, OF, LB, WO as in `christianity.md`. Status: P3, ME/SD, NEW, NEW SRC.

**Method.** Pāli discourses (Sujato, SuttaCentral/Bilara) as baseline → Buddhaghosa and Theravāda synthesis →
Mahāyāna, Pure Land and Zen only where they change an answer. Buddhist devotion is never described as worship of a
creator; "worship" is used comparatively for refuge, reverence, offering and homage.

## 1. Core account

A human being is a conditioned stream of five aggregates, none of them a self, yet a real conventional agent defined
by intention (P3: SN 22.59; Vism XIX.19–20; AN 6.63). The human birth is rare and uniquely suited to awakening
(SN 56.47–48, P3), and its proper aim is the "noble quest" for what is free of birth, aging and death—extinguishment
—rather than the "ignoble quest" for things themselves liable to be reborn, a list that includes "partners and
children" (MN 26:5–13). Household life is honored and given a full ethic: the Buddha redirects Sigālaka's ritual
reverence of the six directions into reciprocal duties between parents and children, husband and wife, teachers,
friends, workers and renunciants (DN 31:27–34); a family where children honor their parents "lives with divinity"
(AN 4.63); parents cannot be repaid except by establishing them in faith, ethics, generosity and wisdom (AN 2.33);
spouses who wish to meet again should be equal in faith, ethics, generosity and wisdom (AN 4.55); parents have five
reasons for wanting a child (AN 5.39). Yet "life at home is cramped and dirty, life gone forth is wide open"
(DN 2:41.4), and the monastic path is the fuller expression of the quest.

Love in the Buddhist sense is *mettā* (and the other immeasurables): cultivated, boundless goodwill whose near enemy
is greed (Vism IX.98, P3; AN 8.1); good friendship is "the whole of the spiritual life" (SN 45.2). Sensual beauty is
real but impermanent—its gratification, drawback and escape must all be seen (MN 13:18–21)—and the eight
*uposatha* precepts renounce shows of dancing, singing and music for a day, imitating the perfected ones (AN 8.41).
The mind is more varied than any painting it devises (SN 22.100, P3). Reverence is a human need even for the Buddha,
who, finding no one to revere, honors the Dhamma he awakened to (SN 6.2); the highest honor to the Buddha is
practice (DN 16:5.3.8–9), though relics are honored with dance, song, music, garlands and fragrance (DN 16:6.23.10).
Pure Land devotion entrusts itself to Amitābha's vow (SD: Inagaki vow 18; Tannishō).

## 3. Question map

- **WM.** *Main:* aggregates without a self; conventional intending agent; luminous mind with visiting defilements;
  precious human birth. *Texts:* SN 22.59; Vism XIX.19–20; AN 1.49–52; SN 56.48 (P3). *Distinction:* Abhidharma,
  Yogācāra, Madhyamaka, buddha-nature, Dōgen (P3). *Caveat:* human/animal difference is opportunity, not kind of
  worth (P3).
- **WA.** *Main:* no creator's purpose; the human life is the rare chance to undertake the noble quest for awakening;
  Mahāyāna adds awakening for all beings. *Texts:* MN 26:5–13; SN 56.47–48; AN 9.21 (P3); Tsongkhapa vv. 2–4 (P3).
  *Distinction:* arahant ideal vs bodhisattva aspiration (SD/P3).
- **OF.** *Main:* lay family life is honored and ethically structured, parents revered as "divinity," spouses
  bound by shared virtue; yet attachment to partners and children belongs to the ignoble quest when sought as
  refuge, and going forth is the higher path. *Texts:* DN 31:27–34; AN 4.63; AN 2.33; AN 4.55; AN 5.39; MN 26:6–7;
  DN 2:41.4. *Distinction:* lay vs monastic vocations (two-tier community); Japanese Jōdo Shinshū married clergy
  (Shinran married—not researched; do not assert in public without a source). *Caveat:* AN/DN gender roles reflect
  their setting (DN 31:30 wife's duties); there is no procreation command.
- **LB.** *Main:* love as cultivated boundless goodwill (mettā) and compassion, distinct from attachment; friendship
  central; beauty real but impermanent, to be seen with its drawback; arts are renounced by monastics and on
  observance days, yet used in devotion. *Texts:* AN 8.1; Vism IX.98 (P3); SN 45.2; MN 13:18–21; AN 8.41; DN
  16:6.23.10; SN 22.100 (P3). *Distinction:* mettā vs sensual love (near enemy greed); Mahāyāna compassion (BCA,
  P3). *Caveat:* Buddhist arts traditions (Zen arts, Tibetan iconography) not researched.
- **WO.** *Main:* reverence and refuge without a creator: the Buddha himself honors the Dhamma; homage by practice is
  highest; offerings and relic veneration are legitimate devotion; Sigālaka's ritual is reinterpreted ethically;
  Pure Land reliance on Amitābha's vow is a distinct devotional form. *Texts:* SN 6.2; DN 16:5.3.8–9, 6.23.10; DN
  31:1–2, 27; Inagaki vow 18; Tannishō (SD). *Distinction:* refuge/reverence vs worship of a creator; Pure Land
  Other Power (SD). *Caveat:* do not call the Buddha a god; devas exist in the cosmology but are not the refuge.

## 5. Important distinctions

- Lay vs monastic paths (OF, WA, LB).
- Mettā/compassion vs attachment (LB).
- Reverence/refuge vs creator-worship; Pure Land devotion (WO).
- Theravāda arahant vs Mahāyāna bodhisattva aim (WA).
