# Rabbinic Judaism: final corpus audit

All 28 answers were read in full, in question order. **Result: 0 BLOCKER, 2 IMPORTANT, 6 MINOR.**

## 1. Overall coherence

The lane is one recognizable rabbinic worldview:

- one Creator and creation ex nihilo (the main line, with Halevi's concession disclosed);
- the human as earth and breath, made in the image, with two created inclinations;
- the Written and Oral Torah from Sinai, interpreted by the authorized court;
- sin as misdirected inclination, not inherited guilt;
- *teshuvah* and mercy;
- the Messiah tested by Maimonides' public criteria;
- resurrection and the World to Come.

Vertical consistency is good. The one under-sourced tension is noted in §8.

## 4. Internal diversity

Medieval diversity is excellent. Saadia, Maimonides, Halevi and Nachmanides disagree visibly on providence, attributes, creation, the Eden narrative and eschatology. Shmuel and R. Yoḥanan disagree on the messianic age. Ḥabad is presented as a variant.

**Modern diversity is weak (JUD-02).**

- Orthodox, Conservative and Reform positions are mentioned in three pages (`what-is-man`, `offspring-family`, flagship), each in one sentence.
- They are never developed where SOURCES.md says they matter most: `ultimate-authority` (the authority of halakha and the Sinaitic origin of the Oral Torah) and `revelation` (progressive revelation; Heschel; Rosenzweig and Buber; Conservative acceptance of critical scholarship).
- The lane is scoped as "Rabbinic Judaism", but a specialist would expect at least a disclosed paragraph on modern streams in these two anchors.

## 8. Cross-page inconsistencies

- `what-is-man` says classical sources speak of a surviving soul *and* a future resurrection, and that "other authorities place more weight on bodily resurrection as the final state".
- `after-death` and `final-end` centre Maimonides' incorporeal World to Come. They state that his view "is not the only interpretation" but never source the alternative.
- The result is an asymmetry, not a contradiction: the resurrection strand that dominates liturgy (the second blessing of the Amidah) and creed (the 13th principle) is asserted but never shown (JUD-01).

## 9. Argument-quality concerns

- The Christian responses classify correctly: the Trinity and Jesus as revelatory disagreements; induction as shared ground.
- The flagship's Christian response is exemplary. It concedes non-sacrificial forgiveness texts (Leviticus 5:11–13; 1 Kings 8) before pressing Isaiah 53:10, and it notes Rashi's reading of the servant as Israel.
- **`ultimate-personal` PQ1 (JUD-06)**, "If God's love is eternal, whom did God love before anything was created?", assumes a Christian premise (that love requires an eternal object). Maimonides' attributes-of-action answer, given in the same page, already addresses it. The same question recurs in `islam/ultimate-personal` (XQ-04).
- **XQ-08:** `guilt` and `self-salvation` press "Christ's completed satisfaction" against Judaism, but the Christian lane never expounds satisfaction.

## Diagnosis and remedy

- **Diagnosis:** sin as a misdirected created inclination that gains mastery through habit, within covenant. Not inherited guilt.
- **Remedy:** Torah (the "antidote"), *teshuvah* (abandoning the sin, regret, confession, and refraining when the temptation returns), mercy, and interpersonal repair (Yoma 8:9).
- **Final state:** the World to Come, with messianic peace as a this-worldly penultimate.
- **Coherence:** high. The remedy fits a diagnosis that treats the inclination as created and governable.
- **The pressure point the corpus supports but does not draw clearly:** the Christian lane's claim that guilt before God needs a satisfaction beyond repentance. This depends on doctrine the Christian lane never explains (XQ-08).
