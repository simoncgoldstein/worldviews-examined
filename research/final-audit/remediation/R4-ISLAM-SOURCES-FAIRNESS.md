## *Taḥrīf*: distinctions as established

The page now distinguishes the following.

1. **The Qur'anic vocabulary.**
   - Twisting with the tongue (3:78).
   - Shifting or distorting words, which Haleem renders as distorting their meaning (4:46; 5:13, 41). In context these concern some of the Jews.
   - Writing by hand and calling it God's (2:79).
   - Concealment (5:15).
   - Of Christians specifically, forgetting part of what they were reminded of (5:14).
   - In the other direction, the Torah and Gospel in their communities' hands are assumed to contain God's judgment (5:43–47, 68). The Qur'an is "confirming… with final authority" (5:48), and 10:94 tells the Prophet to ask those who read the earlier scriptures.
2. ***Taḥrīf al-maʿnā* versus alteration of wording.** The page uses the descriptive terms (meaning and interpretation; wording; copies) rather than the technical labels, because the labels were not verified as Ibn Taymiyya's own vocabulary. His words are *ḥurrifa maʿnāhu wa-taʾwīluhu* and *ḥurrifa lafẓuhu*; *tabdīl alfāẓ*.
3. **The range of Muslim opinion.**
   - Ibn ʿAbbās is reported both ways. In al-Bukhārī's report, which Ibn Kathīr cites on 3:78, no one removes a word of God's books and the People of the Book distort them by interpretation. In Bukhari 7363 they "changed their scripture… and wrote the scripture with their own hands".
   - Wahb b. Munabbih: not a letter changed.
   - Ibn Kathīr: the copies in their hands are altered, while God's books as revealed are preserved.
   - Ibn Taymiyya, *Jawāb* 2:418–420: positions range from no change of wording to treating most of the Gospels as altered. He judges "little altered" more evident, and says that both sides agree meanings were distorted.
4. **Ibn Taymiyya's own position** (no stronger theory is attributed to him):
   - The alteration is mostly of meaning and interpretation, and only a little of the wording (5:123; 2:418–421).
   - Muslims do not claim every copy everywhere was altered (2:418–419).
   - The Injīl is God's speech to Jesus. The four Gospels were written after him by two men who knew him and two who did not, are not mass-transmitted, and record some of his words and deeds (2:395–398; 3:418–419).
   - The Torah, and likewise the Gospel, after Jesus still contain God's judgment, binding where not abrogated. Unaltered text exposes the few altered narrative words (2:434–442).
   - 10:94 implies neither doubt nor asking (2:356–359).
   - The crucifixion report is an error rather than an interpolation. No evangelist or disciple witnessed the execution; a band of Jews did, either deceived by the likeness (the majority view) or lying (some *mutakallimūn* and Ibn Ḥazm). Mistaken disciples are excused (2:301–305).
   - An objector must establish the wording, its meaning and its translation (5:123–125).

## Christian historical response: corrections

The order now follows the brief: Muslim claim (view), then the classical explanation (view, *taḥrīf*), then historical evidence, then Christian evaluation.

- *Taḥrīf* is credited with answering the bare charge that the Qur'an contradicts a scripture it confirms. The R3-era sentence "The Qur'an also presents itself as confirming the Gospel while denying its central event" is removed.
- Ibn Taymiyya's explanation is treated as a historical claim and weighed:
  - Paul knew Cephas and James (Gal 1:18–19).
  - Mark and John name followers at the cross (Mark 15:40–41; John 19:25–26), against his claim that no follower saw it.
  - Tacitus 15.44 and the Talmud presuppose the execution.
  - Mark was written c. 70 (Martin L2).
  - Codex Sinaiticus, of the mid-fourth century, contains the whole New Testament.
- **Limits stated in the text.** The evidence counts heavily against late textual alteration. It does not settle what the Qur'an means by the Injīl. It cannot strictly refute the narrower claim that the first witnesses were deceived; the response says only that no first-century source suggests such a mistake.
- **Levels distinguished.**
  - Historical: the crucifixion.
  - Christian theological: the crucified man is the incarnate Son.
  - Muslim theological: the Qur'an's finality explains the Muslim preference, but does not by itself establish what happened.
- **Classification:** historical conflict, with a disagreement over revelation behind it.
- **"Later means false" principle kept:** "This is not the claim that later means false."
- **`revelation`:** given *taḥrīf*, the confirm-and-correct claim is "coherent on its own terms". The remaining question is historical. Al-Bāqillānī's predictions and unlettered-prophet arguments are classified as historical claims to be weighed. The literary critique of *iʿjāz* is unchanged.
- **Narrow pressure-question correction.** `revelation` PQ2, "If the Qur'an confirms the earlier scriptures, how should it be weighed where it contradicts their central historical claims?", asked a question the tradition had already answered (ISL-01). It now reads: "If, as Ibn Taymiyya held, the earlier scriptures were altered mostly in meaning rather than wording, what evidence apart from the Qur'an shows that the Gospels' account of the crucifixion is among what was changed or mistaken?" No other pressure question was changed.

## Al-Shāfiʿī: findings

The *Risāla* (Shākir 1938), read in Arabic:

- §48: every case has guidance in the Book.
- §§53–59: kinds of *bayān*: text; a duty in the Book detailed by the Prophet; the Prophet's ruling where the Book is silent, binding because the Book commands obedience to him; *ijtihād*.
- §§252–257: the "wisdom" is the Sunna, which explains God's meaning.
- §§259–264: 4:59's "those in authority" are the Prophet's commanders, owed conditional obedience.
- §120: "the way of knowledge is the report in the Book or the Sunna, or consensus, or analogy".
- §§1309–1312: consensus binds because the Sunna cannot escape the whole community, which does not agree against it or on error.
- §§1321–1334: analogy only where Book, Sunna and consensus are silent; "two names for one meaning" with *ijtihād*; certainty only from a text or a Sunna transmitted by the many; analogy right "in the outward".

**Four-source attribution.** Al-Shāfiʿī does name the four routes together (§120). He ranks them: Book/Sunna, then consensus, then analogy. Consensus is made answerable to the Sunna. He argues for each in turn rather than stating the later textbook formula.

The page previously gave Macdonald's order ("Qur'an, the Prophet's usage, analogy … agreement") as al-Shāfiʿī's scheme. It now:

1. states al-Shāfiʿī's own argument from the *Risāla*;
2. presents the later weight of consensus ("must put its stamp on every rule") and the schools' acceptance of the classification as Macdonald's report about later Shāfiʿī law books;
3. notes Macdonald's different order explicitly.

"My People will never agree in an error" is no longer attributed to al-Shāfiʿī's argument. Macdonald himself says "the later church made him so declare".

## Twelver Shi'a disclosure

Source: al-Ṣadūq, *Risālat al-Iʿtiqādāt*, translated by Fyzee as *A Shiʿite Creed* (1942). This is a primary creed in a scholarly translation; Fyzee's notes are treated as secondary.

- **`ultimate-authority`.**
  - After the Prophet, God's "proofs" are twelve Imams, from ʿAlī to Muhammad b. al-Ḥasan. They are "the interpreters of His revelations", immune from sin and error, and "in authority"; their command is God's command (pp. 95–96, 99–100).
  - They are the Prophet's successors (*awṣiyāʾ*), not prophets (pp. 92–95). The received Qur'an is the complete Qur'an (p. 85).
  - Their authority differs in kind from a Sunni law school's fallible reasoning.
  - Contrast: al-Shāfiʿī reads the same phrase of 4:59 as the Prophet's commanders (*Risāla* §§259–264).
- **`history`.**
  - Sunni: Ibn Khaldun reports a long-standing Muslim expectation of a Mahdi of the Prophet's family, with Jesus descending, built on contested reports. On his review only "a very small number" escape criticism, and some scholars held "No Mahdi but Jesus" (de Slane vol. 2, pp. 158–159, 188). The page does not say Sunnis reject the Mahdi.
  - Twelver: the Mahdi is the twelfth Imam, born, present but hidden, who will fill the earth with justice. Jesus prays behind him, and there is no other Qāʾim however long the occultation lasts (Fyzee pp. 95–99).
- **`revelation`.** One sentence: the Imams are successors, not prophets, and the received Qur'an is complete. It affirms Muhammad as the seal of the prophets without implying new prophecy.
- **Not added.** No Shi'a paragraph elsewhere. Twelver is not presented as all Shi'a; the text says "Twelver" throughout. `self-salvation` (intercession of the Imams), suggested as "possible" by the audit, is deferred.
- **Overstatement corrected before commit.** A draft said the received Qur'an is "the whole of what was revealed". Fyzee p. 86 says other revelation, outside the Qur'an, exists, so the text now says "the complete Qur'an".

## Al-Rāzī and divine decree

*Mafātīḥ al-ghayb* on 4:78–79 (altafsir.com Arabic, pp. 1–3 each) contributes what the page lacked.

- **The exegetical dispute.**
  - Al-Jubbāʾī (Muʿtazilī) assigns misfortune to God and sin to the servant.
  - Al-Rāzī reads "all is from God" as covering obedience and disobedience.
  - He takes 4:79's "from yourself" as courtesy of speech (Abraham, 26:80) or a question of denial.
- **The metaphysical argument:** everything other than the Necessary Being is possible in itself and depends on it, angel, inanimate thing or creature's act alike.
- **The reply to the praise-and-blame objection:** it is met by God's foreknowledge.
- **School setting kept.** The paragraph ends by saying that how the agent is answerable is answered differently by acquisition, divine wisdom and the doctrine of moral value in the different schools.
- **R3 corrections preserved:** the parity on hidden wisdom, and the cross not over-read.
