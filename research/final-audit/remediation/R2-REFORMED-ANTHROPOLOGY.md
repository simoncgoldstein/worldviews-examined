## `know-the-good`

**View, rebuilt on Scripture:**

- Genesis 1:26–28: creatures under God's word.
- God's claim "comes before anyone's consent".
- Romans 2:14–15 (quoted): the work of the law written on the heart; conscience as witness.

**Natural law: the Fall damages it without erasing it.**

- Turretin XI.1.7, 11, 20: first principles remain in all; conclusions are corrupted by native corruption, upbringing and custom, until vices are honored as virtues.
- Romans 1:18–21, 32.
- Canons of Dort III/IV.4.
- Calvin II.8.1: conscience "an inward witness and monitor", yet a fallen person is "scarcely able" to form a tolerable idea of acceptable worship.

**The written law:**

- WCF 19.1–2: the law given at creation "continued to be a perfect rule of righteousness".
- Same content as the law on the heart, different clarity.
- Turretin XI.1.22–23: the faded picture renewed by God's voice and hand.

**Objection strengthened.** It now includes naturalist moral realism (Railton pp. 190–194) alongside Darwin.

**Reply: the authority argument.**

1. **Three questions:**
   - causal (how moral cognition arose);
   - epistemic (whether conscience gets anything right);
   - normative (why an obligation binds someone who dislikes it, whose society rejects it, or who would gain by breaking it).

   "A history of moral feeling, however complete, answers the first question. By itself it does not answer the third."
2. **The Christian answer.**
   - Calvin II.8.2: the Creator "entitled to be regarded by us as a Father and Master"; we "are not our own"; God "can only require what is right", so "a natural obligation to obey".
   - Turretin XI.1.4, 9: natural law is commanded because it is just, rooted in God's holiness and wisdom, and could not fail to be prescribed once rational creatures exist.
   - Turretin XI.1.15: rational creatures depend on God morally as in being; denying this imagines a creature independent of its Creator.
   - Conclusion: the good is "neither an arbitrary decree nor a standard above God", which answers the Euthyphro pressure.
   - James 4:12: "one lawgiver and judge".
3. **Fairness.** "This does not mean that morality without God is mere preference." The page links to the naturalist answer. The pressure is comparative and uses Railton's own concession (p. 201): what makes an obligation authoritative over a person who has no reason to comply, and why do such facts fit persons and the minds that perceive them? The argument is classed as "a claim of better explanation, not a demonstration that realism without God is incoherent."

**Deep dive:**

- "Natural law in Reformed theology": Turretin on natural and positive law; against Carneades and Hobbes; same substance as the Decalogue, different mode.
- "Why conscience is uneven": the pattern the account predicts. It also notes that natural knowledge is not saving knowledge (WCF 1.1), so the page does not become `revelation`.
- "Conscience accuses and excuses": Turretin XI.1.18 (suppression is not extinction); Larger Catechism Q. 93, 95–96, quoted.
- "Knowing duty and doing it": links only. The meta sentence "These links preserve the distinction…" was removed.

Moral epistemology (view), ontology and authority (reply) and motivation (link to `fail-the-good`) are kept distinct. R9's naturalism completeness work is not preempted, and the naturalist pages are unchanged.

## Coherence check (Audit 18 reading)

The six pages were read together with `knowledge-possible`, `ultimate-reality`, `fail-the-good`, `self-salvation` and `guilt`. One anthropology emerges:

1. Humans are created rational and embodied image-bearers (`what-is-man`, flagship).
2. They retain real knowledge and capacities after the Fall: Calvin's natural gifts (flagship, `knowledge-possible`) and Turretin's first principles (`know-the-good`).
3. They suppress and distort truth through sin (Romans 1 across all four pages).
4. They remain accountable (Dort III/IV.4; WCF 1.1).
5. They cannot restore themselves spiritually (WCF 9.3; flagship reply; `self-salvation`).
6. They are renewed in Christ (Colossians 3:10; `guilt`).

One apparent tension was checked. Dort says the fallen do not use the light of nature rightly "even in matters of nature and society", while the flagship says they frame just laws. Both are the same Reformed position, reconciled by WCF 16.7 (works good in matter, defective in source and end), which the flagship already quotes. No contradiction was found.
