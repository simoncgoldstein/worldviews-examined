# Reformed Christianity: final corpus audit

Audit-only record (2026-10-07, branch `final-corpus-substantive-audit`, base `main` @ `8a39fda`). All 28 Christian answers were read in full and in question order, with citations, before any research note was consulted for them. They were read again as one continuous document (the visitor-level reading below). No public file was changed.

Finding IDs (`CHR-nn`) are used in `REMEDIATION-PLAN.md`. Severity follows the audit brief: **BLOCKER**, **IMPORTANT**, **MINOR**.

**Result: 0 BLOCKER, 13 IMPORTANT, 18 MINOR.**

No Christian page contains a doctrinal error against the Westminster Standards, a false historical claim, or an argument mislabelled as a contradiction. The problems are of a different kind. Some are omissions at the centre of the gospel, some are thin biblical grounding in older pages, and in ten pages the prose voice breaks.

---

## 1. Scripture primacy

**Overall: strong in 18 pages, adequate in 6, weak in 4.**

`bible-esv` carries 174 of the lane's 554 citations. The Westminster Confession carries 81, Calvin 46 and Turretin 28. In most answers Scripture opens "The Christian view", and the confession then confirms the doctrine. That is the order the methodology asks for. The best examples are `ultimate-reality`, `something-rather-than-nothing`, `ultimate-personal`, `order`, `knowledge-possible`, `induction`, `revelation`, `history`, `jesus`, `why-alive`, `offspring-family` and `worship`.

Weaknesses:

- **`great-and-terrible` (flagship) is the least biblical Christian answer on the site (CHR-02).** Its "Christian view" cites WCF 4.2, WCF 6.2, Calvin II.2.12, Calvin II.2.15 and Augustine XIV.28. Only one Scripture citation appears (Colossians 3:10), and it comes in the closing sentence.
  - The image of God is sourced to WCF 4.2 rather than Genesis 1:26–27.
  - Corruption is sourced to WCF 6.2 rather than Genesis 3, Romans 5:12, Ephesians 2:1–3, Jeremiah 17:9 or Romans 3:10–18.
  - The page predates the Scripture-primacy rule, and it shows.
- **The atonement is never grounded in its central texts anywhere in the lane (CHR-01).** A search of all 28 answers finds:
  - no 2 Corinthians 5:21;
  - no Galatians 3:13;
  - no 1 Peter 2:24 or 3:18;
  - Isaiah 53 only inside the Jewish-reception discussion in `jesus`;
  - no use of the word "propitiation".

  Romans 3:25 "propitiation" appears only in the Christian response *to Hinduism* (`hinduism/great-and-terrible`). Romans 3:21–26 is cited as a range in `self-salvation` and `guilt`, but its content is described abstractly, not expounded.
- **`ultimate-authority` (anchor) omits Christ's own attestation of Scripture (CHR-12).** Missing texts include Matthew 5:17–18, John 10:35 and Luke 24:25–27, 44. This is the standard Reformed argument (Bavinck *RD* I §§13–14; Calvin I.7–8). It also answers the "circular" objection better than general self-attestation does.
- **`evil`, `suffering` and `death` leave out the biblical centre of the Christian answer (CHR-04 to CHR-06):**
  - the cross as God's purpose working through human wickedness (Acts 2:23; 4:27–28);
  - the suffering Son (Hebrews 2:10, 17–18; 4:15);
  - Christ's victory over death (1 Corinthians 15:54–57; Hebrews 2:14–15; John 11:25–26);
  - the promised wiping away of tears (Revelation 21:4).
- **Decorative or overloaded proof texts (MINOR):**
  - *Hebrews 11:3* is made to carry creation "from nothing", "not a portion of God's being" and "a beginning" (`something-rather-than-nothing`; also the Christian response in `buddhism/something-rather-than-nothing`). The verse says the visible was not made from the visible. Add John 1:3 and Colossians 1:16 rather than overload Hebrews 11:3 (CHR-14).
  - *Isaiah 1:18*, "let us reason together", is a covenant lawsuit, not a text about logic (`logic-binding`, CHR-15).

Prooftext-driven pages are rare. `offspring-family` and `love-beauty-creativity` string verses together at concise length, which is acceptable.

## 4. Natural explanatory prose

Imagine the citations removed. Does the page still sound like a knowledgeable Reformed theologian explaining Christianity?

- **Yes (the target voice):** the five Ultimate Reality pages; `knowledge-possible`, `induction` and `ultimate-authority`; `revelation`, `history` and `jesus`; `why-alive` and `worship`. These pages state the doctrine and then attribute distinctive formulations, such as "Turretin distinguishes", "Calvin compares Scripture to glasses" and "Van Til restated".
- **Source tour (CHR-09, CHR-29):** `what-is-man` paragraph 3 ("The theologians explain what the image is. Calvin places… Turretin rejects… Bavinck presses further…") is the textbook pattern the brief describes, in an anchor. Lesser instances occur in `offspring-family` ("Calvin holds… Bavinck treats… Westminster names…"), `love-beauty-creativity` and `one-and-many` ("Bavinck argues…" in the view).
- **Research-note voice (CHR-10):** `logic-binding`.
  - Public sentences include: "None of these sources says that logic simply *is* God's nature"; "although its sources speak of origin and dependence rather than offering a worked-out theory"; "That is a theological inference, not a quotation."
  - The Augustine phrase "exists eternally in the reason of things" is quoted three times on one page.
- **Defensive, meta voice (CHR-08), in all ten Morality & Evil and Salvation & Destiny pages.** Examples:
  - "These links preserve the distinction between moral cognition, authority, reliability, and obedience." (`know-the-good`)
  - "Those distinctions prevent this anchor from becoming either a catalogue of tragedies or a promise…" (`evil`)
  - "This page keeps those wider arguments connected while refusing an unsupported diagnosis…" (`suffering`)
  - "The anchor joins those matters through one remedy rather than treating salvation as the sum of unrelated rewards." (`self-salvation`)
  - "This diagnosis does not mean every person is as vicious as possible…" (`self-salvation`)

  These pages spend much of their length saying what the doctrine does *not* claim. The cause is traceable: these two domains were produced first, before the thesis and opening-paragraph pass. They also never received an independent `REVIEW.md` (see `README.md`).
- **Scripture reported rather than taught (CHR-23).** The same ten pages repeatedly use distancing verbs: "Paul describes", "Romans connects", "Genesis depicts", "Hebrews depicts". The Reformed theologian's own voice rarely affirms the doctrine plainly, for example "Christ bore the curse we deserved."
- **Confessional shorthand (CHR-22).** "Westminster connects / adds / names / summarizes" appears in about eight pages, along with "Dort affirms" and "Belgic 13 limits". The brief's preferred form is "The Westminster Confession says…"

These loci are consistent across all 28 pages:

- Image of God: what-is-man → flagship → great-and-terrible note.
- Fall and noetic corruption: knowledge-possible ("refusal more than incapacity") → flagship → self-deception.
- Natural knowledge and common grace: knowledge-possible, flagship, fail-the-good.
- Revelation: ultimate-authority → revelation → jesus.
- Logic and induction: logic-binding → induction → order.
- Providence and second causes: order → induction → evil.
- Guilt and corruption: flagship → self-salvation → guilt.
- Resurrection: after-death → jesus.
- History: history → final-end.

**No contradictions were found.** Audit 18 (justification, sanctification and related distinctions) passes in full:

- No page implies that justification removes corruption, that sanctification pays guilt, or that repentance earns forgiveness. `guilt` and `self-salvation` explicitly deny the latter.
- No page implies that resurrection itself justifies, or treats regeneration and justification as interchangeable.

Two consistency gaps:

1. **XQ-08** (in `CROSS-QUESTION.md`). The Christian responses to Judaism, Islam, the Hindu traditions and Buddhism all press "Christ's satisfaction" or "propitiation" as the decisive difference. The Christian lane itself never explains what satisfaction is. The critique depends on a doctrine the site does not expound.
2. `what-is-man` defers to the flagship for the historicity of Adam, and `evil` calls the flagship "the human-nature anchor". These labels and deferrals do not line up.

## 11. Final visitor-level Christian reading

The 28 Christian answers were read straight through, without research notes. The question asked: *Does Christianity emerge as one coherent worldview explained by a knowledgeable Reformed theologian?*

**In doctrine, yes. In voice, only for 16 of 28 pages.**

**Questions 1–9 (Ultimate Reality, Knowledge & Truth).** These read as one confident theologian. Scripture comes first, attribution does explanatory work, and claims are honestly bounded ("That is a claim of comparative explanatory adequacy, not a proof"). They are biblical, confessional, historically informed and intellectually serious. `logic-binding` is the one page whose sentences slip into note-taking.

**Questions 10–15 (Man & Human Nature).**

- `why-alive` and `worship` keep the voice.
- `what-is-man`, `offspring-family` and `love-beauty-creativity` become theologian summaries.
- The flagship, the site's featured entry, opens with confession and Calvin rather than Scripture. Its strength lies in its objection and reply, not in its exposition.

**Questions 16–25 (Morality & Evil; Salvation & Destiny).** The voice breaks here.

- **A different author.** The pages read as if written by a careful reviewer anxious not to overclaim. They are hedged, abstract and self-referential ("this answer", "this anchor"), and they report Scripture rather than teach it.
- **Christ is nearly absent where the human problem is answered.** `evil`, `suffering` and `death` never mention the cross or the risen Christ as God's answer. `self-salvation` and `guilt` mention Christ's "obedience and satisfaction" without saying what it means for Christ to bear sin.
- **The effect.** A visitor would learn the Reformed *distinctions* (justification is not sanctification, inability is moral) but not the Reformed *gospel*.

**Questions 26–28 (Revelation & History).** The voice returns at full strength: biblical, historically accountable and confident.

**Pages that break the cumulative voice:** logic-binding, what-is-man, offspring-family, love-beauty-creativity, great-and-terrible (exposition only), know-the-good, fail-the-good, evil, suffering, death, self-deception, self-salvation, guilt, after-death, final-end. That is 15 pages: the ten Morality & Evil and Salvation & Destiny pages plus five others.

The Christian lane is **not** prooftext-driven, **not** confessionally mechanical in most places, and **not** a sequence of theologian summaries outside the Man domain. Its main defect is that the redemptive centre is the least developed part of the site, and the pages that should carry it were written in the corpus's most defensive voice.
