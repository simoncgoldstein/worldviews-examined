# Classical Islam: final corpus audit

All 28 answers were read in full, in question order. **Result: 0 BLOCKER, 3 IMPORTANT, 5 MINOR.**

## 1. Overall coherence

The lane is one recognizable mainstream Sunni worldview:

- *tawḥīd* and the Creator–creature line;
- real attributes;
- God's constant custom;
- *fiṭra*, with the heart as the self;
- Qur'an and Sunna as final authority;
- sin as each soul's own burden;
- *tawba* and mercy (Bukhari 6463);
- resurrection, judgment, paradise and the vision of God;
- Jesus as Messiah and prophet, not crucified.

**Kalām, falsafa, Athari and Mu'tazili positions are never silently merged (Audit 12 passes):**

- Ash'ari versus Maturidi on attributes, natures and reason;
- Ibn Taymiyya's voluntary attributes and wise purposes;
- Ibn Sina as an "alternative, not the creed";
- the Mu'tazila as a "comparative contrast, not a synonym for the Sunni lane";
- Ibn 'Arabi's "oneness of being", with the label's provenance (Ibn Taymiyya) given correctly.

## 4. Internal diversity

The kalām schools are excellent. Two gaps:

**Shi'a Islam is never mentioned in any of the 28 answers (ISL-02).** The lane's own `worldviews.yaml` scope note promises that "Sufi and Shi'a differences are noted where they materially change an answer." They materially change:

- `ultimate-authority`: the Imamate as the infallible interpretive authority;
- `revelation` and `history`: the occultation and the Mahdi;
- possibly `self-salvation`: intercession of the Imams.

Sufism is handled (Ibn 'Arabi; saint veneration in `worship`). The "classical Sunni default" is legitimate, but the promised disclosure is missing.

## 9. Argument-quality concerns

**ISL-01 (IMPORTANT): critique without the tradition's classical answer.**

- `revelation` PQ2 ("If the Qur'an confirms the earlier scriptures, how should it be weighed where it contradicts their central historical claims?") and the Christian response in `jesus` ("The Qur'an also presents itself as confirming the Gospel while denying its central event") press the classic "Islamic dilemma".
- The standard Muslim answer, ***taḥrīf*** (corruption of the earlier scriptures' text or meaning; Ibn Ḥazm; Ibn Taymiyya's *al-Jawāb al-ṣaḥīḥ li-man baddala dīn al-Masīḥ*), is never stated anywhere in the lane. A search for *taḥrīf*, *corrupt* or *altered* in the scriptural sense finds nothing.
- This is exactly "asking a question the opposing view has already answered in its own terms". The Christian response may still press its own point: that *taḥrīf* claims face the manuscript evidence and that the Qur'an's own confirming language (5:47) is in tension with them. But the steelman must come first.

**`jesus` is otherwise a model of historical accountability.** It states: "This is not the claim that later means false." Al-Razi's own objection ("opens the door of sophistry") is used fairly.

**`ultimate-personal` PQ1** ("eternally exercised, and toward whom, before anything was created?") repeats the Judaism pressure question, which the Sunni answer in the same page already addresses (XQ-04).

**`why-alive` PQ3** ("what becomes of those who never had a fair chance") applies equally to Christianity's unevangelized and is asked without acknowledgement (XQ-04).

**The `ultimate-reality` attributes–Trinity parity argument** is labelled "a question about consistency, not a proof", which is acceptable. A specialist would note that attributes are not subjects, so the analogy is weaker than presented (MINOR, folded into XQ-04).

## Diagnosis and remedy

- **Diagnosis:** weakness, desire maturing before intellect, forgetfulness (*ghafla*), the heart's rust and sin, each soul answering for its own. The *fiṭra* can be corrupted but is not inherited guilt.
- **Remedy:** guidance, faith, *tawba* (knowledge, remorse and resolve), purification, and victims' rights. Mercy is decisive ("no one is saved by deeds"), and God turns first (9:118).
- **Final state:** resurrection, judgment, paradise, God's good pleasure and the vision of God.
- **Coherence:** high. The remedy answers a diagnosis of weakness and heedlessness rather than radical inability.
- **The classical internal tension, which the corpus acknowledges:** God creates acts and guides or misguides (Ash'ari *Ibāna*) while each soul answers for its deeds.
- **The comparison the corpus supports but does not draw clearly:** whether mercy needs a ground beyond God's will to forgive. This turns on the Christian doctrine of satisfaction, which the Christian lane does not expound (XQ-08).
