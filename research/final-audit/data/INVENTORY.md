## Theses (all 168, body omitted)

### christianity

- **ultimate-reality** (31w): God alone exists from himself. Everything else exists because the triune God freely made it and keeps it in being, so the world is real but never independent of its Creator.
- **something-rather-than-nothing** (33w): God did not need a world. There is something rather than nothing because God freely chose to create it from nothing, and God himself needs no further explanation, because he exists from himself.
- **ultimate-personal** (33w): Yes. The ultimate reality is a God who knows, wills, loves and speaks, and he is personal in himself as Father, Son and Holy Spirit, not only in his dealings with the world.
- **order** (31w): Reality is orderly because one wise God made it by wisdom and faithfully governs it. The regularities we call laws of nature are his steady ordinances, working through real created causes.
- **one-and-many** (34w): God is one being in three persons, so unity and distinction are both basic in him. The created world's real unity and real variety reflect that, faintly, as the work of one wise Creator.
- **knowledge-possible** (34w): Human beings can know reality because God made both the world and the minds that encounter it. Our knowledge is real but finite, and sin distorts how we use it without making knowledge impossible.
- **logic-binding** (36w): Logic binds every mind because the one wise God made both the world and the minds that reason about it. Its laws are discovered, not invented, and they hold of things, not merely of our thoughts.
- **induction** (33w): Christians expect nature's patterns to continue because a faithful God established its ordinary order and sustains it. That gives a reason to expect regularity; it is not a proof that escapes Hume's circle.
- **ultimate-authority** (36w): God is the final authority, and his written Word is the supreme rule by which every human authority, tradition and argument is judged. Reason and the church serve that Word; they do not sit above it.
- **what-is-man** (27w): Man is a creature of dust and divine breath, one person of body and soul, made male and female as God's image; fallen, he remains that image.
- **why-alive** (25w): Humans are alive because God made them for himself, to glorify and enjoy him forever, receiving ordinary life from him and returning it to him.
- **offspring-family** (22w): Marriage and procreation are creation ordinances, blessed before the Fall; the household passes on covenant faith, and singleness can be a gift.
- **love-beauty-creativity** (27w): Humans love, seek beauty and make things because they image a God who is love, whose glory is beautiful; these gifts are good when ordered to him.
- **worship** (26w): Humans worship because they were made to know and serve God; idolatry is that religious nature turned to created things, and worship belongs to God alone.
- **great-and-terrible** (29w): Man is great because he remains God's image-bearer, and terrible because that one created nature is corrupted at the root of the will, yet restrained and gifted by God.
- **know-the-good** (23w): Moral awareness is a real but damaged knowledge of God's law: conscience survives the Fall, while Scripture corrects its partial and self-serving judgments.
- **fail-the-good** (24w): We can recognize moral truth while loving what opposes it: sin disorders the will, and even renewed believers must struggle against its remaining power.
- **evil** (36w): At root, evil is sin, culpable departure from God's law, and it corrupts the good gifts God created. God governs even evil, as at the cross, without being its author, and Christ will finally defeat it.
- **suffering** (36w): Suffering belongs to a world disordered by sin yet governed by God. The Son entered it and suffered for sinners, so believers' afflictions can become discipline rather than condemnation, though their particular purposes often stay hidden.
- **death** (23w): Death is a judgment within humanity's fallen condition and an enemy; for believers its sting is removed, without making bodily death itself good.
- **self-deception** (19w): We can resist truths we recognize because sinful love redirects attention, interpretation, and self-examination toward protecting what we want.
- **self-salvation** (26w): Man's problem is guilt and corruption before God; Christ reconciles and renews his people through grace, producing real faith, repentance and obedience rather than self-earned rescue.
- **guilt** (31w): God forgives real guilt without setting justice aside. Christ bore the judgment sinners deserve and gives his righteousness to all who trust him; repentance and repair follow, but never buy pardon.
- **after-death** (22w): Death separates body and soul; believers are with Christ while awaiting the resurrection of the same embodied person and the final judgment.
- **final-end** (26w): The final good is holy, embodied communion with God in Christ, shared with his people in renewed creation; everlasting judgment is also part of the claim.
- **revelation** (39w): Yes. God shows himself in the world he made, and he has spoken and acted in history, above all in Jesus Christ, whom Scripture records and interprets. Its central claim, the resurrection, is a public event open to testing.
- **history** (35w): History is linear and going somewhere. It has a real beginning in creation, a center in Christ's death and resurrection, and a real end in judgment and new creation, and God's providence directs it throughout.
- **jesus** (36w): Jesus is the eternal Son of God made man, the promised Messiah, who was crucified for sinners, raised bodily from the dead and exalted as Lord, and who will return to judge and renew the world.

### naturalism

- **ultimate-reality** (33w): Nature is all there is: no God or supernatural order stands behind it. Most naturalists hold that everything is physical or built from the physical, though they still debate minds, numbers and laws.
- **something-rather-than-nothing** (35w): Naturalists do not claim that something came from nothing. Most hold that reality, or its basic laws and state, may simply exist without a deeper reason, or doubt that 'nothing' was ever a real alternative.
- **ultimate-personal** (30w): Naturalism finds no good reason to think a mind or person underlies nature. Minds appeared late and locally in cosmic history, as products of natural processes rather than their source.
- **order** (37w): Nature's regularities are a basic feature of the world, not the work of a designer. Naturalists disagree about what laws are, summaries of what happens or real necessities in nature, but agree that no designer is needed.
- **one-and-many** (29w): Naturalism has no special doctrine of the one and the many. Its answer is that reality is one natural, causally connected world, organized into many real levels and things.
- **knowledge-possible** (27w): Human cognition is part of nature. Perception, memory and reasoning are evolved capacities, reliable enough to live by and continually corrected through experience, criticism and scientific inquiry.
- **logic-binding** (35w): Naturalists agree that logic binds but disagree about why: because it states basic facts about the world, because it is built into our language or cognition, or because it is our most deeply entrenched belief.
- **induction** (35w): Induction works because nature is in fact regular, but naturalists disagree on how to justify expecting that: some accept a benign circle, some infer real laws, and some say induction is simply what reasonableness means.
- **ultimate-authority** (30w): Claims answer finally to a revisable network of observation, reasoning, coherence, predictive success and public inquiry. Naturalism has no final text or institution, and it places no belief beyond revision.
- **what-is-man** (31w): A human being is an evolved social animal whose mind and self are realized in a living body and brain; naturalists differ over how persons persist and how conscious experience fits.
- **why-alive** (25w): No cosmic purpose explains human existence; meaning is conferred by what people care about or found in objectively valuable goods such as love and knowledge.
- **offspring-family** (24w): Reproduction and family are products of evolution, from sexual selection to kin-directed care; their origin explains them without settling how families ought to be.
- **love-beauty-creativity** (26w): Love, the sense of beauty and creativity are evolved capacities shaped by culture; their origin does not make them illusions, and no single theory is settled.
- **worship** (25w): Religious devotion is a natural product of human cognition and emotion; its naturalness neither proves nor disproves religion, and humanists may revere human ideals instead.
- **great-and-terrible** (25w): The same evolved social and cognitive capacities that make conscience, cooperation and culture possible can also be recruited for partiality, deception, dominance and organized violence.
- **know-the-good** (34w): People learn right from wrong through evolved social feelings like sympathy, corrected by reflection, experience and a more impartial view. Realists hold that this can track objective moral facts; anti-realists, that it projects values.
- **fail-the-good** (22w): Moral judgment is one influence among competing motives: immediate desire can override reflective concern, while lasting social feelings return afterward as remorse.
- **evil** (34w): Evil names the gravest harms people inflict, explained by natural motives such as limited sympathy, competition for scarce goods and aggression, not by a supernatural power; naturalists differ over whether its wrongness is objective.
- **suffering** (30w): Suffering is what vulnerable, sentient creatures undergo through injury, disease, aging and harm done by others. It has natural causes to understand and relieve, but no providential purpose behind it.
- **death** (25w): Death ends a person's life through natural processes; its badness is disputed, especially between the absence of later experience and the loss of possible goods.
- **self-deception** (23w): Desire, fear, and self-interest can bias how evidence is selected and interpreted, producing mistaken confidence without a deliberate plan to lie to oneself.
- **self-salvation** (25w): Naturalistic humanism seeks fallible personal and social improvement through knowledge, care and cooperation, with no supernatural salvation or guarantee that every loss will be repaired.
- **guilt** (19w): Real wrongdoing calls for acknowledgment, accountability and repair; interpersonal forgiveness can change a relationship without making the past innocent.
- **after-death** (35w): On the ordinary naturalist view, death ends the person: when the living body stops, no one remains to think or feel. Theories of personal identity differ, but neither bodily nor psychological accounts establish an afterlife.
- **final-end** (18w): Naturalistic humanism seeks finite flourishing, knowledge and humane relationships; it offers no guaranteed personal or cosmic final state.
- **revelation** (31w): Naturalism finds no good evidence that any god has revealed itself. It treats revelation claims as human testimony, and because experience strongly favors natural causes, such claims need unusually strong evidence.
- **history** (36w): Nature gives history no built-in purpose or final goal. Time has a physical direction and life has evolved, but any progress is made by human beings toward goals they value, and nothing guarantees it will last.
- **jesus** (33w): Jesus was a real first-century Jewish prophet who was crucified, and his followers very early came to believe he had risen. Naturalism explains that belief through visions, interpretation and legend, without a resurrection.

### judaism

- **ultimate-reality** (30w): There is one God, the Creator, and the world he made. God depends on nothing, everything else depends wholly on him, and nothing in creation shares his kind of existence.
- **something-rather-than-nothing** (30w): The world exists because God willed it into being. The main line of rabbinic and medieval thought holds that he created it from nothing, with no eternal material beside him.
- **ultimate-personal** (32w): Yes. The God of the Torah speaks, commands, loves and judges, and is addressed in prayer. Jewish philosophers disagree over how far that personal language describes God himself rather than his actions.
- **order** (39w): The world is orderly because God made it with wisdom and set fixed laws for heaven and earth. Jewish thinkers disagree over how much of life runs by that natural order and how much by God's hidden, direct care.
- **one-and-many** (34w): God alone is truly one, with a unity unlike anything in the world. The many belong to creation, which God made as one ordered whole, so plurality is real but always created and dependent.
- **knowledge-possible** (33w): People can know the world because God made them with senses and reason that genuinely work. Wisdom is God's gift, reliable testimony carries knowledge between generations, and some things remain beyond human reach.
- **logic-binding** (35w): Rabbinic tradition reasons rigorously but has no doctrine of why logic binds. Its philosophers held that the impossible stays impossible even for God, because it is nothing at all, not a limit on his power.
- **induction** (36w): Jewish tradition expects the world to keep its course because God established fixed laws for heaven and earth. That is a theological ground for expecting regularity; classical Jewish sources do not take up Hume's problem itself.
- **ultimate-authority** (35w): Final authority belongs to God's revelation at Sinai: the Written Torah and the Oral Torah given with it, as interpreted by the authorized sages. Reason serves the interpretation of that revelation rather than rivalling it.
- **what-is-man** (27w): The human being is earth and divine breath, made in God's image as male and female, unique and free, joining earthly and heavenly traits in one life.
- **why-alive** (25w): Humans are created to serve God through Torah, worship and kindness, doing every ordinary deed for the sake of heaven; Maimonides adds knowledge of God.
- **offspring-family** (27w): Procreation is a commandment rooted in creation, marriage is joy and blessing, and the home passes on the covenant, though the sages record disputes and an exception.
- **love-beauty-creativity** (24w): Love of God and neighbor is commanded, beauty is blessed and offered back to God in beautified commandments, and creativity answers a divine mandate.
- **worship** (26w): Worship belongs to the one God alone; after the Temple it is above all prayer, the service of the heart, and idolatry begins as misdirected honor.
- **great-and-terrible** (30w): Each person bears God's image and a created inclination that both builds the world and pulls toward sin; free and responsible, he is to govern it through Torah and repentance.
- **know-the-good** (37w): A person knows the good through Torah, which is near, to be understood and done, and through reason, which can grasp duties such as gratitude and avoiding harm; some commandments are known only because God gave them.
- **fail-the-good** (24w): A person can intend to obey God while an opposing inclination gains strength through habit; Torah, divine help, and disciplined choice address that struggle.
- **evil** (38w): Moral evil is a person's own wrongdoing: the inclination, good in its place, grows into a master when indulged and is governed through Torah and God's help. Calamity comes under the one Creator's rule, not a rival power.
- **suffering** (20w): Jewish sources offer several accounts of suffering—retribution, loving discipline, human wrongdoing, and acknowledged mystery—without authorizing one explanation for every sufferer.
- **death** (34w): Death is the appointed limit of a finite life, calling for wisdom, repentance and accountability before God. The sages differ over its tie to sin, and the tradition awaits the resurrection of the dead.
- **self-deception** (19w): The inclination can make repeated wrongdoing seem permissible and turn self-approval into rationalization; reflection before God interrupts that process.
- **self-salvation** (28w): Sin and disordered inclination call for covenantal return: Torah, teshuvah, changed conduct and God's mercy restore nearness, with divine help and the World to Come within the hope.
- **guilt** (20w): Teshuvah joins confession and changed conduct with divine mercy; Yom Kippur does not bypass reconciliation for wrongs against another person.
- **after-death** (41w): After death each person faces God's judgment, and the dead will be raised; the Mishnah excludes from the World to Come anyone who denies that resurrection is taught in the Torah. How resurrection, the soul and that World relate is disputed.
- **final-end** (41w): The final good is life with God in the World to Come, beyond the messianic age. Maimonides sees it as the soul's incorporeal knowledge of God; Saadia and Nachmanides give the raised body a share in that life with the soul.
- **revelation** (38w): Yes. God revealed himself publicly to the whole people of Israel at Mount Sinai, and the Torah has been handed down from that event. Its warrant is the shared witness of an entire nation, not one prophet's wonders.
- **history** (34w): History is linear and covenantal, moving from creation through exile toward redemption. God will restore Israel under a messianic king, and beyond that lie the resurrection of the dead and the World to Come.
- **jesus** (26w): Jesus is not the Messiah and is not divine. The redemption the prophets promised has not come, and the unity of God rules out any incarnation.

### islam

- **ultimate-reality** (26w): God alone is uncreated, self-sufficient and beyond comparison. Everything else is his creation, brought into being by his command and dependent on him at every moment.
- **something-rather-than-nothing** (35w): The world exists because God willed it and said 'Be.' Muslim thinkers agree on that dependence but disagree about its timeline: a world with a first moment, an eternal emanation, or God creating without beginning.
- **ultimate-personal** (32w): Yes. God is the Ever-Living who knows, hears, sees, wills, speaks and shows mercy. Yet 'there is nothing like Him,' so his personal life is affirmed without picturing him in human terms.
- **order** (38w): The world is orderly because God creates all things in due measure and his way does not change. Muslim thinkers disagree about whether created things have real causal powers or God directly creates each event by his custom.
- **one-and-many** (33w): God is absolutely one, without partner or rival. The many are his creatures, each a sign of his one wisdom and each praising him, so plurality is real but always created and dependent.
- **knowledge-possible** (32w): People can know because God gave them senses, reason and an inborn disposition toward truth, and filled the world with signs. Knowledge comes through perception, reliable report and reasoning; revelation completes it.
- **logic-binding** (33w): Islamic thought treats the basic laws of reasoning as immediate knowledge that God built into every sound mind. Its scholars all accepted valid inference but disagreed over whether Aristotelian logic deserves special authority.
- **induction** (33w): Muslim theologians expect nature's patterns to continue because God keeps a constant custom in creating. They disagreed over whether created causes have real powers of their own or merely accompany what God creates.
- **ultimate-authority** (34w): The Qur'an and the authentic Sunna of the Prophet are the final authorities. Reason recognizes that revelation is genuine and helps interpret it, and the scholars' consensus and methods extend it to new cases.
- **what-is-man** (26w): The human being is clay given life by God's breathed spirit, honored and born on the fitra; the real self is the heart, accountable to God.
- **why-alive** (23w): God created humans to worship Him, needing nothing from them; life is a test, and al-Ghazali makes love of God its final aim.
- **offspring-family** (27w): Marriage is a divine sign of tranquility and love and the way of the prophets; children are blessing and test, and parents are honored next to God.
- **love-beauty-creativity** (26w): God is the Most Loving and loves beauty; human love and delight in beauty reflect Him, and al-Ghazali makes every true love finally love of God.
- **worship** (27w): Humans were created to worship God alone; since a willing being always loves something as its god, the choice is between worship of the One and shirk.
- **great-and-terrible** (36w): One created nature, honored by God and born on the fitra, holds both a longing to know and a craving to dominate; evil is that created power misdirected, and each soul answers for its own deeds.
- **know-the-good** (38w): God creates every person with a natural disposition (fitra) toward him and a soul inspired to know its own rebellion and piety; revelation guides both. The schools differ over how far reason knows good and evil without revelation.
- **fail-the-good** (25w): Moral awareness can be overridden by desire and habits that darken the heart; responsibility remains because recognizing the good does not prevent choosing against it.
- **evil** (30w): Nothing, good or ill, happens outside God's decree, yet the wrongdoer is truly answerable and blameworthy. Sunni theologians differ over how God's creating of acts and human responsibility fit together.
- **suffering** (23w): Suffering can be testing, consequence, or expiation under God's decree; gratitude and patience express the believer's response without revealing every affliction's particular purpose.
- **death** (22w): God appoints death within a life of testing and accountability; mortality is unavoidable and is not explained as punishment for inherited guilt.
- **self-deception** (25w): A heart shaped by desire can call corruption reform and mistake misguided action for good, even while its bearer retains grounds for recognizing the truth.
- **self-salvation** (23w): Weakness, sin and forgetfulness call for God's guidance, faith and tawba; genuine effort and purification matter, but salvation ultimately depends on divine mercy.
- **guilt** (17w): Tawba receives God's mercy through sincere return, while forgiveness does not make victims' rights or accountability disappear.
- **after-death** (22w): Death is followed by a barrier until bodily resurrection and judgment; divine mercy and permitted intercession remain central to the final outcome.
- **final-end** (23w): The final good includes enduring paradise, God's good pleasure and, in Sunni teaching, seeing God; judgment and divine mercy qualify who attains it.
- **revelation** (30w): Yes. The Qur'an is God's final revelation, sent down to Muhammad. Its matchless character, consistency and message, together with the Prophet's life and signs, show that it comes from God.
- **history** (33w): History is a single line of sacred history, from creation through God's messengers to Muhammad, ending in the Hour of resurrection and judgment. Within it, peoples rise and fall under God's moral law.
- **jesus** (39w): Jesus is the Messiah and a uniquely honored prophet born of Mary, but not God or God's Son. The standard Muslim reading holds that his enemies did not kill or crucify him, and that God raised him to himself.

### hinduism

- **ultimate-reality** (30w): Hindu Vedānta traditions locate ultimate reality in Brahman, the source, support and end of all things, but disagree about whether the world and individual selves are finally distinct from Brahman.
- **something-rather-than-nothing** (37w): Most Hindu traditions do not ask how something began from nothing. Being has always been: the world flows from Brahman and returns to it in beginningless cycles, and the Lord creates not from need but as play.
- **ultimate-personal** (38w): In theistic Vedānta, yes: for Rāmānuja and Madhva the supreme reality is a personal Lord. Advaita treats the personal Lord as real at the level of worship and ordinary experience, but the highest Brahman as beyond all qualities.
- **order** (40w): Hindu traditions root the world's order in Brahman: a cosmic order (ṛta), the Lord's inner rule and the moral law of karma hold it together. It is a moral as well as a natural order, not simply laws of physics.
- **one-and-many** (33w): All Vedānta schools trace the many to one Brahman, but differ on what the many are: names and forms of Brahman (Advaita), Brahman's real body (Rāmānuja), or realities eternally distinct from God (Madhva).
- **knowledge-possible** (35w): Reliable means of knowledge, chiefly perception, inference and trustworthy testimony, put the knower in touch with the world and correct their own errors. Advaita adds that this holds only short of final knowledge of Brahman.
- **logic-binding** (34w): Reasoning binds because a good inference tracks a real connection in the world, such as the invariable link between smoke and fire. Indian schools study logic as the account of how inference yields knowledge.
- **induction** (33w): Inference reaches the unseen because some connections, like smoke and fire, hold without exception, and careful observation checked for hidden conditions can establish them. Materialist critics denied that this can ever be known.
- **ultimate-authority** (34w): For matters beyond ordinary perception, especially dharma and Brahman, the major orthodox traditions give final authority to śruti, the revealed Veda, though they disagree sharply over why it is authoritative and what it means.
- **what-is-man** (39w): A human being is an eternal conscious self embodied in a changing body-mind and reborn under karma. For Advaita that Self is one consciousness in all bodies; for Rāmānuja and Madhva souls are many, real and dependent on God.
- **why-alive** (32w): The rare human birth is for life's legitimate aims and, beyond them, liberation, which Advaita understands as knowing the Self to be Brahman and Rāmānuja as the soul's dependent enjoyment of God.
- **offspring-family** (25w): Household life is a sacred stage of dharma that supports all others and repays debts to the ancestors, within a path that culminates in renunciation.
- **love-beauty-creativity** (22w): Love is above all bhakti, which Rāmānuja says the Lord himself awakens; glory springs from the divine splendor, and Brahman is bliss.
- **worship** (40w): In the Gītā, worship is loving offering to the Lord, from a leaf or a flower to every act of life. Rāmānuja treats such devotion as the soul's highest relation to God; Advaita values it as preparation for liberating knowledge.
- **great-and-terrible** (40w): Humans are great because each is a conscious self that longs for the immortal, and terrible because desire, karma and ignorance obscure that self. The schools differ over the self's relation to God and whether all souls share one destiny.
- **know-the-good** (31w): The good is dharma, known above all from scripture, received tradition and the conduct of the wise. Discerning it means choosing the good over the merely pleasant rather than following desire.
- **fail-the-good** (37w): The Gītā's answer is desire: born of restless passion, it veils knowledge through the senses, mind and understanding and turns to anger when frustrated, so a person can see the right and still be driven against it.
- **evil** (40w): Evil comes from desire, anger and greed and from an intellect that takes wrong for right. Beneath them lies ignorance, which Advaita traces to confusing the Self with body and mind, and Rāmānuja to karma obscuring a real soul's knowledge.
- **suffering** (37w): Suffering belongs to embodied, changing existence and to the fruits of past deeds, which Vedānta says the Lord dispenses justly rather than arbitrarily. Advaita adds that its root is the Self's mistaken identification with body and mind.
- **death** (38w): The body dies but the self does not: it passes to another body as desire and action have shaped it. Death therefore belongs to the round of bondage, and the hope is release from rebirth, not mere survival.
- **self-deception** (38w): In the Gītā, desire veils understanding and a darkened intellect can take wrong for right, so a person keeps some knowledge while attachment steers its use. Advaita adds a deeper confusion of the Self with body and mind.
- **self-salvation** (35w): The human problem is bondage to rebirth through desire, ignorance and karma. The remedy is liberation, reached in Advaita through knowledge of the Self and in theistic Vedānta through devotion, refuge and the Lord's grace.
- **guilt** (30w): Wrongdoing binds the self through karma. For Advaita, liberating knowledge removes karma's power to bind; for Rāmānuja, the Lord, approached in knowledge and devotion, removes sin and his own displeasure.
- **after-death** (36w): At death the self takes another body as its desires and deeds have conditioned it. Liberation ends rebirth: as nondual realization in Advaita, or as the soul's lasting, dependent bliss with the Lord in theistic Vedānta.
- **final-end** (32w): In classical Vedānta the final end is liberation (moksha) from rebirth: for Advaita, realizing that the Self is nondual; for Rāmānuja and Madhva, the liberated soul's lasting, dependent bliss with the Lord.
- **revelation** (31w): Hindu traditions regard the Veda as śruti, an eternal disclosure of truths ordinary perception cannot reach, found by ancient seers rather than composed. The schools disagree about why it is authoritative.
- **history** (31w): Cosmic history repeats through vast cycles of creation, decline, dissolution and renewal. God descends in every age to restore dharma, but the cycles reach no final goal; liberation means escaping them.
- **jesus** (38w): Classical Hindu texts say nothing about Jesus. Modern Hindu thinkers have honored him in different ways, as a moral teacher, a divine teacher or one incarnation among many, while rejecting the claim that he is God's only Son.

### buddhism

- **ultimate-reality** (37w): The Pāli discourses and the classical Indian schools place no permanent being or substance beneath reality. Conditioned things arise from causes and pass away, and liberation is freedom from that process, not union with a hidden ground.
- **something-rather-than-nothing** (30w): Buddhism generally rejects the demand for a first beginning. The round of rebirth has no known start, and things arise from conditions, not from a creator and not from nothing.
- **ultimate-personal** (29w): Buddhism has no personal creator or personal ground of reality. Buddhas and bodhisattvas are persons revered with deep devotion, but nirvāṇa and emptiness are neither persons nor impersonal gods.
- **order** (28w): Reality is orderly because things arise from conditions in a constant, reliable pattern. That pattern holds whether or not anyone discovers it, and no lawgiver stands behind it.
- **one-and-many** (35w): Things are many because they arise from many conditions, and connected because each depends on others. Madhyamaka finds no hidden One beneath them; Huayan holds that each thing contains all others without losing its place.
- **knowledge-possible** (25w): Buddhism treats knowledge as tested cognition: perception and inference can be reliable when checked against experience, and liberating truth is ultimately to be directly realized.
- **logic-binding** (34w): A sound inference binds because it rests on real relations between things: identity of nature, or cause and effect. Buddhism built a sophisticated logic on this, though some schools deny logic any ultimate foundation.
- **induction** (35w): Buddhist logicians held that inference reaches unobserved cases when it rests on real relations of identity or cause and effect, which are discovered through experience. Whether this escapes the problem of induction is still disputed.
- **ultimate-authority** (26w): The Dhamma is the final standard, but its truth is meant to be tested and directly realized rather than accepted merely because a teacher said it.
- **what-is-man** (29w): A human being is a process of five aggregates, none of them a self, yet a real conventional agent; human birth is prized as the rare chance to awaken.
- **why-alive** (25w): No creator assigns life a purpose; the rare human birth is the chance for the noble quest of awakening, which Mahāyāna pursues for all beings.
- **offspring-family** (27w): Lay family life has a full ethic of reciprocal duties, with parents revered like gods; but there is no command to procreate, and going forth is higher.
- **love-beauty-creativity** (22w): Love at its best is boundless goodwill, not attachment; beauty is real but impermanent, and arts serve devotion while monastics renounce shows.
- **worship** (26w): Buddhism worships no creator, yet treats reverence as a human need: the Buddha honors the Dhamma, practice is the highest honor, and devotion takes many forms.
- **great-and-terrible** (28w): Buddhist traditions explain goodness and cruelty through conditioned mental states: greed, hatred and delusion yield harmful action, their opposites yield wholesome action, and these defilements can be uprooted.
- **know-the-good** (34w): Actions rooted in greed, hatred and delusion are unwholesome, and those free of them wholesome. Their harm or benefit to oneself and others shows the difference, and reflection, wise counsel and practice train judgment.
- **fail-the-good** (25w): Understanding that greed, aversion, and delusion are harmful does not uproot their conditioned tendencies; they can still govern action until deeper cultivation changes the mind.
- **evil** (35w): Evil is unwholesome intention and action, such as killing, stealing and lying, rooted in greed, hatred and delusion, and harmful to oneself and others; the schools differ over how such defilement relates to ultimate reality.
- **suffering** (23w): Suffering involves bodily pain and the wider instability of clinging existence; craving, aversion, and ignorance add distress which the path seeks to end.
- **death** (22w): Death arises with birth in conditioned existence; mindful recollection should produce diligence, while the answer to mortality is not an immortal self.
- **self-deception** (22w): Ignorance and craving can distort perception and sustain a mistaken self-view beneath deliberate judgment; possessing correct information does not uproot those tendencies.
- **self-salvation** (36w): Suffering (dukkha) arises from craving and ignorance and ends through the Eightfold Path of ethics, meditation and wisdom. Pure Land relies on Amida Buddha's vow, the Other Power Shinran stresses for those who cannot free themselves.
- **guilt** (33w): Wrongdoing calls for honest recognition, disclosure to a teacher or companion, restraint, and cultivation that can weaken its karmic results. One Pure Land sutra adds Amitābha's help at death even for grave offenders.
- **after-death** (19w): Ordinary rebirth is conditioned continuity without a migrating self; the liberated one's fate resists simple survival or annihilation descriptions.
- **final-end** (25w): The final good is release from craving and ignorance; Mahāyāna foregrounds awakening for others, and Pure Land birth serves Buddhahood rather than an eternal ego.
- **revelation** (34w): Buddhism does not teach revelation from a creator. The Buddha claims to have discovered a timeless path, and his teaching is recognized by its agreement with the Dhamma and confirmed by realization in practice.
- **history** (33w): Saṃsāra has no known beginning, and world-systems arise and pass away over immense ages. Buddhas and their teaching appear and decline within these cycles, and liberation escapes the process rather than completing it.
- **jesus** (33w): Buddhist canonical texts say nothing about Jesus. Modern Buddhist teachers often honor him as a great spiritual teacher, even an enlightened being, while rejecting the Christian beliefs in a Creator, incarnation and resurrection.
