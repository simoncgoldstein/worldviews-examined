All 28 answers were read in full, in question order. **Result: 0 BLOCKER, 0 IMPORTANT, 6 MINOR.**

## 1. Overall coherence

The lane reads as one recognizable family:

- a Nikāya/Theravāda baseline (no-self, dependent origination, the Four Truths, karma as intention, beginningless *saṃsāra*, *nibbāna* as release);
- Madhyamaka where metaphysics matters (emptiness as dependent arising; two truths);
- Yogācāra where mind matters (*kliṣṭa-manas*; cognition-only);
- Dharmakīrti where knowledge matters;
- Buddha-nature, Tiantai, Zen (Dōgen), Pure Land (Shinran) and Tibetan (Tsongkhapa) as named, bounded strands.

**Different schools are not silently combined (Audit 12 passes).** Scopes name the baseline. The Pure Land contrast is labelled "materially different" in `self-salvation`, `guilt`, `after-death` and `final-end`.

## Diagnosis and remedy

- **Diagnosis:** *dukkha* arises from craving, aversion and ignorance (above all, grasping at a self), with latent tendencies that outlast understanding (MN 14).
- **Remedy:** the Eightfold Path (ethics, concentration, wisdom) with teacher and community, or reliance on Amida's vow (Other Power) in Pure Land.
- **Final state:** *nirvāṇa*, which refuses both eternalist and annihilationist descriptions (MN 72; SN 22.85), or Pure Land birth toward Buddhahood.
- **Coherence:** high. The remedy targets the stated causes.
- **The comparison the corpus draws well:** the flagship's question about "a guilt no human party can lift" (Psalm 51:4) and Shinran's closeness to grace. The comparison it assumes but does not explain: whether the one who saves is also the God who was wronged, which again turns on the unexpounded Christian doctrine of satisfaction (XQ-08).
